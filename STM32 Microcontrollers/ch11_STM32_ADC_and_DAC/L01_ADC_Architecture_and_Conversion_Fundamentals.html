2. What is an Analog Signal?

An analog signal is a continuous signal whose amplitude can vary over a range of values. Many physical quantities produce analog signals.

Examples

  • Temperature sensor output voltage.
  • Light sensor output.
  • Microphone signal.
  • Battery voltage.
  • Pressure sensor output.
  • Potentiometer voltage.

For example, a sensor may produce a voltage between 0 V and 3.3 V depending on the measured physical quantity.

Main Components

  • Analog input: Signal supplied to the ADC pin.
  • Sample-and-hold: Samples the input and holds it during conversion.
  • Conversion circuit: Produces the digital representation.
  • Result register: Stores the converted result.
  • Reference voltage: Defines the ADC conversion range.
  • Control logic: Manages conversion and status flags.
The exact internal ADC architecture and sampling implementation vary across STM32 families and ADC instances.

6. ADC Conversion Process

The ADC converts an analog input into a digital code through several stages.

Step 1: Analog Input

An external sensor supplies an analog voltage to an ADC-capable STM32 pin.

Step 2: Sampling

The ADC samples the input voltage. A sampling circuit may hold the sampled voltage while the conversion takes place.

Step 3: Quantization

The sampled voltage is represented by one of the discrete digital levels available at the selected ADC resolution.

Step 4: Encoding

The selected level is represented as a binary number.

Step 5: Digital Output

The conversion result becomes available through an ADC data register or a configured data-transfer mechanism.

7. Types of ADC Architecture

ADC Type Working Principle Typical Characteristics
Flash ADC Uses parallel comparators. Very fast, high hardware complexity.
SAR ADC Uses successive approximation. Good speed and resolution balance.
Dual-Slope ADC Uses integration and timing. Good noise rejection, slower conversion.
Sigma-Delta ADC Uses oversampling and digital filtering. High resolution, often lower bandwidth.
Pipelined ADC Uses multiple conversion stages. High throughput in suitable applications.
Many STM32 microcontrollers use SAR ADC architecture. Always verify the architecture and features for the exact STM32 device.

8. Successive Approximation Register (SAR) ADC

SAR ADC is a common architecture in microcontrollers. It uses a comparator, digital-to-analog conversion circuitry and a successive approximation register. 

             Analog Input
                  |
                  v
            +-----------+
            | Comparator|
            +-----+-----+
                  ^
                  |
            +-----+-----+
            | Internal  |
            | DAC       |
            +-----+-----+
                  ^
                  |
            +-----+-----+
            | SAR Logic |
            +-----+-----+
                  |
                  v
             Digital Output

Working Principle

  1. The SAR logic begins with the most significant bit.
  2. It temporarily sets that bit and generates a trial voltage using the internal DAC.
  3. The comparator compares the trial voltage with the sampled input.
  4. The SAR logic keeps or clears the trial bit according to the comparison.
  5. The process continues toward the least significant bit.
  6. The final register contains the digital conversion result.

Illustrative 4-Bit Example

Assume an ideal 4-bit ADC with a 0 V to 16 V conversion range and an input of 11 V. The SAR tests the binary bits from the most significant bit downward.

Step Trial Code Trial Level Decision
1 1000 8 Keep MSB
2 1100 12 Clear second bit
3 1010 10 Keep third bit
4 1011 11 Keep LSB

Final result: 1011 (binary) = 11 (decimal).

This is an idealized illustration of SAR bit decisions. Actual ADC transfer thresholds, offset, gain error and quantization conventions affect real conversion results.

9. Sampling and Hold Circuit

Sampling means measuring an analog signal at a particular instant. A sample-and-hold circuit maintains the sampled voltage during the conversion interval.

Why is Sampling Required?

  • To capture the input voltage at a defined instant.
  • To keep the input sufficiently stable during conversion.
  • To support accurate conversion within the ADC's specifications.

Sampling Time

Sampling time is the interval during which the ADC input sampling circuit acquires the analog voltage.

A high-impedance sensor may require a longer sampling time so that the ADC input capacitor can settle sufficiently close to the actual input voltage.

10. Sampling Theorem

The Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem states that a band-limited signal must be sampled at a rate greater than twice its highest frequency component for ideal reconstruction.

Nyquist condition: 

Sampling Frequency > 2 × Maximum Signal Frequency

Example

If a signal contains frequency components up to 1 kHz, its sampling frequency must be greater than 2 kHz under the ideal theorem assumptions.

Practical systems generally sample faster and use an analog anti-aliasing filter. The ADC's acquisition time, conversion time and total scan rate must also be considered.

11. STM32 ADC Peripheral Overview

STM32 ADC peripherals convert analog voltages applied to supported ADC input pins into digital conversion results.

Common ADC Features

  • Multiple analog input channels.
  • Selectable conversion resolution on supported devices.
  • Single and continuous conversion modes.
  • Scan conversion sequences.
  • Software and hardware triggering.
  • Interrupt-based conversion.
  • DMA-based data transfer.
  • Analog watchdog on supported devices.
ADC channel count, resolution, conversion modes, internal reference channels and sampling-time options depend on the particular STM32 model.

13. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is ADC?

ADC converts an analog input signal into a digital representation.

Q2. Why is ADC required in a microcontroller?

It allows the microcontroller to process analog measurements from sensors and external circuits.

Q3. What is a SAR ADC?

A SAR ADC uses successive approximation logic and a comparator-based conversion process to determine the digital output.

Q4. What is sampling?

Sampling is measuring an analog signal at discrete time instants.

Q5. What is a sample-and-hold circuit?

It captures an analog voltage and holds it sufficiently stable during conversion.

Q6. What is quantization?

Quantization maps a continuous sampled amplitude to one of a finite number of digital levels.

Q7. What is the Nyquist sampling criterion?

For an ideally band-limited signal, the sampling frequency must be greater than twice its highest frequency component for ideal reconstruction.

Q8. Which ADC architecture is commonly used in STM32?

Many STM32 devices use SAR ADC architecture.

Q9. What is ADC reference voltage?

It is the reference used by the ADC to define its conversion range.

Q10. What is an ADC channel?

It is a selectable analog input path associated with an ADC peripheral.

Q11. What happens if the ADC input exceeds its permitted range?

The input may be clipped at the converter limits or cause electrical damage if absolute maximum ratings are violated.

Q12. What is aliasing?

Aliasing occurs when sampling is insufficient to distinguish higher-frequency signal components, causing them to appear as incorrect lower-frequency components.
