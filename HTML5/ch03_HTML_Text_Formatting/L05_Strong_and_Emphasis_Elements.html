Strong and Emphasis Elements in HTML

HTML5 – Complete Study Course | Chapter 3: HTML Text Formatting

1. Introduction

HTML provides semantic elements to communicate the importance and emphasis of text. Two important elements are:

  • <strong> – Indicates strong importance, seriousness or urgency.
  • <em> – Indicates stress emphasis.

Browsers normally display strong text in bold and emphasized text in italic. However, their main purpose is semantic rather than purely visual.

2. The Strong Element (<strong>)

The <strong> element indicates that its content has strong importance, seriousness or urgency.

Syntax

<strong>Important text</strong>

Example 1: Basic Strong Element

<p>
    <strong>Warning:</strong>
    Save your work before closing the application.
</p>
Expected Output:

Warning: Save your work before closing the application.

Example 2: Important Instruction

<p>
    <strong>Important:</strong>
    Do not switch off the computer during a system update.
</p>
Expected Output:

Important: Do not switch off the computer during a system update.

Example 3: Nested Strong Elements

Strong importance can be nested to indicate greater relative importance for a particular part of the content. 

<p>
    This is an important notice.
    <strong>
        Read this <strong>critical instruction</strong>
        carefully.
    </strong>
</p>
Expected Output:

This is an important notice. Read this critical instruction carefully.

Remember: Use <strong> when the content itself has strong importance. Do not use it merely to make ordinary text bold.

3. The Emphasis Element (<em>)

The <em> element indicates stress emphasis. It can change the intended meaning of a sentence depending on which word is emphasized.

Syntax

<em>Emphasized text</em>

Example 1: Basic Emphasis

<p>
    You <em>must</em> complete this task.
</p>
Expected Output:

You must complete this task.

Example 2: Emphasis Changes Meaning

The position of emphasis can change what a sentence communicates. 

<p>
    <em>I</em> submitted the assignment.
</p>

<p>
    I <em>submitted</em> the assignment.
</p>

<p>
    I submitted <em>the assignment</em>.
</p>
Expected Output:

I submitted the assignment.

I submitted the assignment.

I submitted the assignment.

Example 3: Nested Emphasis

Emphasis can be nested to indicate a stronger level of stress on a particular phrase. 

<p>
    This is <em>very important,
    and <em>extremely necessary</em>
    to understand.</em>
</p>
Expected Output:

This is very important, and extremely necessary to understand.

Remember: Use <em> when you want to stress a word or phrase as part of the sentence's intended meaning.

4. Difference Between <strong> and <em>

Feature <strong> <em>
Purpose Indicates strong importance, seriousness or urgency Indicates stress emphasis
Default appearance Bold Italic
Semantic meaning Importance Stress emphasis
Closing tag Required Required

5. Difference Between b, strong, i and em

These four elements may look similar in a browser, but they communicate different meanings.

Element Purpose Default Appearance
<b> Draws attention without adding importance Bold
<strong> Indicates strong importance Bold
<i> Sets text apart from normal prose Italic
<em> Indicates stress emphasis Italic

Example

<p>
    <b>HTML5</b> is used to structure web pages.
</p>

<p>
    <strong>Warning:</strong> Save your work.
</p>

<p>
    The term <i>frontend development</i>
    refers to the user-facing part of an application.
</p>

<p>
    You <em>must</em> understand HTML fundamentals.
</p>
Expected Output:

HTML5 is used to structure web pages.

Warning: Save your work.

The term frontend development refers to the user-facing part of an application.

You must understand HTML fundamentals.

6. Combining Strong and Emphasis Elements

You can combine <strong> and <em> when content is both important and stressed.

Example

<p>
    <strong>
        You <em>must</em> follow the safety instructions.
    </strong>
</p>
Expected Output:

You must follow the safety instructions.

7. Common Mistakes

  1. Using strong only for bold styling: Use CSS or b if there is no semantic importance.
  2. Using em only for italic styling: Use CSS or i when the text is set apart but is not stressed.
  3. Confusing importance with emphasis: Strong indicates importance; em indicates stress emphasis.
  4. Incorrect nesting: Always close nested elements in the reverse order in which they were opened.

8. Practical Example

Create a safety instruction page using strong and emphasis elements.

Complete HTML Program

<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
<head>
    <meta charset="UTF-8">
    <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">
    <title>Safety Instructions</title>
</head>
<body>

    <h1>Computer Lab Safety Instructions</h1>

    <p>
        <strong>Important:</strong>
        Follow all laboratory safety instructions.
    </p>

    <p>
        You <em>must</em> switch off the equipment
        before disconnecting cables.
    </p>

    <p>
        <strong>Warning:</strong>
        <em>Never touch exposed electrical wires.</em>
    </p>

    <p>
        <strong>Remember:</strong>
        Safety comes first.
    </p>

</body>
</html>
Expected Output:

Computer Lab Safety Instructions

Important: Follow all laboratory safety instructions.

You must switch off the equipment before disconnecting cables.

Warning: Never touch exposed electrical wires.

Remember: Safety comes first.

10. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is the purpose of the strong element?

It indicates that its content has strong importance, seriousness or urgency.

Q2. What is the purpose of the em element?

It indicates stress emphasis in a sentence.

Q3. What is the difference between strong and b?

strong indicates semantic importance, whereas b draws attention without adding importance.

Q4. What is the difference between em and i?

em indicates stress emphasis, whereas i sets text apart from normal prose.

Q5. What is the default appearance of strong?

Browsers normally display strong text in bold.

Q6. What is the default appearance of em?

Browsers normally display emphasized text in italic.

Q7. Can strong elements be nested?

Yes. Nesting can indicate a greater relative level of importance.

Q8. Can em elements be nested?

Yes. Nesting can indicate a stronger level of stress emphasis.

Q9. Are strong and em semantic HTML elements?

Yes. Both elements communicate meaning about their content.

Q10. Can CSS change the appearance of strong and em?

Yes. CSS can change their visual appearance without removing their semantic meaning.

11. Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)

  1. Which element indicates strong importance?
    • A. <b>
    • B. <strong>
    • C. <i>
    • D. <small>
    Answer: B. <strong>
  2. Which element indicates stress emphasis?
    • A. <em>
    • B. <b>
    • C. <hr>
    • D. <div>
    Answer: A. <em>
  3. What is the usual browser appearance of strong?
    • A. Underlined
    • B. Bold
    • C. Italic
    • D. Strikethrough
    Answer: B. Bold
  4. What is the usual browser appearance of em?
    • A. Bold
    • B. Underlined
    • C. Italic
    • D. Highlighted
    Answer: C. Italic
  5. Which element draws attention without indicating importance?
    • A. <strong>
    • B. <b>
    • C. <em>
    • D. <hr>
    Answer: B. <b>
  6. Which element sets text apart from normal prose?
    • A. <i>
    • B. <strong>
    • C. <hr>
    • D. <br>
    Answer: A. <i>
  7. Which pair is normally displayed in bold?
    • A. b and strong
    • B. i and em
    • C. br and hr
    • D. p and div
    Answer: A. b and strong
  8. Which pair is normally displayed in italic?
    • A. b and strong
    • B. i and em
    • C. p and h1
    • D. br and hr
    Answer: B. i and em
  9. Which elements communicate semantic meaning?
    • A. Only b
    • B. Only i
    • C. strong and em
    • D. None of these
    Answer: C. strong and em
  10. Can CSS change the visual appearance of strong and em?
    • A. Yes
    • B. No
    • C. Only strong
    • D. Only em
    Answer: A. Yes

12. Lesson Summary

  • <strong> indicates strong importance, seriousness or urgency.
  • <em> indicates stress emphasis.
  • Strong is normally displayed in bold.
  • Em is normally displayed in italic.
  • Strong and em are semantic elements, not merely styling elements.
  • Both elements require opening and closing tags.
