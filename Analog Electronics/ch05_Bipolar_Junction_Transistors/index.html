Chapter Overview

A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device widely used for signal amplification and electronic switching. It operates using both electrons and holes as charge carriers.

This chapter develops a practical understanding of NPN and PNP transistors, operating regions, current relationships, transistor characteristics and the three basic transistor configurations. It also introduces BJT switching circuits and their practical applications.

Chapter Learning Outcomes

After completing all lectures, you should be able to:

  • Explain BJT physical construction and identify its three terminals.
  • Distinguish between NPN and PNP transistor operation.
  • Analyze cutoff, active and saturation operating regions.
  • Calculate emitter, base and collector currents.
  • Use alpha and beta relationships to solve numerical problems.
  • Interpret BJT input and output characteristic curves.
  • Analyze common-base, common-emitter and common-collector circuits.
  • Compare voltage gain, current gain and input/output impedance.
  • Design basic BJT switching circuits.
  • Perform practical transistor testing and fault diagnosis.
