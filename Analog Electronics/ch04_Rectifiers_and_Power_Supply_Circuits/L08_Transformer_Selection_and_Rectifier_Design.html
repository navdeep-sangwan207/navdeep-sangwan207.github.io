Lesson 8: LC and π Filters

Learn how inductors and capacitors reduce rectifier ripple and produce smoother DC output in power supply circuits.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain the need for LC and π filters.
  • Understand the working principle of an inductor filter.
  • Explain LC or L-section filter operation.
  • Understand the construction and operation of a π filter.
  • Compare capacitor, LC and π filters.
  • Identify practical applications and limitations of these filters.

2. Why Are LC and π Filters Required?

A rectifier converts AC into pulsating DC. Although the output has a DC component, it also contains an unwanted AC component called ripple.

A capacitor filter can reduce ripple, but some applications require a smoother DC output. Additional filtering components such as inductors and capacitors are therefore used.

Filter: An electronic circuit that reduces unwanted AC variations in the rectifier output while allowing the DC component to reach the load.

Common filter types

  1. Capacitor filter
  2. Inductor filter
  3. LC or L-section filter
  4. π (Pi) filter

This lesson focuses on LC and π filters.

3. Inductor Filter

An inductor filter uses an inductor connected in series between the rectifier and the load.

Conceptual representation of a series inductor filter. The load is connected across the output terminals.

Working principle

  • An inductor offers low opposition to steady DC after the transient has settled.
  • It offers greater opposition to changing current.
  • It opposes sudden changes in current through electromagnetic energy storage.
  • The ripple component is therefore reduced.
Inductive Reactance

XL = 2πfL

Where:
f = Frequency in Hz
L = Inductance in Henry
XL = Inductive reactance in Ohms
Important: Inductive reactance increases with frequency. Consequently, an inductor provides greater opposition to higher-frequency ripple components.

Applications

  • Power supply filtering.
  • High-current rectifier circuits.
  • Industrial DC power supplies.

4. LC or L-Section Filter

An LC filter consists of a series inductor followed by a shunt capacitor connected across the load. Its arrangement resembles the letter L, so it is also called an L-section filter.

Simplified LC filter: series inductor and shunt capacitor.

Working principle

  1. The rectifier produces pulsating DC containing ripple.
  2. The series inductor opposes changes in current and reduces the ripple component.
  3. The capacitor provides a low-impedance path for remaining ripple components.
  4. The load receives a smoother DC voltage.
Remember: The inductor is connected in series and the capacitor is connected in parallel with the load.

Advantages

  • Better ripple reduction than a simple filter in suitable designs.
  • Useful in power supplies requiring smoother DC output.
  • Can be designed for relatively high load currents.

Limitations

  • Inductors can be bulky and expensive.
  • Winding resistance causes voltage drop and power loss.
  • Filter performance depends on component values and load current.

5. π (Pi) Filter

A π filter is a filter network consisting of two capacitors with an inductor connected between them. Its circuit arrangement resembles the Greek letter π.

The common arrangement is C-L-C: a capacitor at the input, a series inductor in the middle and another capacitor at the output.

Simplified C-L-C π filter with two shunt capacitors and a series inductor.

Working principle

  1. The first capacitor C1 is connected across the rectifier output and reduces a significant portion of the ripple.
  2. The inductor L opposes the remaining ripple current.
  3. The second capacitor C2 bypasses much of the remaining ripple component.
  4. A smoother DC voltage is delivered to the load.
Key concept: The first capacitor performs initial smoothing, the inductor provides series filtering, and the second capacitor provides additional smoothing.

Advantages

  • Can provide strong ripple attenuation.
  • Produces a smoother DC output in suitable designs.
  • Useful in traditional linear power supply circuits.

Limitations

  • Requires more components than a simple capacitor filter.
  • Large capacitors can produce high initial charging current.
  • Inductor size, cost and resistance can be significant.
  • Output voltage may vary with load and component characteristics.

6. Comparison of Different Filters

Filter Components Basic Function
Capacitor filter Shunt capacitor Stores charge and supplies the load between peaks.
Inductor filter Series inductor Opposes ripple-current variations.
LC filter Series L and shunt C Combines inductive and capacitive filtering.
π filter C-L-C network Provides multiple stages of ripple attenuation.
Filter performance cannot be ranked accurately without specifying the component values, rectifier frequency, load resistance and operating current. A π filter often provides strong smoothing, but it is not automatically superior in every application.

7. Important Design Considerations

1. Inductance value

A larger inductance generally gives greater reactance at the ripple frequency, helping to reduce ripple current. However, the inductor must be designed for the DC current without excessive winding resistance or core saturation.

2. Capacitance value

Larger capacitance generally reduces output ripple. The capacitor must have suitable voltage, ripple-current and temperature ratings.

3. Ripple frequency

For a full-wave rectifier, the dominant ripple frequency is twice the AC supply frequency. For a half-wave rectifier, it is equal to the supply frequency.

4. Load current

Filter performance changes with load. Component selection must account for minimum and maximum load conditions.

5. Component ratings

  • Inductor current and saturation ratings.
  • Capacitor voltage and ripple-current ratings.
  • Temperature and power dissipation.
  • Startup and inrush current.

8. Practical Applications

  • Linear DC power supply circuits.
  • Industrial rectifier power supplies.
  • Audio amplifier power supply sections.
  • DC equipment requiring low ripple voltage.
  • Legacy electronic equipment and instrumentation.
Industry connection: In modern switch-mode power supplies (SMPS), inductors and capacitors are also widely used for output filtering. Their exact arrangement depends on the converter topology.

9. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is an LC filter?

An LC filter uses a series inductor and a shunt capacitor to reduce ripple in a rectifier output.

Q2. Why is an inductor connected in series?

It opposes changes in current and reduces the ripple current flowing toward the load.

Q3. What is a π filter?

It is a C-L-C filter consisting of two shunt capacitors and a series inductor.

Q4. Why is it called a π filter?

Its circuit arrangement resembles the Greek letter π.

Q5. What is the function of the first capacitor in a π filter?

It provides initial smoothing by reducing a large part of the rectifier ripple.

Q6. What is the function of the inductor in a π filter?

It opposes ripple-current variations between the two capacitors.

Q7. What is the function of the second capacitor?

It bypasses much of the remaining ripple component, improving output smoothing.

Q8. What is inductive reactance?

It is the opposition offered by an inductor to alternating current and is given by XL = 2πfL.

Q9. Does an inductor oppose steady DC ideally?

No. After the transient settles, an ideal inductor behaves like a short circuit for steady DC.

Q10. Does a filter provide voltage regulation?

Not necessarily. Filtering reduces ripple, whereas voltage regulation maintains output voltage as input or load conditions change.

Numerical question

An inductor of 0.1 H is operating at a ripple frequency of 100 Hz. Calculate its inductive reactance.

Given:
L = 0.1 H
f = 100 Hz

Solution:
XL = 2πfL
XL = 2 × 3.1416 × 100 × 0.1
XL ≈ 62.83 Ω

Multiple-choice questions

1. An LC filter consists of:
  1. Two resistors
  2. One inductor and one capacitor
  3. Two diodes
  4. One transistor
Answer: B
2. A π filter commonly uses which arrangement?
  1. R-L-R
  2. L-C-L
  3. C-L-C
  4. R-C-R
Answer: C
3. Inductive reactance is given by:
  1. 2πfL
  2. 1 / 2πfL
  3. IR
  4. V / R
Answer: A
4. The inductor in an LC filter is connected:
  1. In parallel with the load
  2. In series with the output path
  3. Across the AC input only
  4. In series with the capacitor branch
Answer: B

11. Lesson Summary

  • Filters reduce unwanted AC ripple in rectifier output.
  • An inductor opposes changes in current.
  • Inductive reactance is XL = 2πfL.
  • An LC filter uses a series inductor and shunt capacitor.
  • A π filter commonly uses a C-L-C arrangement.
  • Filter performance depends on component values and load conditions.
  • Filtering and voltage regulation are different functions.
Placement focus: Be prepared to draw an LC filter and a π filter, explain the role of each component, calculate inductive reactance and compare different rectifier filter arrangements.
