Lesson 9: Voltage Regulation and Load Regulation

Understand how DC power supplies maintain a stable output voltage when input voltage and load current change.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain the need for voltage regulation.
  • Differentiate line regulation and load regulation.
  • Calculate percentage voltage regulation.
  • Understand the function of a voltage regulator.
  • Explain Zener diode voltage regulation.
  • Understand the basic role of IC voltage regulators.
  • Identify practical power supply regulation problems.

2. What Is Voltage Regulation?

A DC power supply is expected to provide a nearly constant output voltage. However, the output voltage can change due to variations in input voltage, load current, temperature and internal component characteristics.

Voltage regulation is the ability of a power supply or regulator to maintain its output voltage close to the desired value despite changes in operating conditions.

Example: A regulated 5 V DC supply should maintain an output close to 5 V even when the connected circuit draws different amounts of current, provided the supply operates within its specified limits.

Why is voltage regulation required?

  • To protect electronic components from excessive voltage variations.
  • To provide a stable supply to microcontrollers and processors.
  • To improve the reliability of electronic equipment.
  • To maintain consistent circuit performance.
  • To compensate for changes in input voltage and load current.

3. Unregulated and Regulated Power Supplies

Unregulated power supply

An unregulated power supply does not actively maintain a constant output voltage. Its output may change when the input voltage or load current changes.

Regulated power supply

A regulated power supply includes a voltage-regulating stage that controls its output voltage within a specified operating range.

Typical linear regulated power supply stages.

Remember: Filtering reduces ripple. Regulation controls the average DC output voltage. A practical power supply may require both.

4. Line Regulation

Line regulation describes the ability of a power supply to maintain a constant output voltage when its input voltage changes, while the load is kept constant.

Line Regulation

Line Regulation (%) = (Change in Output Voltage / Change in Input Voltage) × 100

It is also commonly expressed in millivolts per volt (mV/V), using:

Line Regulation (mV/V) = ΔVOUT / ΔVIN

Example

A regulator has an output voltage of 5.00 V when its input is 10 V. When the input increases to 12 V, the output becomes 5.02 V. Calculate percentage line regulation.

Given:
Initial output = 5.00 V
Final output = 5.02 V
Change in input = 12 − 10 = 2 V

Solution:
Change in output = 5.02 − 5.00 = 0.02 V

Line Regulation = (0.02 / 2) × 100

Answer = 1% (0.01 V/V)
A smaller line-regulation value generally indicates better rejection of input-voltage variations.

5. Load Regulation

Load regulation describes the ability of a power supply to maintain its output voltage when the load current changes, while the input voltage remains constant.

In a typical measurement, output voltage is measured at no load and at full load.

Percentage Load Regulation

Load Regulation (%) = [(VNL − VFL) / VFL] × 100

VNL = No-load output voltage
VFL = Full-load output voltage

Example

A DC power supply gives 12.5 V at no load and 12 V at full load. Calculate its percentage load regulation.

Given:
VNL = 12.5 V
VFL = 12 V

Solution:
Load Regulation = [(12.5 − 12) / 12] × 100

= (0.5 / 12) × 100

Answer ≈ 4.17%
Some datasheets specify load regulation using a different convention, such as mV change over a stated current range. Always check the manufacturer's definition before comparing specifications.

6. Difference Between Line and Load Regulation

Parameter Line Regulation Load Regulation
Meaning Output change due to input-voltage variation. Output change due to load-current variation.
Input voltage Varied during testing. Kept constant.
Load current Kept constant. Varied during testing.
Measures Input-voltage rejection. Load-change compensation.

7. Voltage Regulator

A voltage regulator is an electronic circuit or device that maintains a specified output voltage despite variations in input voltage and load current, within its operating limits.

Main types of voltage regulators

  1. Zener diode voltage regulator.
  2. Series transistor voltage regulator.
  3. IC linear voltage regulator.
  4. Switching voltage regulator.

Basic functions

  • Maintain a stable DC output voltage.
  • Reduce the effect of input-voltage variations.
  • Reduce the effect of load-current variations.
  • Provide protection features in some regulator ICs.

8. Zener Diode Voltage Regulator

A Zener diode can maintain an approximately constant voltage when operated in its reverse-breakdown region with an appropriate current-limiting resistor.

Simplified Zener shunt regulator. The Zener diode and load are connected in parallel across the output.

Working principle

  1. The input DC voltage is applied through a series resistor Rs.
  2. The Zener diode is reverse-biased and operates in its breakdown region.
  3. The output voltage is approximately equal to the Zener voltage under specified operating conditions.
  4. The series resistor limits the current and drops the excess input voltage.
Approximate Output Voltage

VOUT ≈ VZ

Series Resistor Current

IS = (VIN − VZ) / RS

IS = IZ + IL

Where:

  • VIN = Input voltage.
  • VZ = Zener breakdown voltage.
  • RS = Series resistance.
  • IZ = Zener current.
  • IL = Load current.
The Zener must remain within its specified current and power limits. Regulation is lost if the Zener current falls below the required minimum or exceeds its maximum rating.

9. Zener Regulator Numerical Example

A Zener regulator has an input voltage of 15 V, a Zener voltage of 5 V and a series resistor of 500 Ω. Calculate the series current.

Given:
VIN = 15 V
VZ = 5 V
RS = 500 Ω

Solution:
IS = (VIN − VZ) / RS

IS = (15 − 5) / 500

IS = 10 / 500

Answer = 0.02 A = 20 mA
If the load draws 8 mA, the approximate Zener current is 20 − 8 = 12 mA, assuming the Zener remains in regulation and the resistor current is unchanged.

10. IC Voltage Regulators

Integrated circuit voltage regulators provide a convenient way to obtain a regulated DC output. They contain internal circuitry to control the output voltage.

Common examples

IC Typical Function Common Application
7805 Fixed positive 5 V regulator 5 V electronic circuits
7812 Fixed positive 12 V regulator 12 V DC circuits
7905 Fixed negative 5 V regulator Negative supply rails
LM317 Adjustable positive regulator Variable DC power supplies
Output current, input-voltage range, dropout voltage, thermal limits and capacitor requirements depend on the specific IC and package. Consult its datasheet before designing a circuit.

Linear and switching regulators

Feature Linear Regulator Switching Regulator
Operation Controls voltage using a pass element. Uses high-frequency switching and energy storage.
Efficiency Often lower when voltage difference is large. Often higher, depending on design.
Noise Generally low output noise. Switching ripple and EMI require consideration.
Complexity Usually simpler. Usually requires more design considerations.
Examples 7805, LM317 Buck, boost and buck-boost converters

11. Practical Testing and Troubleshooting

Testing with a digital multimeter

  1. Set the multimeter to DC voltage measurement.
  2. Connect the black probe to circuit ground.
  3. Connect the red probe to the regulator output.
  4. Measure the output with no load.
  5. Connect a suitable load and measure again.
  6. Compare the readings with the regulator specification.

Common faults

Problem Possible Cause
Output voltage too low Insufficient input voltage, overload or excessive voltage drop.
Output voltage too high Incorrect regulator connection or faulty regulator.
Output fluctuates Unstable input, poor filtering or unsuitable capacitors.
Regulator overheats Excessive power dissipation or inadequate heat sinking.
Output drops under load Overload, insufficient current capability or input voltage collapse.
Practical tip: Always check input voltage, output voltage, load current, regulator temperature and component connections before replacing a regulator IC.

12. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is voltage regulation?

It is the ability of a power supply to maintain a nearly constant output voltage despite changes in input voltage and load conditions.

Q2. What is line regulation?

It measures output-voltage variation caused by changes in input voltage while the load is constant.

Q3. What is load regulation?

It measures output-voltage variation caused by changes in load current while the input voltage is constant.

Q4. What is a voltage regulator?

It is a circuit or device that maintains a specified output voltage within its operating limits.

Q5. What is the function of a Zener diode in a regulator?

It maintains an approximately constant voltage across itself when operated in the reverse-breakdown region.

Q6. Why is a series resistor used with a Zener diode?

It limits current and drops the excess input voltage.

Q7. What is the function of the 7805 IC?

It is a fixed positive 5 V linear voltage regulator.

Q8. What is the difference between a linear and switching regulator?

A linear regulator controls voltage through a pass element, while a switching regulator controls energy transfer using high-frequency switching.

Q9. Why does a linear regulator become hot?

It dissipates power approximately equal to the input-output voltage difference multiplied by load current.

Q10. Is filtering the same as regulation?

No. Filtering reduces ripple, while regulation maintains the desired DC output voltage.

14. Lesson Summary

  • Voltage regulation maintains a stable DC output voltage.
  • Line regulation describes response to input-voltage changes.
  • Load regulation describes response to load-current changes.
  • Zener diodes can provide simple shunt voltage regulation.
  • IC regulators provide convenient regulated outputs.
  • Linear regulators are generally simple but can dissipate significant heat.
  • Switching regulators can provide high efficiency with additional design complexity.
  • Filtering and voltage regulation perform different functions.
