2. What Is a Full-Wave Centre-Tapped Rectifier?

A full-wave centre-tapped rectifier converts both positive and negative half-cycles of an AC input into a pulsating DC output. It uses:

  • A transformer with a centre-tapped secondary winding.
  • Two rectifier diodes.
  • A load resistor.

The centre tap divides the secondary winding into two equal halves. Each half produces an AC voltage with respect to the centre tap. The two diode paths conduct alternately, but load current always flows in the same direction.

Important notation: In this lecture, \(V_m\) means the peak voltage of one half of the transformer secondary, measured from an end terminal to the centre tap. It does not mean the peak voltage across the complete end-to-end secondary.

3. Circuit Diagram and Connections

Both diode cathodes connect to the positive output node. The load is connected between this output node and the centre tap. The transformer secondary centre tap is the return reference.

4. Working Principle: Alternate Diode Conduction

Positive half-cycle

When the upper end of the secondary is positive with respect to the centre tap, D1 becomes forward biased and conducts. D2 is reverse biased.

Negative half-cycle

During the next half-cycle, the lower end of the secondary becomes positive with respect to the centre tap. D2 conducts, while D1 is reverse biased.

Key concept: D1 and D2 conduct on alternate half-cycles. The current through the load resistor always flows in the same direction. Therefore, both halves of the AC waveform contribute to the output.

6. Input and Output Waveforms

The output waveform is the absolute value of the sinusoidal input when ideal diodes are assumed. Both half-cycles appear as positive pulses.

The output ripple frequency is twice the AC supply frequency:
fripple = 2f
For a 50 Hz supply, the ripple frequency is 100 Hz.

7. Mathematical Analysis

For an ideal full-wave rectifier with a resistive load:

VDC = 2Vm / π = 0.637Vm
VRMS = Vm / √2 = 0.707Vm
IDC = VDC / RL
IRMS = VRMS / RL
PIV per diode = 2Vm
Ripple factor = 0.482 (approximately)
Maximum rectification efficiency = 81.2% (ideal)
Form factor = VRMS / VDC ≈ 1.11

Why is PIV equal to 2Vm?

When D1 conducts, the output is approximately +Vm. The lower secondary terminal is simultaneously at approximately −Vm relative to the centre tap. D2 therefore sees approximately:

PIV = (+Vm) − (−Vm) = 2Vm

The same condition applies to D1 when D2 conducts.

8. Worked Numerical Example

Problem: A centre-tapped transformer is rated 12-0-12 V RMS and supplies a 100 Ω load through an ideal full-wave centre-tapped rectifier. Calculate the average output voltage, RMS output voltage, average load current, RMS load current, PIV and ripple frequency for a 50 Hz input.

Step 1: Peak voltage of one half-secondary

Vm = 12 × √2 = 16.97 V

Step 2: Average output voltage

VDC = 0.637 × 16.97 = 10.81 V

Step 3: RMS output voltage

VRMS = 16.97 / √2 = 12 V

Step 4: Average and RMS load currents

IDC = 10.81 / 100 = 0.1081 A
IRMS = 12 / 100 = 0.12 A

Step 5: PIV per diode

PIV = 2 × 16.97 = 33.94 V

Step 6: Ripple frequency

fripple = 2 × 50 = 100 Hz
Final results: VDC ≈ 10.81 V, VRMS = 12 V, IDC ≈ 108.1 mA, IRMS = 120 mA, PIV ≈ 33.94 V per diode, and ripple frequency = 100 Hz.

In a practical circuit, the conducting diode has a forward voltage drop. The output will therefore be lower than the ideal result. Transformer regulation and winding resistance also affect the measured voltage.

11. Centre-Tapped Transformer: Voltage Interpretation

A 12-0-12 V transformer has:

  • 12 V RMS between the first end and centre tap.
  • 12 V RMS between the centre tap and the second end.
  • 24 V RMS between the two end terminals.
For a 12-0-12 V transformer, use 12 V RMS per half-secondary in the centre-tapped rectifier equations. Do not substitute 24 V RMS for the voltage of one half.

The peak voltage of one half is:

Vm = 12√2 ≈ 16.97 V

12. Practical Considerations

Transformer

Requires a centre-tapped secondary. The two halves should have approximately equal voltage ratings.

Diodes

Each diode should have adequate repetitive reverse-voltage and forward-current ratings.

Load

Check the load current and resistor power rating. Use a suitable power margin.

Filtering

A capacitor filter can reduce ripple, but capacitor charging current and diode peak current increase.

Diode selection

For the ideal example, the calculated PIV is approximately 34 V. A real design must select a diode with a suitably higher VRRM rating after considering transformer voltage tolerance, mains variation, switching transients and the manufacturer's design guidance.

14. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

To construct and study a full-wave centre-tapped rectifier and verify its output waveform and DC voltage.

Components and equipment

  • Isolated low-voltage centre-tapped transformer.
  • Two suitable rectifier diodes.
  • Load resistor, for example 1 kΩ.
  • Breadboard or suitable test board.
  • Digital multimeter and oscilloscope.

Procedure

  1. Identify the two secondary ends and centre tap using the transformer documentation and voltage measurements.
  2. Verify that both half-secondary voltages are approximately equal.
  3. Connect the two diode anodes to the respective secondary ends.
  4. Connect the cathodes together to form the positive output.
  5. Connect the load between the positive output and centre tap.
  6. Apply the isolated low-voltage AC supply.
  7. Observe the output waveform using an oscilloscope.
  8. Measure the DC output voltage and compare it with the calculated value.

Expected observations

ObservationExpected result
Diode conductionAlternate conduction of D1 and D2
Output polarityPositive pulsating DC
Ripple frequencyTwice the input frequency
Load currentSame direction in both half-cycles
Output with practical diodesLower than ideal calculation
Electrical safety: Use an isolated, low-voltage AC source for this laboratory exercise. Do not connect an oscilloscope ground clip to an unverified mains-referenced circuit. Discharge capacitors before handling the circuit. Never probe exposed mains circuitry without appropriate training, isolation and rated equipment.

16. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What is a centre-tapped full-wave rectifier?
    A two-diode rectifier that uses both half-cycles of AC through a centre-tapped transformer.
  2. Why is it called full-wave?
    Both positive and negative input half-cycles contribute to the output.
  3. How many diodes are used?
    Two.
  4. Do both diodes conduct simultaneously?
    No. They conduct alternately.
  5. Why does load current remain unidirectional?
    The transformer winding polarity and diode arrangement direct current through the load in the same direction during both half-cycles.
  6. What is the ripple frequency?
    Twice the AC input frequency.
  7. What is the average output voltage?
    Approximately 0.637Vm for ideal diodes.
  8. What is the RMS output voltage?
    Approximately 0.707Vm.
  9. What is the PIV of each diode?
    2Vm for the ideal centre-tapped circuit without a filter capacitor.
  10. What is the ideal ripple factor?
    Approximately 0.482.
  11. What is the ideal rectification efficiency?
    Approximately 81.2%.
  12. What is the form factor?
    Approximately 1.11.
  13. What is the function of the centre tap?
    It provides the common reference and divides the secondary winding into two halves.
  14. Can a normal non-centre-tapped transformer be used directly?
    Not in this two-diode topology; a centre-tapped secondary is required.
  15. Why is the PIV higher than in a half-wave rectifier?
    The non-conducting diode can see approximately twice the peak voltage of one secondary half.
  16. What happens if one diode becomes open circuit?
    The circuit generally behaves like a half-wave rectifier.
  17. What happens if one diode is short circuit?
    It can create excessive current and may damage the transformer or other components.
  18. Why is a full-wave rectifier better than a half-wave rectifier?
    It provides a higher average output and higher ripple frequency with lower ripple factor.
  19. What is the disadvantage compared with a bridge rectifier?
    It requires a centre-tapped transformer and each diode has a higher PIV requirement.
  20. What happens when a capacitor filter is added?
    The capacitor charges near the waveform peaks and discharges through the load between peaks, reducing ripple.

17. Numerical Practice

  1. A 9-0-9 V transformer feeds a centre-tapped rectifier. Find the ideal average output voltage.
  2. A centre-tapped rectifier has Vm = 20 V. Calculate the PIV per diode.
  3. A 12-0-12 V transformer supplies a 220 Ω load. Find the ideal average load current.
  4. Calculate ripple frequency for a 60 Hz AC supply.
  5. For Vm = 25 V, calculate RMS output voltage and average DC output voltage.
Show answers
  1. Vm = 9√2 = 12.73 V; VDC ≈ 8.11 V.
  2. PIV = 2 × 20 = 40 V.
  3. VDC ≈ 10.81 V; IDC ≈ 49.1 mA.
  4. fripple = 120 Hz.
  5. VRMS ≈ 17.68 V; VDC ≈ 15.92 V.

18. Key Technical Takeaways

  • Two diodes and a centre-tapped transformer form the circuit.
  • D1 and D2 conduct on alternate half-cycles.
  • Load current always flows in the same direction.
  • Ripple frequency is twice the AC input frequency.
  • Ideal average output is 0.637Vm.
  • Each diode has a PIV of 2Vm.
  • Ideal ripple factor is approximately 0.482.
  • Real output depends on diode drops, transformer regulation and load conditions.
