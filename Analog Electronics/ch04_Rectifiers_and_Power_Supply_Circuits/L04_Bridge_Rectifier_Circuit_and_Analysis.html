2. What Is a Bridge Rectifier?

A bridge rectifier is a full-wave rectifier that converts AC into pulsating DC using four diodes connected in a bridge arrangement. It does not require a centre-tapped transformer secondary.

During each half-cycle, two diodes conduct and the remaining two diodes are reverse biased. The conducting pair changes when the AC polarity reverses, but the direction of current through the load remains unchanged.

Important: In a conventional bridge rectifier, current passes through two forward-biased diodes in series during each half-cycle. Therefore, the output has approximately two diode forward drops in the conducting path.

3. Bridge Rectifier Circuit Diagram

The AC input is connected to the two opposite AC terminals. The load is connected across the positive and negative DC output terminals.

The diode orientation is essential. The two cathodes meet at the positive output, while the two anodes meet at the negative output.

4. Positive Half-Cycle Operation

Assume terminal A is positive with respect to terminal B.

  • D1 is forward biased and conducts.
  • D4 is forward biased and conducts.
  • D2 and D3 are reverse biased.

Current path: A → D1 → positive output → load → negative output → D4 → B.

5. Negative Half-Cycle Operation

When terminal B becomes positive with respect to terminal A:

  • D2 is forward biased and conducts.
  • D3 is forward biased and conducts.
  • D1 and D4 are reverse biased.

Current path: B → D2 → positive output → load → negative output → D3 → A.

The load current has the same direction during both half-cycles. The conducting diode pair changes from D1-D4 to D2-D3.

7. Input and Output Waveforms

The bridge rectifier produces a full-wave rectified waveform. The output frequency is twice the input frequency.

fripple = 2finput

For a 50 Hz supply, the unfiltered rectifier output has a ripple frequency of 100 Hz.

8. Mathematical Analysis

Let Vm be the peak value of the AC voltage applied across the bridge input.

VDC = 2Vm / π = 0.637Vm
VRMS = Vm / √2 = 0.707Vm
IDC = VDC / RL
IRMS = VRMS / RL
PIV per diode = Vm
Ripple factor = 0.482 (approximately)
Maximum ideal rectification efficiency = 81.2%
Form factor = VRMS / VDC ≈ 1.11

Practical diode voltage drop

In a conventional silicon bridge, two diodes conduct in series. A first-order approximation is:

Vout,peak ≈ Vm − 2VF

For silicon diodes, a typical forward drop might be around 0.7 V at a particular current and temperature. Actual forward voltage depends on the selected diode and operating conditions. The ideal rectifier equations above do not include these losses.

9. Why Is Bridge Rectifier PIV Equal to Vm?

In the bridge arrangement, the reverse-biased diode is exposed to approximately the peak input voltage, rather than twice the peak voltage as in the centre-tapped full-wave circuit.

For the ideal bridge topology without a filter capacitor, the PIV of each diode is approximately Vm. Always allow design margin for voltage tolerance and transients.

10. Worked Numerical Example

Problem: A bridge rectifier is connected to a 15 V RMS AC secondary and a 220 Ω load. The input frequency is 50 Hz. Calculate ideal average output voltage, RMS output voltage, average load current, RMS load current, diode PIV and ripple frequency.

Step 1: Find peak input voltage

Vm = 15√2 = 21.21 V

Step 2: Average DC output

VDC = 0.637 × 21.21 = 13.51 V

Step 3: RMS output

VRMS = 15 V

Step 4: Load currents

IDC = 13.51 / 220 = 61.4 mA
IRMS = 15 / 220 = 68.2 mA

Step 5: PIV

PIV = Vm = 21.21 V

Step 6: Ripple frequency

fripple = 2 × 50 = 100 Hz
Ideal results: VDC ≈ 13.51 V, VRMS = 15 V, IDC ≈ 61.4 mA, IRMS ≈ 68.2 mA, PIV ≈ 21.21 V and ripple frequency = 100 Hz.

The actual average output will be lower because two diode forward drops are present in the conducting path. Transformer regulation and load current also affect the measured result.

13. Bridge Rectifier vs Centre-Tapped Rectifier

ParameterBridge rectifierCentre-tapped rectifier
Number of diodes42
Transformer centre tapNot requiredRequired
Diodes conducting at a time21
Forward drops in current pathTwo diode dropsOne diode drop
PIV per diodeVm2Vm
Transformer secondary usageEntire secondary each half-cycleOne half-secondary at a time

A bridge rectifier generally makes better use of a non-centre-tapped transformer secondary and has lower PIV per diode. Its main conduction disadvantage is the two series diode drops.

15. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

To construct and test a bridge rectifier and observe full-wave rectification.

Components and equipment

  • Isolated low-voltage AC transformer.
  • Four suitable rectifier diodes or a bridge module.
  • Load resistor, such as 1 kΩ.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Breadboard or suitable test board.

Procedure

  1. Identify the two AC terminals and the positive and negative DC terminals of the bridge.
  2. Verify each diode's polarity using a multimeter diode-test function.
  3. Connect the transformer secondary to the AC terminals.
  4. Connect the load resistor across the DC output.
  5. Apply an isolated low-voltage AC input.
  6. Measure the AC input voltage and DC output voltage.
  7. Observe the output waveform with an oscilloscope.
  8. Compare the measured result with the ideal and practical calculations.

Expected observations

ParameterExpected observation
Conducting pairD1-D4, then D2-D3
Output waveformBoth half-cycles positive
Ripple frequencyTwice input frequency
Current directionSame through the load
Output voltageBelow ideal prediction due to two diode drops
Safety: Use an isolated, low-voltage AC source. Never connect an oscilloscope ground clip to an unverified mains-referenced circuit. Discharge capacitors before handling the circuit. Use correctly rated instruments and components.

17. Diode Testing in a Bridge

Before testing individual bridge diodes, disconnect the power and discharge any filter capacitor. In-circuit measurements can be affected by parallel paths; isolate a diode if the reading is ambiguous.

18. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What is a bridge rectifier?
    A four-diode full-wave rectifier that converts both AC half-cycles into pulsating DC.
  2. How many diodes are used?
    Four.
  3. How many diodes conduct at a time?
    Two.
  4. Is a centre-tapped transformer required?
    No.
  5. Which pair conducts when A is positive relative to B?
    D1 and D4 in the circuit convention used here.
  6. Which pair conducts when B is positive relative to A?
    D2 and D3.
  7. Why is load current unidirectional?
    The bridge routes both alternate input polarities through the load in the same direction.
  8. What is the ripple frequency?
    Twice the input frequency.
  9. What is the ideal average output voltage?
    0.637Vm.
  10. What is the ideal RMS output voltage?
    0.707Vm.
  11. What is the PIV per diode?
    Approximately Vm in the basic bridge topology without a filter capacitor.
  12. What is the ideal ripple factor?
    Approximately 0.482.
  13. What is the ideal maximum efficiency?
    Approximately 81.2%.
  14. Why is the practical bridge output lower?
    Two conducting diodes introduce forward-voltage drops.
  15. What is the main advantage over a centre-tapped rectifier?
    It does not require a centre-tapped transformer secondary.
  16. What is one disadvantage compared with a centre-tapped rectifier?
    Two diode drops occur in the conducting path instead of one.
  17. What happens if one diode becomes open?
    One current path may fail, producing half-wave-like or asymmetric output.
  18. What happens if a diode becomes shorted?
    It can create a high-current fault and damage other components.
  19. Why is a filter capacitor connected after the bridge?
    To reduce output ripple by storing and releasing charge.
  20. Can a bridge rectifier provide regulated DC?
    No. Rectification alone produces pulsating DC; filtering and voltage regulation may be needed.

19. Numerical Practice

  2. A bridge rectifier has Vm = 30 V. Find the PIV per diode.
  3. A 15 V RMS source supplies a 330 Ω load. Find the ideal average DC load current.
  4. Calculate the ripple frequency for a 60 Hz input.
  5. A bridge rectifier has an ideal average output of 18 V. Estimate the peak input voltage.
Show answers
  1. Vm ≈ 16.97 V; VDC ≈ 10.81 V.
  2. PIV = 30 V.
  3. Vm ≈ 21.21 V; VDC ≈ 13.51 V; IDC ≈ 40.9 mA.
  4. 120 Hz.
  5. Vm = 18 / 0.637 ≈ 28.26 V.
