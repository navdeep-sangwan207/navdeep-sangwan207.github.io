1. Introduction to Semiconductor Diode

A semiconductor diode is a two-terminal electronic component formed using a semiconductor junction. A conventional PN junction diode is made by creating P-type and N-type regions within a semiconductor crystal.

The diode allows current to flow easily when it is forward biased and opposes current flow when it is reverse biased, apart from a small leakage current before breakdown.

Basic Characteristics

  • It is a two-terminal semiconductor device.
  • It has an anode and a cathode.
  • It has a nonlinear voltage-current relationship.
  • It provides directional current conduction.
  • It is widely used in rectification and switching.
Definition: A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that conducts significant current under forward bias and blocks most current under reverse bias until breakdown occurs.

2. Construction of PN Junction Diode

A PN junction diode is constructed by forming two differently doped regions in a semiconductor material, generally silicon or germanium.

P-Type Region

  • Formed by adding trivalent impurities.
  • Examples: Boron, Aluminium and Gallium.
  • Holes are the majority carriers.
  • Electrons are the minority carriers.

N-Type Region

  • Formed by adding pentavalent impurities.
  • Examples: Phosphorus, Arsenic and Antimony.
  • Electrons are the majority carriers.
  • Holes are the minority carriers.

PN Junction Structure

P-Type Region
Holes (+)
Depletion Region
N-Type Region
Electrons (−)

A depletion region develops near the interface between the P-type and N-type regions.

Important: A practical diode is fabricated as a single semiconductor structure with differently doped regions. It is not normally made by mechanically joining two separate semiconductor pieces.

3. Diode Symbol and Terminals

A PN junction diode has two terminals: the anode and the cathode.

Anode

The anode is connected to the P-type region. It is the positive terminal during forward bias.

Cathode

The cathode is connected to the N-type region. It is the negative terminal during forward bias.

Diode Symbol

Anode (A)
A ──|>|── K
Cathode (K)

The vertical bar in the standard diode symbol represents the cathode.

Practical Identification: On many physical through-hole diodes, a band or stripe indicates the cathode terminal. Always verify the component datasheet.

4. Working Principle of Diode

The working principle of a PN junction diode is based on the behaviour of charge carriers across the junction under an applied voltage.

Forward Bias Operation

  • P-side is connected to the positive supply.
  • N-side is connected to the negative supply.
  • The depletion region becomes narrower.
  • The potential barrier is reduced.
  • Significant current can flow through the diode.

Reverse Bias Operation

  • P-side is connected to the negative supply.
  • N-side is connected to the positive supply.
  • The depletion region becomes wider.
  • The potential barrier increases.
  • Only a small reverse leakage current normally flows before breakdown.
Forward bias supports majority-carrier injection across the junction. Reverse bias suppresses majority-carrier conduction and increases the depletion-region width.

5. Unidirectional Current Conduction

A conventional PN junction diode is commonly described as a unidirectional device because it allows substantial current in the forward direction while restricting current in the reverse direction.

Forward Direction

When the diode is forward biased sufficiently, it conducts current from anode to cathode in conventional-current direction.

Reverse Direction

When reverse biased, it blocks most current. A small reverse leakage current exists due to minority carriers.

Condition Current Behaviour
Forward Bias Significant current flows.
Reverse Bias Small leakage current flows.
Reverse Breakdown Large reverse current can flow and must be limited.
A diode is not an ideal one-way switch. Real diodes have forward voltage drop, reverse leakage, capacitance and a maximum reverse-voltage rating.

6. Ideal and Practical Diode

Ideal Diode

An ideal diode is a theoretical model used to simplify circuit analysis.

  • Zero voltage drop during forward conduction.
  • Zero resistance during forward conduction.
  • Infinite resistance during reverse bias.
  • Zero reverse leakage current.
  • No power loss.

Practical Diode

A real diode has a finite forward voltage drop, small reverse leakage current and a limited breakdown voltage.

Parameter Ideal Diode Practical Diode
Forward Voltage 0 V Non-zero
Forward Resistance 0 Ω Finite
Reverse Resistance Infinite Very high before breakdown
Reverse Current 0 A Small leakage current

7. Forward Voltage Drop

A practical diode requires a forward voltage to conduct significant current.

Typical Silicon Diode

A conventional silicon diode commonly has a forward voltage drop around 0.6 V to 0.7 V at moderate operating currents.

Typical Germanium Diode

A germanium diode commonly has a forward voltage drop around 0.2 V to 0.3 V.

Material Typical Forward Voltage
Silicon Approximately 0.7 V
Germanium Approximately 0.3 V
These are approximate values, not fixed switching thresholds. Actual forward voltage depends on current, temperature and diode type. Schottky and other special-purpose diodes have different characteristics.

8. Basic Diode Circuit Example

Consider a silicon diode connected in series with a resistor and a DC supply.

Given

  • Supply voltage = 5 V
  • Series resistance = 1 kΩ
  • Diode forward voltage = 0.7 V

Diode Current Calculation

Using Ohm's law:

I = (VS − VD) / R

Substitute the given values:

I = (5 − 0.7) / 1000
I = 4.3 / 1000
Expected Result:

Diode current = 0.0043 A

Diode current = 4.3 mA

This calculation uses the constant forward voltage approximation. The resistor limits the diode current.

9. Applications of Semiconductor Diodes

Application Purpose
Rectifier Circuits Convert AC into pulsating DC.
Switching Circuits Control current flow in electronic circuits.
Voltage Regulation Maintain a reference or regulated voltage using Zener diodes.
Reverse Polarity Protection Protect circuits against incorrect supply connections.
Flyback Protection Provide a current path for inductive loads.
Signal Clipping Limit the amplitude of electrical signals.
Signal Clamping Shift the DC level of a waveform.

12. Lesson Summary

  • A PN junction diode is a two-terminal semiconductor device.
  • The anode is connected to the P-type region.
  • The cathode is connected to the N-type region.
  • Forward bias allows significant current conduction.
  • Reverse bias produces only a small leakage current before breakdown.
  • Practical diodes have a non-zero forward voltage drop.
  • Diodes are used in rectification, switching and protection circuits.
