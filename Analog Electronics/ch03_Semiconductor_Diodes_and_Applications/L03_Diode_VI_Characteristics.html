2. What are Diode V-I Characteristics?

The V-I characteristics of a diode describe the relationship between the voltage across a diode and the current flowing through it.

The voltage-current relationship is nonlinear. The diode current changes significantly with voltage, particularly during forward conduction.

V-I characteristic: A graph showing diode current as a function of diode voltage under specified conditions.

Graph convention used in this lesson

  • Horizontal axis: diode voltage VD.
  • Vertical axis: diode current ID.
  • Positive voltage and positive current: forward region.
  • Negative voltage and negative current: reverse region, including the illustrative breakdown branch.

3. Forward V-I Characteristics

When the diode is forward-biased, current is initially small at low voltage. As the applied voltage increases, the current rises rapidly.

Important forward-region observations

  • At low forward voltage, current is relatively small.
  • As the voltage increases, forward current rises exponentially in the usual diode model.
  • The forward knee region is often used as a practical description of where current becomes substantial.
  • At higher current, series resistance and heating can influence the curve.

Forward knee voltage

The knee voltage is a practical term used to describe the region of the characteristic curve where forward current begins increasing substantially.

Diode Typical approximate knee / forward voltage region
Silicon diode Often around 0.6–0.7 V in simple models
Germanium diode Often around 0.2–0.3 V
Schottky diode Often around 0.2–0.5 V
LED Depends on semiconductor material and emitted color
These values are not fixed switching thresholds. Forward current is governed by the diode's nonlinear characteristic and operating conditions.

4. Reverse V-I Characteristics

When a diode is reverse-biased, it normally conducts only a small reverse leakage current before breakdown.

Reverse-region behavior

  • Reverse current is relatively small before breakdown.
  • Reverse current depends on temperature and device characteristics.
  • Increasing reverse voltage generally increases the electric field in the junction.
  • When the breakdown region is reached, current can increase sharply.
Breakdown caution: Ordinary diodes must not be operated beyond their specified reverse ratings unless the device is designed for that operating condition and the current is controlled.

8. Dynamic Resistance of a Diode

Dynamic resistance describes the small change in voltage divided by the corresponding small change in current around an operating point.

rd = ΔV / ΔI

For a sufficiently small change in voltage around an operating point:

rd ≈ dV / dI

Dynamic resistance is not the same as the static resistance V/I. It is the slope-based resistance at a specified operating point.

Example

If the diode voltage changes by 20 mV and the current changes by 5 mA around an operating point:

rd = 0.020 / 0.005

rd = 4 Ω
Dynamic resistance is useful in small-signal analysis and is dependent on the operating point.

9. Static Resistance vs Dynamic Resistance

Parameter Static Resistance Dynamic Resistance
Definition Voltage divided by current at an operating point Small voltage change divided by current change
Formula R = V/I rd ≈ dV/dI
Meaning Average ratio at the point Local slope of the characteristic
Application Basic DC analysis Small-signal analysis

10. Temperature Effect on Diode Characteristics

Temperature affects the diode voltage-current relationship.

Important effects

  • Reverse leakage current generally increases with temperature.
  • For a silicon diode at a fixed forward current, forward voltage typically decreases as temperature rises.
  • Breakdown behavior depends on the breakdown mechanism and device design.
  • Thermal effects can influence current and power dissipation.
Temperature coefficients are not identical for every diode type, current level or breakdown mechanism.

11. Practical Measurement of V-I Characteristics

Objective

Measure the voltage-current relationship of a diode under controlled conditions.

Required components

  • Silicon diode, such as 1N4007
  • Current-limiting resistor
  • Variable DC supply
  • Digital multimeter
  • Breadboard
  • Connecting wires

Procedure

  1. Connect the diode in forward bias with a series resistor.
  2. Start with a low supply voltage.
  3. Measure diode voltage.
  4. Measure current using an appropriate meter connection or calculate it from the resistor voltage.
  5. Increase the supply in controlled steps within the diode's rated limits.
  6. Record diode voltage and current.
  7. Plot forward V-I characteristics.
  8. Disconnect the supply.
  9. Reverse the diode using the same current-limiting arrangement.
  10. Measure reverse leakage only with suitable instrumentation and safe limits.
  11. Plot reverse characteristics if the measurement resolution is sufficient.
Do not attempt breakdown testing on a general-purpose diode without a properly controlled setup, appropriate current limiting, instrumentation and voltage ratings.

12. Placement Interview Questions

Q1. What are V-I characteristics of a diode?
They describe the relationship between voltage across a diode and the current flowing through it.
Q2. Why is a diode nonlinear?
Its current does not increase proportionally with voltage over its operating range. Its behavior is governed by a nonlinear junction relationship.
Q3. What is knee voltage?
It is a practical term describing the region where forward current becomes substantial on the characteristic curve.
Q4. Does a silicon diode always have a 0.7 V drop?
No. Forward voltage depends on current, temperature, device construction and operating conditions.
Q5. What is reverse leakage current?
A small reverse current flowing before breakdown due to minority-carrier and other generation-related mechanisms.
Q6. What happens in breakdown?
Reverse current increases substantially when the junction enters breakdown. The current must be controlled to prevent damage in devices not designed for uncontrolled breakdown operation.
Q7. What is dynamic resistance?
The small-signal voltage change divided by the corresponding current change around an operating point.
Q8. What is static resistance?
The ratio of diode voltage to diode current at a particular operating point.
Q9. Why does reverse leakage increase with temperature?
Increased thermal carrier generation generally increases the available carriers contributing to reverse leakage.
Q10. Why is a current-limiting resistor important?
It limits current and helps prevent excessive heating and damage.
Q11. What is the difference between static and dynamic resistance?
Static resistance is V/I, while dynamic resistance is the local voltage-current slope.
Q12. How is the diode characteristic measured?
Apply controlled voltage with current limiting, measure voltage and current, and plot the resulting operating points.
Q13. What is the forward characteristic?
The relationship between forward voltage and forward current, typically showing a strong nonlinear increase in current.
Q14. What is the reverse characteristic?
The relationship between reverse voltage and reverse current, including leakage and breakdown behavior where applicable.
Q15. Why are diode V-I characteristics important?
They help engineers select components, analyze circuits, predict current, evaluate power dissipation and understand operating conditions.

Numerical 1

A diode has a voltage of 0.72 V and current of 12 mA at a particular operating point. Calculate its static resistance.

R = V/I

R = 0.72 / 0.012

R = 60 Ω

Numerical 2

Around an operating point, a diode voltage changes by 15 mV while current changes by 3 mA. Calculate dynamic resistance.

rd = ΔV / ΔI

rd = 0.015 / 0.003

rd = 5 Ω

Numerical 3

A 5 V supply is connected to a silicon diode and 470 Ω resistor in series. Assume a forward voltage of 0.7 V. Calculate current and resistor power.

I = (5 − 0.7) / 470

I ≈ 9.15 mA
PR = I²R

PR ≈ 39.4 mW

14. Revision Summary

  • Diode V-I characteristics describe voltage-current behavior.
  • Forward current increases strongly with forward voltage.
  • Knee voltage is a practical description of the forward-current transition.
  • Reverse leakage is small before breakdown.
  • Breakdown causes substantial reverse-current increase.
  • Static resistance is V/I.
  • Dynamic resistance is approximately dV/dI around an operating point.
  • Temperature affects diode characteristics.
  • Current limiting is important for practical diode circuits.
  • V-I graphs are useful for component selection and circuit analysis.
