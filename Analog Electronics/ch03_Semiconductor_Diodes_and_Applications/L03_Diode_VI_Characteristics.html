Chapter 3: Semiconductor Diodes and Applications

Lesson 3: V-I Characteristics of PN Junction Diode

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand the V-I characteristics of a PN junction diode.
  • Explain forward-bias and reverse-bias characteristics.
  • Identify cut-in voltage and reverse breakdown voltage.
  • Understand reverse saturation current.
  • Calculate DC resistance and dynamic resistance.
  • Understand the effect of temperature on diode characteristics.
  • Interpret important diode parameters from a datasheet.

1. Introduction to V-I Characteristics

V-I characteristics describe the relationship between the voltage applied across a semiconductor diode and the current flowing through it.

The voltage across the diode is represented by VD, while the current through the diode is represented by ID.

V-I Characteristics = Relationship between Diode Voltage and Diode Current

The characteristic curve is generally divided into three regions:

  1. Forward-bias region
  2. Reverse-bias region
  3. Reverse breakdown region
Placement Focus: Interviewers frequently ask candidates to explain the diode V-I curve and identify its forward, reverse and breakdown regions.

2. Forward-Bias V-I Characteristics

When a PN junction diode is forward biased, the P-region is connected to the positive terminal and the N-region to the negative terminal of the supply.

As the forward voltage increases, the potential barrier decreases and the diode current increases.

Important Characteristics

  • At very low forward voltage, the current is small.
  • As voltage approaches the knee region, current starts increasing significantly.
  • Beyond the knee region, a small increase in voltage can produce a large increase in current.
  • The forward current must be limited using a suitable external circuit.

Knee Voltage (Cut-in Voltage)

Knee voltage is the approximate forward voltage at which diode current begins to increase rapidly.

Diode Type Approximate Forward Voltage
Silicon PN Junction Diode 0.7 V
Germanium PN Junction Diode 0.3 V
These are approximate textbook values, not fixed switching thresholds. Actual forward voltage depends on diode current, temperature and the particular device.

3. Reverse-Bias V-I Characteristics

In reverse bias, the P-region is connected to the negative terminal and the N-region to the positive terminal.

The depletion region becomes wider and the diode blocks most of the externally applied voltage.

Reverse Saturation Current

A very small current flows through a reverse-biased diode due to thermally generated minority carriers. This current is called reverse saturation current or reverse leakage current.

  • It is normally very small in a standard silicon diode.
  • It is approximately constant over a range of reverse voltage before breakdown, at a fixed temperature.
  • It increases significantly with temperature.
  • It depends on the diode construction and material.
Reverse Current = Small Leakage Current (Before Breakdown)

4. Reverse Breakdown Region

When the reverse voltage reaches a particular value called breakdown voltage, the reverse current increases sharply.

This operating region is known as the reverse breakdown region.

Types of Breakdown

Breakdown Type Basic Mechanism
Zener Breakdown Quantum-mechanical tunnelling in a heavily doped junction.
Avalanche Breakdown Impact ionization caused by accelerated charge carriers.
A diode operating in breakdown must have an external current-limiting arrangement. Excessive current can damage an ordinary diode. Zener diodes are specifically designed for controlled operation in their breakdown region.

How to Interpret the Curve

6. DC or Static Resistance of a Diode

DC resistance is the ratio of the DC voltage across the diode to the DC current flowing through it at a particular operating point.

RDC = VD / ID

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Diode voltage = 0.70 V
  • Diode current = 10 mA

Solution:

RDC = 0.70 / 0.01

RDC = 70 Ω
Final Answer: DC resistance = 70 Ω

7. Dynamic or AC Resistance of a Diode

Dynamic resistance is the ratio of a small change in diode voltage to the corresponding change in diode current around an operating point.

rd = ΔVD / ΔID

Numerical Example

Consider the following two operating points:

Operating Point Voltage Current
Point 1 0.70 V 10 mA
Point 2 0.72 V 20 mA

Solution:

ΔV = 0.72 - 0.70 = 0.02 V

ΔI = 20 mA - 10 mA = 10 mA = 0.01 A

rd = 0.02 / 0.01

rd = 2 Ω
Final Answer: Dynamic resistance = 2 Ω

Small-Signal Dynamic Resistance

For a forward-biased diode, the approximate small-signal resistance can also be calculated using:

rd ≈ nVT / ID
  • n: Ideality factor.
  • VT: Thermal voltage, approximately 25.9 mV at room temperature.
  • ID: DC operating current in amperes.
The small-signal formula is an approximation for a diode operating in forward bias. It is different from calculating the slope between two widely separated points on the V-I curve.

8. Effect of Temperature on V-I Characteristics

Temperature has a significant effect on the electrical characteristics of a semiconductor diode.

Parameter Effect of Increasing Temperature
Forward Voltage Generally decreases at a fixed current for a silicon PN diode.
Reverse Leakage Current Increases significantly.
Breakdown Voltage Temperature coefficient depends on the breakdown mechanism.
Diode Performance Operating point and thermal stability can change.
Practical Importance: Temperature effects must be considered when designing power supplies, rectifiers, protection circuits and temperature-sensitive electronic systems.

9. Important Diode Datasheet Parameters

Engineers use diode datasheets to select a suitable diode for a particular electronic circuit.

Parameter Description
VF Forward voltage drop at a specified current.
IF Forward current rating under specified conditions.
VRRM Maximum repetitive peak reverse voltage.
IR Reverse leakage current at specified conditions.
PD Maximum power dissipation under specified conditions.
TJ Maximum junction temperature rating.
Always check the datasheet test conditions. A voltage, current or power rating is meaningful only with its specified operating and thermal conditions.

10. Practical Applications

  • Rectifier circuits for AC-to-DC conversion.
  • Voltage regulation using Zener diodes.
  • Signal clipping and clamping circuits.
  • Reverse polarity protection.
  • Switching circuits and signal detection.
  • Electronic power supply circuits.

11. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What are V-I characteristics of a diode?

They represent the relationship between the voltage across a diode and the current flowing through it.

Q2. What is knee voltage?

It is the approximate forward voltage beyond which diode current begins increasing rapidly.

Q3. What is the typical knee voltage of a silicon diode?

Approximately 0.7 V under typical textbook assumptions.

Q4. What is reverse saturation current?

It is the small reverse current caused mainly by minority carriers in a reverse-biased diode before breakdown.

Q5. What happens when reverse breakdown voltage is reached?

Reverse current increases sharply. An external current-limiting arrangement is necessary.

Q6. What is DC resistance?

It is the ratio of diode DC voltage to diode DC current at a particular operating point.

Q7. What is dynamic resistance?

It is the ratio of a small change in diode voltage to the corresponding change in diode current.

Q8. How does temperature affect reverse leakage current?

Reverse leakage current generally increases as temperature rises.

Q9. What is the difference between DC and dynamic resistance?

DC resistance is calculated using the voltage-current ratio at one operating point, while dynamic resistance is calculated using the local voltage-current change.

Q10. Why is a current-limiting resistor required?

It prevents excessive forward or reverse breakdown current from damaging the diode.
