1. Introduction to Practical Diode Circuits

Diodes are widely used in electronic circuits for rectification, voltage regulation, switching, indication, signal conditioning and circuit protection.

In practical circuit design, it is important to consider the diode's electrical ratings, operating temperature, current requirements and circuit conditions.

Application Diode Function
Power supply AC-to-DC rectification.
Voltage regulator Maintains an approximately constant output voltage.
LED indicator Converts electrical energy into light.
Relay driver Suppresses inductive voltage spikes.
Signal conditioning Clipping, clamping and voltage limiting.
Input protection Protects against reverse polarity and transients.

2. Practical LED Indicator Circuit

An LED indicator circuit is used to show the operating status of an electronic system. 

       +5V
        |
       330Ω
        |
       |>|  LED
        |
       GND

Working Principle

  • The supply voltage is applied to the LED through a resistor.
  • The LED becomes forward biased.
  • Current flows through the LED and produces light.
  • The resistor limits the current to a safe value.

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Supply voltage = 5 V
  • LED forward voltage = 2 V
  • Series resistance = 330 Ω
I = (VS - VF) / R

I = (5 - 2) / 330

I = 0.00909 A

I ≈ 9.09 mA
Answer: LED current is approximately 9.09 mA.

3. Practical Rectifier Diode Circuit

Rectifier diodes are used to convert AC voltage into pulsating DC voltage.

Half-Wave Rectifier

       AC Input
          ~
          |
         |>|  D1
          |
          +------ Vout
          |
          RL
          |
         GND

During the conducting half-cycle, the diode allows current to flow through the load. During the opposite half-cycle, the diode blocks current.

Full-Wave Bridge Rectifier

A bridge rectifier uses four diodes to convert both half-cycles of an AC input into a unidirectional output.

Full-wave rectification provides a higher average DC output and lower ripple frequency than half-wave rectification for the same AC input frequency.

4. Practical Zener Voltage Regulator

A Zener diode can be used as a shunt voltage regulator to maintain an approximately constant output voltage. 

          +12V
            |
           RS
            |
            +--------- Vout
            |            |
           ZD           RL
            |            |
           GND          GND

       Zener diode is reverse biased.

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Supply voltage = 12 V
  • Zener voltage = 5.1 V
  • Load current = 10 mA
  • Required Zener current = 5 mA
RS = (VS - VZ) / (IL + IZ)

RS = (12 - 5.1) / (0.010 + 0.005)

RS = 460 Ω
Answer: Calculated resistor = 460 Ω. A suitable standard value may be 470 Ω, subject to checking minimum and maximum Zener current under supply and load variations.

Important Checks

  • Ensure the Zener current stays within its operating limits.
  • Check maximum Zener power dissipation.
  • Verify series resistor power rating.
  • Check the regulator under minimum and maximum load conditions.

5. Practical Relay Driver Protection

A relay coil is an inductive load. When its driving transistor switches OFF, the coil can generate a high-voltage transient. 

             +12V
               |
          +----+----+
          |         |
         Relay     |<| D1
          |         |
          +----+----+
               |
          Collector
           Q1 NPN
          Emitter
               |
              GND

        Base receives control signal
        through a suitable resistor.

Purpose of the Flyback Diode

  • Provides a path for the coil current after switch-off.
  • Reduces the voltage spike across the transistor.
  • Protects the transistor from inductive transients.
  • Improves circuit reliability.
Connect the flyback diode with its cathode toward the positive supply and its anode toward the transistor/low side of the coil in a typical low-side relay driver.

6. Diode Testing Using a Digital Multimeter

A digital multimeter with a diode-test function can be used to check whether a diode is functioning correctly.

Testing Procedure

  1. Disconnect power from the circuit.
  2. Discharge capacitors safely.
  3. Isolate at least one diode terminal if parallel circuit paths may affect the reading.
  4. Set the multimeter to diode-test mode.
  5. Connect the red probe to the anode.
  6. Connect the black probe to the cathode.
  7. Record the forward voltage reading.
  8. Reverse the probes and record the reverse reading.

Expected Readings

Test Condition Typical Result
Red probe at anode, black at cathode Forward voltage drop for a normal diode.
Red probe at cathode, black at anode OL or no conduction for a typical healthy diode.
Exact readings depend on the diode type and multimeter. LEDs, Schottky diodes and germanium diodes may show different forward readings. Some LEDs may not visibly glow during a multimeter test.

7. Common Diode Faults

Fault Possible Indication Possible Effect
Open Diode No forward conduction. Rectifier output or load current may disappear.
Shorted Diode Conduction in both directions. Excessive current or supply failure.
Reverse Leakage Unexpected reverse current. Incorrect circuit voltage or power loss.
Increased Forward Drop Higher-than-expected voltage drop. Reduced output voltage and increased heating.
Intermittent Connection Unstable reading or operation. Unreliable circuit behavior.

8. Systematic Diode Troubleshooting

A systematic troubleshooting process helps identify diode-related faults without replacing components unnecessarily.

Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Visual inspection: Look for cracks, burn marks, discoloration and damaged PCB tracks.
  2. Check supply: Verify the input supply voltage and polarity.
  3. Inspect connections: Check solder joints and component orientation.
  4. Perform diode test: Measure forward and reverse readings with power removed.
  5. Measure circuit voltage: Compare expected and actual voltage drops.
  6. Check current: Identify excessive current or an open current path.
  7. Check temperature: Look for abnormal heating under controlled conditions.
  8. Replace and retest: Use a correctly rated replacement and verify normal operation.
Always disconnect power before using resistance or diode-test mode. For live voltage and current measurements, use suitable equipment and safe measurement procedures.

9. Troubleshooting Practical Circuits

Case 1: LED Is Not Glowing

Possible causes:

  • LED connected with incorrect polarity.
  • LED damaged or open.
  • Supply voltage missing.
  • Series resistor value too high.
  • Loose connection or broken PCB track.

Checks:

  • Verify supply voltage.
  • Check LED polarity.
  • Measure voltage across the LED and resistor.
  • Test the LED separately if necessary.

Case 2: Rectifier Output Is Zero

Possible causes:

  • Input AC supply missing.
  • Open rectifier diode.
  • Incorrect diode orientation.
  • Broken PCB connection.
  • Blown fuse or disconnected load path.

Case 3: Zener Output Is Incorrect

Possible causes:

  • Incorrect Zener voltage rating.
  • Zener connected with incorrect polarity.
  • Series resistor value incorrect.
  • Excessive load current.
  • Supply voltage below the required regulation level.

Case 4: Transistor Fails in Relay Driver

Possible causes:

  • Missing flyback diode.
  • Flyback diode connected with incorrect polarity.
  • Relay coil current exceeds transistor rating.
  • Insufficient transistor base drive.
  • Inadequate transistor voltage rating.

10. Diode Datasheet Selection

A diode must be selected according to the actual electrical and environmental requirements of the circuit.

Parameter Why It Matters
Maximum repetitive reverse voltage (VRRM) Ensures the diode can withstand reverse voltage.
Average forward current (IF(AV)) Determines continuous current capability under specified conditions.
Forward voltage (VF) Affects conduction loss and output voltage.
Reverse recovery time (trr) Important for high-frequency switching.
Power dissipation Determines thermal requirements.
Junction temperature Defines safe operating temperature limits.
Reverse leakage current Important for efficiency and high-impedance circuits.
Do not select a diode only by its part number or forward voltage. Verify its ratings under the actual circuit temperature, current and voltage conditions.

Experiment 1: LED Current Measurement

Components:

  • Red LED.
  • 330 Ω resistor.

Expected Result:

Objective: Observe the effect of a flyback diode on an inductive load.

Expected Result:

The flyback diode provides a recirculating current path and reduces the inductive voltage spike across the switching device.

Expected Result:

The output remains approximately equal to the Zener voltage while the diode stays within its specified regulation-current range.

12. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. How do you test a diode using a multimeter?

Use diode-test mode and check forward and reverse readings with the circuit powered OFF.

Q2. What indicates an open diode?

It fails to conduct in the forward direction under normal diode-test conditions.

Q3. What indicates a shorted diode?

It shows very low resistance or conduction in both directions, after accounting for other circuit paths.

Q4. Why is a resistor connected in series with an LED?

To limit current and prevent excessive LED current.

Q5. Why is a flyback diode used with a relay?

To provide a path for inductive current and suppress the voltage spike when the relay coil is switched OFF.

Q6. What causes a diode to overheat?

Excessive current, high forward voltage drop, reverse leakage, insufficient thermal management or incorrect circuit operation.

Q7. What is the purpose of a Zener diode regulator?

To maintain an approximately constant output voltage over a specified operating range.

Q8. Which datasheet parameters are important for diode selection?

Reverse voltage, forward current, forward voltage, power dissipation, recovery time and temperature ratings.

Q9. Why should a diode be isolated before testing?

Other circuit components can create parallel current paths and produce misleading multimeter readings.

Q10. What is the first step in electronic circuit troubleshooting?

Begin with visual inspection and verify the power supply before testing individual components.
