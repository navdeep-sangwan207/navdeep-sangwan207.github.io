Lesson 7: Special Purpose Semiconductor Diodes

1. Introduction to Special Purpose Diodes

Special-purpose diodes are semiconductor devices designed to perform specific electronic functions beyond ordinary rectification.

Different diode structures and semiconductor materials provide different electrical, optical and switching properties.

Diode Type Primary Function
LED Converts electrical energy into light.
Photodiode Converts incident light into electrical current.
Schottky Diode Fast switching and low forward voltage drop.
Varactor Diode Voltage-controlled capacitance.
PIN Diode RF switching and attenuation.
Tunnel Diode Negative differential resistance applications.
TVS Diode Transient voltage protection.
Placement Focus: Interviewers commonly ask about LED, photodiode, Schottky diode and TVS diode applications.

2. Light Emitting Diode (LED)

A Light Emitting Diode is a semiconductor device that converts electrical energy into light energy.

When an LED is forward biased, electrons and holes recombine in the semiconductor material. This process releases energy in the form of photons.

Working Principle

  • LED operates in forward bias during normal light emission.
  • Electrons and holes recombine in the active region.
  • Energy is released as photons.
  • The emitted colour depends mainly on semiconductor material and bandgap.
             LED Symbol

                  ↗  Light
             ↗
        A ----|>|---- K

        A = Anode
        K = Cathode

Typical LED Forward Voltage

LED Colour Indicative Forward Voltage
Red Approximately 1.8–2.2 V
Yellow Approximately 2.0–2.4 V
Green Approximately 2.0–3.3 V
Blue Approximately 2.7–3.5 V
White Approximately 2.7–3.5 V
LED forward voltage varies with device technology and operating current. Always check the datasheet.

LED Current-Limiting Resistor

R = (VS - VF) / IF

Numerical Example:

  • Supply voltage = 5 V
  • LED forward voltage = 2 V
  • Desired LED current = 10 mA
R = (5 - 2) / 0.01

R = 300 Ω
Final Answer: Calculated resistance = 300 Ω. A suitable standard value is 330 Ω.

Applications

  • Status indicators in embedded systems.
  • Display panels and lighting.
  • Optical communication.
  • Automotive lighting.
  • Seven-segment and LED displays.

3. Photodiode

A photodiode is a semiconductor device that converts incident light into electrical current.

When photons enter the depletion region or nearby semiconductor material, they generate electron-hole pairs. The resulting photocurrent is related to the incident optical power.

Working Principle

  • Usually operated in reverse bias for fast and sensitive detection.
  • Incident light generates electron-hole pairs.
  • The electric field separates the generated carriers.
  • Photocurrent changes with incident light intensity.
             Photodiode Symbol

           Light ↘   ↙ Light
                   |
        K --------|<|-------- A

        Light generates photocurrent.

Photodiode Operating Modes

Mode Characteristics
Photoconductive Mode Reverse biased; generally faster response and wider linear range.
Photovoltaic Mode Operates without external reverse bias and can generate a voltage.

Applications

  • Optical communication receivers.
  • Infrared remote-control receivers.
  • Light intensity measurement.
  • Smoke detectors.
  • Automatic lighting systems.
  • Optical encoders.
Photodiodes are frequently used with operational amplifiers in transimpedance amplifier circuits to convert photocurrent into a measurable voltage.

4. Schottky Diode

A Schottky diode is a semiconductor device formed using a metal-semiconductor junction rather than a conventional PN junction.

Characteristics

  • Has a relatively low forward voltage drop.
  • Provides fast switching performance.
  • Uses majority carriers for conduction.
  • Generally has negligible minority-carrier reverse recovery storage.
  • Reverse leakage can be higher than in comparable silicon PN diodes.
Typical Schottky Forward Voltage: Approximately 0.2–0.5 V
Forward voltage and reverse leakage depend on device type, current and temperature. Some Schottky diodes have significant leakage at elevated temperatures.

Applications

  • Switch-mode power supplies (SMPS).
  • High-frequency rectification.
  • Reverse polarity protection.
  • Clamping circuits.
  • High-speed switching circuits.
Interview Question: Why is a Schottky diode faster than a conventional PN junction diode? Because its majority-carrier conduction avoids the usual minority-carrier charge storage responsible for reverse recovery.

5. Varactor Diode (Varicap Diode)

A varactor diode is a specially designed PN junction diode that operates in reverse bias and behaves like a voltage-controlled capacitor.

Its junction capacitance changes when the applied reverse voltage changes.

Cj = εA / W

Where:

  • Cj = Junction capacitance.
  • ε = Permittivity of the semiconductor material.
  • A = Effective junction area.
  • W = Depletion region width.

Working Principle

  • Varactor diode is reverse biased.
  • Increasing reverse voltage widens the depletion region.
  • Junction capacitance decreases.
  • Decreasing reverse voltage increases junction capacitance.
        Reverse Voltage Increases
                   |
                   v
        Depletion Width Increases
                   |
                   v
        Junction Capacitance Decreases

Applications

  • Voltage-controlled oscillators (VCO).
  • Radio frequency tuning circuits.
  • Frequency modulation circuits.
  • Phase-locked loops (PLL).
  • Electronic tuning systems.

6. PIN Diode

A PIN diode consists of three semiconductor regions: P-type, intrinsic and N-type. 

              PIN Diode Structure

          P-Region    Intrinsic    N-Region
         __________  __________  __________
        |          ||          ||          |
        |    P     ||    I     ||    N     |
        |__________||__________||__________|

                  P - I - N

Characteristics

  • Contains a wide intrinsic region.
  • Can provide low RF resistance when forward biased.
  • Can provide high RF impedance when reverse biased.
  • Useful for high-frequency switching and attenuation.

Applications

  • RF switches.
  • RF attenuators.
  • Microwave switching circuits.
  • Photodetection in some PIN photodiode devices.

7. Tunnel Diode

A tunnel diode is a heavily doped PN junction diode that exhibits a region of negative differential resistance.

Due to heavy doping and a very thin depletion region, charge carriers can tunnel through the junction barrier.

Negative Differential Resistance

In a particular region of its V-I characteristic, increasing voltage causes current to decrease.

Negative Differential Resistance:

ΔV / ΔI < 0

In the negative-resistance region

Applications

  • High-frequency oscillators.
  • Microwave circuits.
  • High-speed switching circuits.
  • Specialized amplifier circuits.
Tunnel diodes are specialized devices and are less common in ordinary commercial circuits than LEDs, Schottky diodes and rectifier diodes.

8. TVS Diode (Transient Voltage Suppressor)

A TVS diode is a protection device designed to suppress short-duration voltage transients and protect sensitive electronic circuits.

When the transient voltage reaches the device's clamping region, the TVS conducts strongly and diverts transient current away from the protected circuit.

Characteristics

  • Provides fast transient protection.
  • Available in unidirectional and bidirectional versions.
  • Can absorb or divert short-duration surge energy within its rating.
  • Must be selected according to working voltage and surge requirements.

Important TVS Parameters

Parameter Meaning
VRWM Maximum working peak reverse voltage.
VBR Breakdown voltage under specified test current.
VC Clamping voltage at a specified peak pulse current.
IPP Peak pulse current rating under specified waveform conditions.

Applications

  • USB and communication port protection.
  • Automotive electronic systems.
  • Microcontroller input protection.
  • Power supply input protection.
  • ESD and surge protection circuits.
Embedded Systems Relevance: TVS diodes are useful for protecting UART, CAN, USB and other external interfaces against electrostatic discharge and voltage transients. Select the TVS device according to the interface's signal and protection requirements.

10. Practical Diode Selection

Selecting a suitable diode requires understanding the operating conditions and electrical requirements of the circuit.

Application Suitable Diode Type
Indicator LED LED
Light sensing Photodiode
Low-loss fast rectification Schottky diode, if voltage ratings permit
Electronic RF tuning Varactor diode
RF signal switching PIN diode
High-frequency oscillator Tunnel diode in specialized designs
Transient protection TVS diode

Datasheet Parameters to Check

  • Maximum forward current.
  • Maximum reverse voltage.
  • Forward voltage drop.
  • Reverse leakage current.
  • Power dissipation.
  • Junction capacitance.
  • Switching or recovery time.
  • Temperature operating range.

11. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is an LED?

An LED is a semiconductor diode that emits light when forward biased.

Q2. What is a photodiode?

It is a semiconductor device that converts incident light into electrical current.

Q3. Why is a photodiode commonly reverse biased?

Reverse bias generally provides faster response and a wider useful detection range.

Q4. What is a Schottky diode?

It is a metal-semiconductor diode with relatively low forward voltage and fast switching characteristics.

Q5. What is the main application of a varactor diode?

Voltage-controlled capacitance for RF tuning and frequency control.

Q6. What is a PIN diode?

It is a diode with P-type, intrinsic and N-type regions, commonly used in RF switching and attenuation.

Q7. What is negative differential resistance?

It is a region where current decreases as voltage increases.

Q8. What is a TVS diode?

It is a protection device used to suppress short-duration voltage transients.

Q9. Which diode is commonly used for fast, low-loss rectification?

A Schottky diode is often used when its reverse-voltage and leakage specifications are suitable.

Q10. Which diode is used for light detection?

A photodiode.
