Chapter 3: Semiconductor Diodes and Applications

Lesson 4: Diode Equation and Operating Parameters

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand the mathematical diode equation.
  • Explain the meaning of reverse saturation current.

  • Calculate diode current using the Shockley equation.

  • Calculate diode power dissipation.

1. Introduction to the Diode Equation

A semiconductor diode does not have a linear relationship between its voltage and current. Its current changes approximately exponentially with the applied forward voltage.

The mathematical relationship between diode voltage and current is described by the Shockley diode equation.

ID = IS [eVD/(nVT) - 1]

Where:

Symbol Meaning
ID Diode current in amperes.
IS Reverse saturation current.
VD Voltage across the diode.
n Ideality factor.
VT Thermal voltage.
e Base of natural logarithms, approximately 2.718.
Placement Focus: The Shockley diode equation is important for understanding semiconductor device behaviour and solving diode-current problems.

2. Reverse Saturation Current (IS)

Reverse saturation current is the small current associated with minority carriers in a reverse-biased PN junction.

In the Shockley equation, IS is a parameter that strongly depends on semiconductor material, junction construction and temperature.

Important Properties

  • It is generally very small in silicon PN junction diodes.
  • It increases with temperature.
  • It influences the forward current predicted by the diode equation.
  • It varies considerably between different diode types.
IS is not the same as the maximum permitted reverse current rating. The actual reverse leakage current must be checked under the specified datasheet conditions.

3. Thermal Voltage (VT)

Thermal voltage is the voltage equivalent of thermal energy in a semiconductor device.

VT = kT / q

Where:

  • k: Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10-23 J/K.
  • T: Absolute temperature in Kelvin.
  • q: Electron charge = 1.602 × 10-19 C.

At approximately 300 K (27°C), thermal voltage is:

VT ≈ 25.9 mV
Thermal voltage increases with absolute temperature. Remember that temperature in this equation must be in Kelvin, not directly in Celsius.

4. Ideality Factor (n)

The ideality factor describes how closely the actual diode follows the ideal Shockley diode model.

  • For an ideal diffusion-dominated diode, n is approximately 1.
  • In practical diodes, n can be greater than 1.
  • Its value depends on the device and operating region.
Ideality Factor Interpretation
n ≈ 1 Diffusion-dominated ideal diode behaviour.
n ≈ 2 Recombination effects can be significant.
The values above are simplified models. Actual diode behaviour can vary with current, temperature and device structure.

5. Simplified Diode Equation in Forward Bias

In forward bias, when VD is several times larger than nVT, the exponential term becomes much larger than 1.

Therefore, the Shockley equation can be approximated as:

ID ≈ IS eVD/(nVT)

This simplified equation shows why diode current increases rapidly as forward voltage increases.

6. Numerical Example: Diode Current

Given:

  • Reverse saturation current = 1 μA
  • Forward voltage = 0.1 V
  • Ideality factor = 1
  • Thermal voltage = 25.9 mV

Find: Diode current.

Solution:

ID = IS [eVD/(nVT) - 1]

ID = 1 × 10-6 [e0.1/0.0259 - 1]

ID ≈ 46.9 μA
Final Answer: Diode current ≈ 46.9 μA
This is an idealized model calculation. Practical diode current can differ because of series resistance, leakage, temperature and non-ideal device effects.

7. Forward Voltage (VF)

Forward voltage is the voltage drop across a diode when current flows through it in the forward direction.

Diode Type Typical Forward Voltage Range
Silicon PN Diode Approximately 0.6–0.8 V
Germanium Diode Approximately 0.2–0.3 V
Schottky Diode Approximately 0.2–0.5 V
LED Depends on semiconductor material and colour.
These are indicative ranges, not guaranteed specifications. Always use the manufacturer's datasheet at the intended operating current and temperature.

8. Reverse Voltage and Leakage Parameters

A diode is designed to block reverse voltage up to specified limits. Exceeding the applicable reverse-voltage rating can cause breakdown or permanent damage.

Important Reverse Parameters

  • VRRM: Maximum repetitive peak reverse voltage.
  • VR: Reverse voltage under a specified condition.
  • IR: Reverse leakage current.
  • VBR: Breakdown voltage.
Do not assume that the maximum repetitive reverse voltage and breakdown voltage are interchangeable. Check the manufacturer's definitions and test conditions.

9. Diode Power Dissipation

Power dissipation represents the electrical power converted into heat within the diode.

PD = VD × ID

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Forward voltage = 0.7 V
  • Forward current = 100 mA

Solution:

PD = 0.7 × 0.1

PD = 0.07 W

PD = 70 mW
Final Answer: Diode power dissipation = 70 mW
The calculated power must remain within the device's permitted power and thermal limits. Actual power handling depends on ambient temperature, PCB layout and cooling.

12. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is the Shockley diode equation?

It is the mathematical equation describing the relationship between diode current and applied voltage.

Q2. What is reverse saturation current?

It is a small current associated with minority carriers in a reverse-biased PN junction.

Q3. What is thermal voltage?

Thermal voltage is kT/q and is approximately 25.9 mV at 300 K.

Q4. What is the ideality factor?

It is a parameter that represents how closely the diode follows the ideal diode model.

Q5. Why does diode current increase exponentially?

Forward bias reduces the junction barrier, allowing carrier injection across the junction. The resulting current follows an approximately exponential voltage relationship.

Q6. What is forward voltage?

It is the voltage drop across a diode while it conducts forward current.

Q7. How is diode power dissipation calculated?

Multiply the voltage across the diode by the current flowing through it.

Q8. What is reverse recovery time?

It is the time required for a diode to transition from forward conduction to its reverse-blocking state after the current is reversed.

Q9. Why is a diode datasheet important?

It provides operating limits and electrical characteristics needed to select a safe and suitable component.

Q10. What happens to reverse saturation current when temperature increases?

It generally increases significantly with temperature.
