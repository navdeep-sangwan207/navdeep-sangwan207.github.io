1. Introduction to Diode Biasing

Biasing is the process of applying an external DC voltage to an electronic component to establish its operating condition.

A PN junction diode can operate under two main biasing conditions:

  • Forward bias.
  • Reverse bias.
The polarity of the external voltage determines whether the diode is forward biased or reverse biased.

2. Forward Biasing of PN Junction Diode

A PN junction diode is forward biased when the P-type region is connected to the positive terminal of the external supply and the N-type region is connected to its negative terminal.

A current-limiting resistor is normally connected in series with the diode.

Effects of Forward Bias

  1. The external voltage opposes the built-in potential.
  2. The potential barrier decreases.
  3. The depletion region becomes narrower.
  4. Majority carriers can cross the junction more easily.
  5. Electrons and holes are injected across the junction.
  6. Significant forward current flows.
Forward bias reduces the barrier to majority-carrier injection. The resulting diode current increases rapidly with applied voltage.

3. Forward Current Conduction

Under forward bias, electrons from the N-side are injected into the P-side, while holes from the P-side are injected into the N-side.

Electron Movement

Electrons move from the N-type region toward the P-type region across the junction.

Hole Movement

Holes move from the P-type region toward the N-type region across the junction.

Conventional Current Direction

Conventional current flows from the anode toward the cathode through the forward-biased diode.

Conventional Current →
Cathode (N)
Electron movement is opposite to conventional current direction. Hole movement is in the same direction as conventional current.

4. Depletion Region Under Forward Bias

The depletion region is the region around the PN junction that contains fixed ionized dopant atoms and has a reduced concentration of mobile majority carriers.

When forward bias is applied, the external voltage opposes the built-in electric field and reduces the potential barrier.

Result

  • The depletion region becomes narrower.
  • The effective barrier is reduced.
  • More majority carriers cross the junction.
  • Forward current increases.
Increasing forward voltage generally narrows the depletion region and increases carrier injection, resulting in a rapid rise in current.

5. Forward Voltage Drop

A practical diode has a non-zero voltage across it during forward conduction. This is called the forward voltage drop.

Diode Type Typical Forward Voltage
Silicon PN Diode Approximately 0.7 V
Germanium Diode Approximately 0.3 V
Schottky Diode Often approximately 0.2–0.4 V
LED Depends on semiconductor material and colour
These values are approximate. Forward voltage depends on operating current, temperature and the specific device.

6. Reverse Biasing of PN Junction Diode

A PN junction diode is reverse biased when the P-type region is connected to the negative terminal of the external supply and the N-type region is connected to its positive terminal.

Effects of Reverse Bias

  1. The external voltage reinforces the built-in potential.
  2. The potential barrier increases.
  3. The depletion region becomes wider.
  4. Majority-carrier injection across the junction is suppressed.
  5. A small reverse leakage current flows.
Reverse bias widens the depletion region and strongly restricts majority-carrier conduction.

7. Reverse Leakage Current

Under reverse bias, a small current flows because of thermally generated minority carriers.

This current is called reverse leakage current. It is generally small compared with the forward current.

Characteristics

  • It is produced mainly by minority carriers.
  • It flows in the reverse direction.
  • It is generally small before breakdown.
  • It increases significantly with temperature.
  • Its magnitude depends on diode construction and material.
Reverse leakage current must not be confused with reverse breakdown current. Breakdown produces a much larger reverse current and requires appropriate current limiting.

8. Depletion Region Under Reverse Bias

Reverse bias increases the effective potential barrier and pulls mobile majority carriers away from the junction.

Result

  • The depletion region becomes wider.
  • The electric field in the depletion region increases.
  • Majority-carrier conduction is suppressed.
  • Only a small reverse leakage current normally flows.

9. Breakdown Under Reverse Bias

If reverse voltage reaches the diode's breakdown voltage, reverse current increases sharply.

Two Important Breakdown Mechanisms

1. Zener Breakdown

  • Occurs in heavily doped junctions.
  • Depletion region is very narrow.
  • Strong electric field enables quantum-mechanical tunnelling.
  • Commonly important at relatively low breakdown voltages.

2. Avalanche Breakdown

  • Occurs through impact ionization.
  • Carriers gain energy from the electric field.
  • They generate additional electron-hole pairs.
  • Often occurs at higher breakdown voltages.
Zener and avalanche breakdown mechanisms will be discussed in detail in Lesson 5.

11. Numerical Examples

Example 1: Forward Current Calculation

Given:

  • Supply voltage = 10 V
  • Diode forward voltage = 0.7 V
  • Series resistor = 1 kΩ

Using Ohm's law:

I = (VS − VD) / R
I = (10 − 0.7) / 1000
Expected Result:

Forward current = 9.3 mA

Example 2: Series Resistor Calculation

Given:

  • Supply voltage = 12 V
  • Diode forward voltage = 0.7 V
  • Required current = 10 mA
R = (VS − VD) / I
R = (12 − 0.7) / 0.01
Expected Result:

R = 1130 Ω

A suitable standard value is 1.2 kΩ.

Always verify resistor power rating and diode current rating when designing a real circuit.

12. Practical Applications of Diode Biasing

Application Use
Rectifier Uses forward conduction to convert AC into pulsating DC.
Reverse Polarity Protection Prevents incorrect supply polarity from damaging circuits.
Signal Switching Controls current paths in electronic circuits.
Clipping Circuit Limits the amplitude of a signal.
Zener Regulator Uses controlled reverse breakdown to regulate voltage.
Flyback Protection Provides a path for inductive-load current when switching off.

15. Lesson Summary

  • Forward bias connects P-side to positive and N-side to negative.
  • Forward bias reduces the potential barrier and depletion width.
  • Significant current flows under forward bias.
  • Reverse bias connects P-side to negative and N-side to positive.
  • Reverse bias increases the potential barrier and depletion width.
  • A small reverse leakage current flows before breakdown.
  • Reverse breakdown produces a sharp increase in reverse current.
  • A series resistor is used to control current in practical circuits.
