Chapter 3: Semiconductor Diodes and Applications

Lesson 6: Zener Diode and Voltage Regulation

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand the construction and working of a Zener diode.
  • Explain its operation in forward and reverse bias.
  • Understand the Zener V-I characteristics.
  • Explain the principle of Zener voltage regulation.
  • Calculate series resistance and Zener current.
  • Calculate Zener power dissipation.
  • Understand line and load regulation.
  • Identify practical applications of Zener diodes.

1. Introduction to Zener Diode

A Zener diode is a specially designed PN junction diode intended to operate in its reverse breakdown region.

Unlike an ordinary rectifier diode, a Zener diode can maintain an approximately constant voltage across itself over a specified range of reverse current.

Zener Diode = Semiconductor Diode Designed for Controlled Reverse-Breakdown Operation

Important Characteristics

  • Designed for reverse-breakdown operation.
  • Maintains approximately constant voltage in its regulation region.
  • Available with different nominal Zener voltage ratings.
  • Requires current limiting during normal regulation.
Placement Focus: Zener diode voltage regulation is a frequently asked topic in electronics and embedded hardware interviews.

2. Zener Diode Symbol and Construction

A Zener diode has a PN junction symbol similar to an ordinary diode, but its cathode line is bent. 

       Zener Diode Symbol

              Bent Cathode Line
                     |
          A          K
          |          |
          ----|<|----
              

       A = Anode
       K = Cathode

Zener diodes are designed with junction properties that provide a specified reverse breakdown voltage.

Zener diodes are available in a range of voltage and power ratings. Always select a device based on its datasheet.

3. Working Principle of Zener Diode

Forward-Bias Operation

When forward biased, a Zener diode behaves similarly to an ordinary silicon PN junction diode.

  • Anode is connected to positive supply.
  • Cathode is connected to negative supply.
  • It conducts after sufficient forward voltage is applied.

Reverse-Bias Operation

When reverse biased, only a small leakage current flows until the reverse voltage approaches the Zener breakdown voltage.

Once the breakdown region is reached, reverse current increases while the voltage across the diode remains approximately constant.

VZ ≈ Constant

Within the Specified Zener Regulation Current Range
The Zener voltage is not perfectly constant. It varies slightly with current, temperature and dynamic impedance.

5. Zener Diode as a Voltage Regulator

A Zener diode can be used as a simple shunt voltage regulator. It is connected in reverse bias across the load, while a series resistor limits the current supplied by the source.

Circuit Diagram

                Rs
       +Vs ----/\/\/\-----+---------+
                          |         |
                          |         |
                         K|         RL
                      Zener         |
                         A|         |
                          |         |
       GND ---------------+---------+

                 Output Voltage
                 Vout ≈ VZ

       Zener diode is reverse biased.
       K = Cathode, A = Anode

Working

  1. The input DC supply is applied through the series resistor.
  2. The Zener diode is connected in reverse bias across the load.
  3. The Zener diode operates in its breakdown region.
  4. It maintains an approximately constant output voltage.
  5. The series resistor limits the current.
VOUT ≈ VZ
A Zener regulator works only while the Zener current remains within its permitted regulation range and its power rating is not exceeded.

6. Series Resistor Calculation

The series resistor is selected to limit current and provide sufficient Zener and load current.

RS = (VS - VZ) / (IZ + IL)

Where:

  • VS = Input supply voltage.
  • VZ = Zener voltage.
  • IZ = Zener current.
  • IL = Load current.

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Input voltage = 12 V
  • Zener voltage = 5.1 V
  • Load current = 10 mA
  • Desired Zener current = 5 mA

Solution:

RS = (12 - 5.1) / (0.01 + 0.005)

RS = 6.9 / 0.015

RS = 460 Ω

Select a suitable standard resistor, such as 470 Ω, and verify the actual current.

Final Answer: Calculated series resistance = 460 Ω.

7. Zener Current and Load Current

The current flowing through the series resistor divides between the Zener diode and the load.

IS = IZ + IL

The current through the series resistor is:

IS = (VS - VZ) / RS

Therefore:

IZ = IS - IL

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Input voltage = 12 V
  • Zener voltage = 5 V
  • Series resistor = 700 Ω
  • Load current = 5 mA

Solution:

IS = (12 - 5) / 700

IS = 10 mA

IZ = 10 - 5

IZ = 5 mA
Final Answer: Zener current = 5 mA.

8. Zener Power Dissipation

Zener power dissipation is the product of Zener voltage and the current flowing through the Zener diode.

PZ = VZ × IZ

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Zener voltage = 5.1 V
  • Zener current = 20 mA

Solution:

PZ = 5.1 × 0.02

PZ = 0.102 W

PZ = 102 mW
Final Answer: Zener power dissipation = 102 mW.
The selected Zener diode must have an adequate power rating after considering ambient temperature, PCB layout and thermal derating.

9. Line Regulation

Line regulation describes how much the output voltage changes when the input voltage changes while the load is kept constant.

Line Regulation = ΔVOUT / ΔVIN

A good voltage regulator maintains a nearly constant output voltage despite variations in input voltage.

Line regulation is commonly expressed as mV/V or as a percentage, depending on the specification.

10. Load Regulation

Load regulation describes how much the output voltage changes when the load current changes while the input supply is kept constant.

Load Regulation = (VNL - VFL) / VFL × 100%

Where:

  • VNL = No-load output voltage.
  • VFL = Full-load output voltage.
Some datasheets and engineering specifications use different reference values or units for load regulation. Always check the stated definition.

11. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Simple voltage regulation circuit.
  • Low component count.
  • Provides a useful voltage reference.
  • Can protect circuits against limited overvoltage conditions.
  • Available in several voltage ratings.

Limitations

  • Efficiency can be low because current flows through the series resistor.
  • Output regulation is limited compared with precision regulators.
  • Power dissipation restricts load current.
  • Output voltage changes slightly with current and temperature.
  • Not suitable for every high-current power supply application.

12. Practical Applications

  • Simple DC voltage regulators.
  • Reference voltage generation.
  • Overvoltage protection circuits.
  • Voltage clamping circuits.
  • Input protection for electronic circuits.
  • Biasing and threshold circuits.
  • Power supply regulation stages.

13. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is a Zener diode?

It is a semiconductor diode designed to operate in its reverse breakdown region.

Q2. What is the main application of a Zener diode?

Voltage regulation and reference voltage generation.

Q3. Why is a Zener diode reverse biased in a regulator?

It operates in the breakdown region where its voltage remains approximately constant over a specified current range.

Q4. Why is a series resistor required?

It limits the current flowing through the Zener diode and load.

Q5. What is Zener voltage?

It is the nominal reverse voltage maintained by the Zener diode under specified operating conditions.

Q6. What is Zener power dissipation?

It is the power consumed by the Zener diode, calculated using PZ = VZ × IZ.

Q7. What is line regulation?

It describes output voltage variation due to changes in input voltage.

Q8. What is load regulation?

It describes output voltage variation due to changes in load current.

Q9. Can a Zener diode be used in forward bias?

Yes. It behaves similarly to an ordinary silicon diode when forward biased.

Q10. Why is a Zener diode unsuitable for every power supply?

Its current and power handling are limited, and a simple shunt regulator can waste significant power.

Lesson Summary

  • A Zener diode is designed for controlled reverse-breakdown operation.
  • It maintains an approximately constant voltage in its regulation region.
  • A series resistor limits current in a shunt regulator.
  • Series resistor current equals Zener current plus load current.
  • Zener power dissipation is VZ × IZ.

  • Load regulation measures output changes due to load variations.
