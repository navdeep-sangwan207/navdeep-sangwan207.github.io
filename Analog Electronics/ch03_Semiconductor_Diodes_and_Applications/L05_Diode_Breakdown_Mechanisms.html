Chapter 3: Semiconductor Diodes and Applications

Lesson 5: Diode Breakdown Mechanisms

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand reverse breakdown in a PN junction diode.
  • Explain Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.
  • Differentiate between the two breakdown mechanisms.
  • Understand the effect of doping concentration.
  • Explain breakdown voltage and temperature effects.
  • Understand the need for current limiting.
  • Identify practical applications of breakdown devices.

1. Introduction to Diode Breakdown

A PN junction diode normally blocks current when it is reverse biased. However, when the applied reverse voltage reaches a sufficiently high value, the reverse current increases sharply.

This phenomenon is called reverse breakdown. The reverse voltage at which this rapid increase occurs is called the breakdown voltage.

Reverse Breakdown:

Reverse Voltage reaches VBR
Reverse Current increases sharply

Important Points

  • Breakdown occurs under reverse-bias conditions.
  • The depletion region experiences a strong electric field.
  • Reverse current can become very large.
  • External current limiting is necessary to prevent damage.
Placement Focus: The two major breakdown mechanisms in a PN junction are Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.

2. Electric Field in a Reverse-Biased PN Junction

When a PN junction is reverse biased, the depletion region widens. Increasing reverse voltage increases the electric field across this region.

At a sufficiently high electric field, charge carriers can produce a large reverse current through different physical mechanisms.

3. Zener Breakdown

Zener breakdown is a reverse breakdown mechanism that occurs in a heavily doped PN junction with a very narrow depletion region.

Under a strong reverse electric field, electrons can tunnel through the energy barrier from the valence band of one side to the conduction band of the other side.

This quantum-mechanical phenomenon is called tunnelling.

Characteristics of Zener Breakdown

  • Occurs in heavily doped junctions.
  • Has a narrow depletion region.
  • Associated with a strong electric field.
  • Dominated by quantum-mechanical tunnelling.
  • Commonly important at relatively low breakdown voltages.

Zener breakdown is the operating principle used in Zener diodes for voltage regulation and reference circuits.

4. Avalanche Breakdown

Avalanche breakdown generally occurs in a more lightly doped PN junction with a wider depletion region.

When reverse voltage becomes sufficiently high, minority carriers accelerate through the electric field and gain enough energy to collide with atoms.

These collisions can generate additional electron-hole pairs. The newly generated carriers are also accelerated and create more carriers through further collisions.

This multiplication process is called impact ionization.

Characteristics of Avalanche Breakdown

  • Generally occurs in more lightly doped junctions.
  • Has a wider depletion region.
  • Involves impact ionization.
  • Produces carrier multiplication.
  • Commonly associated with relatively higher breakdown voltages.

5. Difference Between Zener and Avalanche Breakdown

Parameter Zener Breakdown Avalanche Breakdown
Junction Doping Heavily doped Generally lightly doped
Depletion Width Narrow Wider
Mechanism Quantum tunnelling Impact ionization
Electric Field Very strong across a narrow region Accelerates carriers through a wider region
Typical Voltage Range Often lower breakdown voltages Often higher breakdown voltages
Temperature Coefficient Generally negative Generally positive
The breakdown mechanisms can coexist in practical devices. The dominant mechanism depends on junction design and breakdown voltage. The voltage ranges are not absolute boundaries.

6. Breakdown Voltage

Breakdown voltage is the reverse voltage at which the diode enters its breakdown region and reverse current increases sharply.

VBR = Reverse Breakdown Voltage

Factors Affecting Breakdown Voltage

  • Doping concentration of the P and N regions.
  • Width of the depletion region.
  • Junction geometry and semiconductor material.
  • Temperature and physical breakdown mechanism.
Breakdown voltage is a device characteristic. It should be obtained from the datasheet or measured under controlled, current-limited conditions.

7. Temperature Effect on Breakdown

Temperature affects the breakdown voltage of a semiconductor junction. The direction of the change depends on the dominant breakdown mechanism.

Breakdown Mechanism Typical Temperature Coefficient
Zener-dominated breakdown Negative
Avalanche-dominated breakdown Positive

Near approximately 5.6 V in many practical silicon Zener diodes, the two mechanisms can contribute together, resulting in a relatively small temperature coefficient.

The approximate 5.6 V value is a useful design guideline, not a universal boundary. Actual temperature behaviour depends on the particular device.

Important Observation

In the breakdown region, the diode voltage changes relatively little compared with the increase in reverse current for many breakdown devices.

This property is used in Zener voltage regulation circuits.

9. Current Limiting During Breakdown

When a diode enters breakdown, its reverse current can increase to a destructive value if the circuit does not limit the current.

A series resistor or another suitable current-control arrangement is used to keep the current within the diode's specified operating limits.

Example: Series Resistor for a Zener Circuit

Given:

  • Supply voltage = 12 V
  • Zener voltage = 5.6 V
  • Desired Zener current = 10 mA
  • Assume no load is connected.

Solution:

R = (VS - VZ) / IZ

R = (12 - 5.6) / 0.01

R = 640 Ω

A suitable standard resistor could be 680 Ω, subject to checking the resulting current and power ratings.

Using 680 Ω:

IZ = (12 - 5.6) / 680

IZ ≈ 9.41 mA
In a practical Zener regulator with a load, the series resistor current is shared between the load and the Zener. The design must check minimum and maximum input voltage, load current, Zener current and resistor power.

10. Applications of Breakdown Mechanisms

  • Zener voltage regulators.
  • Voltage reference circuits.
  • Overvoltage protection circuits.
  • Transient voltage suppression devices.
  • Surge protection circuits.
  • Clamping and limiting circuits.
Zener and avalanche effects are useful when the device is designed and operated within its specified breakdown limits. Uncontrolled breakdown can damage an ordinary diode.

11. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is diode breakdown?

It is the condition in which reverse current increases sharply when the reverse voltage reaches the breakdown region.

Q2. What are the two main breakdown mechanisms?

Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.

Q3. What is Zener breakdown?

It is reverse breakdown caused by quantum-mechanical tunnelling in a heavily doped junction.

Q4. What is avalanche breakdown?

It is reverse breakdown caused by impact ionization and multiplication of charge carriers.

Q5. Which breakdown occurs in a heavily doped junction?

Zener breakdown is associated with heavily doped junctions.

Q6. Which breakdown involves impact ionization?

Avalanche breakdown.

Q7. Why is the depletion region narrow in Zener breakdown?

Heavy doping on both sides of the junction produces a narrow depletion region.

Q8. Why is current limiting necessary?

It prevents excessive reverse current from overheating and damaging the diode.

Q9. What is the approximate temperature coefficient of Zener breakdown?

It is generally negative.

Q10. What is the temperature coefficient of avalanche breakdown?

It is generally positive.

Q11. Why are breakdown diodes useful?

They can provide voltage regulation, reference voltages and overvoltage protection when correctly designed.

Lesson Summary

  • Reverse breakdown occurs when reverse voltage reaches a critical value.
  • Zener breakdown is based on quantum-mechanical tunnelling.
  • Avalanche breakdown is based on impact ionization.
  • Zener breakdown is associated with heavily doped junctions.
  • Avalanche breakdown is generally associated with more lightly doped junctions.
  • Breakdown voltage depends on junction construction and operating conditions.
  • Current limiting is essential for safe breakdown operation.
  • Breakdown mechanisms are used in voltage regulation and protection circuits.
