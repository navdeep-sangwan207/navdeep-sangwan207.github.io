1. Introduction to Clippers and Clampers

Clippers and clampers are diode-based wave-shaping circuits. They are commonly used to modify the shape or voltage level of an input signal.

Circuit Function
Clipper Removes or limits a portion of an input waveform.
Clamper Shifts the DC level of a waveform without ideally changing its peak-to-peak value.
Placement Focus: A common interview question is: "What is the difference between a clipper and a clamper?"

2. Diode Clipper Circuit

A clipper circuit, also called a limiter, removes a selected portion of an input signal when the signal crosses a specified voltage level.

The remaining portion of the waveform appears at the output. A clipper can be designed using diodes, resistors and, in biased circuits, DC voltage sources.

Basic Operation

  • When the diode is OFF, the signal may pass through the circuit.
  • When the diode becomes forward biased, it conducts.
  • The conducting diode limits the output voltage according to the circuit configuration.
The exact clipping level depends on diode orientation, circuit topology, bias voltage and diode forward voltage.

3. Series Clipper

In a series clipper, the diode is connected in series with the load.

The diode controls whether the input signal reaches the load. When the diode is reverse biased, the load current is blocked in the ideal-diode model.

Operation

  • During one input polarity, the diode may conduct.
  • During the opposite polarity, the diode may block current.
  • The output therefore contains only the portion allowed by the diode orientation.

5. Shunt Clipper

In a shunt clipper, the diode is connected in parallel with the output load. 

         R
Vin ----/\/\/\----+---- Vout
                  |
                  |----|>|---- GND
                  |
                  RL
                  |
                 GND

Working Principle

  • When the diode is OFF, the output is determined by the input and load network.
  • When the diode is ON, it provides a low-resistance path that limits the output voltage.
  • The resistor limits current through the diode.

Series vs Shunt Clipper

Series Clipper Shunt Clipper
Diode is in series with the load. Diode is in parallel with the load.
Diode controls current through the load. Diode diverts current when conducting.
OFF state can block load current. ON state can clamp the output.

6. Biased Clipper

A biased clipper uses a DC voltage source along with a diode to set a clipping level other than approximately zero volts.

The diode begins conducting when the input crosses the bias-adjusted forward conduction threshold.

Approximate Positive Clipping Level:

VCLIP ≈ VB + VF

Where:

  • VB = Bias voltage, with polarity chosen for the circuit.
  • VF = Forward voltage drop of the diode.
The sign and value of the clipping threshold depend on the orientation of the diode and DC source. The formula above applies to a common positive biased-clipping arrangement.

Applications

  • Signal amplitude limiting.
  • Input protection stages.
  • Waveform shaping.
  • Communication circuits.

7. Introduction to Clamper Circuits

A clamper circuit shifts the entire waveform upward or downward by adding a DC level.

It generally uses a diode, capacitor and resistor. The capacitor charges during one part of the input cycle and helps shift the waveform during the remaining cycle.

Important Characteristics

  • Changes the DC reference level of the signal.
  • Ideally preserves the peak-to-peak voltage.
  • Uses a capacitor to store charge.
  • Uses a diode to establish the clamping reference.
A clamper is also called a DC restorer because it can restore or establish a desired DC component in a waveform.

8. Positive Clamper

A positive clamper shifts the input waveform in the positive direction so that its negative peak is approximately clamped to the reference level.

In an ideal positive clamper, the negative peak is approximately zero volts. 

       Input: Centered AC Waveform

             /\
            /  \
-----------/----\-----------
          /      \
         /        \

       Positive Clamped Waveform

              /\
             /  \
------------/----\-----------
           /      \
          /        \

Working Principle

  • During the diode-conducting half-cycle, the capacitor charges.
  • During the opposite half-cycle, the diode is OFF.
  • The stored capacitor voltage adds to the input signal.
  • The waveform shifts upward.

9. Negative Clamper

A negative clamper shifts the input waveform in the negative direction so that its positive peak is approximately clamped to the reference level.

In an ideal negative clamper, the positive peak is approximately zero volts.

Working Principle

  • The diode conducts during the appropriate input half-cycle.
  • The capacitor charges with a polarity opposite to that in a positive clamper.
  • During the other half-cycle, the stored voltage shifts the input downward.

10. Biased Clamper

A biased clamper uses a DC reference voltage to clamp a waveform at a selected voltage level instead of approximately zero volts.

The DC source changes the voltage level at which the diode conducts and establishes the waveform reference.

Clamper Type Output Shift
Positive Clamper Upward
Negative Clamper Downward
Biased Clamper Upward or downward to a selected reference level

11. Clipper vs Clamper

Parameter Clipper Clamper
Function Removes part of a waveform. Shifts the DC level of a waveform.
Main Components Diode and resistor; bias source if required. Diode, capacitor and resistor; bias source if required.
Peak-to-Peak Voltage Generally reduced when clipping occurs. Ideally remains unchanged.
Output Limited waveform. DC-shifted waveform.
Applications Amplitude limiting and protection. DC restoration and level shifting.

12. Important Design Considerations

For Clipper Circuits

  • Check diode orientation and forward voltage.
  • Calculate the clipping threshold.
  • Choose a suitable current-limiting resistor.
  • Verify diode reverse-voltage and power ratings.

For Clamper Circuits

  • Choose a suitable capacitor value.
  • Use a sufficiently large RC time constant.
  • Check capacitor voltage rating and polarity.
  • Consider diode forward voltage and leakage.
  • Account for load resistance and signal frequency.
Clamper Design Guideline:

RC >> T

T = Input signal period
The RC time constant should generally be much larger than the signal period so that the capacitor voltage does not discharge significantly during a cycle.

13. Practical Applications

Clipper Applications

  • Voltage limiting circuits.
  • Waveform shaping circuits.
  • Signal conditioning.
  • Protection against excessive input amplitudes.
  • Communication signal processing.

Clamper Applications

  • DC restoration in video circuits.
  • Voltage level shifting.
  • Signal conditioning.
  • Waveform restoration.
  • Pulse circuits.

14. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is a clipper circuit?

A clipper is a diode-based circuit that removes or limits a selected portion of an input waveform.

Q2. What is a clamper circuit?

A clamper shifts the DC level of a waveform without ideally changing its peak-to-peak voltage.

Q3. What is the difference between a series and shunt clipper?

In a series clipper, the diode is in series with the load. In a shunt clipper, the diode is connected in parallel with the load.

Q4. What is a biased clipper?

A clipper that uses an external DC source to set a non-zero clipping threshold.

Q5. What is a positive clamper?

A circuit that shifts a waveform in the positive direction.

Q6. What is a negative clamper?

A circuit that shifts a waveform in the negative direction.

Q7. Which components are commonly used in a clamper?

A diode, capacitor and resistor.

Q8. Why should the RC time constant be large in a clamper?

To reduce capacitor discharge during a signal cycle and maintain a relatively stable voltage shift.

Q9. Does a clamper ideally change peak-to-peak voltage?

No. It shifts the DC level while ideally preserving peak-to-peak voltage.

Q10. What is another name for a clipper?

A limiter circuit.
