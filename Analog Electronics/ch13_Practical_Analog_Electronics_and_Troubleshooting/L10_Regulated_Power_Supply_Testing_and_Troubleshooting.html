Lesson 10: Regulated Power Supply Testing and Troubleshooting

Voltage Measurement, Load Testing, Ripple and Fault Diagnosis

1. Introduction

A regulated power supply provides a stable DC voltage to electronic circuits even when the input voltage or load current changes within the specified operating range.

Regulated power supplies are used in embedded systems, industrial control panels, laboratory equipment, communication systems and consumer electronic products.

Learning Objective: Learn how to test a regulated power supply, measure input and output voltages, check ripple, perform load testing and diagnose common power supply faults.

2. What is a Regulated Power Supply?

A regulated power supply converts an input electrical source into a controlled DC output suitable for powering electronic circuits.

Basic Blocks of a Regulated Power Supply

  1. AC input or DC input source.
  2. Transformer, where required.
  3. Rectifier.
  4. Filter circuit.
  5. Voltage regulator.
  6. Load.

In a DC-input switching regulator, the transformer and rectifier stages may not be required. The actual architecture depends on the power supply design.

3. Types of Regulated Power Supplies

Linear Regulated Power Supply

A linear regulator controls the output voltage by adjusting the conduction of a series pass element.

It is simple and produces low output noise in many applications, but can dissipate significant power as heat.

Switching Regulated Power Supply

A switching power supply uses high-frequency switching and energy-storage components to regulate the output voltage.

It generally offers higher efficiency and smaller size, but its output may contain switching ripple and electromagnetic noise.

Parameter Linear Supply Switching Supply
Efficiency Often lower Often higher
Heat Can be significant Generally lower for equivalent output power
Circuit Complexity Relatively simple More complex
Electrical Noise Generally low Switching noise must be managed
Applications Low-noise analog circuits Computers, chargers and embedded systems

5. Required Testing Instruments

  • Digital multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Adjustable DC power supply.
  • Electronic load or suitable power resistors.
  • Thermometer or thermal camera, where available.
  • Insulated test leads and probes.

Purpose of Each Instrument

Instrument Testing Purpose
Digital Multimeter Measure DC voltage, AC voltage, current and resistance.
Oscilloscope Observe ripple, noise and transient response.
Adjustable DC Supply Test regulator operation at different input voltages.
Electronic Load Apply controlled load current.
Thermal Camera Identify excessive heating.

6. Initial Visual Inspection

Before applying power, inspect the power supply for visible defects.

Components to Inspect

  • Burnt or cracked resistors.
  • Bulging or leaking electrolytic capacitors.
  • Damaged rectifier diodes.
  • Cracked solder joints.
  • Damaged PCB tracks.
  • Loose connectors and wires.
  • Overheated regulator packages.
  • Incorrect component polarity.
Visual inspection can reveal many common faults before electrical testing begins.

7. Input Voltage Testing

Input voltage must be within the specified operating range of the power supply or voltage regulator.

Procedure

  1. Identify the input terminals from the schematic or datasheet.
  2. Select the appropriate voltage mode on the multimeter.
  3. Measure AC input for an AC-input supply.
  4. Measure DC input for a DC-input regulator circuit.
  5. Compare the measured voltage with the specified input range.
For practical exercises, use a current-limited, isolated low-voltage source. Do not probe mains input terminals without appropriate training, supervision and safety equipment.

8. Rectifier and Filter Stage Testing

Before checking the regulator, verify that the rectifier and filter stages provide the required unregulated DC input.

Testing Steps

  1. Measure the AC voltage at the transformer secondary, if present.
  2. Check the rectifier output voltage.
  3. Measure the filtered DC voltage across the capacitor.
  4. Inspect capacitor polarity and physical condition.
  5. Measure ripple using an oscilloscope.
  6. Compare the readings with expected design values.
A regulator may fail to maintain its specified output if its input voltage falls below the required operating headroom.

9. Voltage Regulator Output Testing

The output voltage is the most important measurement when checking a regulated power supply.

Testing a 7805 Regulator

The 7805 is a fixed positive linear regulator designed to provide a nominal 5 V output under specified operating conditions.

Basic Connection

  • Connect the input supply to the regulator input terminal.
  • Connect the regulator ground terminal to circuit ground.
  • Connect a suitable load between output and ground.
  • Use the recommended input and output capacitors.
  • Measure the output voltage using a multimeter.

10. Load Testing of a Power Supply

Load testing verifies whether a power supply can maintain its output voltage while supplying different load currents.

Procedure

  1. Measure the no-load output voltage.
  2. Connect a suitable load resistor or electronic load.
  3. Apply a known load current.
  4. Measure the output voltage under load.
  5. Increase the load gradually within the rated limits.
  6. Observe voltage drop and temperature rise.

Load Resistor Calculation

R = V / I

P = V x I

Example: A 5 V supply supplying 100 mA.

R = 5 / 0.1

R = 50 ohms

P = 5 x 0.1

P = 0.5 W

A suitable resistor with a power rating comfortably above 0.5 W should be selected, considering its temperature rise and operating conditions.

11. Line and Load Regulation Testing

Line Regulation

Line regulation describes how much the output voltage changes when the input voltage changes while the load is held constant. 

Line Regulation = ΔVOUT / ΔVIN

Load Regulation

Load regulation describes how much the output voltage changes when the load current changes. 

Load Regulation (%) =
[(VNL - VFL) / VFL] x 100
  • VNL = No-load output voltage.
  • VFL = Full-load output voltage.
Follow the regulator datasheet's specified test conditions when comparing measured line and load regulation with published performance.

12. Ripple and Noise Testing

Ripple and noise are unwanted variations present on a DC power supply output.

Common Causes

  • Insufficient filter capacitance.
  • High capacitor ESR.
  • Poor PCB layout.
  • Inadequate grounding.
  • Switching regulator interference.
  • Excessive load current.

Oscilloscope Testing

  1. Connect the probe using a suitable ground reference.
  2. Use an appropriate probe attenuation setting.
  3. Use AC coupling to inspect small ripple on a DC output.
  4. Adjust volts/div and time/div.
  5. Measure peak-to-peak ripple.
  6. Compare the result with the supply specification.
Observe the oscilloscope manufacturer's grounding instructions. Never attach an earth-referenced probe ground to a hazardous live node.

13. Thermal Testing

Voltage regulators dissipate power during operation. Excessive power dissipation increases junction temperature and may activate thermal shutdown or damage the component.

Linear Regulator Power Dissipation

PD ≈ (VIN - VOUT) x IOUT

Example

Assume:

  • Input voltage = 12 V.
  • Output voltage = 5 V.
  • Output current = 0.2 A.
PD = (12 - 5) x 0.2

PD = 1.4 W

The regulator must safely dissipate this power under the actual ambient temperature, package and heatsink conditions.

Thermal Resistance

TJ = TA + (PD x RθJA)
  • TJ = Junction temperature.
  • TA = Ambient temperature.
  • PD = Power dissipation.
  • RθJA = Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance.
Thermal resistance depends on the package, PCB copper area, airflow, heatsink and mounting conditions. Use the manufacturer's thermal data for design verification.

14. Common Power Supply Faults

Problem Possible Cause Testing Method
No output voltage Input failure, open fuse, damaged rectifier or regulator. Check input, rectifier and regulator stages.
Low output voltage Insufficient input voltage or excessive load. Measure input and output under load.
High output voltage Faulty feedback or regulator circuit. Verify regulator connections and feedback components.
Unstable output Faulty capacitors, poor grounding or oscillation. Inspect capacitors and observe output waveform.
Excessive ripple Degraded filter capacitor or excessive load. Measure ripple and inspect capacitor ESR.
Regulator overheats High voltage drop or excessive load current. Calculate power dissipation and check thermal design.
Output shuts down Thermal shutdown, overcurrent or short circuit. Check load, temperature and protection status.
Fuse repeatedly blows Short circuit or failed power component. Inspect rectifier, capacitors and switching devices.

15. Systematic Power Supply Troubleshooting

A systematic troubleshooting method helps identify faults without unnecessarily replacing components.

  1. Understand the power supply block diagram.
  2. Perform a visual inspection.
  3. Check input voltage and protection components.
  4. Check rectifier output.
  5. Check filter capacitor output and ripple.
  6. Check regulator input and output.
  7. Test the supply under a controlled load.
  8. Measure temperature and power dissipation.
  9. Identify the defective stage.
  10. Repair and repeat the electrical tests.
Always isolate the fault to a particular stage before replacing components. This approach is especially useful in electronics R&D and production testing.

16. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Test a 5 V regulated DC power supply using a 7805 regulator and evaluate its output under different load conditions.

Required Components

  • 7805 voltage regulator.
  • Current-limited DC power supply.
  • Input and output capacitors as recommended by the datasheet.
  • Suitable load resistors.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Breadboard or suitable PCB.

Procedure

  1. Verify the regulator pin configuration.
  2. Construct the regulator circuit according to its datasheet.
  3. Check all connections before applying power.
  4. Apply a suitable DC input voltage.
  5. Measure the output voltage without a load.
  6. Connect a suitable load resistor.
  7. Measure output voltage and load current.
  8. Observe ripple using an oscilloscope.
  9. Monitor regulator temperature.
  10. Compare the results with expected values.

Observation Table

Condition Input Voltage Output Voltage Output Current
No Load Record Record Approximately zero external load current
Light Load Record Record Record
Higher Load Record Record Record

Expected Result

The regulator should maintain its specified output voltage within its operating limits. Any output variation, excessive ripple or abnormal temperature rise should be investigated.

18. Lesson Summary

  • A regulated power supply provides a controlled DC output.
  • Linear and switching supplies use different regulation methods.
  • Visual inspection is an important first troubleshooting step.
  • Input, rectifier, filter and regulator stages should be tested separately.
  • Load testing checks output stability under operating conditions.
  • Line and load regulation describe output voltage changes.
  • An oscilloscope is useful for ripple and transient measurements.
  • Regulator power dissipation determines thermal requirements.
  • Systematic testing helps isolate defective circuit stages.
Placement Tip: Practice explaining how you would troubleshoot a 5 V power supply that produces low output voltage, excessive ripple or regulator overheating. Be prepared to demonstrate measurements with a multimeter and oscilloscope.

19. Electrical Safety Precautions

  • Use current-limited low-voltage DC supplies for laboratory testing.
  • Switch OFF power before modifying circuit connections.
  • Check capacitor polarity and voltage ratings.
  • Use load resistors with adequate power ratings.
  • Do not touch hot regulators or heatsinks during testing.
  • Use correctly rated test probes and instruments.
  • Do not work on exposed mains-powered circuits without appropriate training and supervision.
Mains power supplies may contain dangerous voltages and capacitors that retain charge after disconnection. Follow approved electrical safety procedures.
