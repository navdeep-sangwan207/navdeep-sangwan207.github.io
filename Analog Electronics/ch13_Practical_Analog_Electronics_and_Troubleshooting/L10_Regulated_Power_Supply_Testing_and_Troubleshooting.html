Regulated Power Supply Testing and Troubleshooting

Practical testing of voltage regulators, DC output stability, load regulation, dropout voltage and fault diagnosis.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the purpose and operation of regulated DC power supplies.
  • Identify input, ground and output terminals of common regulators.
  • Test 7805, 7812, 7905, 7912 and LM317 regulator circuits.
  • Measure input voltage, output voltage and dropout voltage.
  • Evaluate line regulation and load regulation.
  • Check regulator temperature, current limiting and thermal shutdown symptoms.
  • Diagnose incorrect output, excessive ripple, overheating and unstable voltage.
  • Use DMM and oscilloscope measurements systematically.

3. Fixed Positive and Negative Voltage Regulators

78XX series

Fixed positive output regulators. Examples: 7805 gives nominal +5 V; 7812 gives nominal +12 V.

79XX series

Fixed negative output regulators. Examples: 7905 gives nominal −5 V; 7912 gives nominal −12 V.

LM317

Adjustable positive linear regulator. Its output is controlled by an external resistor network.

4. Input and Output Voltage Testing

Use a DMM in DC voltage mode to measure the regulator input and output relative to the circuit's designated ground reference.

Testing procedure

  1. Inspect regulator package, heat sink, polarity, solder joints and nearby capacitors.
  2. Confirm the circuit is powered from an isolated, suitable low-voltage DC source.
  3. Set the DMM to DC voltage mode.
  4. Measure regulator input voltage relative to ground.
  5. Measure output voltage relative to ground.
  6. Compare the output against the regulator's nominal output and datasheet tolerance.
  7. Repeat at the specified load current and check for voltage changes.
The input must remain sufficiently above the required output voltage. If the input falls below the regulator's operating headroom, the output may fall out of regulation.

5. Dropout Voltage and Headroom

Dropout voltage is the minimum input-to-output voltage difference needed for a regulator to maintain regulation under specified conditions.

\[ V_{dropout}=V_{in}-V_{out} \]

For a conventional 7805, the required input headroom is typically several volts, but the exact minimum depends on load current, temperature and manufacturer specifications. Do not assume a universal dropout value.

Input too low

The regulator may leave regulation and output voltage can decrease.

Input adequate

The regulator can maintain its specified output within its limits.

Input too high

Power dissipation and junction temperature may become excessive.

6. Load Regulation Testing

Load regulation describes how much the output voltage changes when the load current changes while the input supply is held constant.

\[ \%\,Load\ Regulation= \frac{V_{NL}-V_{FL}}{V_{FL}}\times100 \]

Where \(V_{NL}\) is no-load output voltage and \(V_{FL}\) is full-load output voltage. Follow the convention specified in the relevant datasheet or test procedure.

Practical test

  1. Set up the regulator with a suitable isolated DC input.
  2. Measure output voltage with no load or the specified minimum load.
  3. Connect a suitable electronic load or power resistor.
  4. Increase the load gradually without exceeding the regulator's rated current or thermal limits.
  5. Record output voltage at each load point.
  6. Calculate the change in output voltage.

8. LM317 Adjustable Regulator Testing

The LM317 output voltage is determined by the reference voltage and external resistor network.

\[ V_{out}\approx1.25\left(1+\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)+I_{ADJ}R_2 \]

For a first-order design calculation, the adjustment-current term is often neglected when it is small relative to the required accuracy.

9. Line Regulation

Line regulation describes output voltage change caused by input voltage variation while the load remains constant.

\[ Line\ Regulation=\frac{\Delta V_{out}}{\Delta V_{in}} \]

It may be expressed in mV/V or as a percentage, depending on the datasheet.

Test procedure

  1. Use a safe adjustable isolated DC source within the regulator's input rating.
  2. Keep the load current constant.
  3. Measure output voltage at multiple input voltages.
  4. Calculate output change divided by input change.
  5. Compare with the regulator datasheet specification.

10. Regulator Power Dissipation and Thermal Testing

Linear regulators dissipate power when reducing input voltage to a lower output voltage.

\[ P_D\approx(V_{in}-V_{out})I_{load} \]

This simplified estimate neglects regulator ground or quiescent current. Include those terms where relevant for accurate calculations.

Approximate junction temperature:

\[ T_J\approx T_A+P_D\theta_{JA} \]

For a regulator with a separate heat sink, use the appropriate thermal resistance path from junction to case, case to heat sink and heat sink to ambient.

A regulator can overheat even when the load current is below its headline current rating. Thermal limits, safe operating area, package, heat sink and input-output voltage difference all matter.

13. Practical Testing Workflow

  1. Visual inspection: Look for burnt components, damaged capacitors, cracked solder joints and incorrect polarity.
  2. Unpowered checks: Verify continuity, resistance, diode orientation and possible shorts after safely discharging capacitors.
  3. Input check: Measure regulator input voltage relative to the correct reference.
  4. Output check: Measure output voltage and polarity.
  5. Ripple check: Use an oscilloscope to inspect input and output ripple.
  6. Load check: Increase load gradually within rated current and thermal limits.
  7. Thermal check: Observe regulator temperature and verify heat-sink mounting.
  8. Protection check: Investigate current limiting or thermal shutdown only with a controlled, current-limited test setup.
  9. Documentation: Record readings and compare them with datasheet limits.

16. Practical Lab Record

Objective: Test a fixed voltage regulator and determine output stability under different load conditions.

Equipment

  • 7805 or another suitable fixed regulator.
  • Isolated, current-limited low-voltage DC input source.
  • Suitable input and output capacitors according to the datasheet.
  • Load resistors or an electronic load.
  • Digital multimeter and oscilloscope.
  • Heat sink if required by the power dissipation.

Procedure

  1. Verify the regulator pinout from its datasheet.
  2. Assemble the circuit with correct polarity and recommended capacitors.
  3. Set the current limit on the DC supply before energizing the circuit.
  4. Measure input and output voltages without the external load.
  5. Apply a suitable load and record the output voltage.
  6. Increase load current in controlled steps, staying within safe limits.
  7. Observe output ripple and temperature.
  8. Calculate power dissipation and load regulation.

17. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is the purpose of a voltage regulator?
It maintains a relatively constant output voltage despite specified variations in input voltage and load current.
2. What is the difference between 78XX and 79XX regulators?
78XX devices provide fixed positive outputs; 79XX devices provide fixed negative outputs.
3. What is dropout voltage?
It is the minimum input-to-output voltage difference needed to maintain regulation under specified operating conditions.
4. Why does a linear regulator become hot?
It dissipates power approximately equal to the input-output voltage difference multiplied by load current.
5. What is load regulation?
It describes the change in output voltage as load current changes while input voltage remains constant.
6. What is line regulation?
It describes the change in output voltage due to a change in input voltage at a fixed load.
7. What is the function of thermal shutdown?
It protects a regulator by reducing or stopping operation when junction temperature becomes excessive.
8. Why are capacitors connected around a regulator?
They help meet stability, transient-response and noise requirements. Their values and placement should follow the regulator datasheet.
9. How do you test an LM317 output?
Verify the resistor network and pinout, apply a suitable input, and measure output voltage relative to the correct reference.
10. What happens if input voltage is too low?
The regulator may enter dropout and fail to maintain its specified output voltage.

18. Numerical and Design Practice

  1. A 7805 regulator receives 12 V and supplies 0.2 A. Estimate its power dissipation, neglecting quiescent current.
  2. A regulator has 10 V no-load output and 9.9 V full-load output. Calculate load regulation using the full-load output as denominator.
  3. An LM317 uses R1 = 240 Ω and R2 = 720 Ω. Estimate output voltage, neglecting adjustment current.
  4. A regulator input is 7 V and output is 5 V. If its assumed headroom requirement is 2 V, discuss whether it has adequate margin.
  5. A regulator dissipates 2 W and has an effective thermal resistance of 35 °C/W. At 30 °C ambient, estimate junction temperature using the simplified thermal model.
  6. Explain why an output voltage may fall when the load current increases.
  7. Describe a safe procedure for testing current limiting and thermal shutdown.

19. Key Takeaways

  • Always verify regulator pinout and ratings from the exact datasheet.
  • Measure input and output voltages relative to the correct reference point.
  • Dropout voltage determines the minimum input headroom needed for regulation.
  • Load regulation and line regulation describe different operating characteristics.
  • Linear regulator power dissipation increases with voltage drop and load current.
  • Thermal design, recommended capacitors and protection limits are essential for reliable operation.
  • Diagnose faults through measurements instead of replacing components without evidence.
