Lesson 2: Electronic Components Identification and Digital Multimeter Basics

Component Recognition, Measurement Techniques and Practical Testing

1. Introduction

Electronic circuits consist of different components such as resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors, inductors and integrated circuits. Before testing or repairing a circuit, an engineer must be able to identify these components and understand their basic functions.

A Digital Multimeter (DMM) is one of the most commonly used instruments in electronics laboratories, hardware testing, maintenance and troubleshooting.

Learning Objective: Identify common electronic components, understand their markings and use a digital multimeter to measure voltage, current, resistance and continuity safely.

2. Classification of Electronic Components

Electronic components can broadly be classified into passive and active components.

2.1 Passive Components

Passive components do not provide power gain. They store, dissipate or transfer electrical energy.

Component Symbol / Unit Function
Resistor R / Ohm (Ω) Limits current and divides voltage.
Capacitor C / Farad (F) Stores electrical energy in an electric field.
Inductor L / Henry (H) Stores energy in a magnetic field.
Transformer T / VA Transfers AC energy through electromagnetic induction.

2.2 Active Components

Active components control electrical signals and may provide amplification or switching functions.

Component Type Function
Diode Semiconductor Allows current primarily in one direction.
BJT Transistor Amplification and switching.
MOSFET Transistor Voltage-controlled switching and amplification.
LED Optoelectronic Converts electrical energy into light.
IC Integrated Circuit Performs specific electronic functions.
SCR Thyristor Controlled rectification and power switching.

3. Identification of Common Electronic Components

3.1 Resistor

A resistor is a passive component that opposes the flow of electric current. Its resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).

Resistor identification methods:

  • Colour bands.
  • Printed numerical values.
  • Component datasheet.
  • Resistance measurement using a multimeter.

3.2 Capacitor

A capacitor stores electrical energy in an electric field. Capacitors are used in filtering, coupling, decoupling, timing and energy storage circuits.

Capacitor Type Identification
Ceramic Usually small disc or rectangular package.
Electrolytic Usually cylindrical and polarized.
Film Often rectangular plastic package.
Tantalum Compact polarized package.

3.3 Diode

A diode is a semiconductor device that conducts primarily in one direction. The cathode is commonly identified by a band on the diode body.

3.4 Transistor

Transistors are used as electronic switches and amplifiers. Common types include BJT and MOSFET.

3.5 Integrated Circuit

An Integrated Circuit (IC) contains multiple electronic components fabricated on a semiconductor chip. Pin numbers, orientation and functions must be verified using the component datasheet.

6. Introduction to Digital Multimeter (DMM)

A Digital Multimeter is an electronic measuring instrument used to measure different electrical quantities.

Common DMM Measurements

  • AC voltage (VAC).
  • DC voltage (VDC).
  • AC current (AAC), if supported.
  • DC current (ADC).
  • Resistance (Ω).
  • Continuity.
  • Diode forward voltage.
  • Capacitance, on supported models.
  • Frequency, on supported models.

The illustration shows a simplified multimeter front panel. Actual dial positions, terminal labels and available functions vary by model.

7. Digital Multimeter Terminals

A typical digital multimeter has the following input terminals.

Terminal Purpose
COM Common terminal, usually connected to the black probe.
VΩ Voltage, resistance, continuity and diode measurements.
mA / µA Low-current measurements, if provided.
10 A / 20 A High-current measurements within the meter's rated limit.
Safety: Never leave the red probe in a current input terminal when measuring voltage. Doing so can create a short circuit if the probes are connected across a voltage source.

8. Measuring DC Voltage

DC voltage measurement is used to check batteries, regulated power supplies, electronic circuits and DC power rails.

Procedure

  1. Connect the black probe to COM.
  2. Connect the red probe to the VΩ terminal.
  3. Select DC voltage mode (V⎓).
  4. Select a suitable range if the meter is not auto-ranging.
  5. Connect the probes in parallel across the measurement points.
  6. Read the displayed voltage.

Example:

A regulated power supply is expected to provide 12 V DC. If the multimeter displays 11.98 V, the measured output is approximately 12 V.

9. Measuring AC Voltage

AC voltage measurement is used to check mains supplies, transformer secondary voltage and AC power sources.

Procedure

  1. Connect the black probe to COM.
  2. Connect the red probe to the VΩ terminal.
  3. Select AC voltage mode (V~).
  4. Select an appropriate voltage range.
  5. Connect the probes across the AC source using properly rated equipment and safe procedures.
  6. Read the displayed RMS voltage.
Electrical Safety: Mains voltage can cause fatal electric shock. Only trained persons should perform mains measurements using a correctly CAT-rated multimeter, probes and appropriate protective procedures. Never touch exposed conductors.

10. Measuring Resistance

Resistance measurement is used to check resistors, wiring, connections and some component faults.

Procedure

  1. Disconnect power from the circuit.
  2. Discharge capacitors safely.
  3. Connect the black probe to COM.
  4. Connect the red probe to VΩ.
  5. Select resistance mode (Ω).
  6. Place the probes across the resistor.
  7. Read the displayed resistance.
Important: In-circuit resistance readings may be affected by parallel paths and connected components. For accurate resistor testing, isolate at least one terminal when necessary.

11. Continuity Testing

Continuity testing checks whether an electrical path exists between two points.

Many multimeters produce an audible beep when resistance falls below a model-specific threshold.

Applications

  • Checking PCB tracks.
  • Testing wires and cables.
  • Checking switches.
  • Identifying open connections.
  • Testing fuses after removing or isolating them.

Procedure

  1. Switch OFF the circuit.
  2. Discharge capacitors.
  3. Set the multimeter to continuity mode.
  4. Touch the probes together to check the meter response.
  5. Place the probes across the points being tested.
  6. Interpret the displayed resistance and beep indication.
A beep indicates low resistance according to the meter's continuity threshold. It does not automatically prove that a connection can carry the required operating current.

12. Diode Testing Mode

Diode mode applies a small test current and displays the forward voltage drop of a semiconductor junction.

Procedure

  1. Switch OFF the circuit.
  2. Connect black probe to COM.
  3. Connect red probe to VΩ.
  4. Select diode mode.
  5. Connect red probe to anode and black probe to cathode.
  6. Record the forward voltage reading.
  7. Reverse the probes and record the result.
Reading Possible Interpretation
Forward voltage displayed Forward conduction is present.
OL in reverse direction Reverse blocking is observed.
Near zero in both directions Possible shorted diode.
OL in both directions Possible open diode or unsuitable test conditions.

Typical silicon diode forward voltage may be around 0.5 V to 0.8 V, depending on current, temperature and device characteristics.

13. Measuring DC Current

Current measurement determines the amount of current flowing through a circuit branch.

Procedure

  1. Switch OFF the circuit before changing probe connections.
  2. Connect the black probe to COM.
  3. Move the red probe to the appropriate current terminal.
  4. Select DC current mode.
  5. Break the circuit at the measurement point.
  6. Connect the meter in series with the load.
  7. Power the circuit and observe the reading.
  8. Switch OFF the circuit before removing the meter.
  9. Return the red probe to the VΩ terminal.
Critical: A current meter must be connected in series, not directly across a voltage source. Connecting it in parallel can cause a short circuit and damage the meter, probes or circuit.

15. Common Multimeter Errors

  • Using the wrong measurement mode.
  • Connecting the current input across a voltage source.
  • Measuring resistance in a powered circuit.
  • Using an incorrect current input terminal.
  • Exceeding the meter's voltage or current rating.
  • Ignoring probe insulation damage.
  • Using the wrong AC/DC setting.
  • Forgetting to return the red probe to the voltage terminal.
  • Assuming continuity proves a connection is suitable for high current.

16. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Identify common electronic components and measure their electrical properties using a digital multimeter.

Required Components and Equipment

  • Digital multimeter.
  • 1 kΩ resistor.
  • 10 kΩ resistor.
  • 100 nF ceramic capacitor.
  • Electrolytic capacitor.
  • 1N4007 diode.
  • LED.
  • BC547 transistor.
  • Connecting wires and breadboard.
  • Suitable low-voltage DC supply.

Experiment A: Resistor Measurement

  1. Identify the resistor using its colour bands.
  2. Set the multimeter to resistance mode.
  3. Measure the 1 kΩ resistor.
  4. Repeat for the 10 kΩ resistor.
  5. Compare measured values with nominal values and tolerance.

Experiment B: Continuity Testing

  1. Select continuity mode.
  2. Touch the two probes together.
  3. Observe the beep and resistance reading.
  4. Test a connecting wire.
  5. Test an open connection and compare the results.

Experiment C: Diode Testing

  1. Select diode mode.
  2. Connect the probes in forward bias.
  3. Record the forward voltage.
  4. Reverse the probes.
  5. Record the reverse reading.

18. Lesson Summary

  • Electronic components are classified as active and passive.
  • Resistor colour bands indicate resistance and tolerance.
  • Capacitor markings indicate capacitance values.
  • A DMM measures voltage, current and resistance.
  • Continuity mode checks for low-resistance electrical paths.
  • Diode mode checks semiconductor junction behaviour.
  • Voltage measurement is performed in parallel.
  • Current measurement is performed in series.
  • Resistance and continuity tests require a de-energized circuit.
