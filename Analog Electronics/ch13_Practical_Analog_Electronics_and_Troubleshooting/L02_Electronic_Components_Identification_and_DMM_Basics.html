2. Classification of Electronic Components

Electronic components can broadly be classified into passive and active components.

Passive Components

Components that do not provide power gain.

  • Resistors
  • Capacitors
  • Inductors
  • Transformers

Active Components

Components used for amplification, switching or controlled signal operation.

  • Diodes
  • BJTs
  • MOSFETs
  • ICs and op-amps

5. Inductor Identification

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through it.

V = L × (di/dt)
  • Inductance is measured in henry (H).
  • Common units are µH, mH and H.
  • Inductors are used in filters, DC-DC converters, oscillators and power supplies.
  • Inductor current cannot change instantaneously in an ideal inductor.

6. Semiconductor Component Identification

Diode

Look for the cathode band on many axial diodes. Confirm polarity using the datasheet and diode-test mode.

BJT

Identify NPN or PNP type and confirm emitter, base and collector using the datasheet.

MOSFET

Identify gate, drain and source. Check body-diode direction and package pinout.

Integrated Circuit

Locate the pin-1 indicator and verify the package orientation from its datasheet.

Never identify a transistor or MOSFET pinout solely from its package appearance. Manufacturer and part-number variations are common.

9. Voltage Measurement

Voltage is measured by connecting the meter in parallel with the component or circuit under test.

Procedure

  1. Insert the black probe into COM.
  2. Insert the red probe into V/Ω.
  3. Select the appropriate DC or AC voltage mode.
  4. Connect the probes in parallel with the measurement points.
  5. Read the displayed voltage and record the polarity where relevant.

10. Current Measurement

Current is measured by connecting the meter in series with the load.

Procedure

  1. Switch off the circuit before opening the current path.
  2. Move the red probe to the correct current input terminal.
  3. Select the correct current mode and range.
  4. Open the circuit and insert the meter in series.
  5. Power the circuit and record the measurement.
  6. Switch off power before removing the meter and restore the red probe to the voltage terminal.
Never connect a current-mode meter directly across a power supply. Check the maximum current, fuse rating, measurement duration and duty-cycle limits of the meter.

11. Resistance Measurement

Resistance measurement uses the meter's internal test source. The circuit must be de-energized.

R = V/I
  1. Switch off the circuit.
  2. Discharge capacitors safely.
  3. Disconnect one end of the resistor if parallel paths affect the reading.
  4. Connect probes across the resistor.
  5. Select Ω mode and record the reading.
Measuring resistance in a powered circuit can produce incorrect results and may damage the meter.

12. Continuity Test

Continuity mode checks whether an electrical path exists between two points. A buzzer usually indicates resistance below a meter-specific threshold.

Continuity mode is useful for:

  • Checking broken wires.
  • Checking PCB track continuity.
  • Checking fuse continuity.
  • Finding open connections and poor solder joints.
A continuity beep does not prove that a connection can safely carry the required operating current. Contact resistance and current capacity must also be assessed.

13. Diode-Test Mode

Diode mode applies a small test current and displays the forward voltage drop when a semiconductor junction conducts.

In reverse direction, a healthy ordinary diode typically displays OL, subject to meter test voltage and device characteristics.

15. DMM Range and Resolution

Resolution is the smallest change in a measured quantity that the meter can display on a selected range.

Example: A meter displaying 12.34 V has a displayed resolution of 0.01 V on that range.

  • Auto-ranging meters select a range automatically.
  • Manual-ranging meters require the user to select a suitable range.
  • Over-range indications may appear as OL or a similar display.
  • Accuracy is different from resolution.
A display with more digits does not automatically mean the meter is more accurate. Check accuracy specifications, calibration status and measurement conditions.

16. Measurement Safety

  • Inspect test leads and probes for damaged insulation.
  • Check the meter's maximum voltage and current ratings.
  • Check the measurement category (CAT rating) for the environment.
  • Use the correct input terminal and selector mode.
  • Never measure resistance on an energized circuit.
  • Never connect an ammeter in parallel with a supply.
  • Discharge capacitors using an appropriate method before testing.
  • Keep fingers behind probe guards.
  • Use appropriate PPE and isolation procedures for hazardous circuits.
  • For mains work, follow qualified-person procedures and the relevant safety standards.
High-voltage warning: Do not use this introductory exercise to work on exposed mains circuits or charged high-voltage capacitors. Such work requires suitable rated equipment, training, isolation and safe procedures.

17. Practical Lab Exercise

Objective

Identify common components and measure their electrical properties using a digital multimeter.

Required Components and Equipment

  • Resistors: 220 Ω, 1 kΩ, 4.7 kΩ and 10 kΩ.
  • Ceramic and electrolytic capacitors.
  • One silicon diode and one LED.
  • Digital multimeter with suitable test leads.
  • Low-voltage DC battery or isolated laboratory supply.
  • Breadboard and connecting wires.

Procedure

  1. Identify each component and record its marking.
  2. Calculate the expected resistor value from its colour bands.
  3. Measure each resistor using the DMM's resistance mode.
  4. Compare the measured value with its nominal value and tolerance.
  5. Identify capacitor type, value and polarity where applicable.
  6. Use continuity mode to test a wire and a fuse.
  7. Use diode mode to test a diode in forward and reverse directions.
  8. Measure a low-voltage DC source using DC voltage mode.
  9. Record the readings and note any discrepancies.

19. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is a digital multimeter?

A DMM is an instrument used to measure voltage, current, resistance and other electrical quantities.

Q2. Why is a voltmeter connected in parallel?

It measures the potential difference between two points and has high input resistance to minimize circuit loading.

Q3. Why is an ammeter connected in series?

The current through the load must also flow through the meter. An ammeter has low internal resistance.

Q4. What is continuity testing?

It checks whether an electrical path exists between two points, usually indicating a low-resistance path with a buzzer.

Q5. What is the purpose of diode mode?

It applies a small test current to measure the forward voltage drop of a semiconductor junction.

Q6. What does OL indicate?

It commonly indicates an over-range or open-circuit condition, depending on the selected function and meter model.

Q7. What is the difference between accuracy and resolution?

Accuracy describes closeness to the true value. Resolution describes the smallest displayed increment.

Q8. Why must the power be switched off before measuring resistance?

The meter uses its own test signal. External voltage can corrupt the measurement or damage the meter.

Q9. What happens if an ammeter is connected directly across a battery?

Its low internal resistance can create a short circuit, causing high current and possible fuse failure or damage.

Q10. What does CAT III or CAT IV indicate?

These measurement categories classify the electrical environments and transient levels for which equipment is designed, subject to its stated voltage rating.

21. Lecture Summary

  • Electronic components are classified as passive and active components.
  • Resistors can be identified using colour bands or SMD codes.
  • Capacitors and inductors are identified by their markings and physical construction.
  • A DMM measures voltage, current, resistance and other electrical quantities.
  • Voltage is measured in parallel; current is measured in series.
  • Resistance and continuity measurements require a de-energized circuit.
  • Correct probe terminals, measurement mode and safety ratings are essential.
Placement skill: You should now be able to identify common electronic components and perform basic DMM measurements using the correct connection method.
