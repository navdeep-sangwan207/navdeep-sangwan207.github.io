Rectifier and Filter Circuit Testing

Practical measurement, waveform analysis, ripple calculation and troubleshooting of AC-to-DC power supply circuits.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Identify transformer, rectifier diode and filter capacitor terminals.
  • Test half-wave, full-wave centre-tapped and bridge rectifiers.
  • Measure AC and DC voltages using a digital multimeter.
  • Observe rectifier and filtered waveforms using an oscilloscope.
  • Calculate average DC output and ripple frequency.
  • Estimate capacitor-filter ripple voltage.
  • Identify open, shorted and leaky rectifier diodes and capacitors.
  • Perform systematic power-supply troubleshooting.

2. Basic AC-to-DC Power Supply

A conventional linear DC supply contains a transformer, rectifier, filter and voltage regulator. Each stage performs a separate function.

Testing should progress from the AC input to the transformer secondary, rectifier output, filter output and regulator output.

3. Safety Before Testing

Important electrical safety: Mains-connected power supplies can cause fatal electric shock. This practical lesson uses isolated, low-voltage transformer secondary circuits. Do not probe exposed mains terminals or live primary-side circuits. Mains testing and repair should be performed only by qualified personnel using appropriate isolation and safety procedures.
  • Switch OFF the supply before resistance, continuity or diode testing.
  • Discharge filter capacitors through a suitable resistor and verify the voltage is safe before touching the circuit.
  • Never discharge a large capacitor by directly shorting its terminals.
  • Check DMM lead positions and select AC voltage, DC voltage, resistance or diode mode correctly.
  • Use an oscilloscope probe with an appropriate voltage rating and grounding arrangement.
  • Never connect an earth-referenced oscilloscope ground clip to a point that is not safe to earth.

4. Transformer Secondary Testing

The transformer provides isolation and changes the AC voltage to the required secondary value in a conventional linear supply.

Testing procedure

  1. Inspect the transformer for damaged insulation, overheating, loose terminals or burnt smell.
  2. With the secondary safely energized from an isolated transformer, select AC voltage mode on the DMM.
  3. Measure the secondary voltage across the correct winding terminals.
  4. For a centre-tapped winding, measure each half-winding relative to the centre tap and measure end-to-end voltage.
  5. Compare the readings with the transformer rating under the specified load conditions.
Centre-tapped secondary

For a 12-0-12 V transformer, each half is nominally 12 V RMS and end-to-end voltage is 24 V RMS.

Bridge rectifier secondary

A bridge rectifier normally uses a two-terminal AC secondary winding.

An unloaded transformer may show a secondary voltage higher than its rated full-load voltage. Consider regulation and loading when evaluating the result.

5. Half-Wave Rectifier Testing

A half-wave rectifier uses one diode to conduct during one half-cycle of the AC input. It blocks the opposite half-cycle.

Ideal half-wave rectifier relations

\[ V_{DC}=\frac{V_m}{\pi} \] \[ V_{RMS}=\frac{V_m}{2} \]

For a practical silicon diode, the forward voltage drop reduces the output. Use measured waveforms or a suitable circuit model for accurate results.

Testing

  1. Check the diode using DMM diode mode with power OFF.
  2. Apply a safe, isolated low-voltage AC input.
  3. Measure the input AC voltage.
  4. Measure the rectified output using DC mode and observe its waveform on an oscilloscope.
  5. Check whether the output contains pulses at the input frequency.

6. Full-Wave Centre-Tapped Rectifier Testing

A centre-tapped full-wave rectifier uses two diodes. Each diode conducts during alternate half-cycles, producing output pulses for both half-cycles.

For an ideal full-wave rectifier:

\[ V_{DC}=\frac{2V_m}{\pi} \]

Here \(V_m\) is the peak voltage of one half of the centre-tapped secondary, measured relative to the centre tap.

The output ripple frequency is twice the AC supply frequency. For 50 Hz input, the ideal ripple frequency is 100 Hz.

7. Bridge Rectifier Testing

A bridge rectifier uses four diodes. Two diodes conduct during each half-cycle, maintaining the same output polarity across the load.

For an ideal bridge rectifier:

\[ V_{DC}=\frac{2V_m}{\pi} \]

In a practical bridge, two diode forward drops are present in the conducting path during each half-cycle.

Testing procedure

  1. Switch OFF the supply and discharge the filter capacitor safely.
  2. Identify the AC terminals, positive output and negative output.
  3. Use diode mode to test individual diodes if accessible.
  4. Apply an isolated low-voltage AC secondary input.
  5. Measure DC output polarity and voltage.
  6. Observe the waveform and confirm two rectified pulses per input cycle.

9. Capacitor Filter Testing

A capacitor connected across the load charges near the rectified waveform peak and discharges through the load between peaks. This reduces output ripple.

For a full-wave rectifier with a capacitor filter, a useful approximation is:

\[ V_{r(pp)}\approx\frac{I_{load}}{f_r C} \]

Where:

  • \(I_{load}\) is load current in amperes.
  • \(f_r\) is ripple frequency in hertz.
  • \(C\) is capacitance in farads.

For a 50 Hz input and full-wave rectification, \(f_r=100\) Hz.

This is an approximate small-ripple relation for a capacitor-input filter. Transformer resistance, diode drops, capacitor ESR, load variation and conduction angle affect the real ripple waveform.

12. Oscilloscope Testing and Ripple Measurement

Procedure

  1. Use a safe, isolated low-voltage supply.
  2. Verify that the oscilloscope ground connection is safe for the circuit.
  3. Connect the probe ground to the circuit's designated reference point.
  4. Set the probe attenuation correctly, such as 10× when using a 10× probe.
  5. Observe the rectifier output with DC coupling first.
  6. Use AC coupling or an appropriate vertical offset and scale to examine small ripple.
  7. Measure peak-to-peak ripple using cursors or the oscilloscope's automatic measurement function.
  8. Record ripple under no-load and specified-load conditions.

Peak-to-peak ripple:

\[ V_{r(pp)}=V_{max}-V_{min} \]

Ripple factor is the ratio of RMS ripple voltage to DC output voltage:

\[ r=\frac{V_{r(rms)}}{V_{DC}} \]

For small triangular ripple, a rough estimate is:

\[ V_{r(rms)}\approx\frac{V_{r(pp)}}{2\sqrt{3}} \]

13. Worked Practical Numerical

A full-wave rectifier supplies a 100 mA load. The filter capacitor is 1000 µF and the AC frequency is 50 Hz.

Ripple frequency:

\[ f_r=2f=100Hz \]

Approximate peak-to-peak ripple:

\[ V_{r(pp)}=\frac{0.1}{100\times0.001}=1V \]

If the capacitance is increased to 2200 µF:

\[ V_{r(pp)}=\frac{0.1}{100\times0.0022}\approx0.455V \]
Increasing capacitance reduces ripple for the same load current and ripple frequency. However, verify capacitor voltage rating, ripple-current rating, ESR, inrush current and regulator requirements.

16. Practical Lab Record

Objective: Verify rectifier operation and measure capacitor-filter ripple under load.

Equipment

  • Isolated low-voltage AC transformer.
  • Rectifier diodes or a bridge rectifier module.
  • Electrolytic filter capacitor with suitable voltage rating.
  • Resistive load and suitable connecting wires.
  • Digital multimeter and oscilloscope.
  • Breadboard or appropriate prototyping board.

Procedure

  1. Calculate expected rectifier output and ripple frequency.
  2. Inspect component polarity and ratings before powering the circuit.
  3. Measure transformer secondary voltage.
  4. Observe the unfiltered rectifier output waveform.
  5. Connect the filter capacitor with correct polarity and repeat the measurement.
  6. Connect the specified load and measure output voltage and ripple.
  7. Compare measured ripple with the approximate theoretical value.
  8. Record any difference and investigate transformer regulation, diode drop, ESR and load variation.

17. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is the main function of a rectifier?
It converts alternating current into unidirectional, pulsating DC.
2. What is the difference between half-wave and full-wave rectification?
Half-wave uses one half-cycle; full-wave uses both half-cycles.
3. What is the ripple frequency of a full-wave rectifier supplied at 50 Hz?
100 Hz.
4. How many diodes conduct in a bridge rectifier at a time?
Two diodes conduct during each half-cycle.
5. What is the function of a filter capacitor?
It stores charge near waveform peaks and supplies the load between peaks to reduce ripple.
6. Why does ripple increase when load current increases?
The capacitor discharges more rapidly between charging peaks, increasing the voltage drop.
7. How do you test a rectifier diode?
With power OFF, isolate the diode as necessary and use DMM diode mode to check forward conduction and reverse blocking.
8. What does high ESR in a filter capacitor cause?
It can increase ripple and internal heating, degrading supply performance.
9. Why is an oscilloscope preferred for ripple measurement?
It displays ripple shape and peak-to-peak amplitude directly, while DMM AC measurements may be inaccurate for small ripple on DC.
10. Why must an electrolytic capacitor be connected with correct polarity?
Reverse voltage can cause excessive leakage, heating, venting or failure.

18. Numerical and Design Practice

  1. A half-wave rectifier has a peak input voltage of 20 V. Calculate its ideal average DC output.
  2. A full-wave rectifier operates at 50 Hz. Calculate its ripple frequency.
  3. A bridge rectifier has a 15 V RMS secondary. Estimate its peak output before considering load effects and diode drops.
  4. A full-wave capacitor filter supplies 200 mA at 100 Hz with a 2200 µF capacitor. Estimate peak-to-peak ripple.
  5. Calculate the capacitance required for approximately 0.5 V peak-to-peak ripple at 150 mA and 100 Hz.
  6. Explain how an open diode and a shorted diode affect a bridge rectifier.
  7. Describe a safe procedure for measuring output ripple using an oscilloscope.

19. Key Takeaways

  • Test the power supply stage by stage, beginning with the isolated transformer secondary.
  • Half-wave rectification produces one pulse per input cycle; full-wave rectification produces two.
  • A full-wave rectifier supplied at 50 Hz has a 100 Hz ripple frequency.
  • The filter capacitor reduces ripple by supplying the load between rectified peaks.
  • Ripple increases with load current and decreases with capacitance and ripple frequency.
  • Use DMM diode mode for unpowered diode testing and an oscilloscope for detailed waveform analysis.
  • Always discharge capacitors safely and observe polarity and voltage ratings.
