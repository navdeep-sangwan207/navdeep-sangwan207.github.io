Lesson 5: MOSFET Testing Using a Multimeter

Terminal Identification, Body Diode Testing and Fault Diagnosis

1. Introduction

A Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor (MOSFET) is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device widely used in electronic switching and amplification.

MOSFETs are commonly found in power supplies, motor drivers, battery management systems, DC-DC converters, inverters, embedded hardware and switching circuits.

A digital multimeter can help identify MOSFET terminals, check the body diode, detect gate insulation faults and perform basic switching tests on suitable devices.

Learning Objective: Understand MOSFET terminals and operation, distinguish N-channel and P-channel devices, and perform basic MOSFET testing using a digital multimeter.

2. MOSFET Terminals

A MOSFET generally has three main terminals:

  • Gate (G): Controls the conduction of the MOSFET.
  • Drain (D): Terminal through which conventional current enters an N-channel MOSFET in its usual ON state.
  • Source (S): Terminal through which conventional current leaves an N-channel MOSFET in its usual ON state.

Many power MOSFETs also have a body connection internally connected to the source. This creates an intrinsic body diode between drain and source.

This is a simplified terminal illustration, not a manufacturer-specific pinout. Always check the exact MOSFET datasheet before connecting it.

3. Types of MOSFET

MOSFETs are classified according to channel type and operating mode.

3.1 N-Channel MOSFET

An N-channel MOSFET uses an N-type channel. In a typical enhancement-mode device, a sufficiently positive gate-to-source voltage turns the device ON.

Examples include IRFZ44N, IRLZ44N and IRF540N.

3.2 P-Channel MOSFET

A P-channel MOSFET uses a P-type channel. In a typical enhancement-mode device, a sufficiently negative gate-to-source voltage turns the device ON.

P-channel MOSFETs are commonly used in high-side switching applications.

Channel N-type P-type
Typical enhancement-mode ON condition Positive VGS Negative VGS
Typical switching position Low-side switching High-side switching
Common examples IRFZ44N, IRF540N IRF9540N

4. MOSFET Operating Modes

4.1 Enhancement Mode

An enhancement-mode MOSFET is normally OFF when gate-to-source voltage is approximately zero. Applying the appropriate gate voltage creates a conducting channel.

4.2 Depletion Mode

A depletion-mode MOSFET has a conducting channel at zero gate-to-source voltage. Applying an appropriate gate voltage reduces the channel conduction.

Most commonly encountered power MOSFETs, such as IRFZ44N and IRF540N, are enhancement-mode devices.

5. MOSFET Switching Principle

The MOSFET is controlled by the voltage between its gate and source terminals.

This voltage is called gate-to-source voltage (VGS). 

VGS = VG - VS
  • VG = Gate voltage.
  • VS = Source voltage.

Threshold Voltage (VGS(th))

Threshold voltage is the gate-to-source voltage at which a specified small drain current begins to flow under datasheet test conditions.

VGS(th) does not mean that the MOSFET is fully ON. A MOSFET must be driven with an appropriate gate voltage to achieve the required low RDS(on).

6. MOSFET Body Diode

Power MOSFETs commonly contain an intrinsic body diode. It is formed by the semiconductor structure of the device.

For a typical N-channel power MOSFET, the body diode has its anode at the source and cathode at the drain.

For a typical P-channel power MOSFET, the body diode has its anode at the drain and cathode at the source.

The body diode is useful for identifying the expected one-direction conduction path during basic diode-mode testing of many power MOSFETs.

7. MOSFET Testing Preparation

Before testing a MOSFET:

  1. Switch OFF the power supply.
  2. Disconnect the MOSFET from the powered circuit.
  3. Discharge capacitors safely.
  4. Identify the exact MOSFET part number.
  5. Check the datasheet for terminal identification.
  6. Set the DMM to diode mode or resistance mode as required.
MOSFET gates are sensitive to electrostatic discharge. Avoid touching gate pins unnecessarily. Use appropriate ESD precautions and do not apply voltage beyond the datasheet gate-source rating.

9. Testing an N-Channel MOSFET Using a Multimeter

The following basic procedure is suitable for many enhancement-mode N-channel power MOSFETs.

Step 1: Check Gate-to-Source Insulation

  1. Keep the MOSFET disconnected from the circuit.
  2. Discharge the gate by briefly connecting gate and source.
  3. Set the DMM to resistance mode.
  4. Measure between gate and source in both probe directions.

A healthy insulated gate should normally show very high resistance or OL on a suitable meter.

Step 2: Check Gate-to-Drain Insulation

  1. Measure resistance between gate and drain.
  2. Reverse the probe connections.
  3. Observe whether the gate is electrically isolated.

Step 3: Test the Body Diode

  1. Set the DMM to diode mode.
  2. Connect the red probe to source.
  3. Connect the black probe to drain.
  4. Observe the reading.
  5. Reverse the probes and measure again.

For a typical N-channel power MOSFET, the body diode conducts when the red probe is on source and the black probe is on drain.

Test Red Probe Black Probe Typical Observation
Body diode forward Source Drain Forward diode voltage
Body diode reverse Drain Source OL
Gate-source insulation Gate Source Very high resistance
Gate-drain insulation Gate Drain Very high resistance

10. Basic N-Channel MOSFET Switching Test

A suitable enhancement-mode N-channel MOSFET can be checked for basic switching operation using a controlled test arrangement.

Procedure

  1. Keep the MOSFET disconnected from other circuits.
  2. Identify gate, drain and source correctly.
  3. Discharge the gate by connecting gate to source.
  4. Check the drain-source path using diode mode.
  5. Briefly apply an appropriate positive gate-to-source voltage within the device ratings, using a current-limited test arrangement.
  6. Remove the gate drive and discharge the gate again.
  7. Repeat the drain-source measurement.

In many enhancement-mode N-channel MOSFETs, applying a suitable positive gate voltage creates a channel and allows conduction between drain and source.

A DMM-only switching test is not definitive. Meter test voltages, test currents and gate charging behaviour vary. Some MOSFETs require a gate voltage higher than the meter can provide. Use a proper test circuit to evaluate switching performance.

11. Testing a P-Channel MOSFET

P-channel MOSFETs have an intrinsic body diode with the opposite direction to that of typical N-channel power MOSFETs.

Body Diode Test

  1. Disconnect the MOSFET from the circuit.
  2. Discharge the gate by connecting gate to source.
  3. Set the DMM to diode mode.
  4. Connect the red probe to drain.
  5. Connect the black probe to source.
  6. Reverse the probes and record the readings.
Test Red Probe Black Probe Typical Observation
Body diode forward Drain Source Forward diode voltage
Body diode reverse Source Drain OL

Gate-to-source and gate-to-drain insulation checks are performed in the same general way as for an N-channel MOSFET.

12. Common MOSFET Faults

12.1 Gate-to-Source Short

A damaged MOSFET may develop a short circuit between gate and source, causing abnormal gate leakage or preventing normal switching.

12.2 Gate-to-Drain Short

A damaged gate insulation layer may create an electrical connection between gate and drain.

12.3 Drain-to-Source Short

A shorted MOSFET may show very low resistance or conduction between drain and source in both directions, even after the gate is discharged.

12.4 Open MOSFET

An internally damaged MOSFET may fail to conduct when a suitable gate drive is applied.

12.5 Excessive Leakage

A MOSFET may exhibit excessive drain-source leakage even when it should be OFF. A basic DMM test may not detect all leakage problems.

A normal body diode reading and high gate resistance do not guarantee that a MOSFET is fully functional. Further testing may be required under controlled operating conditions.

16. Lesson Summary

  • A MOSFET is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device.
  • Its main terminals are Gate, Drain and Source.
  • MOSFETs are available in N-channel and P-channel types.
  • Enhancement-mode MOSFETs are normally OFF at approximately zero VGS.
  • The body diode provides a characteristic conduction path.
  • A DMM can check the body diode and basic gate insulation.
  • The gate should be discharged before testing.
  • Gate insulation faults can cause MOSFET failure.
  • Datasheets are essential for correct terminal identification.
  • A basic DMM test cannot fully verify MOSFET performance.
