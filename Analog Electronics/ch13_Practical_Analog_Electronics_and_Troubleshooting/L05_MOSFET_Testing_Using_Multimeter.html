MOSFET Testing Using a Multimeter

Terminal identification, body-diode testing, gate charge behaviour and practical MOSFET fault diagnosis.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Identify the gate, drain and source terminals of a MOSFET.
  • Understand N-channel and P-channel enhancement MOSFET operation.
  • Recognize the intrinsic body diode and its role in testing.
  • Perform basic MOSFET checks using a digital multimeter.
  • Understand gate capacitance, charge retention and safe gate discharge.
  • Identify possible gate-source, drain-source and gate-drain faults.
  • Recognize the limitations of a basic DMM test.

2. MOSFET Construction and Terminals

A metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET) is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device. Its gate voltage controls the conduction channel between drain and source.

Gate (G)

Controls channel formation. It is insulated from the channel by a thin dielectric layer in a conventional insulated-gate MOSFET.

Drain (D)

One end of the controlled channel. Its electrical role depends on device polarity and operating condition.

Source (S)

The other end of the controlled channel. The gate-to-source voltage is a key control variable.

Body / substrate

Internal semiconductor region. In many discrete power MOSFETs, it is internally connected to source, creating an intrinsic body diode.

Typical N-channel enhancement MOSFET symbol

Enhancement MOSFET operation

N-channel enhancement

A sufficiently positive gate-to-source voltage forms an inversion channel and enables drain-source conduction.

P-channel enhancement

A sufficiently negative gate-to-source voltage relative to source enables channel conduction.

The threshold voltage \(V_{GS(th)}\) is the voltage at which a small specified drain current is measured. It does not mean the MOSFET is fully enhanced or suitable for a particular load current.

3. Intrinsic Body Diode

Many discrete power MOSFETs contain an intrinsic body diode between drain and source. For a typical N-channel power MOSFET with body tied to source, the diode anode is at source and cathode is at drain.

With the gate discharged, a diode test may show forward conduction from source to drain and blocking in the opposite direction. This is a useful initial check, but the actual device's internal construction and protection features must be considered.

Some MOSFETs have additional internal structures or unusual configurations. Confirm the datasheet before interpreting terminal measurements.

4. Safety Before MOSFET Testing

  • Switch off and disconnect the power source.
  • Discharge high-voltage capacitors through an appropriate resistor.
  • Verify that the circuit is de-energized.
  • Remove the MOSFET or isolate its terminals when in-circuit paths may affect the test.
  • Use an antistatic work area and handle the gate carefully.
  • Discharge the gate-source capacitance before performing a baseline test.
ESD caution: The insulated gate can be damaged by electrostatic discharge. Avoid touching the gate lead unnecessarily, and use suitable ESD precautions.

5. Basic N-Channel MOSFET Testing

The following procedure is for a typical discrete enhancement N-channel power MOSFET with an intrinsic body diode and no unusual internal protection network.

Step 1: Identify the terminals

Use the exact part number and datasheet to identify G, D and S. Do not assume that all TO-220 or TO-92 MOSFETs share the same pin arrangement.

Step 2: Discharge the gate

Briefly connect gate to source using an appropriate safe method to remove stored gate charge. This gives a known starting condition.

Step 3: Test the body diode

Red probeBlack probeTypical N-channel result
SourceDrainForward body-diode drop, often around 0.4–0.8 V
DrainSourceUsually OL with gate discharged

Step 4: Check gate insulation

Measure between gate and source, then gate and drain in both probe directions. A conventional healthy insulated gate should not behave like a low-resistance short. The actual reading depends on the meter and internal protection structures.

Step 5: Optional switching check

A controlled gate-charge test can demonstrate channel switching on some devices. This test is not a substitute for a datasheet-based electrical test, and it is not reliable for every MOSFET or DMM.

6. Basic P-Channel MOSFET Testing

For a typical discrete P-channel enhancement MOSFET, the body diode polarity is opposite to that of a typical N-channel device: the body diode anode is at drain and cathode is at source.

For P-channel enhancement MOSFETs, a sufficiently negative \(V_{GS}\) turns the channel on. Always use the device datasheet to establish the appropriate test conditions.

A MOSFET can pass a simple diode test and still fail under load because of increased on-resistance, damaged gate oxide, thermal damage or inadequate gate drive.

11. Worked Numerical Problems

Example 1: MOSFET conduction loss

A MOSFET has \(R_{DS(on)}=0.08\ \Omega\) and carries 2 A. Estimate conduction loss.

\[ P=I_D^2R_{DS(on)}=2^2\times0.08=0.32\ W \]

Estimated conduction loss is 0.32 W under the assumed on-resistance and current.

Example 2: Drain-source voltage drop

A MOSFET carries 3 A with an on-resistance of 0.05 Ω.

\[ V_{DS(on)}=I_DR_{DS(on)}=3\times0.05=0.15\ V \]

The estimated on-state drop is 0.15 V.

Example 3: Gate-drive charge current

A gate driver transfers 30 nC of gate charge in 300 ns. Estimate average gate current during this interval.

\[ I_G=\frac{Q_G}{t}=\frac{30\,nC}{300\,ns}=0.1\ A \]

The average gate current is 100 mA. Actual gate-drive current varies during switching, especially around the Miller plateau.

Procedure

13. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

1. What are the three main MOSFET terminals?
Gate, drain and source.
2. Why is a MOSFET called a voltage-controlled device?
The gate-to-source voltage controls the channel conductivity and therefore drain current.
3. What is the function of the body diode?
It is an intrinsic PN junction in many power MOSFETs and provides a current path in one direction when the channel is not conducting.
4. What is VGS(th)?
The gate-source threshold voltage at which a specified small drain current is reached. It is not the guaranteed fully-on gate voltage.
5. Why must the gate be discharged before testing?
Stored gate charge can leave the MOSFET partially or fully switched on and distort the initial test result.
6. What is RDS(on)?
The drain-source resistance when the MOSFET is on under specified gate voltage, current and temperature conditions.
7. How does the body diode polarity differ between typical N-channel and P-channel MOSFETs?
For a typical N-channel device, the body diode anode is at source and cathode at drain. For a typical P-channel device, the polarity is opposite.
8. Can a DMM prove that a MOSFET is fully healthy?
No. It provides basic checks but does not establish on-resistance, gate charge, switching performance, leakage at rated voltage or thermal reliability.
9. Why can a MOSFET overheat even when it is not shorted?
Insufficient gate drive, excessive RDS(on), high current, switching losses or poor thermal design can cause overheating.
10. Why is the datasheet important when testing a MOSFET?
It identifies the pinout, internal diode orientation, ratings, gate limits and test conditions.

16. Key Takeaways

  • Identify MOSFET terminals from the exact datasheet, not package appearance alone.
  • Discharge the gate before performing baseline tests.
  • The intrinsic body diode provides a useful first-level diagnostic.
  • Check gate-source and gate-drain insulation for unexpected low-resistance paths.
  • Understand the difference between threshold voltage and fully enhanced operation.
  • A DMM test is not a substitute for controlled on-resistance, leakage and switching tests.
