2. Standard Operational Amplifier Symbol

An operational amplifier is generally represented by a triangular symbol. The two input terminals are located on the left side, while the output terminal is located on the right side.

  • Non-inverting input (+): Positive signal input.
  • Inverting input (−): Negative signal input.
  • Output: Amplified output signal.
  • Positive supply: Powers the internal circuit.
  • Negative supply: Provides the negative supply rail in dual-supply operation.

3. Functions of Op-Amp Terminals

3.1 Non-Inverting Input (+)

The non-inverting input is marked with a plus (+) symbol. A positive change in this input voltage tends to produce a positive change in the output voltage.

3.2 Inverting Input (−)

The inverting input is marked with a minus (−) symbol. A positive change in this input voltage tends to produce a negative change in the output voltage.

3.3 Output Terminal

The output terminal provides the amplified signal. Its voltage depends on the differential input voltage, the gain and the available supply voltages.

3.4 Positive and Negative Supply Terminals

These terminals provide the energy required by the internal transistor stages of the Op-Amp.

Important: The supply pins are not signal input terminals. Never apply an input signal or connect a load to them as though they were the output terminal.

4. IC 741 Operational Amplifier

The µA741 (741) is a classic general-purpose operational amplifier widely used for learning basic analog circuit concepts.

It is commonly available in an eight-pin dual in-line package (DIP-8). The device includes two signal inputs, one output, two supply terminals and two offset-null terminals.

5. IC 741 Pin Description

Pin Number Pin Name Function
1 Offset Null Used with pin 5 to adjust input offset voltage.
2 Inverting Input (−) Inverting signal input.
3 Non-Inverting Input (+) Non-inverting signal input.
4 −VCC / V− Negative power supply.
5 Offset Null Used with pin 1 for offset adjustment.
6 Output Amplified output signal.
7 +VCC / V+ Positive power supply.
8 NC No internal connection.
Placement Tip: Remember pins 2 and 3 as the signal inputs, pin 6 as output, pin 4 as negative supply and pin 7 as positive supply. Pins 1 and 5 are for offset adjustment; pin 8 is NC.

6. IC 741 Pin Numbering and Orientation

When viewing a DIP-8 IC from the top, with the notch facing upward, pin numbering starts at the upper-left corner.

  1. Pin 1 is at the upper-left corner.
  2. Pin numbers increase down the left side from 1 to 4.
  3. Pin 5 is at the lower-right corner.
  4. Numbers increase upward on the right side from 5 to 8.
Do not identify IC pins by looking at the text orientation alone. Locate the notch or pin-1 dot first, and confirm the package orientation from its datasheet.

7. Internal Block Diagram of an Op-Amp

An operational amplifier contains several internal functional stages. The exact circuit implementation varies between devices. A traditional bipolar Op-Amp such as the 741 uses multiple transistor stages.

Main Internal Stages

  1. Differential input stage: Amplifies the difference between the input signals and provides high input impedance.
  2. Intermediate gain stage: Provides additional voltage gain.
  3. Level shifting stage: Establishes suitable DC voltage levels between internal stages.
  4. Output stage: Provides load-driving capability and a relatively low output impedance.
  5. Bias and compensation circuits: Establish operating currents and help maintain stability.

8. Offset Null Terminals

Ideally, an Op-Amp should produce zero output when both inputs are at the same voltage. In a practical device, small internal mismatches can cause an input offset voltage.

In the 741, pins 1 and 5 are provided for offset adjustment. An appropriate external potentiometer arrangement can be used to reduce the offset voltage.

Offset-null connections should follow the manufacturer's datasheet. They are not ordinary signal inputs and should not be connected arbitrarily.

9. Practical Identification of IC 741

Before using a 741 IC in a laboratory circuit:

  1. Identify the IC package and its part number.
  2. Locate the notch or pin-1 identification mark.
  3. Determine the pin numbering direction.
  4. Identify pins 2, 3 and 6 for signal connections.
  5. Identify pins 4 and 7 for power supply connections.
  6. Check the permitted supply voltage in the datasheet.
  7. Check the IC orientation before applying power.

Important Practical Considerations

  • Do not reverse the power supply connections.
  • Do not exceed the absolute maximum ratings.
  • Use suitable supply decoupling capacitors near the IC.
  • Keep input voltages within the permitted common-mode range.
  • Do not assume the 741 is suitable for low-voltage, single-supply circuits.

10. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What are the three main signal terminals of an Op-Amp?

Non-inverting input, inverting input and output terminal.

Q2. What is the function of the non-inverting input?

A positive change in this input tends to produce a positive change in output.

Q3. What is the function of the inverting input?

A positive change in this input tends to produce a negative change in output.

Q4. How many pins does the commonly used 741 DIP IC have?

It has eight pins.

Q5. Which pin is the output of IC 741?

Pin number 6.

Q6. Which pins are the power supply terminals of IC 741?

Pin 7 is the positive supply and pin 4 is the negative supply.

Q7. What is the purpose of pins 1 and 5?

They are offset-null terminals used to adjust input offset voltage.

Q8. What is pin 8 of the 741 used for?

It is marked NC (no internal connection).

Q9. What is the function of the differential input stage?

It senses and amplifies the difference between the two input signals.

Q10. What is the purpose of the output stage?

It provides output drive capability and a relatively low output impedance.

12. Lesson Summary

  • The standard Op-Amp symbol is triangular.
  • It has inverting and non-inverting inputs and one output.
  • IC 741 is a classic eight-pin operational amplifier.
  • Pin 2 is the inverting input; pin 3 is the non-inverting input.
  • Pin 4 is negative supply; pin 7 is positive supply.
  • Pin 6 is output; pins 1 and 5 are offset null; pin 8 is NC.
  • Internal stages include differential input, voltage gain, level shifting and output stages.
  • Correct pin identification and supply connections are essential for safe operation.
