2. Introduction to JFET Characteristics

JFET characteristics describe the relationship between drain current, drain-source voltage and gate-source voltage.

Drain characteristicsShow the relationship between ID and VDS for fixed values of VGS.
Transfer characteristicsShow the relationship between ID and VGS when the JFET operates in its drain-current saturation region.

For an N-channel JFET, making VGS more negative reduces the drain current. At VGS = 0, the saturation drain current is approximately IDSS.

3. Drain Characteristics of an N-Channel JFET

The drain characteristic is a graph of drain current ID against drain-source voltage VDS for a fixed gate-source voltage VGS.

Several curves are plotted for different gate voltages. A typical family contains:

  • VGS = 0 V
  • VGS = −1 V
  • VGS = −2 V
  • VGS = −3 V
  • VGS approaching VGS(off)

The diagram is conceptual. Actual curves depend on device geometry, temperature, channel-length effects and the specific JFET datasheet.

4. Operating Regions

A. Ohmic or Linear Region

At relatively small VDS, the channel behaves approximately like a voltage-controlled resistor. Drain current increases as VDS increases.

B. Saturation or Pinch-Off Region

As VDS increases, the depletion region expands more strongly near the drain. The channel becomes constricted near the drain end and ID becomes approximately constant for a fixed VGS.

C. Cutoff Region

When VGS reaches the gate-source cutoff voltage, the channel is effectively closed and drain current approaches zero.

D. Breakdown Region

At excessive drain-source voltage, avalanche or other breakdown mechanisms can cause a rapid increase in current. Operation here can damage the device.

Terminology: JFET saturation means drain current is approximately constant with increasing VDS. It does not mean the device is switched OFF. This differs from the usual low-resistance ON state of a MOSFET used as a switch.

5. Saturation Onset and Gate Voltage

For an ideal long-channel N-channel JFET, the approximate drain-source voltage at the onset of saturation is:

VDS(sat) ≈ VGS − VGS(off)

Example: if VGS = −2 V and VGS(off) = −6 V:

VDS(sat) ≈ −2 − (−6) = 4 V

Increasing the magnitude of negative VGS reduces the drain current and changes the voltage at which saturation begins.

This expression uses signed negative VGS(off) for an N-channel device. Always check the voltage convention used by the textbook or datasheet.

7. Transfer Characteristics

The transfer characteristic shows the relationship between drain current ID and gate-source voltage VGS while the JFET is operating in its saturation region.

For an N-channel JFET, the ideal Shockley equation is:

ID = IDSS × (1 − VGS / VGS(off))²

Here:

  • ID = drain current.
  • IDSS = saturation drain current at VGS = 0.
  • VGS = gate-source voltage.
  • VGS(off) = signed gate-source cutoff voltage, normally negative for an N-channel JFET.

9. Shockley Equation – Numerical Examples

Example 1

An N-channel JFET has IDSS = 12 mA and VGS(off) = −6 V. Calculate ID when VGS = −2 V.

ID = 12 × [1 − (−2/−6)]²
ID = 12 × (1 − 1/3)²
ID = 12 × (2/3)² = 5.33 mA

Example 2

IDSS = 10 mA, VGS(off) = −5 V and VGS = −2.5 V.

ID = 10 × [1 − (−2.5/−5)]²
ID = 10 × (0.5)² = 2.5 mA

Example 3

IDSS = 8 mA, VGS(off) = −4 V and VGS = 0 V.

ID = 8 × (1 − 0)² = 8 mA

At zero gate-source voltage, the ideal saturation current equals IDSS.

Example 4

IDSS = 15 mA, VGS(off) = −5 V. Find the ideal drain current at VGS = −5 V.

ID = 15 × [1 − (−5/−5)]² = 0 mA

12. Practical Applications

  • High-input-impedance amplifier: JFET gate draws very little current in normal operation.
  • Voltage-controlled resistor: A JFET can operate in its ohmic region for signal-dependent resistance applications, within suitable limits.
  • Constant-current source: JFET current characteristics can be used in simple current-source circuits.
  • Analog signal switching: JFETs can be used in low-level analog switching and signal routing.
  • Low-noise input stages: Selected JFETs are used where their noise characteristics suit the source impedance and frequency range.

13. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Plot the drain and transfer characteristics of an N-channel JFET.

Equipment

  • N-channel JFET and its datasheet.
  • Two current-limited DC supplies or a suitable gate-bias arrangement.
  • Digital multimeter(s).
  • Current-limiting resistor and breadboard/test fixture.

Part A: Drain characteristics

  1. Connect the JFET using the datasheet pinout.
  2. Set VGS to 0 V.
  3. Increase VDS in small steps within device ratings.
  4. Measure ID at every step.
  5. Repeat for progressively more negative VGS values.
  6. Plot ID against VDS for each VGS.

Part B: Transfer characteristics

  1. Choose a VDS that keeps the device in its saturation region over the intended gate-voltage range.
  2. Change VGS in small steps from 0 V toward VGS(off).
  3. Measure the corresponding ID.
  4. Plot ID against VGS.
  5. Compare the measurements with the Shockley equation.

15. Placement Interview Questions

1. What are JFET drain characteristics?

They are plots of drain current against drain-source voltage for fixed gate-source voltages.

2. What are transfer characteristics?

They show drain current versus gate-source voltage while the JFET is in its saturation region.

3. What is IDSS?

The saturation drain current at VGS = 0 V.

4. State the Shockley equation.

ID = IDSS[1 − VGS/VGS(off)]² for the ideal N-channel JFET saturation model.

5. What is the drain-current value at VGS = 0?

ID = IDSS in the ideal saturation model.

6. What happens at VGS = VGS(off)?

The channel is effectively cut off and drain current approaches zero.

7. What is the ohmic region?

The low-VDS region where drain current changes significantly with drain-source voltage.

8. What is the saturation region?

The region where drain current is approximately constant for a fixed VGS.

9. Does pinch-off mean cutoff?

No. Pinch-off marks the onset of drain-current saturation; cutoff is the condition where drain current approaches zero.

10. What happens to ID when VGS becomes more negative?

ID decreases in an N-channel JFET.

11. What is breakdown in a JFET?

A high-voltage condition where current increases sharply and may damage the device.

12. Why can actual transfer curves differ from Shockley predictions?

Device parameter variation, temperature, channel geometry and nonideal effects influence the actual curve.

13. What is the approximate saturation-onset voltage?

For an ideal N-channel JFET, VDS(sat) ≈ VGS − VGS(off).

14. Which graph is useful for finding transconductance?

The transfer characteristic, because transconductance is the slope dID/dVGS at a specified operating point.

15. What is the unit of transconductance?

Siemens (S), commonly millisiemens (mS).

17. Lecture Summary

  • Drain characteristics plot ID against VDS for different fixed VGS values.
  • Transfer characteristics plot ID against VGS in saturation.
  • The ohmic region behaves approximately like a voltage-controlled resistance.
  • The saturation region has nearly constant ID for a fixed VGS.
  • Cutoff occurs when the gate voltage closes the channel.
  • Breakdown is an excessive-voltage condition.
  • The Shockley equation models the ideal JFET transfer characteristic.
  • Measured device characteristics can differ from ideal calculations.
