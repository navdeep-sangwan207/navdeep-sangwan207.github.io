Lesson 3: JFET Drain and Transfer Characteristics

Understand JFET output curves, transfer curves, operating regions and pinch-off.

1. Introduction to JFET Characteristics

The characteristics of a Junction Field Effect Transistor describe the relationship between its drain current, drain-to-source voltage and gate-to-source voltage.

Two important characteristic curves are used to understand JFET operation:

  • Drain or Output Characteristics: Show the relationship between drain current and drain-to-source voltage for different fixed gate-to-source voltages.
  • Transfer Characteristics: Show the relationship between drain current and gate-to-source voltage when the JFET operates in its saturation region.
Important: For an N-channel JFET, the drain current generally decreases as the gate-to-source voltage becomes more negative. The exact curves depend on the device parameters.

2. Drain Characteristics of N-Channel JFET

The drain characteristics show how drain current \(I_D\) changes with drain-to-source voltage \(V_{DS}\) for different fixed values of gate-to-source voltage \(V_{GS}\).

To obtain these characteristics, keep \(V_{GS}\) constant and gradually increase \(V_{DS}\). Repeat the process for several gate-to-source voltages.

Typical Drain Characteristic Curve

Typical N-channel JFET drain characteristics. The saturation current decreases as the gate voltage becomes more negative.

Explanation

Each curve represents a different fixed value of \(V_{GS}\). For example, the upper curve corresponds to \(V_{GS}=0\), while lower curves correspond to increasingly negative gate voltages.

  • At \(V_{GS}=0\), drain current is highest in the normal set of curves.
  • As \(V_{GS}\) becomes more negative, drain current decreases.
  • When \(V_{GS}\) reaches the cutoff voltage, the channel is effectively cut off and drain current becomes approximately zero.

3. Operating Regions of a JFET

3.1 Ohmic or Linear Region

At small values of \(V_{DS}\), the JFET channel behaves approximately like a voltage-controlled resistor. Drain current increases as drain-to-source voltage increases.

  • The channel is not yet pinched off.
  • Drain current changes significantly with \(V_{DS}\).
  • The device can be used as a voltage-controlled resistance in suitable circuits.

3.2 Saturation or Active Region

When \(V_{DS}\) reaches the required value for pinch-off at the drain end, the JFET enters its saturation region. For a fixed \(V_{GS}\), drain current becomes approximately constant as \(V_{DS}\) increases further.

  • Drain current is mainly controlled by \(V_{GS}\).
  • The channel is pinched near the drain end.
  • This region is commonly used for JFET amplification.

3.3 Cutoff Region

When the reverse gate bias reaches the cutoff condition, the depletion regions close the conducting channel. Drain current becomes approximately zero, apart from small leakage currents.

Cutoff Condition for an N-Channel JFET

\(V_{GS} \leq V_{GS(off)}\)

Here \(V_{GS(off)}\) is negative for a conventional N-channel JFET.

3.4 Breakdown Region

At excessively high drain-to-source voltage, the electric field can become strong enough to cause breakdown. Drain current may rise sharply and damage the device if current is not limited.

Do not confuse the saturation region with breakdown. Saturation is a normal operating region; breakdown is generally an unsafe condition unless specifically permitted by the device rating.

4. Pinch-Off Voltage

Pinch-off occurs when the depletion region becomes wide enough near the drain end to strongly constrict the conducting channel. Beyond this condition, drain current becomes approximately constant for a fixed gate voltage.

For an idealized N-channel JFET, the approximate drain voltage at the onset of saturation is:

\(V_{DS(sat)} \approx V_{GS}-V_P\)

where \(V_P\) is the signed pinch-off parameter for the N-channel device under the convention used in this lesson.

For example, if \(V_P=-4V\) and \(V_{GS}=-1V\):

Calculation:

\(V_{DS(sat)} \approx -1-(-4)\)

\(V_{DS(sat)} \approx 3V\)

Many textbooks use the magnitude of the pinch-off parameter rather than its signed value. In that notation, \(V_{DS(sat)} \approx |V_P|-|V_{GS}|\). Always check the sign convention before substituting values.

5. Transfer Characteristics of JFET

Transfer characteristics show the relationship between drain current \(I_D\) and gate-to-source voltage \(V_{GS}\) while the JFET is operating in its saturation region.

For an N-channel JFET, drain current is maximum at \(V_{GS}=0\) and decreases as \(V_{GS}\) becomes more negative.

Typical N-channel JFET transfer characteristics.

Important Observations

  • At \(V_{GS}=0\), drain current is approximately \(I_{DSS}\).
  • As \(V_{GS}\) becomes more negative, drain current decreases.
  • At \(V_{GS}=V_{GS(off)}\), drain current is approximately zero.
  • The transfer curve is nonlinear and approximately parabolic under the ideal Shockley model.

6. Shockley Equation for JFET

The Shockley equation describes the idealized relationship between drain current and gate-to-source voltage for a JFET operating in its saturation region.

Shockley Equation

\(I_D=I_{DSS}\left(1-\frac{V_{GS}}{V_{GS(off)}}\right)^2\)

Where:

  • \(I_D\) = Drain current.
  • \(I_{DSS}\) = Drain current at \(V_{GS}=0\), in the saturation region.
  • \(V_{GS}\) = Gate-to-source voltage.
  • \(V_{GS(off)}\) = Gate-to-source cutoff voltage.
For an N-channel JFET, both \(V_{GS}\) and \(V_{GS(off)}\) are normally negative or zero under the standard source-referenced convention. Their ratio in the Shockley equation is therefore handled with the signed values.

7. Numerical Example Using Shockley Equation

Problem:

An N-channel JFET has the following parameters:

  • \(I_{DSS}=12mA\)
  • \(V_{GS(off)}=-6V\)
  • \(V_{GS}=-2V\)
Find the drain current.

Solution

Using Shockley's equation:

\(I_D=I_{DSS} \left(1-\frac{V_{GS}}{V_{GS(off)}}\right)^2\)

Substitute the values:

\(I_D=12 \left(1-\frac{-2}{-6}\right)^2 mA\)

\(I_D=12(1-0.3333)^2 mA\)

\(I_D=12(0.6667)^2 mA\)

\(I_D\approx12(0.4444)mA\)

Final Answer

\(I_D\approx5.33mA\)

8. Important JFET Parameters from Characteristics

8.1 Drain Current at Zero Gate Bias (\(I_{DSS}\))

\(I_{DSS}\) is the drain current when the gate and source are at the same potential, with the JFET operating in its saturation region.

8.2 Gate Cutoff Voltage (\(V_{GS(off)}\))

It is the gate-to-source voltage at which the channel is effectively cut off and drain current becomes very small.

8.3 Transconductance (\(g_m\))

Transconductance measures how much the drain current changes when the gate-to-source voltage changes, with drain-to-source voltage held constant.

\(g_m=\frac{\Delta I_D}{\Delta V_{GS}}\)

Small-signal definition: \(g_m=\left.\frac{\partial I_D}{\partial V_{GS}}\right|_{V_{DS}}\)

Its unit is Siemens (S), commonly expressed in millisiemens (mS).

10. Practical Importance in Electronics

  • Amplifier Design: Transfer characteristics help determine the operating point and small-signal transconductance.
  • Biasing: Drain characteristics help identify a suitable operating region.
  • Analog Switching: The ohmic and cutoff regions are useful for understanding resistive and off-state behaviour.
  • Device Selection: \(I_{DSS}\), cutoff voltage and transconductance help compare JFET devices.
  • Troubleshooting: Abnormal current or gate behaviour can indicate a damaged device or incorrect biasing.

11. Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What are drain characteristics?

    They show the variation of drain current with drain-to-source voltage for fixed gate-to-source voltages.

  2. What are transfer characteristics?

    They show the variation of drain current with gate-to-source voltage while the JFET is operating in its saturation region.

  3. What is the ohmic region?

    It is the low-\(V_{DS}\) region in which the JFET behaves approximately as a voltage-controlled resistance.

  4. What is the saturation region?

    It is the region in which drain current is approximately constant with increasing \(V_{DS}\) for a fixed \(V_{GS}\).

  5. What is \(I_{DSS}\)?

    It is the drain current at zero gate-to-source voltage when the JFET is in its saturation region.

  6. What is \(V_{GS(off)}\)?

    It is the gate-to-source voltage at which the JFET channel is effectively cut off.

  7. State the Shockley equation.

    \(I_D=I_{DSS} (1-V_{GS}/V_{GS(off)})^2\)

  8. What is transconductance?

    It is the rate of change of drain current with respect to gate-to-source voltage at a specified operating point and fixed drain voltage.

  9. Does pinch-off mean zero drain current?

    No. Pinch-off normally indicates the onset of saturation, not cutoff.

  10. Which region is commonly used for JFET amplification?

    The saturation or active region.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Drain characteristics represent \(I_D\) versus \(V_{DS}\).
  • Transfer characteristics represent \(I_D\) versus \(V_{GS}\).
  • The main operating regions are ohmic, saturation, cutoff and breakdown.
  • Pinch-off marks the onset of drain-current saturation.
  • \(I_{DSS}\) is the drain current at zero gate bias in saturation.
  • \(V_{GS(off)}\) is the gate cutoff voltage.
  • The Shockley equation estimates drain current in the saturation region.
  • Transconductance measures the sensitivity of drain current to gate voltage.
