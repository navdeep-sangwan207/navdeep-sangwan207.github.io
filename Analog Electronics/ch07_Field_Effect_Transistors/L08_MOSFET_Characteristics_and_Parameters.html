2. MOSFET Operating Regions

An enhancement-mode NMOS transistor can operate in three important regions depending on its gate-source voltage (VGS) and drain-source voltage (VDS).

Overdrive voltage (VOV)

The overdrive voltage represents the amount by which gate-source voltage exceeds the threshold voltage.

VOV = VGS − VTH

  • VGS ≤ VTH: MOSFET is ideally OFF.
  • VGS > VTH: Channel forms and conduction begins.
  • VDS < VOV: Triode/ohmic region.
  • VDS ≥ VOV: Saturation region in the long-channel model.

3. MOSFET Output Characteristics

The output characteristics represent drain current (ID) against drain-source voltage (VDS) for different fixed gate-source voltages.

Interpretation: Increasing VGS increases the available channel charge and generally increases drain current. The curves shown are conceptual, not calibrated to a specific device.

5. MOSFET Equations

A. Cutoff Region

When the gate-source voltage does not exceed the threshold voltage:

VGS ≤ VTH

In the ideal model, ID = 0.

B. Triode Region (Ohmic Region)

When VGS > VTH and VDS < VOV:

ID = kn[(VGS − VTH)VDS − VDS²/2]

Here, kn is the simplified device parameter used in this lesson. Its definition can differ between textbooks and datasheets.

C. Saturation Region

When VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VOV:

ID = ½ kn(VGS − VTH)²(1 + λVDS)

For the basic model with channel-length modulation neglected (λ = 0):

ID = ½ kn(VOV)²

In this lesson's equations, kn is defined so that the saturation equation is ID = ½knVOV². Always check the parameter definition in the reference being used.

Channel-Length Modulation (λ)

In an ideal long-channel MOSFET, saturation current is constant with increasing VDS. In practical devices, drain current may increase slightly as VDS increases in saturation.

This effect is represented by the channel-length modulation factor λ.

6. Transfer Characteristics

The transfer characteristic shows ID as a function of VGS, usually at a fixed VDS chosen to keep the device in its intended region.

For the ideal square-law model in saturation:

ID = ½kn(VGS − VTH)²

For VGS > VTH, when channel-length modulation is neglected.

8. Threshold Voltage vs Fully ON Voltage

Important placement question: Is a MOSFET fully ON when VGS = VTH?

No. VTH is normally measured at a small specified drain current. A MOSFET may begin conducting at VTH but still have a relatively high RDS(on). Select a MOSFET using RDS(on) specified at the gate-drive voltage actually available in your circuit.

For example, if a microcontroller supplies 3.3V to a MOSFET gate, check whether the datasheet specifies RDS(on) at 2.5V or 3.3V. A device with VTH = 2V is not automatically suitable for a 3.3V gate drive.

9. Conduction Loss Calculation

When a MOSFET is ON and operating as a switch, a first-order estimate of conduction loss is:

Pcond = ID² × RDS(on)

Worked Example

Given:

  • Drain current = 3A
  • RDS(on) = 0.08Ω

Calculation:

Pcond = 3² × 0.08

Pcond = 9 × 0.08

Pcond = 0.72W

The approximate ON-state voltage drop:

VDS = ID × RDS(on) = 3 × 0.08 = 0.24V

10. Switching Loss Calculation

A rough switching-loss estimate for a hard-switched device with approximately linear voltage/current overlap is:

Pswitch ≈ ½ × VDS × ID × (tr + tf) × f

Example

  • VDS = 12V
  • ID = 2A
  • tr + tf = 100ns
  • f = 20kHz

Pswitch ≈ 0.5 × 12 × 2 × 100 × 10⁻⁹ × 20 × 10³

Pswitch ≈ 0.024W

This is an approximate switching-overlap calculation. Actual switching loss depends on gate drive, waveform shape, Miller plateau, load, parasitic inductance, capacitance, reverse recovery and operating conditions.

12. Transconductance (gm)

Transconductance describes the change in drain current with respect to gate-source voltage at a particular operating point.

gm = ΔID / ΔVGS

Its unit is Siemens (S), commonly expressed as mS.

Example

Drain current changes from 2A to 2.4A when VGS changes from 4V to 4.2V.

gm = (2.4 − 2)/(4.2 − 4)

gm = 0.4/0.2

gm = 2S

In practical MOSFET datasheets, gm is typically measured under specified bias conditions and is not constant across the operating range.

13. Gate Charge and MOSFET Switching

During switching, gate charge is supplied or removed by the driver. The gate-drain capacitance and Miller effect can make the gate voltage temporarily plateau while the drain voltage changes.

  • Higher Qg generally requires more gate-drive charge.
  • Gate drive current affects switching time.
  • Switching faster can increase ringing and EMI.
  • Slower switching can increase switching loss.

14. Datasheet Selection Checklist

  • VDS rating with adequate transient margin.
  • Continuous and pulsed current ratings under realistic thermal conditions.
  • RDS(on) at the actual gate-drive voltage.
  • Maximum VGS rating.
  • Qg and switching performance.
  • Thermal resistance and package.
  • Safe operating area (SOA).
  • Body diode specifications if applicable.
  • Avalanche and transient protection considerations.
  • Temperature dependence of RDS(on).

15. Practical Lab

Objective

Observe the drain characteristics of an enhancement-mode NMOS transistor.

Equipment

  • Small-signal NMOS transistor or suitable power MOSFET.
  • Current-limited low-voltage DC supply.
  • Resistors for current limiting.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Breadboard and jumper wires.

Procedure

  1. Identify drain, source and gate using the device datasheet.
  2. Connect the source to circuit ground.
  3. Apply a controlled gate-source voltage.
  4. Apply a low drain-source voltage through a current-limiting resistor.
  5. Measure drain current and VDS.
  6. Increment VDS while maintaining VGS.
  7. Record drain current.
  8. Repeat for different gate-source voltages.
  9. Plot ID versus VDS for each VGS.

Expected Observation

At low VDS, the MOSFET behaves approximately as a voltage-controlled resistance. At sufficiently large VDS, the curves transition toward the saturation region under the simplified long-channel model.

Observe maximum VGS, VDS and current ratings. Use ESD precautions. Do not use mains voltage.

19. Summary

  • NMOS operation depends on VGS and VDS.
  • Cutoff, triode and saturation are the three simplified operating regions.
  • VOV = VGS − VTH.
  • Triode operation is generally useful for a low-resistance switching ON-state.
  • Saturation is commonly used in analog amplifier and current-source operation.
  • RDS(on) determines much of the switching conduction loss.
  • Qg and capacitances influence switching performance.
  • Datasheets must be evaluated using real circuit operating conditions.
