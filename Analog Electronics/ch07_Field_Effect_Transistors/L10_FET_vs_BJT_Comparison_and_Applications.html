2. Introduction

Both Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJTs) and Field Effect Transistors (FETs) are semiconductor devices used for amplification, switching and signal processing.

BJT Collector current is controlled by base current and base-emitter voltage, with behavior dependent on operating conditions. FET Drain current is primarily controlled by gate-source voltage in a MOSFET or gate-channel voltage in a FET.

5. Input Impedance Comparison

BJT

The base-emitter junction is forward biased during normal forward-active operation. Base current is required to establish and control collector current.

MOSFET

The gate is insulated from the channel by an oxide layer. A MOSFET normally has very high DC gate input resistance, but its gate capacitances and gate charge require current during transitions.

CharacteristicBJTMOSFET
DC input behaviorBase-emitter junctionInsulated gate
Input currentContinuous base drive in typical active operationPrimarily gate charging/discharging current during transitions
Input impedanceGenerally lowerGenerally very high at DC
Drive considerationsBase current and base voltageGate voltage, gate charge and driver capability

6. Current Gain and Transconductance

BJT current gain

β = IC/IB

β represents the DC current gain in the forward-active region. It varies between devices and with operating conditions.

MOSFET transconductance

gm = ΔID/ΔVGS

Transconductance represents the change in drain current for a change in gate-source voltage at a particular operating point.

ParameterMeaningUnit
βBJT DC current gainDimensionless
gmTransconductanceSiemens
αBJT common-base current gainDimensionless
BJT beta and FET transconductance are not interchangeable parameters. They describe different device relationships.

7. Amplification Comparison

BJT common-emitter amplifier

  • Base-emitter junction provides input control.
  • Collector resistor or active load converts current variations into voltage variations.
  • Common-emitter configuration typically produces phase inversion.
  • Biasing and temperature stability are important.

MOSFET common-source amplifier

  • Gate-source voltage controls drain current.
  • Drain resistor or active load converts current variations into output voltage.
  • Common-source configuration typically produces phase inversion.
  • Gate biasing and operating point are important.

Phase diagram is illustrative. Common-emitter and common-source amplifiers typically provide approximately 180° phase inversion under their normal small-signal operating conditions.

9. Thermal Behavior

BJT

  • Collector current and junction temperature influence device behavior.
  • Thermal stability is important in biasing.
  • In some circuits, temperature-induced changes in current can contribute to thermal runaway.
  • Emitter resistors and proper bias networks help improve stability.

MOSFET

  • RDS(on) generally increases with temperature in many power MOSFETs.
  • Conduction loss depends on current and ON-state resistance.
  • Thermal design must consider package, PCB, airflow and heatsinking.
  • Parallel MOSFET behavior depends on device characteristics and circuit layout.
Thermal behavior is device- and circuit-dependent. BJTs and MOSFETs should not be compared using a single universal thermal rule.

13. Practical Applications of BJTs

  • Small-signal amplification.
  • Audio signal stages.
  • Sensor signal conditioning.
  • Switching circuits.
  • Oscillator circuits.
  • Analog circuit design.
  • Current-source and bias circuits.

14. Practical Applications of FETs

  • Power supply switching.
  • DC motor control.
  • LED switching and PWM.
  • High-impedance sensor interfaces.
  • Analog amplifier stages.
  • Battery load switching.
  • Embedded system outputs.
  • CMOS digital logic.
  • Power converter circuits.

15. Practical Selection Scenarios

Scenario 1: MCU controls a DC load

A suitable logic-level MOSFET is often a practical choice because the MCU can control its gate and the MOSFET can provide a low ON-state resistance. Verify actual RDS(on), gate voltage, current, transient ratings and protection requirements.

Scenario 2: Small-signal amplifier

A BJT or FET may be appropriate. The choice depends on gain, noise, source impedance, power, bandwidth, bias stability and circuit topology.

Scenario 3: High-impedance sensor

A FET-based input may be advantageous because its gate draws very little steady-state current. However, noise, input capacitance, leakage and bandwidth must be considered.

Scenario 4: Linear power control

A BJT or MOSFET may be used depending on voltage, current, power dissipation, control requirements and safe operating area. A switching solution may be preferable if efficiency is important.

Scenario 5: RF circuit

Device selection depends on frequency, gain, noise figure, input/output matching, parasitic capacitances, stability and power. Neither device family is universally superior.

16. Practical Lab

Objective

Compare BJT and MOSFET behavior as low-voltage electronic switches.

Equipment

  • NPN BJT.
  • Logic-level NMOS MOSFET.
  • LED and current-limiting resistor.
  • Base resistor.
  • Gate resistor.
  • Gate pull-down resistor.
  • Low-voltage DC supply.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Switch or GPIO.

Procedure

  1. Construct a BJT low-side switching circuit.
  2. Apply control through a base resistor.
  3. Measure base voltage and collector-emitter voltage.
  4. Observe the LED ON and OFF states.
  5. Construct an NMOS low-side switching circuit.
  6. Apply control voltage to the gate.
  7. Measure VGS and VDS.
  8. Compare the control-input requirements.
  9. Compare ON-state voltage drops under similar load conditions.
  10. Record observations and explain differences.

Expected Result

Both devices can control the load. Their drive requirements, ON-state losses, input behavior and switching characteristics differ.

Use low-voltage DC only. Observe current limits and device ratings. Do not connect the laboratory circuit to mains voltage.

20. Summary

  • BJTs and FETs are both used for switching and amplification.
  • BJT operation depends on junction behavior and base drive.
  • MOSFET operation depends on gate voltage and channel behavior.
  • MOSFETs typically have high DC gate input resistance.
  • BJT beta and FET transconductance describe different relationships.
  • Both devices can be used as amplifiers.
  • Both devices can be used as electronic switches.
  • Device selection depends on circuit requirements, operating conditions, cost, thermal design and datasheet specifications.
