Lesson 10: FET/MOSFET Testing and Troubleshooting

Learn practical testing methods using a digital multimeter, identify common device failures and troubleshoot FET-based electronic circuits.

1. Introduction

FETs and MOSFETs are commonly used in amplifiers, switching circuits, power supplies, motor drivers and electronic control systems. A faulty transistor can cause excessive current, overheating, incorrect output voltage or complete circuit failure.

Testing helps determine whether a device is healthy, open-circuit, short-circuit or behaving abnormally.

This lesson focuses on practical troubleshooting techniques used in electronics testing, maintenance and hardware engineering.

2. Common Faults in FETs and MOSFETs

Fault Possible Cause Typical Symptom
Drain-source short Overcurrent, overheating or voltage surge Load remains ON or supply current becomes excessive.
Gate-source short ESD or excessive gate voltage Gate control becomes ineffective.
Gate-drain short Internal dielectric damage Abnormal gate and drain behavior.
Open circuit Bond-wire or semiconductor damage Load does not operate.
Increased RDS(on) Device degradation or thermal damage Excessive voltage drop and heating.
Gate leakage Gate dielectric damage Unstable or incorrect switching.
Thermal failure Insufficient cooling or excessive power loss Intermittent operation or permanent damage.

3. Precautions Before Testing

Important electrical safety precautions:
  • Disconnect the circuit from its power supply.
  • Discharge capacitors safely using an appropriate resistor.
  • Verify that the circuit is de-energized before testing.
  • Never use resistance or diode mode on a powered circuit.
  • Use ESD precautions when handling MOSFETs.
  • Check the device datasheet for pin configuration and voltage ratings.
  • Do not apply an external voltage to an unknown gate terminal.

4. Identifying MOSFET Terminals

A typical discrete MOSFET has three main terminals:

  • Gate (G): Controls channel conduction.
  • Drain (D): Drain-current terminal.
  • Source (S): Source-current terminal.

Some MOSFET structures also expose a separate body terminal, but most common discrete power MOSFETs internally connect the body to the source.

Simplified terminal representation. Always verify the actual package pinout from the datasheet.

5. Testing an N-Channel Enhancement MOSFET Using a DMM

A digital multimeter with diode-test mode can help identify a shorted MOSFET and check its intrinsic body diode. The following method applies to a typical N-channel enhancement power MOSFET with an accessible gate and source-referenced body diode.

Step 1: Identify the Pins

Find Gate, Drain and Source from the component datasheet. Do not assume the pin order is the same for every package.

Step 2: Discharge the Gate

Briefly connect Gate to Source through a suitable discharge path with the device isolated from the circuit. This places the gate near 0 V relative to the source.

Step 3: Check Drain-Source for a Short

With the gate discharged, use diode-test mode to check the drain-source path in both polarities.

  • A typical N-channel MOSFET body diode conducts with the red probe on Source and black probe on Drain.
  • In the opposite direction, the body diode should block when the gate is discharged, apart from leakage or meter-dependent effects.
  • A near-zero reading in both directions may indicate a drain-source short.

Step 4: Check Gate Insulation

With the device discharged and isolated, check for an unintended low-resistance short between Gate and Source, and between Gate and Drain.

A multimeter cannot conclusively prove that a MOSFET is healthy. Some faults appear only under operating voltage, temperature or load. Use the datasheet and a suitable test circuit for further verification.

6. Simple MOSFET Switching Test

A low-voltage test circuit can demonstrate whether an N-channel enhancement MOSFET responds to a gate signal.

Conceptual low-side switching circuit. Use a correctly selected gate voltage and a series LED current-limiting resistor.

Working

  1. Connect the source to circuit ground.
  2. Connect the drain to the load's low-side terminal.
  3. Connect the load's other terminal to the positive supply.
  4. Apply a suitable gate-to-source voltage within the datasheet limits.
  5. Observe whether the load switches ON and OFF.
The MOSFET must be specified for low RDS(on) at the applied gate voltage. VGS(th) alone does not guarantee that the MOSFET can drive the load properly.

7. Testing a JFET

A JFET can be checked using a multimeter by examining its gate junction and channel resistance. Exact readings depend on device type, polarity, meter test voltage and device characteristics.

Basic Checks for an N-Channel JFET

  1. Identify Gate, Drain and Source using the datasheet.
  2. Check the gate-to-channel PN junction in diode mode.
  3. Check for an unintended short between Gate and Source or Gate and Drain.
  4. Check the Drain-Source path for an open or short condition.
  5. Compare the measurements with a known-good device or datasheet guidance.
Unlike a MOSFET, a JFET has a PN-junction gate. Its gate junction should behave like a diode when forward biased. Do not apply excessive forward gate current during testing.

8. Troubleshooting Common MOSFET Circuit Problems

Problem Possible Cause Corrective Action
Load always ON Drain-source short or gate held HIGH Check MOSFET short and gate-control signal.
Load never turns ON Incorrect pinout, insufficient VGS or open device Verify pinout, gate voltage and device condition.
MOSFET overheats High RDS(on), excessive current or poor cooling Calculate losses and improve device or thermal selection.
Controller resets Supply droop, switching noise or ground disturbance Review decoupling, grounding and switching layout.
Unstable switching Gate ringing, floating gate or poor driver layout Use suitable gate resistance and gate-source pull-down.
Repeated MOSFET failure Voltage spikes, inductive transients or inadequate ratings Check transient suppression, layout and voltage margin.
Excessive switching loss Slow gate transitions or unsuitable driver Review gate charge, driver capability and switching frequency.

9. Troubleshooting with a Digital Multimeter

Measurement Meter Mode Purpose
Gate-source resistance Resistance Check for an unintended low-resistance short.
Gate-drain resistance Resistance Check for gate insulation damage.
Body diode Diode test Check the expected diode conduction direction.
Supply voltage DC voltage Verify circuit supply voltage.
Gate-source voltage DC voltage Verify the gate drive relative to source.
Drain-source voltage DC voltage Check the switching state under controlled operating conditions.

10. Troubleshooting with an Oscilloscope

An oscilloscope is useful for diagnosing switching waveforms, gate-drive problems, ringing and transient voltage spikes.

Signals to Observe

  • Gate-to-source voltage (VGS).
  • Drain-to-source voltage (VDS).
  • Drain current using a suitable current probe or shunt measurement.
  • Switching rise time and fall time.
  • Gate ringing and drain voltage overshoot.
Oscilloscope safety: A conventional bench oscilloscope probe ground is commonly connected to protective earth. Never attach it to a switching node or floating high-side circuit without checking the measurement arrangement. Use an appropriate differential probe or isolated measurement system where required.

12. Practical Lab Exercise – MOSFET Switching Test

Objective

To verify the ON/OFF switching behavior of an N-channel enhancement MOSFET using a low-voltage DC supply.

Required Components

  • N-channel logic-level enhancement MOSFET.
  • 5 V regulated DC supply.
  • LED and suitable series current-limiting resistor.
  • Gate pull-down resistor, for example 10 kΩ.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Connecting wires and breadboard.

Procedure

  1. Identify the MOSFET terminals from its datasheet.
  2. Connect source to ground.
  3. Connect drain to the LED and its series resistor.
  4. Connect the other end of the LED-resistor path to +5 V.
  5. Connect a 10 kΩ pull-down resistor between gate and source.
  6. Apply a suitable HIGH gate voltage relative to source.
  7. Observe the LED and measure VGS and VDS.
  8. Remove the gate signal and confirm that the LED turns OFF.

Expected Result

Gate Condition MOSFET State LED
VGS = 0 V OFF OFF
Suitable positive VGS ON ON
Use a MOSFET whose datasheet specifies low RDS(on) at the selected gate voltage. The gate must never exceed its absolute maximum rating.

13. Important Interview Questions

Q1. How do you test a MOSFET using a multimeter?

Identify its pins, discharge the gate, check the body diode in diode mode and check for unintended shorts between gate and source or drain.

Q2. What indicates a shorted MOSFET?

A low-resistance drain-source path in both directions after accounting for the body diode may indicate a short.

Q3. Why must a MOSFET gate be discharged before testing?

Gate capacitance can retain charge and keep the MOSFET conducting, affecting drain-source measurements.

Q4. What is the purpose of a gate pull-down resistor?

It prevents the gate from floating and helps keep an N-channel low-side MOSFET OFF when the controller output is disconnected or in a high-impedance state.

Q5. What causes MOSFET overheating?

Excessive current, high RDS(on), insufficient gate drive, switching losses, poor thermal contact and inadequate cooling.

Q6. How can you identify a damaged MOSFET gate?

Check for unintended low resistance or leakage between gate and source or gate and drain. Confirm using appropriate datasheet-based tests.

Q7. Why is an oscilloscope useful in MOSFET troubleshooting?

It reveals gate-drive waveforms, switching transitions, ringing, voltage overshoot and timing problems.

Q8. What is ESD damage?

Electrostatic discharge damage occurs when static electricity produces a voltage or current transient that damages sensitive semiconductor structures, including MOSFET gate dielectrics.

15. Lesson Summary

  • FET and MOSFET testing helps identify short circuits, open circuits and abnormal leakage.
  • Always verify device pinout using the datasheet.
  • Discharge MOSFET gate capacitance before testing.
  • Use diode mode to check the intrinsic body diode.
  • Check gate-source and gate-drain insulation for unintended shorts.
  • Use a controlled low-voltage circuit to verify switching behavior.
  • Use an oscilloscope to inspect switching waveforms and transients.
  • Gate pull-down resistors prevent unwanted floating-gate operation.
  • ESD protection and thermal management improve device reliability.
  • Systematic troubleshooting reduces unnecessary component replacement.
