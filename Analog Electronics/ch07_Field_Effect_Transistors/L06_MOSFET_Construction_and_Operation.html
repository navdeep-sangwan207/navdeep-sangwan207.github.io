Lesson 6: MOSFET Characteristics and Operating Regions

Understand output and transfer characteristics, cutoff, ohmic, saturation and breakdown regions of MOSFETs.

1. Introduction to MOSFET Characteristics

MOSFET characteristics describe the relationship between its terminal voltages and drain current. These characteristics help us understand how the MOSFET behaves as a switch or an amplifier.

The two important characteristic curves of an enhancement-mode MOSFET are:

  • Drain or output characteristics: Relationship between drain current ID and drain-to-source voltage VDS for different fixed values of VGS.
  • Transfer characteristics: Relationship between drain current ID and gate-to-source voltage VGS at a specified VDS.
This lesson primarily uses an N-channel enhancement MOSFET. For a P-channel device, the voltage and current polarities are reversed when using the usual source-referenced convention.

2. Operating Regions of an N-Channel Enhancement MOSFET

An enhancement-mode NMOS operates in different regions depending on the gate-to-source voltage and drain-to-source voltage.

A. Cutoff Region

The MOSFET is in the cutoff region when the gate-to-source voltage is below the threshold voltage.

Cutoff condition:

VGS < VTH

For an ideal long-channel enhancement NMOS:

ID ≈ 0

  • No strong inversion channel is formed.
  • Drain current is approximately zero in the ideal model.
  • Only leakage current flows in a practical device.
  • The MOSFET behaves approximately like an open switch.

B. Triode or Ohmic (Linear) Region

When VGS exceeds VTH and VDS is relatively small, a conducting channel forms between the source and drain. The MOSFET behaves approximately like a voltage-controlled resistance.

Triode-region conditions:

VGS > VTH

0 ≤ VDS < VGS − VTH

Drain current equation:

ID = kn [(VGS − VTH)VDS − VDS²/2]

Where:

  • ID = Drain current
  • kn = μnCox(W/L), the process and geometry parameter
  • μn = Electron mobility
  • Cox = Gate-oxide capacitance per unit area
  • W = Channel width
  • L = Channel length
  • VTH = Threshold voltage

At small VDS, the current is approximately proportional to VDS. This is why the region is also called the linear or ohmic region.

C. Saturation Region (Active Region)

As VDS increases, the channel voltage near the drain approaches the threshold condition. The channel becomes pinched off near the drain end, and the drain current becomes approximately independent of VDS in the ideal long-channel model.

Saturation-region condition:

VGS > VTH

VDS ≥ VGS − VTH

Ideal drain current equation:

ID = (kn/2)(VGS − VTH)²

  • The channel is formed and carries drain current.
  • Current is mainly controlled by VGS in the ideal model.
  • The device is commonly operated in this region for amplification.
  • Practical drain current can still increase slightly with VDS because of channel-length modulation.

D. Breakdown Region

If VDS becomes excessively high, the electric field inside the MOSFET can become large enough to cause breakdown.

  • Drain current may rise sharply.
  • Excessive power dissipation can damage the device.
  • Voltage and current ratings must be respected.
  • Safe operating area (SOA) limits must be checked for practical circuits.
Breakdown is not a normal operating region. Do not intentionally operate a MOSFET beyond its rated voltage limits.

3. MOSFET Output Characteristics

Output characteristics show how drain current ID changes with drain-to-source voltage VDS for several fixed gate-to-source voltages VGS.

Higher gate-to-source voltage produces higher drain current. Each curve rises in the linear region and becomes approximately flat in saturation for the ideal long-channel model.

Observations

  1. For VGS below threshold, the drain current is approximately zero in the ideal model.
  2. As VGS increases above threshold, drain current increases.
  3. At low VDS, the MOSFET operates in the linear region.
  4. At sufficiently high VDS, it enters saturation.
  5. In practical devices, the saturation curves may have a slight positive slope.

4. MOSFET Transfer Characteristics

Transfer characteristics show the relationship between drain current ID and gate-to-source voltage VGS while VDS is maintained at a suitable constant value, typically in saturation for the basic transfer curve.

In the ideal square-law model, current begins when VGS exceeds VTH and rises approximately with the square of the overdrive voltage.

Ideal saturation transfer equation:

ID = (kn/2)(VGS − VTH)²

Valid for VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VGS − VTH in the ideal long-channel model.

5. MOSFET Overdrive Voltage

Overdrive voltage is the amount by which the gate-to-source voltage exceeds the threshold voltage.

Overdrive voltage:

VOV = VGS − VTH

For an N-channel enhancement MOSFET operating above threshold, VOV is positive. It is an important parameter in MOSFET current equations and analog circuit design.

6. Numerical Example 1 – Identifying the Operating Region

Given:

  • Threshold voltage VTH = 2 V
  • Gate-to-source voltage VGS = 5 V
  • Drain-to-source voltage VDS = 1 V

Step 1: Check whether the device is above threshold.

VGS = 5 V > VTH = 2 V

The device is ON in the ideal enhancement-mode model.

Step 2: Calculate the overdrive voltage.

VOV = VGS − VTH

VOV = 5 − 2 = 3 V

Step 3: Compare VDS with VOV.

VDS = 1 V

VDS < VOV = 3 V

Operating region: Triode / Linear region.

7. Numerical Example 2 – Calculating Drain Current

Given:

  • kn = 2 mA/V²
  • VTH = 2 V
  • VGS = 5 V
  • VDS = 1 V

Step 1: Identify the region.

VOV = VGS − VTH = 5 − 2 = 3 V

Since VDS = 1 V < 3 V, the device is in the triode region.

Step 2: Apply the triode-region equation.

ID = kn [(VGS − VTH)VDS − VDS²/2]

ID = 2 [(5 − 2)(1) − (1²/2)] mA

ID = 2 [3 − 0.5] mA

ID = 2 × 2.5

ID = 5 mA

8. Numerical Example 3 – Saturation Drain Current

Given:

  • kn = 2 mA/V²
  • VTH = 2 V
  • VGS = 5 V
  • VDS = 4 V

Step 1: Calculate overdrive voltage.

VOV = 5 − 2 = 3 V

Step 2: Check saturation condition.

VDS = 4 V ≥ VOV = 3 V

The MOSFET operates in saturation.

Step 3: Calculate drain current.

ID = (kn/2)(VGS − VTH)²

ID = (2/2)(5 − 2)² mA

ID = 1 × 9

ID = 9 mA

9. Channel-Length Modulation

In an ideal long-channel MOSFET, drain current remains constant in saturation. In a practical MOSFET, increasing VDS can slightly increase the drain current because the effective channel length becomes shorter.

This effect is called channel-length modulation.

Practical saturation current model:

ID ≈ (kn/2)(VGS − VTH)²(1 + λVDS)

Where λ is the channel-length modulation parameter.

  • λ represents the channel-length modulation effect.
  • Higher VDS can produce a slight increase in ID.
  • The effect gives the MOSFET a finite output resistance in saturation.
  • It is important in analog amplifier design.

10. Comparison of MOSFET Operating Regions

Region Condition Behavior Typical Use
Cutoff VGS < VTH OFF, negligible ideal current Switch OFF
Triode / Linear VGS > VTH and VDS < VOV Voltage-controlled resistance Switch ON, resistive operation
Saturation VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VOV Current mainly controlled by VGS Amplification
Breakdown VDS exceeds rated breakdown limit Excessive current Not a normal operating region
Placement Tip: Do not confuse MOSFET saturation with BJT saturation. A MOSFET in saturation is commonly used for amplification, whereas a BJT in saturation is commonly used as a fully ON switch.

11. Important Interview Questions

Q1. What are the main MOSFET operating regions?

Cutoff, triode (linear or ohmic), and saturation. Breakdown is an abnormal region that must be avoided during normal operation.

Q2. What is the condition for MOSFET cutoff?

For an enhancement NMOS, VGS < VTH.

Q3. What is the condition for the triode region?

VGS > VTH and 0 ≤ VDS < VGS − VTH.

Q4. What is the condition for saturation?

VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VGS − VTH.

Q5. Why is the triode region called the ohmic region?

At sufficiently small VDS, drain current is approximately proportional to VDS, so the MOSFET behaves like a voltage-controlled resistance.

Q6. Why is saturation used in MOSFET amplifiers?

In saturation, drain current is primarily controlled by VGS, which enables voltage variations at the gate to control the output current.

Q7. What is overdrive voltage?

VOV = VGS − VTH.

Q8. What is channel-length modulation?

It is the reduction in effective channel length as VDS increases in saturation, causing a slight increase in drain current.

Q9. What is the difference between ideal and practical saturation characteristics?

The ideal curve is flat, while a practical curve may have a slight positive slope because of channel-length modulation and other non-ideal effects.

Q10. Is MOSFET saturation the same as BJT saturation?

No. MOSFET saturation is commonly used for amplification, whereas BJT saturation is commonly associated with its ON switching state.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Output characteristics show ID versus VDS for different fixed VGS values.
  • Transfer characteristics show ID versus VGS at a specified VDS.
  • Cutoff region: the enhancement NMOS is OFF.
  • Triode region: the MOSFET behaves approximately as a voltage-controlled resistance.
  • Saturation region: drain current is mainly controlled by VGS in the ideal model.
  • Overdrive voltage is VOV = VGS − VTH.
  • Channel-length modulation causes practical saturation current to increase slightly with VDS.
  • Always check the MOSFET datasheet for actual operating limits and characteristics.
