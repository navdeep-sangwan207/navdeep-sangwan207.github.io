2. Introduction to MOSFET

MOSFET stands for Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor.

It is a semiconductor device in which the electric field generated by the gate voltage controls the conductivity of a channel between the source and drain.

The MOSFET is widely used in:

  • Digital logic circuits.
  • Microprocessors and microcontrollers.
  • Power electronics.
  • Switching power supplies.
  • Motor drivers.
  • Amplifiers.
  • Embedded systems.
  • CMOS integrated circuits.
Gate (G)Controls the channel through an electric field.
Drain (D)Terminal through which drain current enters an NMOS in typical operation.
Source (S)Terminal through which carriers are supplied in typical NMOS operation.
Body (B)Semiconductor substrate or bulk terminal.

4. MOSFET Construction

Enhancement-mode N-channel MOSFET

A typical enhancement NMOS structure consists of:

  • P-type substrate: Base semiconductor region.
  • N+ source: Heavily doped region for carrier supply.
  • N+ drain: Heavily doped region for carrier collection.
  • Gate oxide: Thin insulating layer, commonly silicon dioxide or an engineered dielectric stack.
  • Gate electrode: Metal or polysilicon electrode used to control the channel.
  • Body terminal: Contact to the substrate.

Animated cross-sectional structure

In an enhancement-mode NMOS, the channel is not normally present as a conducting inversion channel at VGS = 0. A sufficiently positive gate voltage attracts electrons toward the oxide-semiconductor interface. When the gate voltage exceeds the device threshold, an inversion channel forms beneath the gate.

The gate oxide electrically isolates the gate electrode from the semiconductor channel. The gate controls the channel primarily through an electric field, not through continuous DC gate current in ideal steady-state operation.

5. Gate Oxide and Insulated Gate

The MOSFET gate is separated from the semiconductor by an insulating dielectric.

Why the gate is insulated

  • Allows electric-field control of the channel.
  • Provides very high DC input resistance under normal conditions.
  • Reduces steady-state gate current.
  • Creates gate capacitances that must be charged and discharged.
  • Allows efficient control in digital and switching circuits.
Ideal steady-state gate current ≈ 0 A
MOSFETs are sensitive to electrostatic discharge (ESD). Excessive gate voltage can damage the gate dielectric. Always observe the datasheet maximum VGS rating and use appropriate handling precautions.

Gate capacitance

The gate is not an open circuit for transient signals. The gate has capacitance, including gate-to-source, gate-to-drain and gate-to-body capacitance. A switching circuit must supply or remove gate charge to change the device state.

QG = Total gate charge required for a specified switching condition
IG ≈ QG / t

For a simple approximation, the average gate current required to move a gate charge QG in time t is QG/t. Actual driver current varies during switching.

7. Enhancement-Mode NMOS Operation

An enhancement-mode NMOS is normally OFF when VGS = 0 for a typical positive-threshold device.

Step-by-step operation

  1. At VGS = 0, the inversion channel is absent or insufficiently formed.
  2. Applying a positive voltage to the gate creates an electric field.
  3. Electrons are attracted toward the oxide interface.
  4. As VGS increases, the surface reaches inversion.
  5. Above the threshold region, a conductive channel can connect source and drain.
  6. With an appropriate VDS, drain current flows.
  7. Increasing VGS above threshold generally increases channel conductivity.
For a positive-threshold enhancement NMOS: VGS > VTH is required for strong channel formation in the simplified model.
VTH is the threshold voltage under a datasheet-defined test condition. It is not the gate voltage at which a MOSFET is guaranteed to be fully ON. For power switching, RDS(on) must be specified at the intended gate-drive voltage.

PMOS conceptual operation

For an enhancement PMOS, the source is typically at a higher potential than the gate during ON operation. A sufficiently negative gate-source voltage relative to the source creates the conducting channel.

PMOS ON condition (simplified): VGS < VTH

Both VGS and VTH are negative for a typical enhancement PMOS. The magnitude of the gate-source voltage must be sufficient relative to the threshold and datasheet conditions.

9. Depletion-Mode MOSFET

A depletion-mode MOSFET has a conducting channel already present at VGS = 0.

N-channel depletion MOSFET

  • Channel exists at zero gate voltage.
  • At VGS = 0, it can conduct when VDS is applied.
  • A negative gate voltage reduces channel conductivity.
  • At sufficiently negative VGS, the channel can be depleted and current reduced toward cutoff.
  • Positive gate voltage can increase channel conductivity.
Depletion NMOS: Channel exists at VGS = 0

Enhancement versus depletion

11. Body Terminal and Intrinsic Body Diode

Many discrete MOSFETs have a body terminal that is internally connected to the source. In MOSFET integrated circuits, the body may be connected separately depending on the design.

Why is the body important?

  • The body affects the threshold voltage through body bias.
  • Source-body and drain-body junctions form semiconductor junctions.
  • Power MOSFETs commonly contain an intrinsic body diode.
  • The body connection can influence reverse-current paths.
  • Body bias must remain within datasheet limits.

Intrinsic body diode in a typical discrete NMOS

For a typical discrete N-channel power MOSFET with the body internally tied to source, the intrinsic body diode has its anode at source and cathode at drain. This means it can conduct when the source is sufficiently more positive than the drain.

Body-diode orientation and internal connections must be verified from the actual device datasheet. The diagram is conceptual and not a physical package pinout.

13. Worked Numerical Examples

Example 1: Determine the overdrive voltage

A MOSFET has VTH = 2 V and VGS = 5 V.

VOV = VGS − VTH
VOV = 5 − 2 = 3 V

The overdrive voltage is 3 V.

Example 2: Simplified drain-current calculation

Given K = 0.5 mA/V², VTH = 2 V and VGS = 4 V.

ID = K(VGS − VTH)²
ID = 0.5(4 − 2)²
ID = 0.5 × 4 = 2 mA

This is the simplified square-law model result, assuming saturation-region model conditions.

Example 3: Threshold voltage and switching

A logic circuit provides 3.3 V to a MOSFET gate. The device datasheet specifies a threshold range of 1 V to 2.5 V.

Although 3.3 V is above the threshold range, this alone does not prove that the device is suitable for a 3.3 V logic-driven load.

Check the datasheet for RDS(on) specified at VGS = 2.5 V, 3 V or 3.3 V, as applicable. Threshold voltage indicates the beginning of channel formation under a specified test condition, not a guaranteed low-resistance switching state.

14. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Observe the gate-controlled switching operation of an enhancement-mode NMOS.

Equipment

  • Logic-level N-channel MOSFET.
  • 5 V low-voltage supply.
  • LED.
  • Current-limiting resistor.
  • Gate resistor, optional.
  • Gate pull-down resistor.
  • Breadboard.
  • Digital multimeter.

Low-voltage circuit

Construct a low-side switch:

  • Connect the LED and its series current-limiting resistor between +5 V and the MOSFET drain.
  • Connect the MOSFET source to ground.
  • Connect the gate to a control voltage through an optional gate resistor.
  • Use a gate-to-source pull-down resistor to keep the MOSFET OFF when the control signal is disconnected.

Procedure

  1. Verify the MOSFET pinout from the datasheet.
  2. Verify that the selected MOSFET is suitable for the control voltage.
  3. Build the low-side switching circuit.
  4. Apply 0 V to the gate relative to the source.
  5. Observe that the LED is OFF.
  6. Apply a suitable gate voltage within the datasheet limit.
  7. Observe the LED switching ON.
  8. Measure VGS and VDS.
  9. Measure or calculate the load current.
  10. Remove the gate voltage and observe switching OFF.

16. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is MOSFET?

A voltage-controlled field-effect transistor whose gate electric field controls the conductivity of a channel between source and drain.

2. What does MOSFET stand for?

Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor.

3. What are the four MOSFET terminals?

Gate, drain, source and body.

4. Why is the MOSFET gate insulated?

To allow electric-field channel control while minimizing steady-state gate current.

5. What is threshold voltage?

The specified gate voltage associated with a defined onset of channel conduction under a particular test condition.

6. Does VTH indicate fully ON operation?

No. It does not guarantee low RDS(on). A suitable gate-drive voltage must be checked in the datasheet.

7. How does an enhancement NMOS turn ON?

A sufficiently positive VGS creates an inversion channel and allows current under appropriate drain-source conditions.

8. What is the gate oxide?

An insulating dielectric separating the gate electrode from the semiconductor.

9. Why is MOSFET input impedance high?

The gate is insulated from the channel, so DC gate current is very small under normal conditions.

10. Why does a MOSFET still require gate current during switching?

Its gate capacitances and gate charge must be charged or discharged to change the gate voltage.

11. What is an enhancement MOSFET?

A MOSFET in which a conducting inversion channel is induced by suitable gate voltage.

12. What is a depletion MOSFET?

A MOSFET that has a channel present at zero gate bias and whose channel conductivity can be reduced by gate bias.

13. What is the body terminal?

The substrate or bulk terminal of the MOSFET.

14. What is the body diode?

An intrinsic junction diode associated with the MOSFET's body and source/drain structure.

15. What is the difference between NMOS and PMOS?

They differ in channel carrier type, substrate arrangement, gate-control polarity and typical circuit use.

16. What is gate charge?

The charge required to change the gate voltage and device state under specified switching conditions.

17. What is a logic-level MOSFET?

A MOSFET designed with switching performance specified at relatively low gate-drive voltages suitable for logic-level applications.

18. What is ESD?

Electrostatic discharge, which can damage sensitive MOSFET gate structures.

19. Why is a pull-down resistor used?

It keeps an NMOS gate near source potential when the control signal is inactive or disconnected, helping prevent unintended switching.

20. Where are MOSFETs used?

Switching power supplies, motor drivers, amplifiers, CMOS logic, microprocessors, embedded systems and many other circuits.

18. Lecture Summary

  • MOSFET stands for Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor.
  • Its principal terminals are gate, drain, source and body.
  • The gate is insulated from the semiconductor by a dielectric.
  • Enhancement NMOS channel formation requires suitable positive gate bias.
  • Enhancement PMOS channel formation requires suitable negative gate bias relative to the source.
  • Depletion MOSFETs have a channel at zero gate voltage.
  • Gate voltage controls channel conductivity through an electric field.
  • MOSFETs have high DC gate input resistance, but gate charge must be managed during switching.
  • Power MOSFETs commonly contain an intrinsic body diode.
  • Threshold voltage is not the same as the gate voltage required for fully ON switching.
  • Datasheet limits and ESD precautions are essential.
