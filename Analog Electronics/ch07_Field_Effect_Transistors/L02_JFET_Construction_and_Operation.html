Lesson 2: JFET Construction, Symbols and Working

Understand JFET structure, channel formation, gate junctions and current control.

1. Introduction to JFET

A Junction Field Effect Transistor (JFET) is a three-terminal, voltage-controlled semiconductor device. It controls the current flowing through a semiconductor channel using an electric field produced by a reverse-biased gate-channel PN junction.

The three main terminals are Gate (G), Drain (D) and Source (S). The conducting channel is made from either N-type or P-type semiconductor material.

Important concept: A JFET is normally operated with its gate-channel PN junction reverse biased. Changing the gate-to-source voltage changes the depletion-region width and therefore controls the conducting channel.

2. Construction of N-Channel JFET

An N-channel JFET consists of a narrow bar of N-type semiconductor material that forms the conducting channel. Two heavily doped P-type regions are formed on opposite sides of the channel. These P-type regions are connected together to form the Gate terminal.

Ohmic contacts are connected at the two ends of the N-type channel. These form the Source and Drain terminals.

Simplified N-channel JFET construction. The two P-type gate regions form PN junctions with the N-type channel.

Construction Details

  • Channel: N-type semiconductor material.
  • Gate: Two P-type regions connected together.
  • Drain: Ohmic contact at one end of the channel.
  • Source: Ohmic contact at the opposite end.
  • Depletion region: Forms around the reverse-biased gate-channel PN junctions.
In an N-channel JFET, electrons are the majority carriers responsible for channel conduction.

3. Construction of P-Channel JFET

A P-channel JFET has a P-type semiconductor bar that forms the conducting channel. Two N-type regions are formed on opposite sides of the channel and connected together to form the Gate terminal.

Simplified P-channel JFET construction. N-type gate regions form PN junctions with the P-type channel.

Construction Details

  • Channel: P-type semiconductor material.
  • Gate: Two N-type regions connected together.
  • Drain and Source: Ohmic contacts at opposite ends.
  • Depletion region: Forms around the reverse-biased gate-channel junctions.
In a P-channel JFET, holes are the majority carriers responsible for channel conduction.

4. JFET Circuit Symbols

JFET circuit symbols represent the channel, Gate, Drain and Source terminals. The direction of the gate arrow distinguishes N-channel and P-channel JFETs.

Simplified JFET symbols. The arrow points into the channel for an N-channel JFET and out of the channel for a P-channel JFET.

Symbol rule: Remember: N-channel JFET arrow points inward; P-channel JFET arrow points outward. The arrow represents the direction from P-type material to N-type material.

5. Working of N-Channel JFET

To understand N-channel JFET operation, consider the Source terminal as the voltage reference. The Drain is made positive with respect to Source, and the Gate is maintained at a voltage that reverse biases the gate-channel PN junction.

Case 1: Gate-to-Source Voltage is Zero

When \(V_{GS}=0\), the gate and source are at the same potential. If a positive drain-to-source voltage is applied, electrons move through the N-type channel from Source toward Drain.

Conventional drain current \(I_D\) flows from Drain toward Source. The reverse-biased gate-channel junction creates depletion regions that narrow the conducting channel.

Case 2: Negative Gate-to-Source Voltage

When a negative gate-to-source voltage is applied to an N-channel JFET, the reverse bias across the gate-channel junction increases.

  • The depletion region becomes wider.
  • The effective conducting channel becomes narrower.
  • The channel resistance increases.
  • The drain current decreases for a given drain voltage.

Case 3: Increasing Drain-to-Source Voltage

With a fixed negative \(V_{GS}\), increasing \(V_{DS}\) changes the reverse bias along the channel. The depletion region becomes wider near the drain end, where the channel potential is higher.

At a sufficiently high drain voltage, the channel becomes strongly constricted near the drain. This condition is associated with pinch-off and the onset of the saturation region of the drain characteristics.

Pinch-off does not mean that the drain current suddenly becomes zero. In the normal saturation region, the drain current remains approximately constant for a fixed gate-to-source voltage.

6. Working of P-Channel JFET

A P-channel JFET works on the same field-effect principle, but the semiconductor types and voltage polarities are reversed compared with an N-channel JFET.

  • The channel is made of P-type semiconductor material.
  • Holes are the majority carriers.
  • The Gate consists of N-type regions.
  • The gate-channel junction is reverse biased during normal operation.
  • Corresponding drain and gate voltage polarities are opposite to those of an N-channel JFET.

7. Depletion Region and Channel Control

A depletion region is formed around the PN junction between the Gate and channel. It contains very few mobile charge carriers compared with the conducting channel.

Applying reverse bias increases the depletion-region width. Since the depletion region extends into the channel, it reduces the available conducting cross-section.

8. Important JFET Terminology

8.1 Drain Current (\(I_D\))

The conventional current flowing through the channel between the Drain and Source terminals.

8.2 Gate-to-Source Voltage (\(V_{GS}\))

The voltage difference between the Gate and Source terminals. It controls the depletion-region width in a JFET.

\(V_{GS}=V_G-V_S\)

8.3 Drain-to-Source Voltage (\(V_{DS}\))

The voltage difference between the Drain and Source terminals.

\(V_{DS}=V_D-V_S\)

8.4 Gate Current (\(I_G\))

Since the gate-channel PN junction is reverse biased in normal operation, the gate current is very small and is mainly due to reverse leakage.

\(I_G \approx 0\)

This is an ideal approximation. A real JFET has a small reverse leakage current.

8.5 Pinch-Off

Pinch-off is the condition in which the channel becomes sufficiently constricted near the drain end that the drain current enters its approximate saturation region.

Pinch-off voltage terminology can vary between textbooks and datasheets. The drain-to-source voltage at the onset of saturation and the gate cutoff voltage are related but are not interchangeable quantities.

9. Practical Applications of JFET

  • High-Impedance Amplifiers: Used in input stages where loading must be minimized.
  • Buffer Circuits: Used for impedance matching between circuit stages.
  • Low-Noise Circuits: Selected for suitable low-noise amplifier designs.
  • Analog Switching: Used in certain signal switching and control applications.
  • Constant-Current Circuits: Used in simple current-regulating circuits under appropriate operating conditions.
  • Sensor Interfaces: Useful in circuits connected to high-impedance sensing elements.

10. Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What is a JFET?

    A JFET is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device that uses a reverse-biased gate-channel PN junction to control current through a channel.

  2. What are the terminals of a JFET?

    Gate, Drain and Source.

  3. What is the channel material in an N-channel JFET?

    N-type semiconductor material.

  4. What are the majority carriers in a P-channel JFET?

    Holes.

  5. Why is the gate-channel junction reverse biased?

    Reverse bias expands the depletion region and allows the gate voltage to control channel width without significant gate current.

  6. What happens when the negative gate voltage of an N-channel JFET increases in magnitude?

    The depletion region widens, the channel narrows and the drain current decreases for a given drain-to-source voltage.

  7. What is pinch-off in a JFET?

    It is the condition associated with channel constriction near the drain and the onset of drain-current saturation.

  8. What is the gate current in a JFET?

    It is very small in normal reverse-biased operation and consists mainly of reverse leakage current.

  9. How can N-channel and P-channel JFET symbols be distinguished?

    The gate arrow points into the channel for an N-channel JFET and out of the channel for a P-channel JFET.

  10. Does pinch-off mean that the JFET turns off?

    No. Pinch-off normally marks the onset of the saturation region, where drain current continues to flow.

12. Lesson Summary

  • JFET stands for Junction Field Effect Transistor.
  • It has three terminals: Gate, Drain and Source.
  • An N-channel JFET has an N-type channel and P-type gate regions.
  • A P-channel JFET has a P-type channel and N-type gate regions.
  • JFET operation depends on the electric field produced by reverse gate bias.
  • Increasing reverse gate bias widens the depletion region.
  • A wider depletion region narrows the conducting channel.
  • Pinch-off is associated with the onset of drain-current saturation.
  • JFETs are used in amplifiers, buffers and analog circuits.
