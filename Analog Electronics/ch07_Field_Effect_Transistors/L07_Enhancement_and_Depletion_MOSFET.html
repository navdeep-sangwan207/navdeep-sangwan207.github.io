2. MOSFET Mode Classification

MOSFETs are classified according to the channel behavior at zero gate-source voltage and the way gate voltage controls channel conductivity.

ModeChannel at VGS = 0Typical behavior
EnhancementNo strong inversion channelGate voltage induces channel formation.
DepletionConducting channel existsGate voltage can reduce or increase channel conductivity.

3. Enhancement-Mode MOSFET

An enhancement-mode MOSFET is a device in which the channel is induced by applying a suitable gate-source voltage.

Enhancement NMOS

  • Typical device has a positive threshold voltage.
  • At VGS = 0, the device is OFF in the simplified enhancement-channel model.
  • Positive gate voltage attracts electrons toward the oxide-semiconductor interface.
  • When VGS exceeds the threshold under the device's specified conditions, an inversion channel forms.
  • Channel conductivity increases with appropriate gate voltage.
Enhancement NMOS: VGS > VTH → channel induced

Enhancement PMOS

  • Typical device has a negative threshold voltage.
  • At VGS = 0, the device is OFF in the simplified enhancement-channel model.
  • A sufficiently negative gate voltage relative to the source induces the conducting channel.
  • Hole conduction is associated with the P-channel.
Enhancement PMOS: VGS < VTH → channel induced
The equations above are simplified channel-formation conditions. Threshold voltage is a specified parameter, not a guarantee of low ON resistance.

5. Depletion-Mode MOSFET

A depletion-mode MOSFET has a conducting channel already present at zero gate voltage.

N-channel depletion MOSFET

  • An N-type channel is physically present between source and drain.
  • At VGS = 0, the channel can conduct.
  • A negative gate voltage repels electrons from the channel region and reduces its conductivity.
  • At sufficiently negative VGS, the channel can be depleted toward cutoff.
  • A positive gate voltage generally enhances channel conductivity.
At VGS = 0: channel already exists

P-channel depletion MOSFET

  • A P-type conducting channel exists at zero gate bias.
  • A positive gate voltage relative to the source reduces channel conductivity.
  • A negative gate voltage generally enhances P-channel conductivity.

10. Transfer Characteristics

Enhancement NMOS

For a simplified square-law saturation model:

ID = K(VGS − VTH)², VGS > VTH

Below the threshold region, the simplified model predicts negligible channel current.

Depletion NMOS

A depletion NMOS may conduct at VGS = 0. A negative gate voltage reduces the current and may move the device toward cutoff.

The actual depletion MOSFET transfer characteristic depends on its device parameters. Do not assume the JFET Shockley equation applies to every depletion MOSFET.

11. Worked Numerical Problems

Example 1: Enhancement NMOS channel condition

A MOSFET has VTH = 2 V and VGS = 3.5 V.

VOV = VGS − VTH
VOV = 3.5 − 2 = 1.5 V

The gate voltage is above the threshold under the simplified model, and the overdrive is 1.5 V.

Example 2: Simplified enhancement NMOS current

Given K = 0.4 mA/V², VTH = 2 V and VGS = 5 V:

ID = K(VGS − VTH)²
ID = 0.4(5 − 2)²
ID = 0.4 × 9 = 3.6 mA

Example 3: Depletion NMOS qualitative operation

A depletion MOSFET has an existing channel at VGS = 0. What happens when VGS becomes more negative?

For a typical N-channel depletion device, the negative gate voltage reduces electron concentration in the channel, reducing conductivity and drain current under otherwise comparable conditions.

Example 4: Selecting an enhancement MOSFET for a logic circuit

A microcontroller operates at 3.3 V and needs to switch a DC load.

  • Identify a MOSFET with RDS(on) specified at the actual available gate-drive voltage.
  • Check VDS rating.
  • Check continuous and pulsed current ratings.
  • Check gate charge if switching frequency matters.
  • Check power dissipation and thermal resistance.
  • Check the body diode and load type.
Do not select the device solely because VGS(th) is below 3.3 V.

12. Applications

Digital logicEnhancement MOSFETs are used extensively in CMOS logic and digital switching.
Power switchingEnhancement power MOSFETs are used in switching supplies, motor drivers and load switches.
AmplifiersMOSFETs are used in common-source, source-follower and other amplifier configurations.
Biasing circuitsDepletion MOSFETs can be used in specialized biasing and current-control applications.
Embedded systemsMOSFETs interface microcontrollers with loads such as LEDs, relays and small DC motors.
CMOSComplementary NMOS and PMOS devices are used to build low-static-power logic circuits.

13. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Compare the switching behavior of an enhancement-mode NMOS with its gate voltage.

Equipment

  • Logic-level NMOS.
  • 5 V current-limited DC supply.
  • LED and current-limiting resistor.
  • Gate pull-down resistor.
  • Breadboard and multimeter.

Procedure

  1. Verify the MOSFET pinout using its datasheet.
  2. Connect the source to ground.
  3. Connect the LED and series resistor from the positive supply to the drain.
  4. Connect the gate to the control source through an optional gate resistor.
  5. Connect a pull-down resistor between gate and source.
  6. Apply 0 V to the gate and observe the LED.
  7. Apply a suitable gate voltage within the datasheet limit.
  8. Observe the LED switching state.
  9. Measure VGS and VDS.
  10. Record the results.

15. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is an enhancement MOSFET?

A MOSFET in which the conducting inversion channel is induced by applying suitable gate voltage.

2. What is a depletion MOSFET?

A MOSFET that has a channel at zero gate bias and whose conductivity can be reduced or increased through gate control.

3. What is the difference between enhancement and depletion MOSFETs?

Enhancement MOSFETs require gate-induced channel formation; depletion MOSFETs have an existing channel at zero gate voltage.

4. Why is enhancement NMOS normally OFF?

For a typical positive-threshold device, there is no strong inversion channel at VGS = 0.

5. Why does a depletion NMOS conduct at zero gate voltage?

It has a conducting channel already present in its structure.

6. What happens when negative VGS is applied to depletion NMOS?

Channel electron concentration is reduced and the device moves toward cutoff.

7. What happens when positive VGS is applied to depletion NMOS?

Channel conductivity generally increases under comparable conditions.

8. How does enhancement PMOS turn ON?

A sufficiently negative VGS relative to its source induces the P-channel.

9. Why are enhancement MOSFETs common in switching circuits?

They can be controlled using a gate signal and are widely available with suitable low-resistance switching characteristics.

10. What is VTH?

The threshold voltage associated with a specified test condition for the onset of channel conduction.

11. Is threshold voltage the same as fully ON voltage?

No. It does not guarantee the specified low RDS(on).

12. What is the body diode?

An intrinsic diode associated with the body and source/drain junctions in many MOSFET structures.

13. Why does the gate have high input resistance?

The gate is separated from the semiconductor by an insulating dielectric.

14. What is gate charge?

The charge that must be transferred to change the gate voltage and switching state under specified conditions.

15. What is the difference between NMOS and PMOS?

They differ in channel carrier type, substrate arrangement and the gate-voltage polarity used to induce or control the channel.

16. Where are depletion MOSFETs used?

They are used in specialized biasing, analog and other applications where their normally conducting channel characteristics are useful.

17. Where are enhancement MOSFETs used?

Digital logic, switching power supplies, load switching, motor drivers and amplifier circuits.

18. What is a logic-level MOSFET?

A MOSFET whose switching performance is specified at relatively low gate voltages appropriate for logic-drive applications.

19. What is ESD?

Electrostatic discharge, which can damage MOSFET gate structures.

20. What is the difference between MOSFET mode and operating region?

Enhancement or depletion describes the channel behavior at zero gate bias and its control characteristics. Linear, saturation and cutoff refer to operating conditions defined by the electrical bias and device model.

17. Lecture Summary

  • Enhancement MOSFETs require a gate-induced channel.
  • Depletion MOSFETs have a channel at zero gate bias.
  • Enhancement NMOS devices are typically normally OFF.
  • Depletion NMOS devices are typically normally conducting.
  • Positive gate voltage generally enhances N-channel conductivity.
  • Negative gate voltage generally reduces N-channel depletion-device conductivity.
  • PMOS gate control uses opposite polarity relative to the source.
  • Threshold voltage does not guarantee low RDS(on).
  • Enhancement MOSFETs are widely used in switching, logic and amplification.
  • Depletion MOSFETs are useful in specialized analog and biasing applications.
