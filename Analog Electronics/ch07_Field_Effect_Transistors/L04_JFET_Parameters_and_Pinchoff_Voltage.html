Lesson 4: JFET Parameters and Shockley Equation

Understand JFET electrical parameters, transconductance, drain resistance and numerical calculations.

1. Introduction to JFET Parameters

JFET parameters describe the electrical behaviour of a Junction Field Effect Transistor. They are used to analyse its operating point, amplification capability and response to changes in gate-to-source voltage and drain-to-source voltage.

The important JFET parameters are:

  • Drain current at zero gate bias (\(I_{DSS}\))
  • Gate-to-source cutoff voltage (\(V_{GS(off)}\))
  • Transconductance (\(g_m\))
  • Drain resistance (\(r_d\))
  • Amplification factor (\(\mu\))
Important: These parameters are particularly useful when selecting a JFET, designing amplifier circuits and calculating small-signal voltage gain.

2. Drain Current at Zero Gate Bias (IDSS)

\(I_{DSS}\) is the drain current of a JFET when the gate-to-source voltage is zero and the device is operating in its saturation region.

At zero gate bias:

\(V_{GS}=0\)

\(I_D=I_{DSS}\)

Important Points

  • It represents the maximum normal saturation-region drain current for the specified device and conditions.
  • It is an important parameter for JFET biasing calculations.
  • Its value varies from device to device.
  • It is specified in the transistor datasheet under stated test conditions.
\(I_{DSS}\) is not the maximum current rating of the transistor. It is a characteristic drain-current parameter.

3. Gate-to-Source Cutoff Voltage (VGS(off))

Gate-to-source cutoff voltage is the value of gate-to-source voltage at which the JFET channel is effectively cut off and the drain current becomes very small.

For an N-channel JFET, this voltage is normally negative. For a P-channel JFET, the corresponding voltage is normally positive under the usual source-referenced convention.

Cutoff condition:

\(I_D \approx 0\)

\(V_{GS}=V_{GS(off)}\)

Example

Suppose an N-channel JFET has:
  • \(V_{GS(off)}=-5V\)
  • \(I_{DSS}=10mA\)
At approximately \(V_{GS}=-5V\), the drain current approaches zero under the idealized model.

4. Transconductance (gm)

Transconductance measures how effectively a change in gate-to-source voltage controls the drain current.

It is one of the most important parameters used in small-signal JFET amplifier analysis.

Transconductance:

\(g_m=\frac{\Delta I_D}{\Delta V_{GS}}\)

Small-signal definition: \(g_m=\left.\frac{\partial I_D}{\partial V_{GS}}\right|_{V_{DS}}\)

Unit of Transconductance

The SI unit is Siemens (S). In transistor circuits, transconductance is commonly expressed in millisiemens (mS).

\(1S=1A/V\)

\(1mS=1mA/V\)

Maximum Transconductance (gm0)

For the ideal Shockley model, the maximum transconductance occurs at zero gate-to-source voltage.

\(g_{m0}=\frac{2I_{DSS}}{|V_{GS(off)}|}\)

Here, \(g_{m0}\) is the transconductance at \(V_{GS}=0\).

5. Drain Resistance (rd)

Drain resistance describes the change in drain-to-source voltage for a change in drain current while the gate-to-source voltage is held constant.

Drain Resistance:

\(r_d=\left.\frac{\Delta V_{DS}}{\Delta I_D}\right|_{V_{GS}}\)

Small-signal definition: \(r_d=\left.\frac{\partial V_{DS}}{\partial I_D}\right|_{V_{GS}}\)

Unit

The unit of drain resistance is Ohm (\(\Omega\)).

Important Points

  • It is a small-signal parameter around a specified operating point.
  • It is obtained from the slope of the drain characteristic in the saturation region.
  • It is not necessarily equal to the large-signal ratio \(V_{DS}/I_D\).

6. Amplification Factor (μ)

The amplification factor represents the ratio of the change in drain-to-source voltage to the corresponding change in gate-to-source voltage required to maintain constant drain current.

Amplification Factor:

\(\mu=-\left.\frac{\Delta V_{DS}}{\Delta V_{GS}}\right|_{I_D}\)

Magnitude: \(|\mu|=g_m r_d\)

In standard JFET small-signal analysis, the magnitude of the amplification factor is commonly expressed as:

\(\mu=g_m r_d\)
The negative sign in the derivative definition follows the usual device convention. Textbooks often quote the positive magnitude of the amplification factor.

7. Relationship Between JFET Parameters

Transconductance, drain resistance and amplification factor are related to one another.

Relationship between the three small-signal JFET parameters.

Important Relationship

\(\mu=g_m\times r_d\)

\(g_m=\frac{\mu}{r_d}\)

\(r_d=\frac{\mu}{g_m}\)

This relationship is useful when one parameter is unknown and the other two are available.

8. Shockley Equation

The Shockley equation is used to calculate the drain current of a JFET in its saturation region.

Shockley Equation:

\(I_D=I_{DSS} \left(1-\frac{V_{GS}}{V_{GS(off)}}\right)^2\)

Where:

  • \(I_D\) = Drain current.
  • \(I_{DSS}\) = Drain current at zero gate bias in saturation.
  • \(V_{GS}\) = Applied gate-to-source voltage.
  • \(V_{GS(off)}\) = Gate-to-source cutoff voltage.

Special Cases

Condition Result
\(V_{GS}=0\) \(I_D=I_{DSS}\)
\(V_{GS}=V_{GS(off)}\) \(I_D\approx0\)
Intermediate gate voltage \(0<I_D<I_{DSS}\) for the normal N-channel operating range
The Shockley equation is an idealized model. It is intended for the normal saturation region, not the ohmic region, breakdown region or arbitrary transient conditions.

9. Numerical Example 1 – Drain Current

Problem:

An N-channel JFET has \(I_{DSS}=16mA\) and \(V_{GS(off)}=-8V\). Calculate the drain current when \(V_{GS}=-4V\).

Solution

Using the Shockley equation:

\(I_D=16 \left(1-\frac{-4}{-8}\right)^2 mA\)

\(I_D=16(1-0.5)^2mA\)

\(I_D=16(0.5)^2mA\)

\(I_D=16\times0.25mA\)

Answer: \(I_D=4mA\)

10. Numerical Example 2 – Maximum Transconductance

Problem:

A JFET has \(I_{DSS}=12mA\) and \(V_{GS(off)}=-6V\). Calculate maximum transconductance.

Solution

Formula:

\(g_{m0}=\frac{2I_{DSS}}{|V_{GS(off)}|}\)

Substitute the values:

\(g_{m0}=\frac{2\times12mA}{6V}\)

\(g_{m0}=4mA/V\)

Answer: \(g_{m0}=4mS\)

11. Numerical Example 3 – Transconductance at an Operating Point

Problem:

A JFET has \(g_{m0}=5mS\) and \(V_{GS(off)}=-5V\). Calculate transconductance at \(V_{GS}=-2V\) using the ideal Shockley model.

Solution

Formula:

\(g_m=g_{m0} \left(1-\frac{V_{GS}}{V_{GS(off)}}\right)\)

Substitute the values:

\(g_m=5 \left(1-\frac{-2}{-5}\right)mS\)

\(g_m=5(1-0.4)mS\)

\(g_m=5\times0.6mS\)

Answer: \(g_m=3mS\)

12. Numerical Example 4 – Amplification Factor

Problem:

A JFET has transconductance \(g_m=2.5mS\) and drain resistance \(r_d=40k\Omega\). Calculate its amplification factor.

Solution

Formula:

\(\mu=g_m r_d\)

Convert transconductance:

\(g_m=0.0025S\)

Substitute:

\(\mu=0.0025\times40000\)

Answer: \(\mu=100\)

13. JFET Parameter Comparison

Parameter Symbol Unit Meaning
Zero-gate drain current \(I_{DSS}\) A Drain current at zero gate bias in saturation
Cutoff voltage \(V_{GS(off)}\) V Gate voltage at effective channel cutoff
Transconductance \(g_m\) S Change in drain current per change in gate voltage
Drain resistance \(r_d\) Ω Small-signal drain voltage/current ratio at fixed gate voltage
Amplification factor \(\mu\) Dimensionless Product of transconductance and drain resistance

14. Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What is \(I_{DSS}\)?

    It is the drain current at zero gate-to-source voltage when the JFET is operating in saturation.

  2. What is \(V_{GS(off)}\)?

    It is the gate-to-source voltage at which the JFET channel is effectively cut off.

  3. Define transconductance.

    It is the change in drain current per unit change in gate-to-source voltage at fixed drain voltage.

  4. What is the unit of transconductance?

    Siemens (S), commonly millisiemens (mS).

  5. What is maximum transconductance?

    It is the transconductance at zero gate-to-source voltage in the ideal Shockley model.

  6. Define drain resistance.

    It is the small-signal change in drain-to-source voltage divided by the corresponding change in drain current at constant gate-to-source voltage.

  7. What is the amplification factor?

    It is the magnitude of the ratio of drain-voltage change to gate-voltage change at constant drain current.

  8. State the relationship between JFET parameters.

    \(\mu=g_mr_d\)

  9. Where is the Shockley equation applicable?

    It is used to estimate drain current in the saturation region under the ideal JFET model.

  10. Does \(I_{DSS}\) represent the maximum rated drain current?

    No. It is the zero-gate-bias saturation current, not the absolute maximum current rating.

16. Lesson Summary

  • \(I_{DSS}\) is the drain current at zero gate bias in saturation.
  • \(V_{GS(off)}\) is the gate-to-source cutoff voltage.
  • Transconductance measures gate-voltage control of drain current.
  • Maximum transconductance is \(g_{m0}=2I_{DSS}/|V_{GS(off)}|\) in the ideal model.
  • Drain resistance is the small-signal output resistance at fixed gate voltage.
  • Amplification factor is related to transconductance and drain resistance.
  • The relationship is \(\mu=g_mr_d\).
  • The Shockley equation calculates drain current in the saturation region.
