3. IDSS – Drain Saturation Current at Zero Gate Bias

IDSS is the drain current measured when:

VGS = 0 V

and the JFET is operating in its drain-current saturation region.

For an ideal N-channel JFET, the transfer characteristic starts at:

VGS = 0 → ID = IDSS

IDSS is an important parameter for determining the operating current of a JFET and for designing self-bias circuits.

Example

If a JFET has IDSS = 8 mA, then under the ideal saturation model at VGS = 0:

ID = 8 mA
IDSS is device-dependent. Two JFETs of the same part number may have different IDSS values within their datasheet specification limits.

4. VGS(off) – Gate-Source Cutoff Voltage

VGS(off) is the gate-source voltage at which the JFET channel is effectively cut off.

For an N-channel JFET, this voltage is normally negative relative to the source.

At VGS = VGS(off) → ID ≈ 0

For a P-channel JFET, the corresponding cutoff polarity is reversed.

Example

An N-channel JFET has VGS(off) = −4 V.

  • At VGS = 0 V, the device can conduct its IDSS in saturation.
  • At VGS = −1 V, drain current is reduced.
  • At VGS = −2 V, drain current is further reduced.
  • At VGS = −4 V, the ideal model predicts cutoff.

5. Pinch-off Voltage vs Cutoff Voltage

The terminology used for JFET pinch-off voltage varies between textbooks and datasheets. It is important to distinguish two related concepts.

Drain pinch-offAs VDS increases, depletion regions constrict the channel near the drain. This marks the onset of approximate drain-current saturation.
Gate cutoffA sufficiently large reverse gate-source voltage effectively closes the channel and reduces drain current toward zero.

For an ideal N-channel JFET, the approximate saturation-onset voltage is:

VDS(sat) ≈ VGS − VGS(off)

Here, VGS(off) is signed and negative.

Worked example

Given:

  • VGS = −2 V
  • VGS(off) = −6 V
VDS(sat) ≈ −2 − (−6) = 4 V

The ideal model predicts that saturation begins approximately at VDS = 4 V.

6. Shockley Drain-Current Equation

For an N-channel JFET operating in the ideal saturation region:

ID = IDSS × (1 − VGS / VGS(off))²

Where:

  • ID is the drain current.
  • IDSS is the saturation drain current at VGS = 0.
  • VGS is the gate-source voltage.
  • VGS(off) is the signed negative cutoff voltage.

Example

Given:

  • IDSS = 10 mA
  • VGS(off) = −5 V
  • VGS = −2 V
ID = 10 × (1 − (−2 / −5))²
ID = 10 × (1 − 0.4)²
ID = 10 × 0.6²
ID = 3.6 mA

7. Transconductance (gm)

Transconductance measures how much drain current changes when gate-source voltage changes, with drain-source voltage held constant in the appropriate operating region.

gm = ΔID / ΔVGS

For small-signal analysis, the incremental transconductance is:

gm = dID / dVGS

Its unit is the Siemens (S). JFET transconductance is commonly expressed in mS.

Interpretation

A higher gm means a small change in VGS can produce a larger change in drain current at the specified operating point.

8. Maximum Transconductance (gm0)

For the ideal Shockley model, the maximum transconductance occurs at VGS = 0 V.

gm0 = 2IDSS / |VGS(off)|

This formula assumes IDSS is expressed in amperes and VGS(off) in volts, giving gm0 in Siemens.

Worked example

Given:

  • IDSS = 12 mA
  • VGS(off) = −6 V
gm0 = 2 × 12 mA / 6 V
gm0 = 4 mS

9. Transconductance at an Operating Point

For the ideal Shockley model, transconductance changes with gate-source voltage:

gm = gm0 × (1 − VGS / VGS(off))

This expression applies within the modeled conducting range of the N-channel JFET.

Example

Given gm0 = 4 mS, VGS = −2 V and VGS(off) = −6 V:

gm = 4 × (1 − (−2 / −6))
gm = 4 × (1 − 1/3)
gm = 4 × 2/3
gm = 2.67 mS

10. Alternative Transconductance Equation

From the Shockley model, transconductance can also be calculated from the drain current:

gm = 2√(IDSS × ID) / |VGS(off)|

This is useful when ID is known instead of VGS.

Worked example

Given IDSS = 16 mA, ID = 4 mA and VGS(off) = −8 V:

gm = 2√(16 × 4) / 8
gm = 2 × 8 / 8
gm = 2 mS

In this calculation, current values are entered in mA and the resulting transconductance is in mS.

11. Drain Resistance (rd)

Drain resistance is the small-signal resistance measured between drain and source when VGS is held constant and the JFET operates in its saturation region.

rd = ΔVDS / ΔID

For incremental analysis:

rd = dVDS / dID at constant VGS

In the ideal saturation model, rd is very large. Real devices have finite drain resistance due to nonideal channel behaviour and other effects.

Worked example

At constant VGS, drain voltage changes by 2 V and drain current changes by 0.2 mA.

rd = 2 V / 0.2 mA = 10 kΩ

12. Amplification Factor (μ)

The JFET amplification factor relates transconductance and drain resistance:

μ = gm × rd

μ is dimensionless because Siemens multiplied by Ohms equals one.

Worked example

Given:

  • gm = 2 mS
  • rd = 10 kΩ
μ = 0.002 × 10,000
μ = 20

13. Relationship Between JFET Parameters

μ = gm × rd
gmDrain-current sensitivity to gate-source voltage.
rdDrain-voltage sensitivity to drain current at constant VGS.
μCombined voltage-amplification parameter of the small-signal device.

These parameters are small-signal quantities and depend on the operating point. They are not universal constants for a particular JFET.

16. Datasheet Parameter Interpretation

When selecting a JFET from a datasheet, examine:

ParameterWhy it matters
IDSSEstablishes the zero-gate-bias current and current range.
VGS(off)Determines the gate-voltage range required to reduce current.
VDS(max)Defines the maximum drain-source voltage rating.
VGS(max)Defines allowable gate-source voltage limits.
PD(max)Determines the allowable power dissipation under specified thermal conditions.
gmIndicates gate-voltage sensitivity of drain current.
Noise parametersImportant when designing low-noise input stages.
Temperature characteristicsHelp assess variation in current, cutoff and performance with temperature.
Datasheets commonly specify minimum and maximum ranges rather than exact values. Design bias circuits to tolerate parameter spread, temperature and supply variation.

17. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Determine JFET parameters experimentally from drain and transfer measurements.

Equipment

  • N-channel JFET and datasheet.
  • Current-limited DC supplies.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Suitable test circuit and current-limiting resistors.

Procedure

  1. Identify the JFET terminals and ratings.
  2. Set VGS = 0 V and establish a suitable saturation-region VDS.
  3. Measure drain current to estimate IDSS.
  4. Reduce drain current using progressively more negative VGS.
  5. Record VGS and ID at each point.
  6. Estimate VGS(off) from the transfer curve within measurement limitations.
  7. Calculate gm0 using the measured IDSS and VGS(off).
  8. Estimate gm at an operating point from a local slope of the transfer curve.
  9. Measure rd from drain-current and drain-voltage changes at constant VGS.
  10. Calculate μ = gm × rd.

19. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is IDSS?

The saturation drain current when VGS = 0 V.

2. What is VGS(off)?

The gate-source voltage at which drain current approaches zero in the cutoff condition.

3. State the Shockley equation.

ID = IDSS[1 − VGS/VGS(off)]² for the ideal N-channel saturation model.

4. What is transconductance?

The incremental change in drain current per change in gate-source voltage at a specified operating point.

5. What is the unit of gm?

Siemens, commonly mS.

6. What is gm0?

The ideal maximum transconductance at VGS = 0.

7. State the gm0 formula.

gm0 = 2IDSS / |VGS(off)|.

8. How is gm calculated at an operating point?

gm = gm0[1 − VGS/VGS(off)] for the ideal Shockley model.

9. What is drain resistance?

Small-signal drain-source resistance at constant VGS, given by rd = ΔVDS/ΔID.

10. What is amplification factor?

μ = gm × rd.

11. Is μ dimensionless?

Yes. Siemens multiplied by Ohms is dimensionless.

12. Why is JFET gm operating-point dependent?

Because the transfer curve is nonlinear, so its slope changes with VGS.

13. What is the difference between drain pinch-off and gate cutoff?

Drain pinch-off marks the onset of approximate drain-current saturation; gate cutoff reduces current toward zero.

14. Why is IDSS not identical for every JFET?

Manufacturing variation changes device parameters, and datasheets specify a range.

15. What is the significance of rd in amplifier design?

It influences the small-signal output resistance and gain, particularly when combined with external circuit resistances.
