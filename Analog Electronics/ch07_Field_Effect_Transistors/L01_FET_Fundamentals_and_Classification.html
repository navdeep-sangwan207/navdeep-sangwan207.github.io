Analog Electronics – Field Effect Transistors

Lesson 1: Introduction to Field Effect Transistors

Understand FET fundamentals, terminals, working principles, types and applications.

1. Introduction to Field Effect Transistors

A Field Effect Transistor (FET) is a semiconductor device in which the current flowing through a channel is controlled by an electric field produced by an applied gate-to-source voltage.

Unlike a Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT), which uses both electrons and holes for conduction, a FET primarily uses one type of charge carrier for channel conduction. Therefore, a FET is also known as a unipolar device.

Definition: A Field Effect Transistor is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device that controls the current between its drain and source terminals by varying the electric field at its gate terminal.

Why are FETs Important?

  • They provide high input impedance in many configurations.
  • They require relatively little input current for gate control.
  • They are widely used in analog and digital electronic circuits.
  • MOSFET technology enables high-density integrated circuits.
  • Power MOSFETs are widely used in switching power converters.

2. Basic Structure of a FET

A typical FET has three main terminals: Gate, Drain and Source. A semiconductor region called the channel provides the conduction path between the drain and source.

Conceptual representation of the three main FET terminals and the channel controlled by the gate.

Main FET Terminals

  1. Gate (G): The control terminal. An applied gate-to-source voltage establishes an electric field that controls channel conductivity.
  2. Drain (D): The terminal through which conventional drain current is generally considered to enter the channel in the usual operating convention for an N-channel device.
  3. Source (S): The terminal through which charge carriers enter the channel in the usual operating convention.
Important: Some MOSFETs have a fourth terminal called the Body or Substrate (B). In many discrete MOSFETs, the body is internally connected to the source. Integrated circuits may provide separate body connections.

3. Working Principle of a FET

The operation of a FET is based on the control of channel conductivity using an electric field. The voltage applied between the gate and source changes the channel condition, which controls the current flowing between drain and source.

Basic Working Steps

  1. A voltage is applied between the drain and source terminals.
  2. A gate-to-source voltage is applied to control the channel.
  3. The electric field produced by the gate changes the channel's conductivity or effective channel width.
  4. The drain current changes according to the applied voltages and the device characteristics.
Basic FET Current Relationship

\(I_D = f(V_{GS}, V_{DS})\)

Drain current depends on gate-to-source voltage and drain-to-source voltage.

The exact relationship depends on whether the device is a JFET or MOSFET, its channel type, and its operating region. These characteristics are studied in later lessons.

Gate Control in Different FETs

  • JFET: The gate-channel PN junction is normally reverse biased during standard operation. Changing the gate voltage controls the channel width.
  • MOSFET: An insulated gate produces an electric field through the gate dielectric, controlling the channel. An enhancement MOSFET can form a conducting channel when the appropriate gate voltage is applied.
Gate-voltage polarity is not identical for every FET. N-channel and P-channel devices generally require opposite voltage polarities for corresponding operating conditions.

4. Classification of Field Effect Transistors

Field Effect Transistors are mainly classified into two major families: Junction Field Effect Transistors and Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistors.

Basic classification of FETs.

4.1 Junction Field Effect Transistor (JFET)

A JFET controls the drain current by varying the width of a conducting semiconductor channel using a reverse-biased gate-channel PN junction.

  • N-channel JFET
  • P-channel JFET

4.2 Metal Oxide Semiconductor FET (MOSFET)

A MOSFET has an insulated gate separated from the semiconductor by a thin dielectric layer, commonly silicon dioxide in conventional silicon MOSFET technology.

MOSFETs are classified by their operating mode:

  • Enhancement-mode MOSFET: The channel is normally absent or non-conducting at zero gate-to-source voltage in the standard enhancement-mode device.
  • Depletion-mode MOSFET: A conducting channel exists at zero gate-to-source voltage and can be depleted by an appropriate gate voltage.

Both enhancement-mode and depletion-mode MOSFETs are available as N-channel and P-channel devices.

5. FET vs BJT

FETs and BJTs are both transistor families used for signal amplification and electronic switching. Their control mechanisms and input characteristics are different.

Parameter FET BJT
Full Form Field Effect Transistor Bipolar Junction Transistor
Device Type Unipolar Bipolar
Control Mechanism Electric field controlled by gate voltage Base current controls collector current in active operation
Input Impedance Generally high Generally lower than an insulated-gate MOSFET
Input Current Very small for an insulated MOSFET gate; JFET has gate leakage Requires base current during normal active operation
Charge Carriers Majority carriers in the channel Electrons and holes participate in device operation
Typical Applications CMOS logic, buffers, analog circuits and power switching Amplifiers, signal conditioning and switching
Remember: A FET is described as voltage-controlled because its channel current is controlled by gate voltage. This does not mean every FET has absolutely zero input current or consumes no power.

6. Important Characteristics of FETs

6.1 High Input Impedance

Many FETs, particularly MOSFETs with insulated gates, have very high input impedance. This reduces the loading effect on circuits connected to their input.

6.2 Voltage-Controlled Operation

The gate-to-source voltage controls channel conductivity. This makes FETs useful in voltage-controlled amplifiers and electronic switching circuits.

6.3 Low Input Power in Many Applications

An insulated MOSFET gate draws very little steady-state DC current. However, charging and discharging the gate capacitance requires current during switching.

6.4 Thermal Behaviour

FETs do not all have identical thermal characteristics. Many power MOSFETs have a positive temperature coefficient of on-resistance under specified operating conditions, which can help current sharing when devices are connected in parallel. Proper thermal design is still necessary.

6.5 Easy Integration

MOSFETs can be fabricated in very large numbers on a single semiconductor chip. This makes them essential components of microprocessors, memory devices and CMOS integrated circuits.

7. Applications of FETs

  • Amplifier Circuits: Used in audio amplifiers, RF amplifiers and sensor signal conditioning circuits.
  • Buffer Circuits: Used where high input impedance is required to reduce loading between circuit stages.
  • Electronic Switching: MOSFETs are used to switch loads such as motors, LEDs, relays and power supplies.
  • Digital Electronics: CMOS technology uses complementary MOSFETs to implement logic gates and digital integrated circuits.
  • Power Electronics: Power MOSFETs are used in DC-DC converters, inverters, motor drives and battery management systems.
  • Embedded Systems: MOSFETs are commonly used as interface devices between microcontroller outputs and external loads.
  • Sensor Circuits: FET input stages are useful in circuits that process signals from high-impedance sensors.
Placement Connection: For embedded and electronics R&D roles, understand how a microcontroller drives a MOSFET gate, why a gate resistor may be used, how a pull-down resistor prevents unwanted turn-on, and why an inductive load needs a suitable flyback protection path.

8. Practical Example: MOSFET as an Electronic Switch

Consider an N-channel enhancement MOSFET used to control a DC load from a microcontroller.

Conceptual low-side switching arrangement. The actual MOSFET pin layout and symbol must be taken from its datasheet.

Operation

  1. When the GPIO output provides a suitable gate-to-source voltage, the N-channel MOSFET turns on.
  2. Current flows from the positive supply through the load and MOSFET drain-to-source path.
  3. When the gate voltage is reduced sufficiently, the MOSFET turns off and the load current stops.
Practical design considerations: Select a logic-level MOSFET whose on-resistance is specified at the available GPIO drive voltage. Check its current, voltage, power dissipation and thermal ratings. A gate pull-down resistor can keep the MOSFET off while the microcontroller pin is floating during startup. For an inductive load, provide appropriate flyback protection.

9. Interview Questions and Answers

  1. What is a FET?

    A FET is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device in which an electric field controls the current flowing through a channel.

  2. Why is a FET called a unipolar device?

    Because channel conduction is primarily due to one type of majority charge carrier: electrons or holes.

  3. What are the three main terminals of a FET?

    Gate, Drain and Source.

  4. What is the function of the gate terminal?

    It controls the channel conductivity through the electric field established by the gate voltage.

  5. What are the two major types of FET?

    JFET and MOSFET.

  6. What is the main difference between a JFET and MOSFET?

    A JFET uses a reverse-biased gate-channel PN junction, while a MOSFET uses an insulated gate separated from the semiconductor by a dielectric.

  7. Why are MOSFETs widely used in digital circuits?

    They support low-power switching and can be integrated at very high density using CMOS technology.

  8. What is the difference between enhancement and depletion MOSFETs?

    An enhancement MOSFET is normally off at zero gate bias, whereas a depletion MOSFET has a conducting channel at zero gate bias.

  9. Why is a MOSFET gate pull-down resistor used?

    It holds the gate at a defined low voltage when the driving circuit is disconnected or its output is in a high-impedance state.

  10. What is the body terminal?

    It is the semiconductor substrate terminal of a MOSFET. It may be internally connected to the source in discrete devices.

11. Lesson Summary

  • FET stands for Field Effect Transistor.
  • A FET is a voltage-controlled, unipolar device.
  • Its three main terminals are Gate, Drain and Source.
  • The gate electric field controls channel conductivity.
  • The two major families are JFET and MOSFET.
  • FETs are available in N-channel and P-channel forms.
  • MOSFETs are classified as enhancement-mode and depletion-mode.
  • FETs are widely used in amplifiers, buffers, switching circuits, embedded systems and power electronics.
