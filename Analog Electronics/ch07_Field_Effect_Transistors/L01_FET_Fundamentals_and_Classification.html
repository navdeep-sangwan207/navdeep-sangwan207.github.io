2. What Is a Field Effect Transistor?

A Field Effect Transistor (FET) is a semiconductor device in which an electric field controls the current flowing through a conductive channel between two terminals called the source and drain.

The third terminal, called the gate, controls the channel conductivity. In normal operation, the gate voltage changes the channel's electric field and therefore controls drain current.

Drain current ID = f(Gate-to-source voltage VGS, Drain-to-source voltage VDS)
Fundamental idea: The gate voltage controls the current flowing between drain and source. A FET is therefore commonly described as a voltage-controlled device.

Basic terminals

Gate (G)Control terminal. Its voltage controls channel conductivity.
Source (S)Terminal through which majority carriers enter the channel in the conventional N-channel operating direction.
Drain (D)Terminal through which conventional drain current leaves the channel in an N-channel device.

The illustration is conceptual. Actual semiconductor construction differs between JFETs and MOSFETs.

3. How Does a FET Work?

Consider an N-channel FET. Electrons are the majority carriers in the channel. A voltage applied between drain and source establishes an electric field that drives channel current. The gate voltage controls the effective channel width or conductivity.

  1. A drain-to-source voltage VDS establishes the electric field along the channel.
  2. Charge carriers move through the channel and produce drain current ID.
  3. The gate-to-source voltage VGS changes the channel electric field.
  4. The channel may become more conductive or less conductive, depending on device type and gate-voltage polarity.
  5. The drain current changes accordingly.
The exact gate-voltage polarity depends on the device. An N-channel enhancement MOSFET requires a sufficiently positive VGS to form a conducting channel, while an N-channel depletion MOSFET and an N-channel JFET have a channel at VGS = 0 and are normally controlled toward cutoff by a negative VGS.

4. Classification of Field Effect Transistors

FETs are commonly divided into two major families:

JFET – Junction Field Effect Transistor

  • Gate forms a PN junction with the channel.
  • The gate-channel junction is normally reverse biased during ordinary operation.
  • The depletion region changes channel width and controls current.
  • Available in N-channel and P-channel forms.

MOSFET – Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistor

  • Gate is insulated from the semiconductor channel by a thin dielectric layer.
  • Gate voltage controls the channel through an electric field.
  • Very high DC gate input resistance is possible.
  • Available in enhancement-mode and depletion-mode forms.
  • Both N-channel and P-channel versions are widely used.

5. N-Channel and P-Channel FETs

N-Channel
  • Majority carriers are electrons.
  • Electron mobility is higher than hole mobility in silicon.
  • Widely used for low-side power switching.
  • Gate-control polarity depends on device mode.
P-Channel
  • Majority carriers are holes.
  • Hole mobility is lower than electron mobility in silicon.
  • Often used for high-side switching.
  • Gate-control polarity is opposite to the corresponding N-channel device.

Carrier movement and conventional current

For an N-channel device under the usual positive VDS operating condition, electrons drift from source toward drain. Conventional drain current ID is defined in the opposite direction, from drain toward source.

For a P-channel device under its usual operating polarity, holes are the majority carriers. Their drift direction corresponds to conventional current direction.

Always distinguish electron flow from conventional current. Circuit symbols and datasheets normally define current using conventional-current reference directions.

6. Why Is a FET Called a Unipolar Device?

In normal channel conduction, a FET primarily uses one type of majority carrier:

  • N-channel: electrons.
  • P-channel: holes.

Therefore, FET conduction is described as unipolar, unlike a BJT where both electrons and holes participate in transistor action.

FET → Majority-carrier conduction
BJT → Bipolar carrier operation

This is a useful classification concept, but it does not mean minority-carrier effects are absent from every physical process in a FET.

8. Important FET Parameters

Gate-to-source voltage (VGS)

Voltage measured between the gate and source. It controls the channel in conjunction with the device structure and drain voltage.

VGS = VG − VS

Drain-to-source voltage (VDS)

Voltage measured between drain and source.

VDS = VD − VS

Drain current (ID)

Current through the drain-source channel. Its value depends on the device type, VGS and VDS.

Threshold voltage (VTH)

For an enhancement MOSFET, the threshold voltage is the approximate gate-to-source voltage at which an inversion channel begins to form under specified measurement conditions. It is not the voltage at which the MOSFET is guaranteed to be fully ON.

Transconductance (gm)

Small-signal change in drain current per change in gate-to-source voltage at a specified operating point and drain voltage.

gm = ∂ID / ∂VGS

Unit: Siemens (S), equivalent to A/V.

10. Practical Applications of FETs

Voltage AmplifiersFETs are used in high-input-impedance amplifier stages and signal-conditioning circuits.
Buffer AmplifiersSource followers provide impedance transformation with voltage gain near unity.
Electronic SwitchingMOSFETs switch LEDs, relays, motors and other loads in suitable driver circuits.
Power ConvertersPower MOSFETs are key switching elements in DC-DC converters and switching power supplies.
CMOS LogicComplementary N-channel and P-channel MOSFETs form the basis of CMOS digital logic.
Analog Integrated CircuitsMOSFETs are used in current mirrors, differential pairs and integrated amplifier stages.

11. Worked Conceptual Questions

Example 1: Identify the FET family

A device has an insulated gate separated from the semiconductor by an oxide layer. Identify the device family.

Answer: MOSFET. The insulated gate is the defining structural feature of a metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor.

Example 2: Identify the majority carriers

What are the majority carriers in an N-channel FET?

Answer: Electrons.

Example 3: Calculate VGS

Gate voltage VG = 5 V and source voltage VS = 2 V.

VGS = VG − VS = 5 − 2 = 3 V

Example 4: Calculate VDS

Drain voltage VD = 12 V and source voltage VS = 3 V.

VDS = 12 − 3 = 9 V

Example 5: Calculate transconductance

Drain current changes by 2 mA when gate voltage changes by 0.5 V around the operating point.

gm ≈ ΔID/ΔVGS = 2mA/0.5V = 4 mS

12. Practical Laboratory Exercise

Objective

Observe the control of drain current by gate-to-source voltage using a suitable low-voltage FET test circuit.

Equipment

  • N-channel enhancement MOSFET with datasheet.
  • Current-limited DC power supply.
  • Digital multimeter.
  • Resistors suitable for the chosen transistor and supply.
  • Breadboard or suitable prototyping arrangement.
  • Connecting wires.

Procedure

  1. Identify the MOSFET gate, drain and source pins from its datasheet.
  2. Connect the source to the circuit reference ground.
  3. Connect the drain to the positive supply through a suitable current-limiting resistor.
  4. Apply a controlled gate voltage relative to the source.
  5. Measure VGS, VDS and the voltage drop across the drain resistor.
  6. Calculate drain current using ID = VR/ R.
  7. Repeat for several gate voltages without exceeding device ratings.
  8. Plot the measured drain current against gate voltage.

14. Placement Interview Questions

1. What is a Field Effect Transistor?

A voltage-controlled semiconductor device in which an electric field controls current through a channel.

2. Why is a FET called a unipolar device?

Because normal channel conduction primarily involves one type of majority carrier: electrons in N-channel devices or holes in P-channel devices.

3. Name the three terminals of a FET.

Gate, source and drain.

4. What is the difference between JFET and MOSFET?

A JFET uses a reverse-biased PN-junction gate to control its channel. A MOSFET uses an insulated gate and an electric field across a dielectric.

5. Why does a MOSFET have high input resistance?

Its gate is insulated from the channel by a dielectric, so ideal steady-state gate current is zero. Real devices have leakage and require transient gate-charging current.

6. What is VGS?

The voltage between gate and source: VGS = VG − VS.

7. What is VDS?

The voltage between drain and source: VDS = VD − VS.

8. What are majority carriers in an N-channel FET?

Electrons.

9. What are majority carriers in a P-channel FET?

Holes.

10. What is transconductance?

The rate of change of drain current with gate-source voltage at a specified operating point: gm = ∂ID/∂VGS.

11. What is MOSFET threshold voltage?

The specified gate-source voltage associated with the onset of channel formation under defined test conditions. It does not guarantee low ON resistance.

12. What is the difference between enhancement and depletion MOSFET?

An enhancement MOSFET is normally OFF at VGS = 0, while a depletion MOSFET has a conducting channel at VGS = 0.

13. Why are N-channel MOSFETs widely used?

Electron mobility is generally higher than hole mobility in silicon, often enabling lower channel resistance for comparable device structures.

14. Can a FET be used as an amplifier?

Yes. A suitable DC bias point allows small input-voltage changes to control output current and produce an amplified output signal.

15. Why is a MOSFET gate resistor sometimes used?

It limits peak gate-charging current, controls switching transitions and can reduce ringing or electromagnetic interference.

16. Lecture Summary

  • FETs control channel current using an electric field.
  • The three terminals are gate, source and drain.
  • FETs are classified into JFET and MOSFET families.
  • Both families have N-channel and P-channel forms.
  • MOSFETs have an insulated gate; JFETs use a PN-junction gate.
  • FETs are primarily majority-carrier devices.
  • VGS controls channel conductivity, while VDS establishes the drain-source operating condition.
  • FETs are widely used in amplifiers, buffers, power switching and integrated circuits.
