1. Learning Objectives

  • Calculate base, collector and emitter currents and transistor voltages.

  • Solve placement-level numerical problems systematically.

2. Essential BJT Formula Reference

Current relationships

IE = IB + IC
β = IC / IB
α = IC / IE
β = α/(1 − α)    |    α = β/(β + 1)

Fixed-bias circuit

IB = (VBB − VBE)/RB
IC = βIB   (active region only)
VCE = VCC − ICRC

Collector-feedback bias

IB = (VCC − VBE)/[RB + (β + 1)RC]
VCE = VCC − (IC + IB)RC

Voltage-divider bias

VTH = VCC × R2/(R1 + R2)
RTH = R1 || R2
IB = (VTH − VBE)/[RTH + (β + 1)RE]
IE = (β + 1)IB   ;   IC ≈ βIB
VE = IERE   ;   VC = VCC − ICRC   ;   VCE = VC − VE

Load line and amplifier

VCE = VCC − ICRC
IC(sat) ≈ (VCC − VCE(sat))/RC
Av = Vout/Vin   ;   Ai = Iout/Iin   ;   Ap = Pout/Pin
Av(dB) = 20log10|Av|   ;   Ap(dB) = 10log10(Ap)

3. Numerical Problem-Solving Method

  1. Identify the transistor configuration and bias network.
  2. Write down all given values with units.
  3. Assume a suitable operating model. For a silicon transistor, a first approximation is VBE ≈ 0.7 V.
  4. Calculate the base current using the correct base-loop equation.
  5. Calculate the collector and emitter currents.
  6. Calculate all node voltages and VCE.
  7. Check whether the assumed operating region is physically possible.
  8. Calculate transistor power dissipation and check ratings where data is available.
  9. State the final Q-point as (ICQ, VCEQ).
Never accept IC = βIB without checking the collector circuit. If the calculated current requires VCE below saturation voltage, the transistor is saturated and the active-region equation is no longer valid.

4. Problem 1 – Fixed-Bias Circuit

Given: VCC = 12 V, VBB = 5 V, RB = 220 kΩ, RC = 2.2 kΩ, β = 100 and VBE = 0.7 V.

Step 1: Base current

IB = (5 − 0.7)/220k = 19.55 µA

Step 2: Collector current

IC = 100 × 19.55 µA = 1.955 mA

Step 3: Collector-emitter voltage

VCE = 12 − (1.955mA × 2.2kΩ)
VCE ≈ 7.70 V
Answer: IB ≈ 19.55 µA, IC ≈ 1.955 mA and VCE ≈ 7.70 V. The transistor is in the forward-active region under the assumed model.

5. Problem 2 – Collector-to-Base Feedback Bias

Given: VCC = 12 V, RB = 470 kΩ, RC = 2.2 kΩ, β = 100 and VBE = 0.7 V.

Step 1: Base current

IB = (12 − 0.7)/[470k + 101(2.2k)]
IB = 11.3/692.2k ≈ 16.33 µA

Step 2: Collector and emitter currents

IC = 100 × 16.33 µA ≈ 1.633 mA
IE = 101 × 16.33 µA ≈ 1.649 mA

Step 3: Collector voltage

The current through RC is IC + IB because the base current is supplied from the collector node.

VC = 12 − (1.649mA × 2.2kΩ) ≈ 8.37 V
VCE ≈ 8.37 V. The collector voltage has fallen as collector current increases, providing negative feedback through RB.

6. Problem 3 – Voltage-Divider Bias

Given: VCC = 12 V, R1 = 47 kΩ, R2 = 10 kΩ, RC = 2.2 kΩ, RE = 1 kΩ, β = 100 and VBE = 0.7 V.

Step 1: Thevenin equivalent

VTH = 12 × 10/(47 + 10) = 2.105 V
RTH = 47k || 10k = 8.246 kΩ

Step 2: Base current

IB = (2.105 − 0.7)/[8.246k + 101(1k)]
IB ≈ 12.87 µA

Step 3: Collector and emitter currents

IC ≈ 1.287 mA   ;   IE ≈ 1.300 mA

Step 4: Node voltages

VE = 1.300 V
VC = 12 − (1.287mA × 2.2kΩ) ≈ 9.169 V
VCE = 9.169 − 1.300 = 7.869 V
Q-point: (ICQ ≈ 1.287 mA, VCEQ ≈ 7.869 V). This is a forward-active operating point.

7. Problem 4 – Transistor Operating Region

Given: VCC = 10 V, RC = 1 kΩ, β = 100 and IB = 150 µA. Assume an ideal active-region calculation initially.

IC(active) = 100 × 150 µA = 15 mA

However, the collector circuit can supply at most approximately:

IC(sat) ≈ 10V/1kΩ = 10 mA

The requested 15 mA is greater than the available collector current. The active-region assumption is invalid.

Conclusion: The transistor is driven into saturation. Actual collector current is limited by the supply, RC, load and VCE(sat), not by βIB.

8. Problem 5 – DC Load-Line Analysis

Given: VCC = 12 V, RC = 2 kΩ and VCE(sat) = 0.2 V.

Cutoff intercept

IC = 0, VCE = 12 V

Practical saturation intercept

IC(sat) = (12 − 0.2)/2k = 5.9 mA

Ideal midpoint approximation

ICQ ≈ 3 mA   ;   VCEQ ≈ 6 V

The midpoint is a useful starting estimate for symmetrical DC load-line swing. The actual maximum undistorted AC swing depends on the AC load line and both cutoff and saturation limits.

9. Problem 6 – AC Load Line and Output Swing

Given: VCC = 12 V, RC = 2 kΩ, RL = 4 kΩ, ICQ = 3 mA, VCEQ = 6 V and VCE(sat) = 0.2 V.

AC collector load

Rac = RC || RL = (2 × 4)/(2 + 4) = 1.333 kΩ

Cutoff-side current swing

ΔIC(cutoff) = ICQ = 3 mA

Saturation-side voltage swing

ΔVCE(saturation) = 6 − 0.2 = 5.8 V

The AC load line has slope −1/Rac and passes through the Q-point. The available voltage swing toward cutoff is approximately:

ΔVCE(cutoff) = ICQ × Rac = 3mA × 1.333kΩ ≈ 4 V
The cutoff side limits the symmetrical output voltage swing to approximately 4 V peak in this example. The Q-point at the DC midpoint is not necessarily the optimum AC swing point when an external load is connected.

10. Problem 7 – Stability Factor

For a simplified voltage-divider/Thevenin base network with emitter resistor, the stability factor with respect to collector-base leakage current is:

S = (β + 1)(RB + RE) / [RB + (β + 1)RE]

Given: β = 100, RB = 10 kΩ and RE = 1 kΩ.

S = 101(10 + 1)/[10 + 101(1)]
S = 1111/111 ≈ 10.01

For fixed bias without emitter stabilization, the corresponding simplified stability factor is approximately:

Sfixed = β + 1 = 101
A lower stability factor indicates reduced sensitivity to the leakage-current change represented by this model. The emitter resistor improves thermal stability through negative feedback.

11. Problem 8 – Transistor Power Dissipation

Given: VCE = 6 V and IC = 3 mA.

PC ≈ VCE × IC
PC = 6 × 0.003 = 18 mW

More precisely, transistor terminal power is approximately VCEIC + VBEIB. For many basic design calculations, the base-power contribution is small and neglected.

Always compare the calculated dissipation with the transistor's power rating and thermal conditions. A device's maximum rating may require derating as ambient temperature rises.

12. Problem 9 – CE Amplifier Voltage Gain

For a bypassed-emitter common-emitter stage, a useful midband approximation is:

Av ≈ −gm(RC || RL)
gm = IC/VT

At room temperature, VT is approximately 25.9 mV.

Given: IC = 1 mA, RC = 4.7 kΩ and RL = 10 kΩ.

RC || RL = (4.7 × 10)/(4.7 + 10) = 3.197 kΩ
gm = 1mA/25.9mV = 0.0386 S
Av ≈ −0.0386 × 3197 ≈ −123.4
Approximate gain magnitude is 123.4. The negative sign represents 180° phase inversion. This estimate neglects transistor output resistance, source loading, unbypassed emitter resistance and frequency-dependent effects.

13. Problem 10 – Common-Collector Voltage Gain

A simplified emitter-follower voltage gain is:

Av ≈ (β + 1)RE' / [rπ + (β + 1)RE']

where RE' is the AC emitter load, often RE || RL, and rπ is the small-signal base-emitter resistance.

Given: β = 100, rπ = 2.6 kΩ and RE' = 1 kΩ.

Av = 101 × 1k/[2.6k + 101 × 1k]
Av ≈ 0.975
The emitter-follower voltage gain is approximately 0.975, close to unity but less than one. It does not invert the signal and provides current gain.

14. Problem 11 – Common-Base Current Gain

Given: α = 0.98 and IE = 2 mA.

IC = αIE = 0.98 × 2 = 1.96 mA
IB = IE − IC = 2 − 1.96 = 0.04 mA
β = α/(1 − α) = 0.98/0.02 = 49
IC = 1.96 mA, IB = 40 µA and β = 49.

15. Problem 12 – Voltage Gain in Decibels

Given: Vin = 25 mV RMS and Vout = 2.5 V RMS.

Av = 2.5/0.025 = 100
Av(dB) = 20 log10(100) = 40 dB

16. Problem 13 – Source Loading

Given: Source voltage Vs = 2 V, source resistance Rs = 5 kΩ and amplifier input resistance Rin = 20 kΩ.

Vin = Vs × Rin/(Rs + Rin)
Vin = 2 × 20/(5 + 20) = 1.6 V

The amplifier receives 1.6 V rather than the source's 2 V open-circuit voltage.

17. Problem 14 – Output Resistance

Given: Open-circuit output voltage = 6 V, loaded output voltage = 4 V and RL = 2 kΩ.

Rout = RL(Vopen/Vload − 1)
Rout = 2k(6/4 − 1) = 1 kΩ

21. Placement Practice – Mixed Numerical Questions

Question 1: Calculate β when IC = 4 mA and IB = 40 µA.

β = 4mA/40µA = 100.

Question 2: Calculate α when β = 99.

α = β/(β + 1) = 99/100 = 0.99.

Question 3: A fixed-bias transistor has VBB = 3 V, VBE = 0.7 V and RB = 115 kΩ. Find IB.

IB = 2.3/115k = 20 µA.

Question 4: If β = 80 and IB = 25 µA, calculate active-region IC.

IC = 80 × 25µA = 2 mA, subject to collector-circuit limits.

Question 5: VCC = 15 V, RC = 3 kΩ and IC = 2 mA. Find VCE for an emitter-grounded circuit.

VCE = 15 − 2mA × 3kΩ = 9 V.

Question 6: A CE amplifier has input 10 mV and output 1 V. Find voltage gain magnitude.

|Av| = 1/0.01 = 100.

Question 7: Convert a voltage gain of 50 into dB.

20log10(50) = 33.98 dB.

Question 8: A source resistance is 1 kΩ and amplifier input resistance is 9 kΩ. Find the input transfer ratio.

9/(1 + 9) = 0.9 or 90%.

Question 9: Why does an emitter resistor improve bias stability?

An increase in emitter/collector current raises the emitter voltage, reducing VBE and opposing the current increase.

Question 10: Why is a CC amplifier suitable for impedance matching?

It typically offers high input impedance and low output impedance, with voltage gain close to unity.

22. Practical Laboratory and Measurement Checklist

Objective

Verify a BJT amplifier's DC operating point, gain and loading response using low-voltage laboratory equipment.

Procedure

  1. Build a voltage-divider biased NPN common-emitter circuit using a suitable small-signal transistor.
  2. Verify supply polarity, resistor values and transistor pinout before applying power.
  3. Apply a current-limited low-voltage DC supply.
  4. Measure VB, VE, VC and VCE using a digital multimeter.
  5. Calculate IC from the measured voltage drop across RC.
  6. Apply a small sinusoidal input signal and measure Vin and Vout on the oscilloscope.
  7. Calculate voltage gain and identify phase inversion.
  8. Connect different safe resistive loads and record output voltage changes.
  9. Compare the measured results with the calculated small-signal model.

23. Common Numerical Mistakes

  • Using βIB as actual collector current after the transistor enters saturation.
  • Ignoring base current through the collector resistor in collector-feedback bias.
  • Using VCE = VC instead of VC − VE when the emitter is not grounded.
  • Forgetting that the emitter current is IE = IC + IB.
  • Using RC instead of RC || RL in a simplified loaded CE midband gain calculation.
  • Ignoring phase inversion in a CE stage.
  • Confusing transistor β with amplifier current gain.
  • Using 20log for power gain instead of 10log.
  • Assuming a DC load-line midpoint always provides maximum symmetrical AC swing.
  • Ignoring transistor power dissipation and thermal limits.
