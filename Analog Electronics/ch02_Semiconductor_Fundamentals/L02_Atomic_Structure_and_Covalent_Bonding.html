Lesson 2: Atomic Structure and Covalent Bonding

Understand atomic structure, electrons, protons, neutrons, electron shells, valence electrons and covalent bonding in semiconductor materials.

1. Introduction to Atomic Structure

All materials are made up of atoms. The electrical properties of a material depend significantly on its atomic structure and the behavior of its electrons.

In semiconductor physics, understanding the outermost electrons of an atom is especially important because they participate in chemical bonding and electrical conduction.

An atom consists of:

  • Protons
  • Neutrons
  • Electrons

2. Subatomic Particles

Particle Charge Location Approximate Mass
Proton Positive (+1) Nucleus 1 atomic mass unit
Neutron Neutral (0) Nucleus 1 atomic mass unit
Electron Negative (-1) Electron cloud Approximately 1/1836 of a proton's mass

Important Terms

  • Atomic number (Z): Number of protons in an atom.
  • Mass number (A): Total number of protons and neutrons.
  • Neutral atom: Number of electrons equals number of protons.
Atomic Number: Z = Number of Protons

Mass Number: A = Protons + Neutrons

Example: Silicon Atom

Silicon has atomic number 14.

  • Protons = 14
  • Electrons = 14 in a neutral atom
  • Common silicon-28 isotope has 14 neutrons.

3. Electron Shells and Energy Levels

Electrons occupy allowed energy states around the nucleus. In the basic atomic shell model, these states are represented by electron shells.

The commonly used shell labels are:

  • K shell: n = 1
  • L shell: n = 2
  • M shell: n = 3
  • N shell: n = 4

The maximum number of electrons in a shell according to the basic shell capacity rule is:

Maximum Electrons = 2n²
Shell Principal Quantum Number (n) Maximum Capacity
K 1 2
L 2 8
M 3 18
N 4 32

Actual electron filling follows quantum-mechanical rules, including subshell energies and the Aufbau principle.

4. Valence Electrons

Valence electrons are electrons in the outermost occupied shell of an atom. They play an important role in chemical bonding and the electrical behavior of materials.

Examples

Element Atomic Number Electron Configuration Valence Electrons
Silicon 14 2, 8, 4 4
Germanium 32 2, 8, 18, 4 4
Boron 5 2, 3 3
Phosphorus 15 2, 8, 5 5

Important for Semiconductor Physics

Silicon and germanium have four valence electrons. This makes them suitable for forming covalent bonds in crystalline semiconductor structures.

5. Atomic Structure of Silicon

Silicon is a Group 14 element in the periodic table. Its atomic number is 14, and it has four valence electrons.

Electron Configuration

Silicon: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p²

Shell Distribution: 2, 8, 4

Important Properties

  • Atomic number = 14
  • Valence electrons = 4
  • Period = 3
  • Group = 14
  • Crystal structure = Diamond cubic

Each silicon atom can form four covalent bonds with four neighboring silicon atoms in its ideal crystal lattice.

6. Covalent Bonding

A covalent bond is formed when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons.

In silicon, each atom shares its four valence electrons with four neighboring atoms, forming four covalent bonds.

Characteristics

  • Involves sharing of electrons.
  • Helps form a stable atomic structure.
  • Common in elemental silicon and germanium crystals.
  • Important for understanding semiconductor conduction.

Silicon Crystal Example

Every silicon atom forms four covalent bonds with four neighboring silicon atoms.

At very low temperatures in an ideal pure crystal, these bonding electrons remain in their bonding states.

When sufficient thermal energy is supplied, some electrons can gain enough energy to leave their bonding states and contribute to conduction.

7. Crystal Structure of Semiconductors

A crystal is a solid in which atoms are arranged in a regular repeating three-dimensional pattern.

Silicon Crystal Structure

  • Silicon has a diamond cubic crystal structure.
  • Each atom has four nearest neighbors.
  • The neighboring atoms are connected through covalent bonds.
  • The arrangement repeats throughout the crystal.

Importance

The crystal arrangement determines many semiconductor properties, including carrier movement, energy bands and electrical conductivity.

8. Electron-Hole Pair Generation

When a valence electron receives sufficient energy, it can move from a bonding state into a mobile conduction state.

This process leaves behind an unoccupied electronic state called a hole.

Electron

An electron is a negatively charged particle that can move through the semiconductor and contribute to electric current.

Hole

A hole is the absence of an electron in an otherwise occupied bonding state. Its movement through the semiconductor is modeled as a positive charge carrier.

Energy Supplied → Electron Excitation → Electron-Hole Pair Generation

This process is fundamental to semiconductor electrical conduction.

9. Bond Breaking and Recombination

Bond Breaking

When an electron gains sufficient energy, it can leave a covalent bonding state and become available for conduction. A hole is left behind.

Recombination

Recombination occurs when a free electron loses energy and occupies an available hole state.

  • The free electron disappears as a mobile carrier.
  • The hole is filled.
  • Energy is released, often as heat or sometimes as light.

Generation and recombination processes influence semiconductor carrier concentration.

10. Effect of Temperature on Covalent Bonds

As the temperature of a semiconductor increases, thermal energy becomes available to excite electrons.

  • More electron-hole pairs can be generated.
  • Intrinsic carrier concentration increases.
  • Electrical conductivity generally increases.
  • Electrical resistivity generally decreases.
Temperature ↑ → Thermal Energy ↑ → Electron-Hole Pair Generation ↑

12. Lesson Summary

  • An atom contains protons, neutrons and electrons.
  • Atomic number is the number of protons.
  • Mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.
  • Valence electrons occupy the outermost occupied shell.
  • Silicon has 14 electrons and four valence electrons in a neutral atom.
  • Silicon has the electron configuration 2, 8, 4 by shell.
  • Covalent bonding involves sharing electron pairs.
  • Each silicon atom forms four covalent bonds in its ideal crystal structure.
  • Electron excitation can generate an electron-hole pair.
  • Recombination occurs when an electron fills a hole state.
