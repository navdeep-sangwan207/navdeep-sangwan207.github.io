Lesson 1: Introduction to Semiconductors

Understand conductors, insulators and semiconductors, their electrical properties, common semiconductor materials and their applications in electronic devices.

1. Introduction to Semiconductor Materials

A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator under specified conditions.

Semiconductor conductivity can be controlled by changing temperature, adding impurities through doping or applying an electric field.

Semiconductors form the foundation of modern electronic devices such as diodes, transistors, integrated circuits, microprocessors and memory chips.

Common Semiconductor Materials

  • Silicon (Si)
  • Germanium (Ge)
  • Gallium Arsenide (GaAs)
  • Silicon Carbide (SiC)
  • Gallium Nitride (GaN)

2. Conductors

Conductors are materials that allow electric current to flow easily because they contain a large number of mobile charge carriers.

Characteristics

  • High electrical conductivity.
  • Low electrical resistivity.
  • Many mobile electrons are available for conduction.
  • Generally have overlapping or partially filled energy bands.

Examples

  • Copper
  • Aluminium
  • Silver
  • Gold

Applications

  • Electrical wiring
  • PCB tracks
  • Electrical connectors
  • Power transmission cables

3. Insulators

Insulators are materials that strongly resist the flow of electric current because they have very few free charge carriers under normal operating conditions.

Characteristics

  • Very low electrical conductivity.
  • High electrical resistivity.
  • Large forbidden energy gap in the band model.
  • Electrons are generally tightly bound to their atoms.

Examples

  • Glass
  • Rubber
  • Mica
  • Plastic
  • Ceramic

Applications

  • Electrical cable insulation
  • PCB substrate materials
  • Electrical safety equipment
  • Insulating supports in electrical systems

4. Semiconductors

Semiconductors have electrical conductivity between conductors and insulators. Their conductivity can be controlled for electronic applications.

Characteristics

  • Moderate electrical conductivity compared with typical conductors and insulators.
  • Conductivity is strongly affected by temperature.
  • Conductivity can be increased by doping.
  • Conduction involves electrons and holes.
  • They have a relatively small forbidden energy gap.

Examples

  • Silicon (Si)
  • Germanium (Ge)
  • Gallium Arsenide (GaAs)
  • Silicon Carbide (SiC)

Applications

  • Diodes and LEDs
  • BJT and MOSFET transistors
  • Microprocessors
  • Memory devices
  • Solar cells
  • Power electronic devices

6. Silicon (Si)

Silicon is the most widely used semiconductor material in conventional integrated circuits and many electronic devices.

Important Properties

  • Atomic number: 14
  • Valence electrons: 4
  • Crystal structure: Diamond cubic
  • Energy band gap at approximately 300 K: 1.12 eV
  • Widely available in nature as silicon compounds.

Advantages

  • Abundant raw material.
  • Good thermal stability.
  • Suitable for large-scale integrated circuit manufacturing.
  • Forms a useful silicon dioxide (SiO2) insulating layer.

Applications

  • Integrated circuits
  • Microcontrollers
  • Memory chips
  • Power MOSFETs
  • Solar cells

7. Germanium (Ge)

Germanium is another elemental semiconductor that was widely used in early semiconductor devices.

Important Properties

  • Atomic number: 32
  • Valence electrons: 4
  • Crystal structure: Diamond cubic
  • Energy band gap at approximately 300 K: 0.66 eV
  • Higher intrinsic carrier concentration than silicon at the same temperature.

Characteristics

  • Lower band gap than silicon.
  • Higher intrinsic carrier concentration.
  • Historically important in early diodes and transistors.
  • Can be useful in specialized high-speed and optical applications.

Applications

  • Specialized semiconductor devices
  • Infrared optical systems
  • High-speed electronic applications
  • Some specialized transistor technologies

8. Compound Semiconductors

Compound semiconductors are materials formed by combining two or more different elements. They are selected for applications requiring specific electrical, optical or thermal properties.

Gallium Arsenide (GaAs)

  • Direct band-gap semiconductor.
  • Band gap approximately 1.42 eV at 300 K.
  • Used in high-frequency and optoelectronic devices.
  • Used in LEDs, laser diodes and RF circuits.

Silicon Carbide (SiC)

  • Wide-band-gap semiconductor.
  • High breakdown electric field.
  • Good high-temperature performance.
  • Used in high-voltage power devices.

Gallium Nitride (GaN)

  • Wide-band-gap semiconductor.
  • Suitable for high-frequency and high-power operation.
  • Used in RF power amplifiers.
  • Used in efficient power switching devices and LEDs.

9. Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors

Intrinsic Semiconductor

An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure semiconductor material without intentionally added impurity atoms.

  • Electron and hole concentrations are equal.
  • Conduction is mainly due to thermally generated electron-hole pairs.
  • Examples include pure silicon and pure germanium.
n = p = ni

Extrinsic Semiconductor

An extrinsic semiconductor is formed by intentionally adding controlled impurity atoms to a pure semiconductor through a process called doping.

  • Conductivity increases through controlled doping.
  • It can be classified as P-type or N-type.
  • It is widely used in semiconductor devices.

Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductor behavior will be studied in detail in upcoming lessons.

10. Effect of Temperature on Semiconductors

Temperature has a significant effect on semiconductor conductivity.

When temperature increases, more covalent bonds can break and generate electron-hole pairs.

  • Electron concentration increases.
  • Hole concentration increases.
  • Intrinsic carrier concentration increases.
  • Electrical conductivity generally increases.
  • Resistivity generally decreases.
Temperature ↑ → Intrinsic Carrier Concentration ↑ → Conductivity ↑

This is the typical intrinsic semiconductor trend. In doped semiconductors, the exact behavior also depends on doping concentration and temperature range.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Semiconductors have controllable electrical conductivity.
  • Conductors have high conductivity and insulators have very low conductivity.
  • Silicon and germanium are elemental semiconductors.
  • Silicon has four valence electrons and a band gap of approximately 1.12 eV at 300 K.
  • Germanium has a band gap of approximately 0.66 eV at 300 K.
  • GaAs is used in high-frequency and optoelectronic applications.
  • SiC and GaN are important wide-band-gap materials.
  • Intrinsic semiconductors are pure materials.
  • Extrinsic semiconductors are intentionally doped materials.
  • Temperature strongly affects semiconductor conductivity.
