1. Introduction to Charge Carrier Transport

Charge carrier transport refers to the movement of free electrons and holes inside a semiconductor material.

These mobile charge carriers are responsible for electrical current in semiconductor devices.

Two Main Transport Mechanisms

Charge Carrier Transport

↓

Drift
Diffusion

1. Drift

Drift is the movement of charge carriers caused by an applied electric field.

2. Diffusion

Diffusion is the movement of charge carriers from a region of higher concentration toward a region of lower concentration.

Important: Drift is caused by an electric field, whereas diffusion is caused by a carrier concentration gradient.

2. Drift Current

When an external electric field is applied across a semiconductor, electrons and holes experience an electrical force and acquire an average directed velocity.

The resulting current is called drift current.

Electron Movement

Electrons have negative charge. Therefore, they drift opposite to the direction of the applied electric field.

Hole Movement

Holes behave as positively charged carriers. Therefore, they drift in the direction of the applied electric field.

Electric Field →

Electron Drift Direction: ←

Hole Drift Direction: →

Remember: Conventional current due to electron drift is in the direction of the electric field. Conventional current due to hole drift is also in the direction of the electric field.

3. Drift Velocity

Drift velocity is the average directed velocity acquired by charge carriers under the influence of an electric field.

Electron Drift Velocity

vn = -μnE

Hole Drift Velocity

vp = μpE

Where:

  • vn = Electron drift velocity.
  • vp = Hole drift velocity.
  • μn = Electron mobility.
  • μp = Hole mobility.
  • E = Applied electric field.

The negative sign in the electron expression indicates that electrons move opposite to the electric field.

Numerical Example

Given:
  • Electron mobility = 1350 cm²/V·s
  • Electric field = 10 V/cm
Calculate the magnitude of electron drift velocity.
|vn| = μnE

|vn| = 1350 × 10

|vn| = 13500 cm/s
Answer: Electron drift velocity magnitude = 13,500 cm/s. Its direction is opposite to the electric field.

4. Carrier Mobility

Mobility is the measure of how easily charge carriers move through a semiconductor under an applied electric field.

It is defined as the magnitude of drift velocity per unit electric field.

μ = |vd| / E

Electron Mobility

μn = |vn| / E

Hole Mobility

μp = vp / E

Unit of Mobility

cm²/V·s or m²/V·s

Factors Affecting Mobility

  • Temperature.
  • Impurity concentration.
  • Crystal defects.
  • Carrier scattering.
  • Electric field strength.
Important: In silicon under comparable low-field conditions, electron mobility is generally higher than hole mobility.

5. Drift Current Density

Current density is the electric current flowing through a unit cross-sectional area.

Electron Drift Current Density

Jn = qnμnE

Hole Drift Current Density

Jp = qpμpE

Total Drift Current Density

J = q(nμn + pμp)E

Where:

  • J = Total current density.
  • q = Magnitude of electronic charge.
  • n = Electron concentration.
  • p = Hole concentration.
  • μn = Electron mobility.
  • μp = Hole mobility.
  • E = Electric field.
Placement Point: Both electron and hole drift currents contribute positively to conventional current density along the electric field.

6. Drift Current Numerical Example

Calculate the electron drift current density in a semiconductor using the following values:

  • Electron concentration, n = 1 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³
  • Electron mobility, μn = 1200 cm²/V·s
  • Electric field, E = 10 V/cm
  • Electronic charge, q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
Jn = qnμnE

Jn = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) (1 × 10¹⁶)(1200)(10)

Jn = 192 A/cm²
Answer: Electron drift current density = 192 A/cm².

7. Diffusion in Semiconductors

Diffusion is the movement of charge carriers from a region of higher concentration toward a region of lower concentration.

This movement occurs because of the concentration gradient of electrons or holes.

Example

Suppose one side of a semiconductor contains a high concentration of electrons, while the other side contains a lower concentration.

Electrons tend to move from the high-concentration region toward the low-concentration region.

High Electron Concentration

Electrons diffuse →

Low Electron Concentration

Important Characteristics

  • Diffusion occurs because of concentration differences.
  • It does not require an externally applied electric field.
  • It occurs for both electrons and holes.
  • It is important in PN junction formation and operation.

8. Concentration Gradient

A concentration gradient represents the rate at which carrier concentration changes with distance.

Electron Concentration Gradient

dn / dx

Hole Concentration Gradient

dp / dx

Here, x represents position inside the semiconductor.

A larger concentration difference over a given distance generally produces a larger diffusion current density.

The direction of diffusion is from higher concentration toward lower concentration. The mathematical sign of the concentration gradient depends on the selected coordinate direction.

9. Diffusion Current Density

When electrons and holes diffuse through a semiconductor, they produce diffusion current.

Electron Diffusion Current Density

Jn(diffusion) = qDn (dn/dx)

Hole Diffusion Current Density

Jp(diffusion) = -qDp (dp/dx)

Where:

  • q = Magnitude of electronic charge.
  • Dn = Electron diffusion coefficient.
  • Dp = Hole diffusion coefficient.
  • dn/dx = Electron concentration gradient.
  • dp/dx = Hole concentration gradient.
Important: These expressions use the conventional-current sign convention and a one-dimensional coordinate. The signs follow from the negative electron charge and positive hole charge.

10. Diffusion Coefficient

The diffusion coefficient describes how quickly charge carriers spread through a semiconductor due to concentration differences.

Electron Diffusion Coefficient

Dn

Hole Diffusion Coefficient

Dp

Unit

cm²/s or m²/s

Factors Affecting Diffusion

  • Temperature.
  • Carrier mobility.
  • Semiconductor material.
  • Carrier scattering mechanisms.
A higher diffusion coefficient means that carriers spread more rapidly under comparable concentration-gradient conditions.

11. Einstein Relation

The Einstein relation connects the diffusion coefficient of charge carriers with their mobility.

For Electrons

Dn / μn = kT / q

For Holes

Dp / μp = kT / q

Where:

  • Dn = Electron diffusion coefficient.
  • Dp = Hole diffusion coefficient.
  • μn = Electron mobility.
  • μp = Hole mobility.
  • k = Boltzmann constant (1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K).
  • T = Absolute temperature in Kelvin.
  • q = Electronic charge (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C).

At Room Temperature

At approximately 300 K:

kT / q ≈ 0.0259 V

Therefore:

Dn / μn ≈ 0.0259 V

Dp / μp ≈ 0.0259 V
Placement Point: The Einstein relation is frequently used to calculate diffusion coefficients when carrier mobility is known.

12. Einstein Relation Numerical Example

Calculate the electron diffusion coefficient at 300 K if electron mobility is 1200 cm²/V·s.

Given

  • μn = 1200 cm²/V·s
  • kT/q = 0.0259 V
Dn = μn × (kT/q)

Dn = 1200 × 0.0259

Dn = 31.08 cm²/s
Answer: Electron diffusion coefficient = 31.08 cm²/s.

13. Difference Between Drift and Diffusion

Property Drift Diffusion
Cause Applied electric field Carrier concentration gradient
Movement Due to electric force From higher to lower concentration
External Field Required for field-driven drift Not required
Parameter Mobility Diffusion coefficient
Velocity Drift velocity Random thermal motion with net diffusion
Important Application Current flow under an applied voltage PN junction and semiconductor carrier transport

14. Total Current Density

In a semiconductor, total current density contains both drift and diffusion components.

Electron Current Density

Jn = qnμnE + qDn(dn/dx)

Hole Current Density

Jp = qpμpE - qDp(dp/dx)

Total Current Density

J = Jn + Jp
Important: Semiconductor current may contain drift and diffusion contributions simultaneously. Their relative contributions depend on the electric field and carrier concentration profiles.

15. Drift and Diffusion in a PN Junction

Drift and diffusion play an important role in the formation and operation of a PN junction.

During Junction Formation

  1. The P-type and N-type regions are brought together.
  2. Electrons diffuse from the N-side toward the P-side.
  3. Holes diffuse from the P-side toward the N-side.
  4. Recombination occurs near the junction.
  5. Fixed ionized dopants form a depletion region.
  6. An internal electric field develops across the depletion region.

Equilibrium Condition

At thermal equilibrium, drift and diffusion currents balance for each carrier type.

Jn(total) = 0

Jp(total) = 0
Placement Point: The internal electric field produces drift currents that balance the diffusion currents at thermal equilibrium.
