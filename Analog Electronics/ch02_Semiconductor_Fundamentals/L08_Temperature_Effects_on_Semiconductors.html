1. Introduction to PN Junction

A PN junction is formed by joining a P-type semiconductor region with an N-type semiconductor region within the same semiconductor crystal.

The P-type region contains holes as majority carriers, while the N-type region contains electrons as majority carriers.

Basic Structure

P-Type Region
Majority: Holes
PN Junction
N-Type Region
Majority: Electrons

Importance of PN Junction

  • Forms the basic structure of semiconductor diodes.
  • Used in rectifier circuits.
  • Forms the basis of many transistor structures.
  • Used in LEDs, photodiodes and solar cells.

2. Formation of PN Junction

When P-type and N-type regions are formed adjacent to each other in a semiconductor crystal, a PN junction is established.

Step-by-Step Formation

  1. The P-type region contains a high concentration of holes.
  2. The N-type region contains a high concentration of free electrons.
  3. Due to the concentration difference, electrons diffuse from the N-side toward the P-side.
  4. Holes diffuse from the P-side toward the N-side.
  5. Electrons and holes recombine near the junction.
  6. This leaves behind fixed ionized donor and acceptor atoms.
  7. A depletion region and internal electric field develop near the junction.
Important: A practical PN junction is normally fabricated within a single semiconductor crystal using controlled doping processes, rather than by simply pressing two separate pieces together.

3. Diffusion of Electrons and Holes

Immediately after the P and N regions are brought into junction contact, a concentration gradient exists across the junction.

Electron Diffusion

The electron concentration is high on the N-side and low on the P-side. Therefore, electrons diffuse from N to P.

Hole Diffusion

The hole concentration is high on the P-side and low on the N-side. Therefore, holes diffuse from P to N.

P-Type
Junction
N-Type

Electron diffusion: N → P

Hole diffusion: P → N

Remember: Electrons and holes diffuse in opposite physical directions across the junction.

4. Recombination Near the Junction

When electrons diffuse from the N-side into the P-side, they encounter holes and may recombine with them.

Similarly, holes diffusing from the P-side into the N-side can recombine with electrons.

What Happens During Recombination?

  • A free electron combines with a hole.
  • The mobile electron-hole pair disappears.
  • Energy is released during recombination.
  • Fixed ionized dopant atoms remain in the crystal.
Recombination near the junction reduces the concentration of mobile carriers in that region, contributing to depletion-region formation.

5. Depletion Region

The depletion region is the region around a PN junction that is depleted of most mobile electrons and holes compared with the neutral semiconductor regions.

It contains fixed ionized donor and acceptor atoms.

Formation of Depletion Region

  1. Electrons diffuse from N-side to P-side.
  2. Holes diffuse from P-side to N-side.
  3. Recombination occurs near the junction.
  4. Positive ionized donor atoms remain on the N-side.
  5. Negative ionized acceptor atoms remain on the P-side.
  6. The region around the junction becomes depleted of mobile majority carriers.

Negative acceptor ions are on the P-side; positive donor ions are on the N-side.

Important: The depletion region is not completely devoid of all charge. It contains immobile ionized dopant charges and may contain a small number of mobile carriers.

6. Fixed Ionized Donor and Acceptor Atoms

When mobile carriers diffuse away from the junction region, the dopant atoms they leave behind become exposed as fixed ionized charges.

On the N-Side

Donor atoms that have supplied electrons become positively charged donor ions.

Donor atom → Positive ion + Electron

On the P-Side

Acceptor atoms that accept electrons become negatively charged acceptor ions.

Acceptor atom + Electron → Negative ion
Region Fixed Ion Charge
P-side depletion region Acceptor ions Negative
N-side depletion region Donor ions Positive

7. Built-in Electric Field

The fixed negative charges on the P-side and fixed positive charges on the N-side create an electric field across the depletion region.

This internal electric field is called the built-in electric field.

Direction of Electric Field

The electric field points from positive charge toward negative charge.

P-side (−)
← Electric Field
N-side (+)

Therefore, the built-in electric field points from the N-side toward the P-side.

Effect on Charge Carriers

  • Electrons experience a force opposite to the electric field.
  • Holes experience a force in the direction of the electric field.
  • The resulting drift motion opposes further majority-carrier diffusion.
The built-in electric field acts as an internal barrier against further majority-carrier diffusion.

8. Barrier Potential

The electric field in the depletion region creates an internal potential difference across the PN junction.

This potential difference is called the built-in potential or barrier potential.

Typical Values at Room Temperature

Material Approximate Built-in Potential
Silicon About 0.6–0.8 V
Germanium About 0.2–0.3 V
These are approximate textbook values, not universal constants. The actual built-in potential depends on doping concentrations and temperature.

Importance of Barrier Potential

  • Opposes further majority-carrier diffusion.
  • Influences PN junction current flow.
  • Changes when external bias is applied.
  • Is important in diode operation.

9. Drift Current in a PN Junction

The built-in electric field causes minority carriers near the depletion region to drift across the junction.

Electron Drift

Electrons are forced opposite to the electric field, from the P-side toward the N-side when they are present in or near the depletion region.

Hole Drift

Holes are forced in the direction of the electric field, from the N-side toward the P-side when they are present in or near the depletion region.

Drift current is associated with carriers swept across the depletion region by the built-in electric field.

10. Equilibrium Condition of PN Junction

At thermal equilibrium, the PN junction has no externally applied voltage and its temperature is constant.

Diffusion current and drift current balance each other for electrons and holes.

Electron Current

Jn(diffusion) + Jn(drift) = 0

Hole Current

Jp(diffusion) + Jp(drift) = 0

Total Current

Jtotal = 0
At equilibrium, the net current through the junction is zero. This does not mean that individual carrier movements stop. Drift and diffusion processes continue but balance one another.

11. PN Junction Energy-Band Diagram

An energy-band diagram represents the energy levels of electrons in a semiconductor.

Important Energy Levels

  • Conduction band edge (Ec).
  • Valence band edge (Ev).
  • Forbidden energy gap (Eg).
  • Fermi level (Ef).

Energy-Band Behaviour at Equilibrium

When P-type and N-type regions form a junction, charge redistribution produces band bending near the junction.

At thermal equilibrium, the Fermi level becomes constant throughout the junction.

P-Type Region
Band Bending
N-Type Region

Equilibrium Fermi level: constant across the junction.

Placement Point: At thermal equilibrium, the Fermi level is flat across a PN junction. The conduction and valence band edges bend because of the built-in electrostatic potential.

12. Width of Depletion Region

The depletion region extends into both P-type and N-type sides of the junction.

Its width depends on doping concentration, temperature and applied voltage.

Effect of Doping Concentration

  • A heavily doped side generally has a narrower depletion region.
  • A lightly doped side generally has a wider depletion region.
  • The depletion region extends farther into the more lightly doped side.

Charge Balance

For an ideal abrupt junction under equilibrium, charge neutrality across the depletion region gives:

NA xP = ND xN

Where:

  • NA = Acceptor concentration.
  • ND = Donor concentration.
  • xP = Depletion width into the P-side.
  • xN = Depletion width into the N-side.
The depletion width is inversely related to doping concentration on each side through the charge-balance relationship.

13. Effect of External Voltage on the PN Junction

Applying an external voltage changes the potential barrier and depletion width.

Forward Bias

  • P-side is connected to the positive terminal.
  • N-side is connected to the negative terminal.
  • The external voltage opposes the built-in potential.
  • The depletion region becomes narrower.
  • The barrier is reduced and substantial current can flow.

Reverse Bias

  • P-side is connected to the negative terminal.
  • N-side is connected to the positive terminal.
  • The external voltage reinforces the built-in potential.
  • The depletion region becomes wider.
  • Only a small reverse leakage current flows before breakdown.
Forward and reverse bias will be studied in detail in the next chapter on semiconductor diodes and their applications.

14. Important Differences

Term Meaning
PN Junction Interface between P-type and N-type regions.
Diffusion Carrier movement caused by concentration gradient.
Recombination Combination of an electron and a hole.
Depletion Region Region depleted of most mobile majority carriers.
Built-in Electric Field Internal electric field created by fixed ionized dopants.
Barrier Potential Potential difference produced by the built-in field.
Equilibrium Condition where drift and diffusion currents balance.
