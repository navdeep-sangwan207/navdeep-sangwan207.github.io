Lesson 7: Precision Rectifier

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Understand how op-amps and diodes rectify very small AC signals accurately.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the working principle of a precision rectifier.
  • Explain why an ordinary diode cannot accurately rectify very small signals.
  • Understand the super-diode circuit.
  • Differentiate between half-wave and full-wave precision rectifiers.
  • Understand circuit operation and output waveforms.
  • Identify practical applications of precision rectifiers.

2. Introduction

A Precision Rectifier is an electronic circuit that uses an operational amplifier and diodes to rectify AC signals, including signals with amplitudes smaller than the forward voltage drop of an ordinary diode.

A conventional silicon diode has a forward voltage drop of approximately 0.7 V under typical operating conditions. Therefore, very small input signals may not be rectified accurately using a simple diode circuit.

A precision rectifier uses the op-amp's feedback action to compensate for the diode voltage drop.

Conventional silicon diode forward drop ≈ 0.7 V

Precision rectifier → Can rectify signals smaller than 0.7 V

3. Why Is a Precision Rectifier Required?

Consider a small AC input signal with a peak amplitude of 100 mV.

A conventional silicon diode may not conduct sufficiently because the input voltage is much smaller than its typical forward voltage drop.

As a result, the output of an ordinary diode rectifier may be very small or distorted.

4. Basic Precision Rectifier (Super Diode)

A basic precision rectifier, often called a super diode, uses an op-amp with a diode in its feedback path.

The op-amp adjusts its output to overcome the diode's forward voltage drop. When the diode conducts, negative feedback forces the output voltage to closely follow the input voltage.

This diagram illustrates the feedback principle rather than a complete connection-level schematic. A practical super-diode circuit must connect the diode between the op-amp output and the inverting feedback node, with the input applied to the non-inverting terminal.

Working Principle

When the input is positive, the op-amp output moves positive enough to forward-bias the diode. Negative feedback then makes the output follow the input.

When the input is negative, the diode becomes reverse biased and the output is approximately zero in the basic half-wave configuration.

For positive input:
Vout ≈ Vin

For negative input:
Vout ≈ 0 V

5. Precision Half-Wave Rectifier

A precision half-wave rectifier allows one polarity of the input signal to appear at the output while suppressing the opposite polarity.

For a positive half-wave precision rectifier:

Vout = Vin, when Vin > 0

Vout ≈ 0, when Vin < 0

Operation

  • During the positive half-cycle, the op-amp drives the diode into conduction.
  • Negative feedback makes the output follow the input.
  • During the negative half-cycle, the diode is reverse biased.
  • The output remains approximately zero.

The rectified output contains only the positive portions of the input waveform.

6. Precision Full-Wave Rectifier

A precision full-wave rectifier produces an output proportional to the absolute value of the input signal. Both positive and negative half-cycles appear as positive output pulses.

Vout = |Vin|

Practical full-wave precision rectifiers use suitable op-amp and diode networks. A common implementation uses two op-amp stages.

Working Principle

  • During the positive half-cycle, the circuit routes the signal to the output with positive polarity.
  • During the negative half-cycle, another feedback path reverses its polarity.
  • Both half-cycles therefore appear positive at the output.

For an ideal full-wave rectified sine wave, the output frequency is twice the input frequency.

Output Frequency = 2 × Input Frequency

8. Numerical Example

A precision full-wave rectifier receives a sinusoidal input with a peak voltage of 200 mV and frequency of 50 Hz.

Determine:

  1. Peak output voltage.
  2. Output frequency.
  3. Average output voltage for an ideal full-wave rectified sine wave.

Step 1: Peak Output Voltage

Vout(peak) = Vin(peak)

Vout(peak) = 200 mV

Step 2: Output Frequency

fout = 2 × fin

fout = 2 × 50

fout = 100 Hz

Step 3: Average Output Voltage

Vavg = 2Vm / π

Vavg = (2 × 0.2) / 3.1416

Vavg ≈ 0.1273 V

Final Answer:

  • Peak output = 200 mV
  • Output frequency = 100 Hz
  • Average output voltage ≈ 127.3 mV

9. Applications

  • AC signal measurement: Rectifies small AC signals for measurement circuits.
  • Instrumentation: Used in precision measurement systems.
  • Signal processing: Produces absolute-value signals for further processing.
  • AC voltmeters: Used in signal conditioning stages of measurement equipment.
  • Audio circuits: Used in envelope detection and signal-level measurement.
  • Sensor interfaces: Processes small bipolar sensor signals.
  • Peak detection: Can be combined with additional circuitry to detect signal peaks.

10. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Can rectify very small AC signals.
  • Compensates for diode forward voltage drop through feedback.
  • Provides improved accuracy for low-level signal processing.
  • Useful in instrumentation and measurement systems.

Limitations

  • Requires an operational amplifier and additional components.
  • Bandwidth is limited by the op-amp.
  • Slew rate can affect high-frequency signal accuracy.
  • Some configurations experience recovery delays when diodes switch.
  • Input and output ranges depend on the selected op-amp and supply voltage.

11. Practical Design Considerations

  • Select an op-amp with suitable bandwidth and slew rate.
  • Check the input common-mode voltage range.
  • Ensure the op-amp output can drive the required diode current.
  • Use suitable low-leakage, fast-switching diodes where necessary.
  • Choose resistor values to obtain the required gain.
  • Check the circuit's response near zero crossing.
  • Use appropriate supply voltages and decoupling capacitors.
Precision rectifiers are generally used for low-level signal processing, not as replacements for high-current power rectifiers.

12. Interview Questions

Q1. What is a precision rectifier?

A precision rectifier uses an op-amp and diodes to rectify small AC signals accurately.

Q2. Why is it called a super diode?

The op-amp compensates for the diode forward voltage drop through feedback, allowing accurate rectification of small signals.

Q3. What is the typical forward voltage drop of a silicon diode?

Approximately 0.7 V under typical operating conditions.

Q4. What is the output of a full-wave precision rectifier?

The output is proportional to the absolute value of the input signal.

Q5. What is the output frequency of a full-wave rectifier?

Twice the input frequency.

Q6. Why is an op-amp used?

It provides gain and feedback action to compensate for the diode's forward voltage drop.

Q7. Can a precision rectifier rectify a 100 mV signal?

Yes, provided the selected op-amp and circuit configuration support the signal amplitude and frequency.

Q8. Where are precision rectifiers used?

They are used in instrumentation, AC measurement, signal processing, and sensor interface circuits.

  • A precision rectifier uses op-amps and diodes.
