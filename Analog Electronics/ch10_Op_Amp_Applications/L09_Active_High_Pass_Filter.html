Lesson 9: Active High-Pass Filter

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Understand how an active high-pass filter passes high-frequency signals and attenuates low-frequency signals.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the concept of an active high-pass filter.
  • Explain the role of an op-amp in an active HPF.
  • Understand cutoff frequency and voltage gain.
  • Derive the cutoff frequency equation.
  • Analyze the frequency response.
  • Identify practical applications of active HPFs.

2. Introduction

An Active High-Pass Filter (HPF) is an electronic circuit that allows high-frequency signals to pass while attenuating low-frequency signals.

It uses an operational amplifier along with resistors and capacitors. The op-amp can provide voltage gain and buffering.

The filter passes frequencies above a specified cutoff frequency and attenuates frequencies below it.

Passband: f > fc

Stopband: f < fc

3. What Is a High-Pass Filter?

A high-pass filter allows high-frequency components to pass while reducing low-frequency components.

For example, if an HPF has a cutoff frequency of 1 kHz:

  • A 100 Hz signal is attenuated.
  • A 1 kHz signal is at the cutoff frequency.
  • A 10 kHz signal passes with approximately its passband gain.
At the cutoff frequency, the output magnitude is approximately 70.7% of its passband value, corresponding to −3 dB.

4. Circuit Diagram: First-Order Active HPF

A common first-order active high-pass filter uses a non-inverting op-amp configuration.

The input capacitor is connected in series with the signal, while the resistor provides a path to ground. This RC network drives the non-inverting input of the op-amp.

The op-amp feedback network consists of Rf from output to the inverting input and R1 from the inverting input to ground. The RC network determines the first-order high-pass response.

5. Working Principle

At Low Frequencies

At low frequencies, capacitive reactance is high. The capacitor blocks most of the input signal, so the voltage reaching the op-amp input is small.

At High Frequencies

At high frequencies, capacitive reactance decreases. The capacitor allows the input signal to reach the op-amp input with less attenuation.

Capacitive Reactance:

XC = 1 / (2πfC)

As frequency increases, capacitive reactance decreases, allowing more of the input signal to pass through.

6. Cutoff Frequency

The cutoff frequency is the frequency at which the filter output reaches approximately 70.7% of its passband magnitude.

fc = 1 / (2πRC)

Where:

  • fc = Cutoff frequency in Hz
  • R = Resistance in ohms
  • C = Capacitance in farads

This equation applies to the first-order RC input network.

7. Voltage Gain

The passband gain of the non-inverting op-amp configuration is:

Av = 1 + (Rf / R1)

The ideal first-order magnitude response is:

|H(f)| = Av / √[1 + (fc/f)²]

At frequencies much lower than fc, the output is strongly attenuated. At frequencies much higher than fc, the gain approaches Av.

At f = fc:

|H(fc)| = 0.707 × Av

8. Frequency Response

The frequency response shows:

  • Low frequencies are attenuated.
  • Gain increases as frequency approaches cutoff.
  • At cutoff frequency, gain is 0.707 times passband gain.
  • High frequencies pass with approximately constant gain.
First-order HPF roll-off:

+20 dB/decade below cutoff frequency

9. Numerical Example

Given:

  • R = 10 kΩ
  • C = 0.01 µF
  • Rf = 10 kΩ
  • R1 = 10 kΩ

Step 1: Calculate Cutoff Frequency

fc = 1 / (2πRC)

fc = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × 10000 × 0.01 × 10⁻⁶)

fc ≈ 1591.5 Hz

Step 2: Calculate Passband Gain

Av = 1 + (Rf / R1)

Av = 1 + (10000 / 10000)

Av = 2

Step 3: Calculate Gain at Cutoff

Av(fc) = 0.707 × 2

Av(fc) = 1.414

Final Answer:

  • Cutoff frequency ≈ 1.59 kHz
  • Passband gain = 2
  • Gain at cutoff ≈ 1.414

10. Active HPF vs Passive HPF

Parameter Passive HPF Active HPF
Components Resistors and capacitors Op-amp, resistors, capacitors
External power supply Not normally required Required for op-amp
Amplification No active voltage gain Can provide voltage gain
Buffering Not inherent Can provide buffering
Loading effect Can be significant Can be reduced

11. Applications

  • Audio systems: Removes unwanted low-frequency components.
  • Signal conditioning: Removes DC offsets and low-frequency disturbances.
  • Instrumentation: Helps isolate higher-frequency signal components.
  • Communication systems: Used in signal-processing stages.
  • Vibration measurement: Can remove slow variations from vibration signals.
  • Sensor circuits: Helps reject unwanted low-frequency variations when appropriate.
  • Waveform processing: Used in coupling and pulse-conditioning circuits.

12. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Can provide voltage amplification.
  • Offers high input impedance.
  • Can provide low output impedance.
  • Useful for removing low-frequency components.
  • Can be cascaded to form higher-order filters.

Limitations

  • Requires an external power supply.
  • Op-amp bandwidth limits high-frequency operation.
  • Slew rate may limit large-signal performance.
  • Component tolerances affect cutoff frequency.
  • Noise and offset can affect circuit performance.

13. Interview Questions

Q1. What is an active high-pass filter?

It is a filter that passes high-frequency signals and attenuates low-frequency signals using an active device such as an op-amp.

Q2. What is the function of the capacitor?

It blocks or attenuates low-frequency signals and allows higher-frequency signals to pass.

Q3. What is cutoff frequency?

It is the frequency at which the output magnitude is approximately 70.7% of its passband value.

Q4. What is the roll-off rate of a first-order HPF?

Approximately +20 dB/decade below cutoff frequency.

Q5. What is the gain at cutoff frequency?

Approximately 0.707 times the passband gain.

Q6. What happens to capacitive reactance when frequency increases?

Capacitive reactance decreases.

Q7. What is the difference between LPF and HPF?

An LPF passes low frequencies, whereas an HPF passes high frequencies.

Q8. Why are HPFs used in audio circuits?

They can remove unwanted low-frequency components and help separate frequency bands.

15. Summary

  • An active HPF passes high-frequency signals and attenuates low-frequency signals.
  • It uses an op-amp, resistors, and capacitors.
  • The cutoff frequency is fc = 1/(2πRC) for the first-order RC network.
  • At cutoff, gain is 0.707 times the passband gain.
  • A first-order HPF has a +20 dB/decade rising response below cutoff.
  • Active HPFs can provide amplification and buffering.
  • They are used in audio, instrumentation, communication, and signal conditioning.
