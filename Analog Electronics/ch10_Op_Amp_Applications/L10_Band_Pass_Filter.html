Lesson 10: Band-Pass Filter

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Learn how to select and amplify signals within a particular frequency range.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the concept of a band-pass filter.
  • Explain the operation of active band-pass filters.
  • Understand lower and upper cutoff frequencies.
  • Calculate bandwidth and center frequency.
  • Understand quality factor (Q).
  • Identify practical applications of band-pass filters.

2. Introduction

A Band-Pass Filter (BPF) is an electronic circuit that allows signals within a selected frequency range to pass while attenuating frequencies below and above that range.

An active band-pass filter uses an operational amplifier along with resistors and capacitors. It can provide amplification and buffering.

Lower Cutoff Frequency: fL

Upper Cutoff Frequency: fH

Passband: fL < f < fH

3. Working Principle

A band-pass filter can be formed by combining a high-pass filter and a low-pass filter.

  • The high-pass stage attenuates frequencies below fL.
  • The low-pass stage attenuates frequencies above fH.
  • Frequencies between fL and fH form the passband.

The high-pass stage determines the lower cutoff frequency, while the low-pass stage determines the upper cutoff frequency.

The block diagram illustrates the filtering concept. A practical active BPF can use cascaded active stages or a dedicated multiple-feedback or state-variable filter configuration.

4. Lower and Upper Cutoff Frequencies

Lower Cutoff Frequency (fL)

The lower cutoff frequency is the lower boundary of the passband. Frequencies below this value are attenuated.

Upper Cutoff Frequency (fH)

The upper cutoff frequency is the upper boundary of the passband. Frequencies above this value are attenuated.

Lower Cutoff Frequency:
fL = 1 / (2πR1C1)

Upper Cutoff Frequency:
fH = 1 / (2πR2C2)

These equations apply to the individual first-order RC sections when their loading is negligible or they are properly buffered.

5. Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the range of frequencies between the lower and upper cutoff frequencies.

BW = fH − fL

Where:

  • BW = Bandwidth in Hz
  • fH = Upper cutoff frequency in Hz
  • fL = Lower cutoff frequency in Hz

A wider bandwidth means a larger range of frequencies is allowed to pass.

6. Center Frequency

The center frequency is the frequency at which the band-pass filter has its maximum response in a conventional band-pass design.

f0 = √(fL × fH)

The center frequency is the geometric mean of the lower and upper cutoff frequencies.

7. Quality Factor (Q)

The quality factor indicates how selective a band-pass filter is around its center frequency.

Q = f0 / BW

Q = f0 / (fH − fL)
  • High Q: Narrower bandwidth and greater selectivity.
  • Low Q: Wider bandwidth and lower selectivity.
For a standard second-order band-pass response, Q describes the relationship between center frequency and bandwidth measured at the −3 dB points.

8. Frequency Response

The response has three main regions:

  • Lower stopband: Frequencies below fL are attenuated.
  • Passband: Frequencies between fL and fH pass.
  • Upper stopband: Frequencies above fH are attenuated.
This is a conceptual response curve. The exact peak shape and slopes depend on the filter order, topology, and quality factor.

9. Numerical Example

Given:

  • Lower cutoff frequency, fL = 500 Hz
  • Upper cutoff frequency, fH = 5000 Hz

Step 1: Calculate Bandwidth

BW = fH − fL

BW = 5000 − 500

BW = 4500 Hz

Step 2: Calculate Center Frequency

f0 = √(fL × fH)

f0 = √(500 × 5000)

f0 = √2500000

f0 ≈ 1581.1 Hz

Step 3: Calculate Quality Factor

Q = f0 / BW

Q = 1581.1 / 4500

Final Answer:

  • Bandwidth = 4500 Hz
  • Center frequency ≈ 1.58 kHz
  • Quality factor ≈ 0.35

10. Types of Band-Pass Filters

1. Passive Band-Pass Filter

Uses resistors, capacitors, and sometimes inductors to select a frequency band. It does not provide active voltage amplification.

2. Active Band-Pass Filter

Uses an op-amp with resistors and capacitors. It can provide amplification and buffering.

3. Wide Band-Pass Filter

Designed to pass a relatively broad range of frequencies.

4. Narrow Band-Pass Filter

Designed to select a narrow frequency range around a center frequency.

11. Applications

  • Audio systems: Selects specific audio frequency bands.
  • Communication systems: Selects a desired signal channel.
  • Instrumentation: Extracts signals within a required frequency range.
  • Biomedical equipment: Used in signal-conditioning stages for physiological signals.
  • Wireless receivers: Helps select a desired frequency band.
  • Vibration analysis: Isolates vibration components in a selected frequency range.
  • Tone control circuits: Selects particular audio frequency ranges.

12. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Selects a specific range of frequencies.
  • Active designs can provide voltage gain.
  • Can provide high input impedance and low output impedance.
  • Useful in noise reduction and signal selection.
  • Can be designed for different bandwidths.

Limitations

  • Requires an external power supply for active designs.
  • Component tolerances affect cutoff frequencies.
  • Op-amp bandwidth limits high-frequency performance.
  • High-Q designs can be sensitive to component variations.
  • Design may require careful adjustment and testing.

13. Interview Questions

Q1. What is a band-pass filter?

A filter that allows a selected range of frequencies to pass while attenuating frequencies outside that range.

Q2. What are lower and upper cutoff frequencies?

They are the lower and upper boundaries of the filter's passband.

Q3. What is bandwidth?

Bandwidth is the difference between upper and lower cutoff frequencies.

Q4. What is center frequency?

It is the frequency of maximum response for a conventional band-pass filter.

Q5. What is quality factor?

Q is the ratio of center frequency to bandwidth. It indicates the selectivity of the filter.

Q6. What happens when Q increases?

For a fixed center frequency, bandwidth decreases and frequency selectivity increases.

Q7. How can a band-pass filter be constructed?

It can be constructed by combining high-pass and low-pass filtering stages or by using a dedicated active filter topology.

Q8. What is the difference between LPF, HPF, and BPF?

LPF passes low frequencies, HPF passes high frequencies, and BPF passes a selected range of frequencies.

15. Summary

  • A band-pass filter passes a selected frequency range.
  • It has lower and upper cutoff frequencies.
  • Bandwidth is BW = fH − fL.
  • Center frequency is f0 = √(fL × fH) for the conventional band-pass definition.
  • Quality factor is Q = f0/BW.
  • Active BPFs use op-amps, resistors, and capacitors.
  • They are used in communication, audio, instrumentation, and signal processing.
