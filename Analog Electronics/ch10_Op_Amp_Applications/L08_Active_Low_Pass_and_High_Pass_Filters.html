Lesson 8: Active Low-Pass Filter

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Understand how an op-amp filter passes low-frequency signals and attenuates high-frequency signals.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the working principle of an active low-pass filter.
  • Explain the role of an operational amplifier in filtering.
  • Understand cutoff frequency and passband gain.
  • Derive the cutoff frequency equation.
  • Analyze the frequency response.
  • Identify practical applications of active low-pass filters.

2. Introduction

An Active Low-Pass Filter (LPF) is an electronic circuit that allows low-frequency signals to pass while attenuating high-frequency signals.

It uses an operational amplifier along with resistors and capacitors. The operational amplifier provides amplification and buffering.

The filter passes frequencies below a specified cutoff frequency and reduces the amplitude of frequencies above that frequency.

Passband: f < fc

Stopband: f > fc

3. What Is a Low-Pass Filter?

A low-pass filter allows low-frequency components to pass with little attenuation while reducing higher-frequency components.

For example, if an LPF has a cutoff frequency of 1 kHz:

  • A 100 Hz signal passes with little attenuation.
  • A 1 kHz signal is at the cutoff frequency.
  • A 10 kHz signal is significantly attenuated.
The cutoff frequency is conventionally defined as the frequency at which the output magnitude falls to approximately 70.7% of its passband value.

4. Circuit Diagram: First-Order Active LPF

A common first-order active low-pass filter uses a non-inverting op-amp configuration.

Circuit clarification: The standard first-order non-inverting active LPF has an RC network at the non-inverting input and a separate resistor feedback network at the inverting input. The RC network consists of a series resistor and a capacitor connected from the input node to ground.

5. Working Principle

At Low Frequencies

At low frequencies, the capacitive reactance is high. The capacitor has little effect on the input signal, so the signal reaches the op-amp input.

At High Frequencies

At high frequencies, capacitive reactance becomes low. The capacitor provides a path to ground for high-frequency components, reducing the voltage at the op-amp input.

Capacitive Reactance:

XC = 1 / (2πfC)

As frequency increases, capacitive reactance decreases. This is why high-frequency signals are attenuated.

6. Cutoff Frequency

The cutoff frequency is the frequency at which the filter's gain decreases to 0.707 times its passband gain.

fc = 1 / (2πRC)

Where:

  • fc = Cutoff frequency in Hz
  • R = Filter resistance in ohms
  • C = Filter capacitance in farads

This equation applies to the first-order RC section of the filter.

7. Voltage Gain

For a non-inverting active low-pass filter, the passband voltage gain is determined by the op-amp's feedback resistors.

Av = 1 + (Rf / R1)

Where:

  • Rf = Feedback resistor
  • R1 = Resistor between the inverting input and ground

The ideal first-order magnitude response is:

|H(f)| = Av / √[1 + (f/fc)²]

At frequencies much lower than fc, the gain is approximately Av. At the cutoff frequency, the gain is 0.707Av.

8. Frequency Response

The frequency response shows that:

  • Low frequencies pass with approximately constant gain.
  • At cutoff frequency, gain falls by 3 dB.
  • Above cutoff frequency, gain decreases.
  • A first-order LPF has a roll-off of approximately −20 dB/decade.
Gain at cutoff = 0.707 × Passband Gain

Attenuation at cutoff = −3 dB

9. Numerical Example

An active low-pass filter has:

  • R = 10 kΩ
  • C = 0.01 µF
  • Rf = 10 kΩ
  • R1 = 10 kΩ

Step 1: Calculate Cutoff Frequency

fc = 1 / (2πRC)

fc = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × 10000 × 0.01 × 10⁻⁶)

fc ≈ 1591.5 Hz

Step 2: Calculate Passband Gain

Av = 1 + (Rf / R1)

Av = 1 + (10000 / 10000)

Av = 2

Step 3: Calculate Gain at Cutoff

Av(fc) = 0.707 × 2

Av(fc) = 1.414

Final Answer:

  • Cutoff frequency ≈ 1.59 kHz
  • Passband gain = 2
  • Gain at cutoff ≈ 1.414

10. Active vs Passive Low-Pass Filter

Parameter Passive LPF Active LPF
Components Resistors and capacitors Op-amp, resistors, capacitors
External power supply Not normally required Required for op-amp
Amplification No active voltage gain Can provide voltage gain
Buffering Not inherent Can provide buffering
Loading effect Can be significant Can be reduced

11. Applications

  • Audio systems: Reduces high-frequency noise and unwanted components.
  • Sensor circuits: Filters high-frequency noise from sensor signals.
  • Power supplies: Used in signal-conditioning stages to reduce unwanted ripple components.
  • Instrumentation: Removes high-frequency interference from measurement signals.
  • Communication systems: Used in baseband and signal-conditioning circuits.
  • Embedded systems: Provides analog filtering before ADC inputs.
  • Control systems: Smooths noisy feedback signals.

12. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Can provide voltage amplification.
  • Offers high input impedance.
  • Can provide low output impedance.
  • Useful for low-frequency signal conditioning.
  • Can be cascaded to create higher-order filters.

Limitations

  • Requires an external power supply.
  • Bandwidth is limited by the op-amp.
  • Slew rate can affect large-amplitude high-frequency signals.
  • Component tolerances affect cutoff frequency.
  • Noise and offset may affect low-level signals.

13. Interview Questions

Q1. What is an active low-pass filter?

It is a filter using an active device such as an op-amp to pass low frequencies and attenuate high frequencies.

Q2. Why is an op-amp used in an active filter?

It provides amplification, buffering, and improved control of filter characteristics.

Q3. What is cutoff frequency?

It is the frequency at which the output magnitude becomes approximately 70.7% of the passband value.

Q4. What is the roll-off rate of a first-order LPF?

Approximately −20 dB/decade.

Q5. What is the gain at cutoff frequency?

Approximately 0.707 times the passband gain.

Q6. What happens to capacitive reactance as frequency increases?

It decreases.

Q7. What is the difference between active and passive filters?

Active filters use active components and can provide gain, while passive filters use passive components and cannot provide active voltage amplification.

Q8. Why is an LPF used before an ADC?

It helps attenuate unwanted high-frequency components and can act as an anti-aliasing filter.

15. Summary

  • An active low-pass filter passes low-frequency signals and attenuates high-frequency signals.
  • It uses an op-amp, resistors, and capacitors.
  • The cutoff frequency is fc = 1/(2πRC) for the first-order RC network.
  • At cutoff, gain is 0.707 times the passband gain.
  • A first-order LPF has a roll-off of −20 dB/decade.
  • Active filters can provide amplification and buffering.
  • They are widely used in audio, instrumentation, sensor circuits, and ADC signal conditioning.
