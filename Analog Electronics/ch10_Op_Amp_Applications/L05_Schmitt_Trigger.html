Lesson 5: Schmitt Trigger

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Understand positive feedback, hysteresis, and noise-resistant switching circuits.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the working principle of a Schmitt trigger.
  • Explain the role of positive feedback.

  • Calculate upper and lower threshold voltages.

  • Understand practical applications of Schmitt triggers.

2. Introduction

A Schmitt Trigger is a comparator circuit that uses positive feedback to provide two different switching threshold voltages.

Unlike a basic comparator, a Schmitt trigger has hysteresis. This means the voltage at which the output switches from LOW to HIGH is different from the voltage at which it switches from HIGH to LOW.

This feature prevents unwanted output transitions caused by small noise signals around the switching threshold.

Hysteresis = Upper Threshold Voltage − Lower Threshold Voltage

VH = VUT − VLT

3. Why Do We Need a Schmitt Trigger?

Consider a noisy input signal applied to a basic comparator. If the signal fluctuates around the reference voltage, the comparator output may switch repeatedly.

These unwanted transitions are called chattering. A Schmitt trigger solves this problem by introducing two switching thresholds.

Basic Comparator Schmitt Trigger
Single switching threshold Two switching thresholds
No intentional hysteresis Has hysteresis
More sensitive to noise near threshold Better noise immunity
Normally uses no positive feedback Uses positive feedback

4. Positive Feedback

Positive feedback means a portion of the output voltage is fed back to the non-inverting input in a way that reinforces the existing output state.

When the output is HIGH, the feedback raises the switching threshold. When the output is LOW, the feedback lowers the switching threshold.

Positive Feedback → Two Switching Thresholds → Hysteresis

The output therefore does not change state until the input crosses the appropriate threshold.

5. Inverting Schmitt Trigger

In an inverting Schmitt trigger, the input signal is applied to the inverting terminal. A resistor network connects the output to the non-inverting terminal and establishes positive feedback.

Circuit note: This is a conceptual illustration of an inverting Schmitt trigger. The positive-input node is connected to the output through a feedback resistor and to ground through a reference resistor. The input is applied to the inverting terminal.

Working Principle

When the input voltage rises above the upper threshold voltage, the output switches from positive saturation to negative saturation.

When the input voltage falls below the lower threshold voltage, the output switches from negative saturation to positive saturation.

Vin > VUT → Vout ≈ −Vsat

Vin < VLT → Vout ≈ +Vsat

Between VLT and VUT, the output retains its previous state.

6. Upper and Lower Threshold Voltages

Consider an inverting Schmitt trigger with:

  • R1 connected between output and the non-inverting input.
  • R2 connected between the non-inverting input and ground.
  • Input signal connected to the inverting terminal.

The feedback network forms a voltage divider. The voltage at the non-inverting input depends on the output state.

β = R2 / (R1 + R2)

VUT = +βVsat

VLT = −βVsat

These equations assume symmetrical output saturation levels of +Vsat and −Vsat, with the lower resistor connected to ground.

Hysteresis Voltage

VH = VUT − VLT

VH = 2βVsat
If the positive and negative output saturation magnitudes are unequal, calculate the two thresholds separately using the actual output levels.

7. Numerical Example

An inverting Schmitt trigger has the following parameters:

  • R1 = 10 kΩ
  • R2 = 10 kΩ
  • Output saturation voltage = ±12 V

Step 1: Calculate Feedback Factor

β = R2 / (R1 + R2)

β = 10 / (10 + 10)

β = 0.5

Step 2: Calculate Upper Threshold Voltage

VUT = β × Vsat

VUT = 0.5 × 12

VUT = +6 V

Step 3: Calculate Lower Threshold Voltage

VLT = −β × Vsat

VLT = −0.5 × 12

VLT = −6 V

Step 4: Calculate Hysteresis Voltage

VH = VUT − VLT

VH = 6 − (−6)

VH = 12 V

Final Answer:

  • Upper threshold voltage = +6 V
  • Lower threshold voltage = −6 V
  • Hysteresis voltage = 12 V

8. Hysteresis Transfer Characteristics

The transfer characteristic shows how the output changes as the input voltage increases and decreases.

For an inverting Schmitt trigger:

  • As input rises and crosses VUT, output switches HIGH to LOW.
  • As input falls and crosses VLT, output switches LOW to HIGH.
  • Between the two thresholds, output depends on its previous state.
The transfer characteristic is illustrative. The horizontal sections represent the two output saturation states.

9. Non-Inverting Schmitt Trigger

In a non-inverting Schmitt trigger, the input signal is applied to the non-inverting terminal, while positive feedback is provided to the same terminal through a resistor network. The inverting terminal receives the reference voltage.

For a symmetrical reference of 0 V and symmetrical output saturation levels:

VUT = +βVsat

VLT = −βVsat

Its switching direction is different from the inverting configuration. When the input rises above the upper threshold, the output switches to positive saturation. When the input falls below the lower threshold, the output switches to negative saturation.

Parameter Inverting Schmitt Trigger Non-Inverting Schmitt Trigger
Input terminal Inverting (−) Non-inverting (+)
Feedback Positive feedback Positive feedback
Output for rising input above VUT Negative saturation Positive saturation
Output for falling input below VLT Positive saturation Negative saturation

10. Applications

  • Noise removal: Reduces unwanted switching due to small input fluctuations.
  • Switch debouncing: Helps produce clean digital transitions from mechanical switch signals.
  • Wave shaping: Converts slowly varying signals into sharp digital-like transitions.
  • Square-wave generation: Used in oscillator circuits with suitable timing components.
  • Sensor interfacing: Converts noisy sensor signals into stable switching signals.
  • Signal conditioning: Improves the reliability of threshold detection.
  • Digital circuits: Used in input conditioning and pulse-shaping circuits.

11. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Good noise immunity around switching thresholds.
  • Prevents unwanted repeated output transitions.
  • Provides clean switching transitions.
  • Simple circuit implementation.
  • Useful for analog-to-digital signal conditioning.

Limitations

  • Threshold voltages depend on resistor values and output levels.
  • Hysteresis width may require careful design.
  • Input and output voltage ranges depend on the selected op-amp.
  • Op-amp propagation delay and saturation recovery can limit switching speed.

12. Interview Questions

Q1. What is a Schmitt trigger?

A Schmitt trigger is a comparator circuit with positive feedback that provides two switching thresholds.

Q2. What is hysteresis?

Hysteresis is the difference between the upper and lower switching threshold voltages.

Q3. Why is positive feedback used?

Positive feedback creates two distinct switching thresholds and improves noise immunity.

Q4. What are UTP and LTP?

UTP is the Upper Trigger Point, and LTP is the Lower Trigger Point. They are also commonly called the upper and lower threshold voltages.

Q5. What is the hysteresis voltage for symmetrical thresholds?

VH = VUT − VLT. For thresholds of +6 V and −6 V, the hysteresis voltage is 12 V.

Q6. What is the main advantage of a Schmitt trigger over a basic comparator?

It provides better noise immunity by preventing repeated switching near a single threshold.

Q7. Which feedback is used in a Schmitt trigger?

Positive feedback.

Q8. Can a Schmitt trigger convert a sine wave into a square wave?

Yes. With suitable threshold levels, it can convert a sinusoidal input into a square-like output.

14. Summary

  • A Schmitt trigger is a comparator with positive feedback.
  • It has two switching thresholds: upper and lower.
  • The difference between the thresholds is called hysteresis voltage.
  • Hysteresis improves noise immunity.
  • Inverting and non-inverting configurations have different switching polarities.
  • Schmitt triggers are used in waveform shaping, switch debouncing, and sensor interfacing.
