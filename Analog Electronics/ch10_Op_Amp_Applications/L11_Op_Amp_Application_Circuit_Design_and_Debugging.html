Lesson 11: Band-Stop Filter

Analog Electronics | Chapter 10: Op-Amp Applications

Understand how a filter rejects a selected frequency range while allowing frequencies outside that range to pass.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the concept of a band-stop filter.
  • Explain the working principle of an active band-stop filter.
  • Understand lower and upper cutoff frequencies.
  • Calculate bandwidth and notch frequency.
  • Understand quality factor (Q).
  • Identify practical applications of band-stop filters.

2. Introduction

A Band-Stop Filter (BSF) is an electronic circuit that attenuates signals within a selected frequency range while allowing frequencies below and above that range to pass.

An active band-stop filter uses an operational amplifier along with resistors and capacitors to achieve the required filtering response.

Lower Cutoff Frequency = fL

Upper Cutoff Frequency = fH

Rejected Band: fL < f < fH

3. Working Principle

A band-stop filter can be understood as the opposite of a band-pass filter.

  • Frequencies below fL are allowed to pass.
  • Frequencies between fL and fH are attenuated.
  • Frequencies above fH are allowed to pass.

A band-stop response can be implemented using suitable combinations of low-pass and high-pass paths, or using a dedicated active filter topology.

This is a conceptual block diagram, not a complete component-level circuit. Practical active band-stop filters can use multiple-feedback or state-variable configurations.

4. Cutoff Frequencies

Lower Cutoff Frequency (fL)

The lower cutoff frequency marks the beginning of the rejected frequency band.

Upper Cutoff Frequency (fH)

The upper cutoff frequency marks the end of the rejected frequency band.

fL = Lower Cutoff Frequency

fH = Upper Cutoff Frequency

At the cutoff frequencies, the response is conventionally measured at −3 dB relative to the passband level.

5. Bandwidth

The bandwidth of a band-stop filter is the width of the rejected frequency range.

BW = fH − fL

Where:

  • BW = Rejected bandwidth in Hz
  • fH = Upper cutoff frequency in Hz
  • fL = Lower cutoff frequency in Hz

6. Notch Frequency

The notch frequency is the frequency at which the filter provides maximum attenuation.

f0 = √(fL × fH)

For a conventional second-order notch response, f0 is the geometric mean of the lower and upper cutoff frequencies.

An ideal notch filter can theoretically produce zero output at its notch frequency. A practical circuit has finite attenuation due to component tolerances and circuit limitations.

7. Quality Factor (Q)

The quality factor describes the selectivity of a band-stop filter around its notch frequency.

Q = f0 / BW

Q = f0 / (fH − fL)
  • High Q: Narrow rejected frequency band.
  • Low Q: Wider rejected frequency band.

8. Frequency Response

The frequency response contains three regions:

  • Lower passband: Frequencies below fL pass.
  • Stopband: Frequencies between fL and fH are attenuated.
  • Upper passband: Frequencies above fH pass.
The response shown is conceptual. The actual notch depth and shape depend on filter topology, component matching, and quality factor.

9. Notch Filter

A notch filter is a narrow band-stop filter that strongly attenuates a particular frequency or a very narrow range of frequencies.

A common application is the rejection of power-line interference.

  • 50 Hz notch filter: Used to suppress 50 Hz interference.
  • 60 Hz notch filter: Used to suppress 60 Hz interference.
A notch filter is a special type of band-stop filter. It is designed for narrowband rejection rather than rejecting a wide range of frequencies.

10. Twin-T Notch Filter

The Twin-T filter is a commonly studied notch-filter network made from two T-shaped RC networks.

One network acts as a low-pass section and the other as a high-pass section. Their combination produces strong attenuation near a selected frequency.

For the balanced Twin-T network:

f0 = 1 / (2πRC)

In the standard balanced network, the resistors and capacitors use specific ratios:

  • Two resistors have value R.
  • The third resistor has value R/2.
  • Two capacitors have value C.
  • The third capacitor has value 2C.
A passive Twin-T network can have limited notch depth under loading. An op-amp may be added for buffering or active feedback to improve practical performance.

11. Numerical Example

Given:

  • Lower cutoff frequency, fL = 900 Hz
  • Upper cutoff frequency, fH = 1100 Hz

Step 1: Calculate Bandwidth

BW = fH − fL

BW = 1100 − 900

BW = 200 Hz

Step 2: Calculate Notch Frequency

f0 = √(fL × fH)

f0 = √(900 × 1100)

f0 = √990000

f0 ≈ 994.99 Hz

Step 3: Calculate Quality Factor

Q = f0 / BW

Q = 994.99 / 200

Q ≈ 4.97

Final Answer:

  • Bandwidth = 200 Hz
  • Notch frequency ≈ 995 Hz
  • Quality factor ≈ 4.97

12. Applications

  • Power-line interference removal: Rejects 50 Hz or 60 Hz interference.
  • Biomedical equipment: Removes power-line noise from ECG and other measurement signals.
  • Audio systems: Suppresses unwanted tones and feedback frequencies.
  • Communication systems: Rejects unwanted frequency bands.
  • Instrumentation: Removes narrowband electrical interference.
  • Industrial electronics: Suppresses unwanted periodic noise in measurement circuits.

13. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Rejects unwanted frequency bands.
  • Useful for removing narrowband interference.
  • Active designs can provide buffering and gain.
  • Can be designed for specific notch frequencies.

Limitations

  • Requires an external power supply for active designs.
  • Component tolerances affect notch frequency and depth.
  • High-Q designs require careful component matching.
  • Op-amp bandwidth limits high-frequency operation.

14. Interview Questions

Q1. What is a band-stop filter?

It is a filter that attenuates a selected frequency range while allowing frequencies outside that range to pass.

Q2. What is a notch filter?

It is a narrow band-stop filter that strongly attenuates a particular frequency.

Q3. What is notch frequency?

It is the frequency at which the filter provides maximum attenuation.

Q4. What is bandwidth?

It is the width of the rejected frequency range, calculated as fH − fL.

Q5. What is quality factor?

It is the ratio of notch frequency to bandwidth.

Q6. What is a Twin-T filter?

It is an RC notch-filter network made from two interconnected T-shaped networks.

Q7. Why is a 50 Hz notch filter used?

It is used to suppress unwanted 50 Hz power-line interference in electronic measurement systems.

Q8. What is the difference between BPF and BSF?

A BPF passes a selected frequency range, whereas a BSF attenuates a selected frequency range.

16. Summary

  • A band-stop filter attenuates a selected frequency range.
  • It allows frequencies below and above the rejected band to pass.
  • Bandwidth is BW = fH − fL.
  • Notch frequency is f0 = √(fL × fH) for a conventional second-order response.
  • Quality factor is Q = f0/BW.
  • A notch filter is a narrow band-stop filter.
  • Twin-T is a commonly used RC notch-filter network.
  • Band-stop filters are useful for power-line noise rejection and instrumentation.
