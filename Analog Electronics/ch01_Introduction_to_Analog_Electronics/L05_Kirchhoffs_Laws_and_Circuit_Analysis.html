Lesson 5: Passive and Active Electronic Components

Understand the classification, working principles, characteristics and applications of passive and active electronic components.

1. Introduction to Electronic Components

Electronic components are the basic building blocks used to construct electronic circuits and systems.

They perform functions such as controlling current, storing energy, amplifying signals, switching and processing electrical signals.

2. Passive Electronic Components

Definition: Passive electronic components do not provide electrical power gain. They generally dissipate or store energy supplied by an external source.

Characteristics

  • Do not provide power amplification.
  • Can store or dissipate electrical energy.
  • Generally do not require an external bias supply to perform their basic function.
  • Used in filtering, timing, coupling and voltage division circuits.

Examples of Passive Components

  1. Resistor
  2. Capacitor
  3. Inductor
  4. Transformer

3. Resistor

Definition: A resistor is a passive electronic component that opposes the flow of electric current.

Symbol and Unit

  • Symbol: R
  • Unit: Ohm (Ω)

Working Principle

A resistor produces a voltage drop when current flows through it.

V = IR

Types of Resistors

  • Fixed resistor
  • Variable resistor
  • Potentiometer
  • Thermistor
  • Light Dependent Resistor (LDR)

Applications

  • Current limiting
  • Voltage division
  • Transistor biasing
  • Pull-up and pull-down circuits
  • Signal conditioning
Placement Example:

A resistor is connected in series with an LED to limit current and protect the LED from excessive current.

4. Capacitor

Definition: A capacitor is a passive electronic component that stores electrical energy in an electric field.

Symbol and Unit

  • Symbol: C
  • Unit: Farad (F)

Working Principle

A capacitor consists of two conductive plates separated by an insulating material called a dielectric.

Q = CV

Where:

  • Q = Stored charge in coulombs
  • C = Capacitance in farads
  • V = Voltage across the capacitor

Types of Capacitors

  • Ceramic capacitor
  • Electrolytic capacitor
  • Film capacitor
  • Tantalum capacitor
  • Variable capacitor

Applications

  • Power supply filtering
  • Signal coupling and decoupling
  • Timing circuits
  • Noise suppression
  • Energy storage
Placement Example:

A capacitor connected across a DC power supply can help reduce voltage fluctuations and electrical noise.

5. Inductor

Definition: An inductor is a passive electronic component that stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through it.

Symbol and Unit

  • Symbol: L
  • Unit: Henry (H)

Working Principle

When current flows through an inductor, a magnetic field is produced around its coil. A changing current induces a voltage that opposes the change in current.

V = L × (di/dt)

The polarity of the induced voltage depends on the reference direction used; the inductor opposes changes in current.

Applications

  • DC-DC converters
  • Power supply filters
  • Oscillator circuits
  • EMI filters
  • Energy storage in switching regulators

6. Transformer

Definition: A transformer is a passive electrical device that transfers AC electrical energy between windings through electromagnetic induction.

Main Parts

  • Primary winding
  • Secondary winding
  • Magnetic core

Working Principle

An alternating current in the primary winding produces a changing magnetic flux in the core. This flux induces a voltage in the secondary winding.

Vs / Vp = Ns / Np

Where:

  • Vs = Secondary voltage
  • Vp = Primary voltage
  • Ns = Secondary turns
  • Np = Primary turns

Applications

  • AC voltage step-up and step-down
  • Electrical isolation
  • Power supply circuits
  • Measurement systems

7. Active Electronic Components

Definition: Active electronic components can control current or voltage and may provide signal or power gain when appropriately powered and biased.

Characteristics

  • Can provide amplification or controlled switching.
  • Often require an external power supply or bias.
  • Can control electrical signals.
  • Used in amplifiers, oscillators and electronic control circuits.

Examples of Active Components

  1. Diode
  2. Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)
  3. Field Effect Transistor (FET)
  4. MOSFET
  5. Operational Amplifier (Op-Amp)
  6. Integrated Circuit (IC)

8. Diode

Definition: A diode is a semiconductor device that primarily allows current to flow in one direction under normal operating conditions.

Terminals

  • Anode
  • Cathode

Operating Conditions

Forward Bias:

The anode is at a higher potential than the cathode. The diode conducts when its forward voltage is sufficient.

Reverse Bias:

The anode is at a lower potential than the cathode. Only a small leakage current normally flows until breakdown conditions are reached.

Applications

  • Rectifier circuits
  • Reverse polarity protection
  • Signal clipping and clamping
  • Voltage regulation using Zener diodes

9. Transistors

Definition: A transistor is a semiconductor device used to amplify signals or operate as an electronic switch.

Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

A BJT is a current-controlled device with three terminals.

  • Emitter
  • Base
  • Collector

Field Effect Transistor (FET)

A FET is a voltage-controlled device in which an electric field controls current flow.

  • Source
  • Gate
  • Drain

Applications

  • Signal amplification
  • Electronic switching
  • Motor control circuits
  • Power management
  • Embedded electronic systems

10. Operational Amplifier (Op-Amp)

Definition: An operational amplifier is a high-gain differential amplifier with inverting and non-inverting inputs.

Terminals

  • Inverting input (−)
  • Non-inverting input (+)
  • Output terminal
  • Positive and negative power supply terminals

Applications

  • Voltage amplifiers
  • Summing amplifiers
  • Comparators
  • Active filters
  • Signal conditioning circuits

11. Passive vs Active Components

Passive Components Active Components
Do not provide power gain. Can provide signal or power gain.
Store or dissipate energy. Control current or voltage.
Generally do not need bias power for their basic function. Often require suitable biasing or power.
Resistor, capacitor and inductor. Transistor and operational amplifier.
Used in filtering and energy storage. Used in amplification and switching.
Important Technical Note: A diode is commonly classified as an active semiconductor component in basic electronics curricula, although it does not provide power gain. The key distinction is that a diode controls conduction rather than amplifying a signal.

12. Practical Applications in Embedded Systems

1. Resistor

Used for LED current limiting and microcontroller input pull-up or pull-down circuits.

2. Capacitor

Used for power supply decoupling and filtering near microcontroller power pins.

3. Inductor

Used in switching regulators and power conversion circuits.

4. Diode

Used for reverse polarity protection and inductive load flyback protection.

5. Transistor and MOSFET

Used to control relays, motors, LEDs and other loads that cannot be driven directly by microcontroller GPIO pins.

Lesson Summary

  • Electronic components are the building blocks of electronic circuits.
  • Passive components do not provide power gain.
  • Resistors oppose current flow.
  • Capacitors store energy in an electric field.
  • Inductors store energy in a magnetic field.
  • Transformers transfer AC energy through electromagnetic induction.
  • Active semiconductor components control electrical signals.
  • Diodes, transistors and Op-Amps are important semiconductor components.
  • Component selection is essential for reliable embedded hardware design.
