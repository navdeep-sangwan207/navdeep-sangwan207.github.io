2. Introduction

Buck and boost converters are switching DC-DC converters used to efficiently convert one DC voltage level into another.

ConverterFunctionTypical example
Buck converterStep-down DC voltage12 V to 5 V
Boost converterStep-up DC voltage5 V to 12 V

Both converters use switching, energy storage, a power switch, an inductor and an output capacitor. Their topology determines how energy is transferred.

The buck and boost converters covered in this lecture are the basic non-isolated topologies operating in continuous conduction mode (CCM), using ideal component assumptions for fundamental equations.

4. Buck Converter Operation

Mode 1: Switch ON

  • The switch conducts.
  • Input voltage is applied to the inductor and output path.
  • Inductor current increases.
  • The inductor stores energy in its magnetic field.
  • The load receives energy from the input and inductor path.
  • The output capacitor supports the output voltage and participates in ripple filtering.
\[ V_L=V_{IN}-V_{OUT} \]
\[ \frac{di_L}{dt}=\frac{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}{L} \]

Mode 2: Switch OFF

  • The main switch is turned OFF.
  • The inductor resists the reduction in current.
  • The inductor current continues through the freewheel diode in a conventional buck converter.
  • Inductor energy continues supplying the load.
  • The inductor current decreases.
\[ V_L\approx -V_{OUT} \]
\[ \frac{di_L}{dt}\approx-\frac{V_{OUT}}{L} \]
In steady-state CCM, the inductor current has a triangular ripple around its average value. Its average current is approximately equal to the output current in an ideal buck converter.

6. Buck Converter Output Voltage Equation

For an ideal buck converter in CCM under steady-state conditions, inductor volt-second balance gives the output voltage relationship.

During ON time

\[ V_L=V_{IN}-V_{OUT} \]
\[ \Delta I_{L,ON}=\frac{(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})T_{ON}}{L} \]

During OFF time

\[ V_L=-V_{OUT} \]
\[ \Delta I_{L,OFF}=-\frac{V_{OUT}T_{OFF}}{L} \]

For steady-state operation, the net change in inductor current over one cycle is zero.

\[ (V_{IN}-V_{OUT})D T=V_{OUT}(1-D)T \]
\[ V_{IN}D=V_{OUT} \]
\[ \boxed{V_{OUT}=D V_{IN}} \]

Duty cycle

\[ D=\frac{V_{OUT}}{V_{IN}} \]
These equations are for the ideal buck converter in CCM. In a practical converter, losses, control limits, parasitic elements and operating conditions affect the output.

8. Buck Converter Inductor Ripple

Inductor current ripple is the change in inductor current during one switching interval.

\[ \Delta I_L=\frac{(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})D}{Lf} \]
Where:
  • VIN = input voltage
  • VOUT = output voltage
  • D = duty cycle
  • L = inductance
  • f = switching frequency

For an ideal steady-state CCM buck converter, an equivalent expression is:

\[ \Delta I_L=\frac{V_{OUT}(1-D)}{Lf} \]

Worked example

Given:

  • VIN = 12 V
  • VOUT = 5 V
  • D = 5/12
  • L = 100 µH
  • f = 50 kHz
\[ \Delta I_L=\frac{(12-5)(5/12)}{100\times10^{-6}\times50000} \]
\[ \Delta I_L\approx0.583A \]
Result: Approximately 0.583 A peak-to-peak inductor current ripple under ideal CCM assumptions.

9. Buck Output Voltage Ripple

For a simplified ideal buck converter operating in CCM with triangular inductor current ripple, negligible capacitor ESR and a suitable output capacitor:

\[ \Delta V_{OUT}\approx\frac{\Delta I_L}{8fC} \]

Where:

  • ΔIL = peak-to-peak inductor ripple current
  • f = switching frequency
  • C = output capacitance

Worked example

Assume:

  • ΔIL = 0.5 A
  • f = 100 kHz
  • C = 100 µF
\[ \Delta V\approx\frac{0.5}{8\times100000\times100\times10^{-6}} \]
\[ \Delta V\approx0.00625V \]
Ripple ≈ 6.25 mV
This simplified capacitor ripple estimate ignores ESR, ESL, layout effects, control behavior and other ripple sources. Actual ripple may be substantially different.

11. Boost Converter Operation

Mode 1: Switch ON

  • The switch conducts and connects the switch node toward ground.
  • Input voltage is applied across the inductor.
  • Inductor current increases.
  • Energy is stored in the inductor magnetic field.
  • The output diode is reverse-biased in the conventional ideal topology during the switching ON interval.
  • The output capacitor supplies the load during this interval.
\[ V_L=V_{IN} \]
\[ \frac{di_L}{dt}=\frac{V_{IN}}{L} \]

Mode 2: Switch OFF

  • The main switch turns OFF.
  • Inductor current continues flowing.
  • The inductor voltage adds to the input voltage.
  • The diode conducts to transfer energy to the output.
  • The output voltage can be higher than the input voltage.
\[ V_L=V_{IN}-V_{OUT} \]
\[ \frac{di_L}{dt}=\frac{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}{L} \]
The inductor current in a boost converter is generally the input current. In ideal CCM operation, the average inductor current is greater than the output current when the converter is stepping up voltage.

13. Boost Converter Output Voltage Equation

For an ideal boost converter in CCM, steady-state volt-second balance gives the output voltage relationship.

During ON time

\[ V_L=V_{IN} \]
\[ \Delta I_{L,ON}=\frac{V_{IN}DT}{L} \]

During OFF time

\[ V_L=V_{IN}-V_{OUT} \]
\[ \Delta I_{L,OFF}=\frac{(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})(1-D)T}{L} \]

For steady-state CCM, the net change in inductor current over one switching cycle is zero.

\[ V_{IN}D+(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})(1-D)=0 \]
\[ V_{IN}=V_{OUT}(1-D) \]
\[ \boxed{V_{OUT}=\frac{V_{IN}}{1-D}} \]

Duty cycle equation

\[ D=1-\frac{V_{IN}}{V_{OUT}} \]

15. Boost Converter Inductor Ripple

For an ideal boost converter in CCM, the inductor current ripple is calculated from the ON interval.

\[ \Delta I_L=\frac{V_{IN}D}{Lf} \]

Worked example

Given:

  • VIN = 5 V
  • D = 0.5
  • L = 100 µH
  • f = 50 kHz
\[ \Delta I_L=\frac{5\times0.5}{100\times10^{-6}\times50000} \]
\[ \Delta I_L=0.5A \]
Result: The ideal peak-to-peak inductor current ripple is 0.5 A under these assumptions.

Peak inductor current

\[ I_{L,peak}=I_{L,avg}+\frac{\Delta I_L}{2} \]

The inductor must be selected so that its saturation-current rating, thermal rating and operating current are adequate for the intended design.

16. Boost Output Capacitor Ripple

During the boost converter switch ON interval, the output capacitor supplies the load in the ideal conventional topology.

A simplified CCM estimate of the capacitive output ripple, neglecting ESR and other effects, is:

\[ \Delta V_{OUT}\approx\frac{I_{OUT}D}{fC} \]

Worked example

Given:

  • IOUT = 0.5 A
  • D = 0.4
  • f = 100 kHz
  • C = 100 µF
\[ \Delta V\approx\frac{0.5\times0.4}{100000\times100\times10^{-6}} \]
\[ \Delta V\approx0.02V \]
Ripple ≈ 20 mV
This is a simplified capacitive ripple estimate. ESR, ESL, current waveform, layout, operating mode and controller behavior affect actual ripple.

20. Practical Applications

Buck converter applications

  • 5 V logic supply from 12 V input.
  • 3.3 V microcontroller supply.
  • Embedded board power supply.
  • Battery-powered electronic systems.
  • Automotive accessory power rails.
  • FPGA and processor supply rails.

Boost converter applications

  • LED driver power circuits.
  • Battery-powered systems requiring a higher DC voltage.
  • Portable electronic devices.
  • Solar-powered DC systems.
  • DC bus voltage conversion.
  • Sensor and embedded system power supply circuits.

21. Practical Lab: Buck Converter

Objective

Measure the output voltage, duty cycle and ripple of a low-voltage buck converter module.

Equipment

  • DC power supply with current limiting.
  • Buck converter module.
  • Multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Suitable load resistor.

Procedure

  1. Read the converter specifications and input range.
  2. Identify VIN, GND and VOUT.
  3. Set a low input voltage within the permitted range.
  4. Initially use a light load.
  5. Measure input and output voltages.
  6. Measure input and output currents.
  7. Observe the switching waveform using an appropriate probe setup.
  8. Observe output ripple.
  9. Increase the load gradually within the rated limits.
  10. Record the output voltage and ripple.
  11. Calculate ideal duty cycle.
  12. Compare the measured behavior with the ideal equations.
  13. Observe temperature and efficiency.

27. Key Takeaways

  • Buck converters step down voltage.
  • Boost converters step up voltage.
  • Both use switching and energy storage.
  • Inductor current changes during ON and OFF intervals.
  • Ideal buck CCM relationship: VOUT = D × VIN.
  • Ideal boost CCM relationship: VOUT = VIN/(1-D).
  • Inductor ripple depends on voltage, inductance, frequency and duty cycle.
  • Output capacitor reduces voltage ripple.
  • Actual converter design requires component ratings, thermal analysis, control-loop stability and PCB layout.
  • Ideal equations are useful for placement numerical problems, but practical designs require nonideal analysis.
