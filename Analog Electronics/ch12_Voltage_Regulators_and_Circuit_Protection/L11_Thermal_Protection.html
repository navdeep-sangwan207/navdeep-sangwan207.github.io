2. Why Thermal Protection Is Required

Electronic components generate heat due to electrical losses. Excessive temperature can cause performance degradation, accelerated aging, reduced reliability or permanent damage.

Common Causes of Excessive Temperature

  • Excessive current.
  • High input-to-output voltage difference in linear regulators.
  • Power transistor conduction and switching losses.
  • Insufficient heatsinking.
  • Poor PCB thermal design.
  • High ambient temperature.
  • Blocked ventilation.
  • Faulty cooling fan.
  • Overloaded circuit.
  • Incorrect component selection.

Thermal protection detects excessive temperature and responds by reducing power, limiting current, shutting down the circuit or interrupting the supply.

3. Temperature, Power and Heat

Power dissipated in electronic components is converted into heat. The temperature rise depends on dissipated power, thermal resistance, thermal capacitance, mounting and ambient conditions.

Power Dissipation

P = V × I

For a linear regulator:

PD ≈ (VIN − VOUT) × IOUT

For a resistor:

P = I²R = V²/R

For a MOSFET operating as a switch, conduction loss can be approximated as:

PCOND ≈ I² × RDS(on)

This approximation is valid for the conduction portion under suitable operating assumptions. Switching loss, gate-drive loss and other losses must also be considered in a switching design.

4. Junction Temperature

Junction temperature is the temperature of the semiconductor die inside the component.

TJ = TA + PD × θJA

Where:

  • TJ: Semiconductor junction temperature.
  • TA: Ambient temperature.
  • PD: Power dissipation.
  • θJA: Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance.

Worked Example

  • Ambient temperature = 35°C.
  • Power dissipation = 1.5 W.
  • Thermal resistance = 40°C/W.
TJ = 35 + (1.5 × 40)

TJ = 95°C

The estimated junction temperature is 95°C under the assumed thermal conditions.

Thermal resistance values depend on the package, PCB, airflow, mounting and measurement conditions. Use the applicable datasheet and thermal model rather than treating a single θJA value as universal.

5. Thermal Resistance

Thermal resistance describes how much temperature rise occurs for a given heat flow.

θ = ΔT / P

Unit:

°C/W

Common Thermal Resistance Terms

ParameterMeaning
θJAJunction-to-ambient thermal resistance
θJCJunction-to-case thermal resistance
θCSCase-to-heatsink thermal resistance
θSAHeatsink-to-ambient thermal resistance

Thermal Path

When a heatsink is used, a simplified thermal path can be expressed as:

TJ = TA + P × (θJC + θCS + θSA)

This approximation applies when the described thermal path is the dominant path and the thermal resistance values are appropriate for the actual assembly.

6. Heatsink Selection

A heatsink transfers heat from a semiconductor to the surrounding environment.

Required Heatsink Thermal Resistance

θSA ≤ (TJ(max) − TA)/P − θJC − θCS

Worked Example

  • Maximum design junction temperature = 125°C.
  • Ambient temperature = 40°C.
  • Power dissipation = 2 W.
  • θJC = 5°C/W.
  • θCS = 1°C/W.
θSA ≤ (125 − 40)/2 − 5 − 1

θSA ≤ 42.5 − 6

θSA ≤ 36.5°C/W

The heatsink must meet the calculated requirement or be better, subject to design margin, actual mounting and operating conditions.

Heatsink Selection Factors

  • Thermal resistance.
  • Power dissipation.
  • Ambient temperature.
  • Airflow.
  • Mounting arrangement.
  • Heatsink material.
  • Size and surface area.
  • Thermal interface material.
  • Electrical isolation requirements.
Heatsink thermal resistance is not a fixed value under all conditions. Natural convection, airflow, orientation, enclosure and mounting can substantially affect actual thermal performance.

8. Thermal Shutdown

Many regulators, MOSFET drivers, power ICs and other semiconductor devices contain thermal protection circuitry.

Operation

  1. The semiconductor dissipates power.
  2. Internal temperature increases.
  3. An internal temperature sensor detects excessive temperature.
  4. The protection circuitry reduces or disables output operation.
  5. The device cools.
  6. Depending on the device, it may automatically restart or require a reset.

Important Parameters

  • Thermal shutdown threshold.
  • Thermal hysteresis.
  • Response time.
  • Automatic restart behavior.
  • Maximum permitted junction temperature.
  • Thermal resistance.
Thermal shutdown is a safety mechanism, not a substitute for proper thermal design. A circuit that repeatedly enters thermal shutdown is operating under conditions that require investigation.

9. NTC Thermistor

An NTC (negative temperature coefficient) thermistor decreases in resistance as temperature increases.

NTC Characteristics

TemperatureNTC resistance behavior
Low temperatureHigher resistance
Moderate temperatureResistance decreases
High temperatureLower resistance

NTC Divider Circuit

In the arrangement shown, a fixed resistor is connected above the sense node and an NTC thermistor is connected from the sense node to ground.

VSENSE = VCC × RNTC/(RFIXED + RNTC)

As the NTC resistance decreases, the sense voltage decreases in this particular arrangement.

Applications

  • Temperature monitoring.
  • Overtemperature detection.
  • Battery temperature measurement.
  • Power supply thermal monitoring.
  • Fan control.
  • Temperature compensation.

10. NTC Temperature Calculation

An NTC can be approximated using the Beta equation:

R(T) = R0 × exp[B × (1/T − 1/T0)]

Where:

  • R(T) = Resistance at temperature T.
  • R0 = Resistance at reference temperature T0.
  • B = Beta constant.
  • T and T0 = Absolute temperature in kelvin.

Worked Example

Assume:

  • R0 = 10 kΩ at 25°C.
  • B = 3950 K.
  • Temperature = 50°C.
T = 50 + 273.15 = 323.15 K

T0 = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K
R(50°C) = 10,000 × exp[3950 × (1/323.15 − 1/298.15)]

Approximate result:

R(50°C) ≈ 3.59 kΩ

The NTC resistance is significantly lower at 50°C than at 25°C for this example.

11. PTC Thermistor Thermal Protection

PTC thermistors can be used in temperature protection circuits because their resistance increases significantly over a particular temperature range.

Operating Principle

  1. Normal temperature: relatively low resistance.
  2. Temperature rises toward its transition region.
  3. Resistance increases substantially.
  4. The increased resistance limits current or changes a monitoring signal.
  5. The system responds to the detected condition.

Applications

  • Motor winding protection.
  • Transformer protection.
  • Power supply monitoring.
  • Overtemperature sensing.
  • Self-resetting protection circuits.
Different PTC types have different characteristics. A PTC used for current protection and a PTC used for temperature sensing are not necessarily interchangeable.

12. Thermal Fuse and Thermostat

Thermal Fuse

A thermal fuse is designed to interrupt the circuit when its specified temperature condition is reached. Many thermal fuses are one-time devices and require replacement after operation.

Thermostat

A thermostat switches a circuit according to a temperature condition. Depending on its type, it can be resettable, mechanically controlled or electronically controlled.

ParameterThermal FuseThermostat
OperationThermal interruptionTemperature-controlled switching
ResetOften one-timeOften resettable
Typical useSafety protectionTemperature control or protection
ResponseSpecified thermal cutoff behaviorSwitching around specified temperature

Applications

  • Power supply transformers.
  • Appliances.
  • Motor winding protection.
  • Heaters.
  • Battery packs.
  • Electrical equipment.
Thermal fuses and thermostats must be selected for the correct temperature, current, voltage, interrupting capability, placement and application. They must not be bypassed to keep equipment operating.

13. Thermal Derating

Derating means reducing the allowable operating power, current or voltage as operating conditions become more severe.

Why Derating Is Required

  • Higher ambient temperature.
  • Limited cooling.
  • Reduced PCB copper area.
  • Higher component temperature.
  • Reliability margin.
  • Enclosure limitations.
  • Component aging.

16. Practical Thermal Protection Design

Example: 7805 Thermal Evaluation

  • Input voltage = 12 V.
  • Output voltage = 5 V.
  • Load current = 0.3 A.

Power Dissipation

P = (12 − 5) × 0.3

P = 2.1 W

Estimated Junction Temperature

Assume a hypothetical thermal resistance of 50°C/W for an illustrative assembly and an ambient temperature of 35°C.

TJ = 35 + (2.1 × 50)

TJ = 140°C

This estimated temperature may be too high for the intended operating conditions. The actual thermal resistance must be verified for the package and PCB.

Possible Design Improvements

  • Reduce the regulator input voltage.
  • Use a buck converter to reduce the voltage before the linear regulator.
  • Use a suitable heatsink.
  • Improve PCB copper thermal area.
  • Reduce load current.
  • Choose a regulator with appropriate thermal capability.

17. Practical Laboratory

Objective

Study the relationship between power dissipation and temperature and understand thermal protection behavior.

Required Equipment

  • Low-voltage DC power supply.
  • Linear regulator.
  • Resistor load or electronic load.
  • Multimeter.
  • Temperature probe or thermocouple.
  • Heatsink.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Thermal monitoring circuit if available.

Procedure

  1. Build a regulator circuit with the recommended capacitors.
  2. Verify input and output voltages.
  3. Measure the load current.
  4. Calculate regulator power dissipation.
  5. Measure temperature at the package or heatsink using an appropriate method.
  6. Observe temperature changes as load increases within device ratings.
  7. Compare operation with and without the appropriate heatsink where safe.
  8. Record ambient temperature and airflow conditions.
  9. Calculate estimated junction temperature using an appropriate thermal model.
  10. Compare the calculated value with the measured case or heatsink temperature, recognizing that these are not the same as junction temperature.
  11. Study the thermal shutdown specification in the datasheet.
  12. Do not deliberately overheat a device to force shutdown.

Observation Table

Load CurrentVINVOUTPower LossMeasured Temperature

Expected Result

  • Temperature rises as power dissipation increases.
  • Thermal resistance affects the temperature rise.
  • A heatsink can reduce junction temperature under suitable conditions.
  • Thermal shutdown protects against certain excessive-temperature conditions.
  • Good thermal design improves reliability.

19. Placement Interview Questions

  1. What is thermal protection?
    A mechanism that limits, interrupts or reduces circuit operation when temperature exceeds permitted conditions.
  2. What is junction temperature?
    The temperature of the semiconductor die inside the device.
  3. What is thermal resistance?
    The temperature difference per unit heat flow, expressed in °C/W.
  4. What is θJA?
    Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance under specified conditions.
  5. What is θJC?
    Junction-to-case thermal resistance.
  6. What is θSA?
    Heatsink-to-ambient thermal resistance.
  7. Why is a heatsink used?
    To improve heat transfer and reduce semiconductor temperature.
  8. How do you calculate junction temperature?
    Using the relevant thermal model, such as TJ = TA + P × θJA, for appropriate conditions.
  9. What is thermal shutdown?
    A protection mechanism that reduces or disables device operation when excessive temperature is detected.
  10. Why is thermal shutdown not a replacement for heatsinking?
    It is a safety mechanism, not a normal operating control strategy; repeated shutdown can indicate inadequate design.
  11. What is an NTC thermistor?
    A thermistor whose resistance decreases as temperature increases.
  12. What is a PTC thermistor?
    A thermistor whose resistance increases as temperature increases, particularly around its transition region.
  13. What is a thermal fuse?
    A temperature-sensitive protective device that interrupts the circuit under specified thermal conditions.
  14. What is a thermostat?
    A temperature-controlled switching device.
  15. What is thermal derating?
    Reducing allowable power, current or voltage as operating conditions become more severe.
  16. Why does a 7805 become hot?
    Because it dissipates power approximately equal to the input-to-output voltage difference multiplied by load current.
  17. How can thermal problems in a linear regulator be reduced?
    Reduce voltage drop, reduce load current, improve thermal management or use a switching regulator.
  18. What is thermal hysteresis?
    A difference between the temperature at which protection activates and the temperature at which it releases or restarts.
  19. Why is airflow important?
    Airflow can reduce thermal resistance between the heatsink or components and ambient air.
  20. What is the purpose of thermal interface material?
    It reduces thermal contact resistance between mating surfaces.
  21. What is the difference between case temperature and junction temperature?
    Case temperature is measured at the package surface; junction temperature is inside the semiconductor die.
  22. Why does ambient temperature affect regulator capability?
    Higher ambient temperature reduces the available temperature margin for dissipating heat.
  23. What is the role of an NTC in a voltage divider?
    It converts temperature-dependent resistance into a measurable voltage.
  24. Why should thermal protection be designed with margin?
    Because tolerances, environmental conditions, mounting and thermal uncertainties can affect the actual temperature.
  25. How do you troubleshoot a thermal shutdown condition?
    Measure input, output, load current, power loss and temperature, then verify the thermal path, device ratings and datasheet specifications.

20. Numerical Practice

Problem 1

A regulator dissipates 2 W. Ambient temperature is 40°C and thermal resistance is 30°C/W. Calculate junction temperature.

TJ = 40 + (2 × 30)

TJ = 100°C

Problem 2

A power transistor dissipates 3 W and has θJA = 25°C/W. Ambient temperature is 35°C.

TJ = 35 + (3 × 25)

TJ = 110°C

Problem 3

A linear regulator receives 15 V and delivers 5 V at 400 mA.

P = (15 − 5) × 0.4

P = 4 W

Problem 4

A device has a maximum design junction temperature of 125°C, ambient temperature of 40°C and power dissipation of 2 W. Calculate the maximum allowable total junction-to-ambient thermal resistance.

θJA = (125 − 40)/2

θJA = 42.5°C/W

Problem 5

A component dissipates 1.8 W. Its thermal resistance is 35°C/W and ambient temperature is 30°C.

TJ = 30 + (1.8 × 35)

TJ = 93°C

Problem 6

A regulator dissipates 2.5 W. The maximum design junction temperature is 120°C, ambient temperature is 35°C, θJC = 4°C/W and θCS = 1°C/W. Calculate the maximum heatsink thermal resistance.

θSA ≤ (120 − 35)/2.5 − 4 − 1

θSA ≤ 34 − 5

θSA ≤ 29°C/W

23. Summary

  • Excessive temperature can damage electronic components.
  • Power dissipation produces heat.
  • Junction temperature depends on power, ambient temperature and thermal resistance.
  • Heatsinks improve thermal transfer.
  • Thermal shutdown can protect semiconductor devices.
  • NTC and PTC thermistors can be used for temperature sensing and protection.
  • Thermal fuses and thermostats provide temperature-related protection.
  • Derating improves thermal reliability.
  • Thermal design must account for the real PCB, enclosure, airflow and mounting.
  • Proper thermal design is more important than relying on shutdown protection.
