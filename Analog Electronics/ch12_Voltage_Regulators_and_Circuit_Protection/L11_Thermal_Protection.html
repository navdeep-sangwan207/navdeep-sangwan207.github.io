Lesson 11: Thermal Protection

Understand temperature monitoring, thermal shutdown, thermal sensors and heat-management techniques used to protect electronic circuits.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand thermal protection in electronic circuits.
  • Identify causes and effects of excessive temperature.
  • Understand temperature sensors and thermal switches.
  • Study thermal shutdown in voltage regulator ICs.
  • Understand heatsinks and thermal resistance.
  • Calculate semiconductor power dissipation and junction temperature.
  • Learn practical thermal troubleshooting techniques.

2. Introduction to Thermal Protection

Thermal protection is a technique used to prevent electronic components and circuits from operating above their permitted temperature limits.

Electronic components generate heat during operation because of electrical power dissipation. If the generated heat is not removed effectively, the component temperature increases.

Common Causes of Excessive Heating

  • Excessive current through a semiconductor device.
  • High input-to-output voltage difference in linear regulators.
  • Short circuits and overload conditions.
  • Insufficient heatsinking or poor ventilation.
  • High ambient temperature.
  • Incorrect component selection.
  • Switching losses in power electronic devices.

Effects of Excessive Temperature

  • Reduced semiconductor reliability.
  • Increased leakage current.
  • Changes in component electrical characteristics.
  • Degradation of capacitors and insulation materials.
  • Thermal runaway in certain circuits.
  • Permanent component failure.

3. Power Dissipation and Heat Generation

Power dissipation is the electrical power converted into heat inside an electronic component.

P = V × I

P = I²R

P = V²/R

Example: Linear Voltage Regulator

Consider a regulator with:

  • Input voltage = 12 V
  • Output voltage = 5 V
  • Load current = 0.3 A
PQ ≈ (Vin − Vout) × Iload

PQ ≈ (12 − 5) × 0.3

PQ = 2.1 W

The regulator converts approximately 2.1 W into heat. A suitable thermal design is therefore required.

This calculation assumes the regulator's ground current and other losses are small. Use the datasheet and actual operating conditions for precise thermal design.

4. Thermal Resistance

Thermal resistance describes how strongly a component or thermal interface resists heat flow. It is similar to electrical resistance, but it relates temperature difference to heat flow.

Rθ = ΔT / P

Unit: °C/W

Common Thermal Resistance Parameters

  • RθJA: Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance.
  • RθJC: Junction-to-case thermal resistance.
  • RθCS: Case-to-heatsink thermal resistance.
  • RθSA: Heatsink-to-ambient thermal resistance.

Thermal path from semiconductor junction to ambient air.

When a heatsink is used, the total junction-to-ambient thermal resistance can be approximated by:

RθJA ≈ RθJC + RθCS + RθSA
Without a separate heatsink, use the appropriate junction-to-ambient thermal resistance specified for the actual PCB layout, copper area and airflow conditions.

5. Junction Temperature Calculation

Junction temperature is the temperature inside the semiconductor die. It is a critical parameter in semiconductor thermal design.

Tj = Ta + (P × RθJA)

Numerical Example

Given:

  • Ambient temperature = 35°C
  • Power dissipation = 1.5 W
  • Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance = 50°C/W
Tj = 35 + (1.5 × 50)

Tj = 35 + 75

Tj = 110°C

The estimated junction temperature is 110°C. It must be compared with the device's maximum permitted junction temperature and reliability requirements.

6. Heatsink and Cooling Techniques

A heatsink transfers heat from a semiconductor device to the surrounding air. It increases the effective surface area available for heat dissipation.

Common Cooling Techniques

  • Natural convection cooling.
  • Forced-air cooling using fans.
  • Aluminium heatsinks.
  • Thermal pads and thermal interface materials.
  • PCB copper planes and thermal vias.

Conceptual heatsink arrangement.

Thermal Design Considerations

  • Maximum junction temperature.
  • Power dissipation of the device.
  • Ambient temperature.
  • Thermal resistance of the heatsink.
  • Airflow and enclosure design.
  • Mounting pressure and thermal interface quality.

7. Thermistor-Based Temperature Protection

A thermistor is a temperature-dependent resistor used to measure or monitor temperature.

Types of Thermistors

Type Resistance Behavior Application
NTC Resistance decreases as temperature increases. Temperature sensing and inrush-current limiting.
PTC Resistance increases as temperature increases. Overtemperature sensing and resettable protection.

NTC Thermistor Working

  1. At lower temperature, NTC resistance is relatively high.
  2. As temperature increases, its resistance decreases.
  3. A voltage divider converts the resistance change into a voltage change.
  4. A comparator or microcontroller monitors the voltage.
  5. When the temperature threshold is exceeded, an alarm or shutdown can be activated.

8. Thermal Switch

A thermal switch is a temperature-controlled switching device. It changes its electrical contact state when a specified temperature is reached.

Working Principle

  1. The switch monitors the temperature of a component or enclosure.
  2. At the specified temperature, its contacts change state.
  3. The circuit can disconnect the load or activate a cooling fan.
  4. Some thermal switches automatically reset after cooling.

Applications

  • Power supply units.
  • Transformers.
  • Electric motors.
  • Battery packs.
  • Industrial control panels.

9. Thermal Shutdown in Voltage Regulators

Many voltage regulator ICs include an internal thermal shutdown circuit to protect the device against excessive junction temperature.

Working Principle

  1. The regulator continuously operates while monitoring its internal temperature.
  2. When the junction temperature reaches the specified thermal shutdown threshold, protection activates.
  3. The regulator reduces or disables its output to reduce heat generation.
  4. After cooling below the reset threshold, the regulator may resume operation automatically.
Thermal shutdown is a last line of protection, not a substitute for proper heatsink selection and thermal design. The shutdown and recovery thresholds vary by IC.

Common Protection Features in Regulator ICs

  • Thermal shutdown.
  • Current limiting.
  • Safe operating area protection.
  • Short-circuit protection.

10. Thermal Runaway

Thermal runaway is a condition in which increasing temperature causes greater power dissipation, which further increases temperature. If this positive feedback is not controlled, the component can be destroyed.

Example: BJT Thermal Runaway

  1. Junction temperature increases.
  2. Collector leakage current generally increases.
  3. Collector current may increase under unsuitable bias conditions.
  4. Power dissipation increases.
  5. The junction temperature rises further.

Prevention Methods

  • Use emitter resistors for bias stabilization.
  • Provide adequate heatsinking.
  • Use thermal compensation techniques.
  • Operate within the safe operating area (SOA).
  • Provide suitable current and thermal protection.

Operation

  1. The NTC thermistor senses the temperature.
  2. The comparator checks the sensor voltage against a reference.
  3. At excessive temperature, the comparator changes its output.
  4. The MOSFET driver disconnects the load or enables a cooling mechanism.
  5. Hysteresis can prevent repeated switching near the trip temperature.

Method Function Typical Application

13. Practical Troubleshooting

Problem Possible Cause Inspection
Regulator becomes very hot High power dissipation or inadequate cooling Measure input voltage, output voltage and load current.
Thermal shutdown occurs repeatedly Overload or poor heatsinking Check junction temperature estimate and heatsink mounting.
Temperature reading is incorrect Faulty thermistor or divider connection Measure thermistor resistance and sensor voltage.
Cooling fan does not start Faulty fan, driver or temperature threshold Check supply, control signal and fan wiring.
Component fails due to heating Thermal runaway or operation beyond ratings Check bias, current, power dissipation and SOA.

14. Advantages

  • Prevents damage caused by excessive temperature.
  • Improves component reliability and service life.
  • Protects power supplies and semiconductor devices.
  • Enables automatic shutdown during overheating.
  • Supports safe operation under varying load conditions.
  • Can be implemented using simple sensors or integrated protection circuits.

15. Limitations

  • Heatsinks increase circuit size and cost.
  • Thermistors require calibration for accurate temperature measurement.
  • Thermal switches have finite switching tolerances.
  • Cooling fans introduce mechanical components and maintenance requirements.
  • Thermal shutdown may interrupt normal operation.
  • Incorrect sensor placement can cause delayed temperature detection.
