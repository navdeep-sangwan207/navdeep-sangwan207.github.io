2. Introduction to Fixed Voltage Regulators

Fixed voltage regulator ICs provide a predetermined output voltage. The output voltage is designed to remain approximately constant over specified input-voltage, load-current and temperature conditions.

The 78XX family is commonly used for positive voltage regulation, while the 79XX family is commonly used for negative voltage regulation.

Examples

The XX suffix usually indicates nominal output voltage magnitude, but exact device naming, package, current rating and specifications must be verified against the manufacturer's datasheet.

3. 78XX Positive Voltage Regulator

The 78XX series is a family of three-terminal positive linear voltage regulator ICs. A typical device contains a reference, error amplifier, pass element and protective circuitry.

Typical TO-220 package example

This pin arrangement is common for many 78XX TO-220 devices, not universal for every package or manufacturer. Confirm the datasheet. The metal tab is often connected to ground in many standard TO-220 78XX devices, but verify before mounting.

4. 7805 Regulator Circuit

The 7805 produces approximately +5 V from a sufficiently higher DC input voltage, within its specified operating limits.

6. 79XX Negative Voltage Regulator

The 79XX series regulates a negative input rail to a fixed negative output voltage. It is useful for circuits requiring a negative supply rail, such as many op-amp circuits.

7. 7905 Negative Regulator Circuit

The 7905 regulates a sufficiently negative DC input to approximately −5 V relative to common ground.

Negative supply capacitor polarity

When the circuit rail is negative relative to ground, polarized capacitors connected between the negative rail and ground must be oriented so their positive terminal is at the more positive potential, usually ground, and their negative terminal is toward the negative rail.

Example: For an electrolytic capacitor between −5 V and ground, the capacitor's positive terminal is connected to ground and its negative terminal is connected to the −5 V rail, if the capacitor voltage rating and circuit arrangement are suitable.

8. Dual Supply Using 7812 and 7912

A dual supply provides positive, ground and negative rails. It is useful for analog circuits such as op-amp systems.

Applications

  • Operational amplifier circuits.
  • Analog signal conditioning.
  • Audio preamplifiers.
  • Sensor interface circuits.
  • Laboratory analog electronics.

9. Capacitors Used with 78XX and 79XX

Input capacitor

  • Helps reduce input disturbances.
  • Provides local energy storage.
  • Can improve circuit behavior when the input source is distant or has significant impedance.

Output capacitor

  • Supports output transient response.
  • Can reduce output disturbances.
  • May be required or recommended for particular devices and layouts.

12. Maximum Current and Thermal Limitations

Three-terminal regulators often advertise a nominal maximum current, but the current available in a real design depends on the input-output voltage difference, thermal design, protection limits and device conditions.

Thermal calculation

PLOSS ≈ (VIN − VOUT) × IOUT
TJ ≈ TA + PLOSS × θJA

Example

Assume a 7805 supplies 0.3 A from 12 V. Assume ambient temperature 30°C and illustrative thermal resistance 50°C/W.

PLOSS = (12 − 5) × 0.3 = 2.1 W
TJ ≈ 30 + (2.1 × 50) = 135°C

This estimate indicates that thermal design must be carefully evaluated. The actual thermal resistance depends on the package, PCB copper, heatsink, mounting, airflow and surroundings.

Thermal protection

  • Thermal shutdown may stop or reduce operation during overheating.
  • Current limiting may restrict current during overload.
  • Safe operating limits may reduce available output current.
  • Thermal protection is not a substitute for proper design.
Never assume a regulator can continuously deliver its advertised current under every voltage-drop and ambient-temperature condition.

14. Practical Lab – 7805 Testing

Objective: Test the input-output behavior, regulation and thermal characteristics of a 7805.

Components

  • 7805 IC.
  • DC supply with current limiting.
  • 0.1 µF input and output capacitors, subject to the datasheet.
  • Resistive load.
  • Multimeter.
  • Oscilloscope.
  • Heatsink if required by the calculated operating conditions.

Procedure

  1. Identify regulator pins from the datasheet.
  2. Verify input voltage, output current and capacitor requirements.
  3. Connect the input, ground and output correctly.
  4. Use a current-limited source and initially use a modest load.
  5. Power the circuit and measure the output voltage.
  6. Vary input voltage within the allowed operating range.
  7. Observe output voltage and ripple.
  8. Vary load current gradually within safe limits.
  9. Measure input and output current.
  10. Estimate power dissipation and monitor temperature.
  11. Record measurements and compare with the datasheet.

15. Practical Lab – 7912 Testing

Objective: Test the operation of a negative fixed-voltage regulator.

Procedure

  1. Read the exact 7912 datasheet.
  2. Identify pinout and capacitor polarity.
  3. Prepare an appropriate negative DC source referenced to ground.
  4. Connect the regulator and capacitors according to the manufacturer's circuit.
  5. Verify the output using a multimeter with correct polarity.
  6. Check that output is approximately −12 V under appropriate conditions.
  7. Apply a suitable load and measure output variation.
  8. Observe temperature and verify the thermal design.
  9. Record input voltage, output voltage, load current and estimated loss.
For negative-voltage circuits, connect the multimeter COM to ground and the positive measurement lead to the negative output rail to display a negative voltage.

17. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

1. What is the 78XX family?

A family of three-terminal fixed positive linear voltage regulators.

2. What is the 79XX family?

A family of three-terminal fixed negative linear voltage regulators.

3. What is the output of a 7805?

Nominally +5 V.

4. What is the output of a 7812?

Nominally +12 V.

5. What is the output of a 7905?

Nominally −5 V.

6. What is the output of a 7912?

Nominally −12 V.

7. What is the difference between 78XX and 79XX?

The 78XX family provides positive fixed outputs; the 79XX family provides negative fixed outputs.

8. What are the typical 7805 TO-220 pin functions?

Pin 1 input, pin 2 ground, pin 3 output in a common package configuration. Check the datasheet.

9. What is the common 79XX TO-220 pin arrangement?

Many devices use pin 1 ground, pin 2 input and pin 3 output, but verify the exact device.

10. Why are capacitors used?

They help with local decoupling, transient response, filtering and, depending on the design, stability.

11. Why does a 7805 become hot?

It dissipates power due to the voltage difference between input and output and the current delivered to the load.

12. Calculate the 7805 loss at 12 V and 100 mA.

P ≈ (12 − 5) × 0.1 = 0.7 W.

13. Can a 7805 convert 3 V to 5 V?

No. It is a linear regulator and cannot step up voltage.

14. Can a 7805 supply 5 V from 7 V?

It depends on dropout/headroom, current and device specifications. Seven volts may be insufficient for some operating conditions; verify the datasheet.

15. What is thermal shutdown?

A protection mechanism that reduces or stops regulator operation when an internal temperature limit is reached.

16. What is current limiting?

A protection mechanism that limits output current during overload or short-circuit conditions.

17. What is a dual supply?

A supply with positive, ground and negative rails, such as +12 V, 0 V and −12 V.

18. Why is the capacitor polarity reversed in a negative rail?

The positive terminal of an electrolytic capacitor must be at the higher potential. Ground is higher in potential than the negative rail in this connection.

19. Can 78XX and 79XX be used together?

Yes, in appropriately designed circuits they can provide positive and negative regulated rails referenced to a common ground.

20. What is the main disadvantage of a linear regulator?

Power loss and heat generation due to the input-output voltage difference.

18. Numerical Practice

  1. A 7805 operates from 12 V and supplies 250 mA. Calculate the approximate power loss.
  2. A 7812 receives 18 V and supplies 100 mA. Calculate power dissipation.
  3. A 7905 operates with an input magnitude of 12 V and output magnitude of 5 V at 200 mA. Calculate power dissipation.
  4. A 7805 supplies 5 V at 300 mA from 10 V. Calculate approximate efficiency ignoring other losses.
  5. A regulator dissipates 1.5 W. Its thermal resistance is 40°C/W and ambient temperature is 35°C. Estimate junction temperature.
  6. Explain why a 7805 cannot provide a 5 V output from a 4 V input.
  7. Explain why 7905 capacitor polarity differs from that of a 7805 supply circuit.
  8. Design a basic ±12 V regulated supply using a 7812 and a 7912. Identify the required rails and common reference.
Show numerical answers
  1. P = (12 − 5) × 0.25 = 1.75 W.
  2. P = (18 − 12) × 0.1 = 0.6 W.
  3. P ≈ (12 − 5) × 0.2 = 1.4 W.
  4. PLOSS = (10 − 5) × 0.3 = 1.5 W. POUT = 5 × 0.3 = 1.5 W. Approximate efficiency = 1.5/3 × 100 = 50%.
  5. TJ ≈ 35 + (1.5 × 40) = 95°C.
  6. A linear regulator cannot boost voltage. It requires input headroom above its output.
  7. The negative rail is below ground potential, so ground is the more positive terminal. Electrolytic capacitors must be connected according to the actual polarity across them.
  8. Use suitable positive and negative DC input rails, a 7812 and 7912 respectively, and establish a common 0 V reference according to the supply architecture.

20. Lecture Summary

  • 78XX regulators provide fixed positive regulated voltage.
  • 79XX regulators provide fixed negative regulated voltage.
  • 7805, 7812, 7905 and 7912 are commonly used devices.
  • Pin configurations differ between families and packages.
  • Input and output capacitors must meet datasheet requirements.
  • Linear regulator power loss depends primarily on voltage drop and current.
  • Thermal design, load current, input voltage and capacitor polarity must be checked.
  • Positive and negative regulators can provide dual rails for analog circuits.
