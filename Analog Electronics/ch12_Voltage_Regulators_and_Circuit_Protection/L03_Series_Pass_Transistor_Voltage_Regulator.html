Lesson 03: Zener-Based Voltage Regulation

Learn how a Zener diode maintains an approximately constant DC output voltage despite variations in input voltage and load.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the principle of Zener diode voltage regulation.
  • Explain the operation of a Zener shunt regulator.
  • Understand the role of the series resistor.
  • Calculate resistor current and Zener current.
  • Design a basic Zener voltage regulator.
  • Identify the limitations of Zener-based regulation.

2. Introduction to Zener Diode

A Zener diode is a specially designed semiconductor diode intended to operate in the reverse breakdown region. When reverse voltage reaches its specified Zener voltage, the diode conducts significantly while maintaining an approximately constant voltage across it.

This characteristic makes the Zener diode useful in voltage regulation, voltage reference circuits and overvoltage protection.

Important: A Zener diode must operate within its specified current and power ratings. A series resistor is normally required to limit reverse current.

3. Zener Diode Voltage Regulation Circuit

A basic Zener voltage regulator consists of:

  • DC input supply (Vin).
  • Series resistor (RS).
  • Reverse-connected Zener diode.
  • Load resistor (RL).

Basic Zener Shunt Regulator

The Zener diode and load are connected in parallel. The Zener diode is reverse-biased.

The series resistor limits current from the input supply. The Zener diode is connected across the load and holds the output voltage near its rated Zener voltage while operating in its regulation region.

4. Working Principle

4.1 When Input Voltage Increases

When input voltage increases, the current through the series resistor increases. The additional current is mainly absorbed by the Zener diode, provided it remains within its safe operating range. The output voltage therefore remains approximately constant.

4.2 When Input Voltage Decreases

When input voltage decreases, the series-resistor current decreases. Zener current also decreases. Regulation continues only while the Zener current remains at or above the minimum required knee current.

4.3 When Load Current Increases

When load current increases, more current is drawn by the load and less current is available to the Zener diode. If Zener current falls below its minimum regulation value, the output voltage begins to decrease.

4.4 When Load Current Decreases

When load current decreases, more current flows through the Zener diode. The output remains regulated as long as the maximum Zener current and power rating are not exceeded.

5. Important Design Equations

5.1 Output Voltage

Vout ≈ VZ

VZ = Zener breakdown voltage

5.2 Series Resistor Current

IS = (Vin − VZ) / RS

5.3 Load Current

IL = VZ / RL

5.4 Zener Current

IZ = IS − IL

5.5 Zener Power Dissipation

PZ = VZ × IZ

5.6 Series Resistor Power

PRS = IS² × RS
Design condition: For proper regulation, the Zener current must remain between its minimum regulation current and maximum permissible current. Also verify the Zener power rating and resistor power rating.

6. Numerical Design Example

Design a Zener regulator with the following specifications:

  • Input voltage = 12 V
  • Zener voltage = 5.1 V
  • Load resistance = 1 kΩ
  • Series resistance = 330 Ω

Step 1: Calculate Output Voltage

Vout ≈ VZ = 5.1 V

Step 2: Calculate Series Resistor Current

IS = (Vin − VZ) / RS

= (12 − 5.1) / 330

= 6.9 / 330

= 20.91 mA

Step 3: Calculate Load Current

IL = VZ / RL

= 5.1 / 1000

= 5.1 mA

Step 4: Calculate Zener Current

IZ = IS − IL

= 20.91 − 5.1

= 15.81 mA

Step 5: Calculate Zener Power

PZ = VZ × IZ

= 5.1 × 0.01581

≈ 0.0806 W

= 80.6 mW

Step 6: Calculate Series Resistor Power

PRS = IS² × RS

= (0.02091)² × 330

≈ 0.144 W

A 330 Ω series resistor with a suitable power rating can be selected. A practical design should provide adequate margin above the calculated resistor dissipation.

Design verification: The calculated Zener current is approximately 15.81 mA. Confirm that this is above the diode's minimum regulation current and below its maximum rated current. Also check the worst-case input voltage and load conditions before finalizing the design.

7. Design of Series Resistor

The series resistor must be selected so that the Zener diode remains in regulation under the minimum input voltage and maximum load current, without exceeding its current or power rating under the maximum input voltage and minimum load current.

RS(max) = (Vin(min) − VZ) / (IL(max) + IZ(min))
RS(min) = (Vin(max) − VZ) / (IL(min) + IZ(max))

A practical resistor value must satisfy both constraints. If the allowable resistor range is empty, the chosen Zener voltage, supply range, load range or device rating must be reconsidered.

8. Advantages of Zener Voltage Regulator

  • Simple circuit design.
  • Low component count.
  • Low cost.
  • Provides a stable reference voltage within its operating range.
  • Useful for low-current applications.
  • Can provide basic voltage clamping and protection.

9. Limitations of Zener Voltage Regulator

  • Limited load-current capability in a basic circuit.
  • Continuous Zener current causes power loss.
  • Efficiency is relatively low in many applications.
  • Output voltage changes slightly with current and temperature.
  • Regulation is lost if Zener current falls below its required minimum.
  • Not suitable for high-power regulated supplies without additional circuitry.

10. Applications

  • Low-current DC voltage regulation.
  • Reference voltage generation.
  • Overvoltage protection circuits.
  • Clipping and clamping circuits.
  • Biasing circuits.
  • Input protection for electronic circuits.
  • Basic power supply circuits.

13. Lesson Summary

  • A Zener diode regulates voltage by operating in its reverse breakdown region.
  • The output voltage is approximately equal to the Zener voltage.
  • A series resistor limits current and protects the diode.
  • The Zener diode and load are connected in parallel.
  • Zener current changes when input voltage or load current changes.
  • Current and power ratings must be checked for safe operation.
  • Zener regulators are suitable mainly for simple, low-current applications.
