2. Introduction to LM317

The LM317 is a three-terminal adjustable positive linear voltage regulator. It maintains a regulated output voltage by controlling the voltage between its output and adjustment terminals and using an external resistor network.

The output voltage can be adjusted by selecting appropriate resistor values. Depending on the particular manufacturer and variant, the device can regulate a broad range of output voltages and supply currents.

Typical LM317 characteristics:
  • Adjustable positive output voltage.
  • Three terminals: IN, OUT and ADJ.
  • Internal reference voltage approximately 1.25 V.
  • Internal current limiting and thermal protection in typical LM317 devices.
  • Requires sufficient input-to-output voltage headroom.
  • External resistors determine the output voltage.

4. Internal Working Principle

The LM317 maintains approximately 1.25 V between its OUT and ADJ terminals under normal regulation conditions.

  1. An input voltage is applied to the IN terminal.
  2. The internal reference circuit establishes approximately 1.25 V between OUT and ADJ.
  3. An external resistor connected from OUT to ADJ establishes a reference current.
  4. The second resistor connected from ADJ to ground determines the additional voltage.
  5. The regulator adjusts its internal pass element to maintain the output voltage.
Feedback principle:
\(V_{OUT}-V_{ADJ}\approx1.25V\)

6. LM317 Output Voltage Equation

\[ V_{OUT}=V_{REF}\left(1+\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)+I_{ADJ}R_2 \]
Where:
  • VREF ≈ 1.25 V
  • R1 = resistance between OUT and ADJ
  • R2 = resistance between ADJ and ground
  • IADJ = adjustment terminal current

For many basic calculations, the adjustment current term is neglected if its contribution is sufficiently small.

\[ V_{OUT}\approx1.25\left(1+\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right) \]

A common illustrative design value for R1 is 240 Ω. Verify resistor choices and reference-current requirements against the datasheet of the specific regulator.

7. Worked Numerical Example

Problem: Calculate the LM317 output voltage if R1 = 240 Ω and R2 = 720 Ω. Neglect adjustment current.

Given:

  • R1 = 240 Ω
  • R2 = 720 Ω
  • VREF = 1.25 V
\[ V_{OUT}=1.25\left(1+\frac{720}{240}\right) \]
\[ V_{OUT}=1.25(1+3) \]
\[ V_{OUT}=1.25\times4 \]
VOUT = 5 V
Result: The theoretical output is approximately 5 V, subject to reference tolerance, adjustment current, resistor tolerance, operating conditions and regulation limits.

8. Resistor Design Calculation

For a target output voltage, calculate R2 from:

\[ R_2=R_1\left(\frac{V_{OUT}}{1.25}-1\right) \]

Example: Design a 9 V output with R1 = 240 Ω.

\[ R_2=240\left(\frac{9}{1.25}-1\right) \]
\[ R_2=240(7.2-1) \]
\[ R_2=240\times6.2 \]
R2 = 1488 Ω

A practical standard resistor value near 1.49 kΩ or 1.5 kΩ can be evaluated. Calculate the resulting actual output voltage using its real value and tolerance.

11. Input Voltage and Dropout Voltage

The LM317 needs a sufficient voltage difference between its input and output to regulate properly.

Design condition:
\[ V_{IN}\ge V_{OUT}+V_{DROP} \]

The dropout or required headroom is not a universal fixed value; it depends on device design, load current, temperature and manufacturer specifications.

Example: If the desired output is 8 V and the design requires approximately 3 V of headroom under the selected conditions, the input must be approximately 11 V or greater.

The 3 V figure is illustrative, not a guaranteed LM317 dropout specification. Check the datasheet for the actual conditions.

12. Power Dissipation and Thermal Analysis

Since the LM317 is a linear regulator, the voltage difference between input and output is dissipated largely as heat in the regulator.

\[ P_D\approx(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})I_{OUT} \]

Additional adjustment-network current and other details can affect the precise power balance, so use the appropriate datasheet calculation for final design.

Worked example

  • VIN = 15 V
  • VOUT = 5 V
  • IOUT = 0.5 A
\[ P_D\approx(15-5)\times0.5 \]
\[ P_D\approx5W \]
5 W is significant heat. The regulator may require a heat sink and thermal analysis. Do not assume a regulator can continuously supply its advertised maximum current under every voltage difference.

Thermal resistance relationship

\[ T_J=T_A+P_D R_{\theta JA} \]

When a heat sink is installed, use a complete thermal resistance path:

\[ T_J=T_A+P_D(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CS}+R_{\theta SA}) \]

14. Capacitors and Protection Diodes

Input and output capacitors

  • Input capacitor: can help prevent instability or unwanted input disturbances when supply wiring is long or impedance is high.
  • Output capacitor: can improve transient response or stability in some configurations, depending on device and capacitor properties.
  • Adjustment capacitor: may improve ripple rejection in suitable designs but must be selected according to the datasheet.

Protection diodes

Protection diodes may be needed when large capacitors are connected to the regulator output or adjustment terminal, or when the input supply can collapse while the output remains charged. These diodes provide safe discharge paths in certain circuit configurations.

Do not add protection diodes blindly. Determine the possible voltage and current paths during power-down, short-circuit and fault conditions, then use the manufacturer's recommended circuit.

16. Practical Lab: Adjustable DC Power Supply

Objective

Construct and test an LM317-based adjustable DC regulator using a low-voltage isolated supply.

Components

  • LM317 regulator
  • Suitable DC input supply
  • 240 Ω resistor
  • Potentiometer or selected resistor values for R2
  • Load resistor
  • Multimeter
  • Capacitors as recommended by the datasheet
  • Heat sink where necessary
  • Breadboard or suitable prototyping board

Procedure

  1. Identify the LM317 terminals using the datasheet.
  2. Connect the input supply to the IN terminal.
  3. Connect R1 between OUT and ADJ.
  4. Connect R2 between ADJ and ground.
  5. Connect the load between output and ground.
  6. Check all wiring and capacitor polarities.
  7. Switch on using a current-limited supply.
  8. Measure the output voltage using a multimeter.
  9. Adjust R2 and record the output voltage.
  10. Increase the load gradually while observing regulation.
  11. Measure the input and output voltages.
  12. Calculate regulator power dissipation.
  13. Observe regulator temperature safely.

Problem 1

R1 = 240 Ω and R2 = 480 Ω. Calculate the output voltage neglecting adjustment current.

\[ V_{OUT}=1.25(1+480/240) \]

Calculate R2 for a 12 V output using R1 = 240 Ω.

\[ R_2=240(12/1.25-1) \]
Answer: 2064 Ω

21. Key Takeaways

  • LM317 is a three-terminal adjustable positive linear voltage regulator.
  • It maintains approximately 1.25 V between OUT and ADJ.
  • External resistors determine the output voltage.
  • Output voltage is calculated using the reference voltage and resistor ratio, with adjustment current considered where needed.
  • Sufficient input-to-output voltage headroom is necessary.
  • Power dissipation is a major design consideration.
  • Heat sinks, capacitors and protection components must be selected according to the datasheet and circuit conditions.
  • LM317 is useful in adjustable DC power supplies and electronics prototyping.
