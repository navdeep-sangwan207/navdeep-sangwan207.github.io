Lesson 06: IC 78XX and 79XX Regulators

Study fixed positive and negative voltage regulator ICs, their pin configurations, working principles and practical power supply applications.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the purpose of 78XX and 79XX regulator ICs.
  • Differentiate between positive and negative voltage regulators.
  • Identify common fixed output voltage ratings.
  • Understand regulator pin configurations.
  • Design basic regulated DC power supply circuits.
  • Calculate regulator power dissipation.
  • Understand capacitors, heatsinks and protection requirements.

2. Introduction to 78XX and 79XX ICs

The 78XX and 79XX series are three-terminal linear voltage regulator IC families commonly used in regulated DC power supplies.

  • 78XX series: Provides fixed positive output voltage.
  • 79XX series: Provides fixed negative output voltage.

The two digits represented by XX indicate the nominal regulated output voltage.

Example: A 7805 provides a nominal +5 V output, while a 7905 provides a nominal −5 V output, subject to the device's specified operating conditions.

Typical 7805 TO-220 front-view pinout: Pin 1 = Input, Pin 2 = Ground, Pin 3 = Output.

Package and manufacturer variations exist. Always verify the exact datasheet before wiring a regulator.

6. Pin Configuration of 7905

A common TO-220 version of the 7905 has a different pin arrangement from the 78XX family.

Typical 7905 TO-220 front-view pinout: Pin 1 = Ground, Pin 2 = Input, Pin 3 = Output.

The 79XX pinout is not interchangeable with the 78XX pinout. Confirm the datasheet for the exact manufacturer and package.

7. Working Principle of 7805

The 7805 is a fixed positive voltage regulator that maintains an output close to +5 V when operated within its specified input voltage, current and thermal limits.

7.1 Main Internal Functions

  • Reference voltage circuit.
  • Error amplifier.
  • Series pass transistor.
  • Current limiting circuit.
  • Thermal shutdown circuit.
  • Safe operating area protection.

7.2 Operation

  1. Unregulated DC voltage is applied to the input terminal.
  2. The internal reference establishes the desired output level.
  3. The error amplifier monitors the output voltage.
  4. The internal pass transistor adjusts conduction.
  5. The output remains approximately +5 V within the specified operating range.

Input and output capacitors are placed close to the IC according to the manufacturer's datasheet.

Typical Connections

  • Connect the positive unregulated supply to the 7805 input.
  • Connect the common circuit ground to the ground pin.
  • Take the regulated +5 V output from the output pin.
  • Use input and output bypass capacitors as recommended by the datasheet.
A 7805 is a linear regulator, not a switching converter. Its input voltage must remain sufficiently above the output voltage to satisfy its dropout/headroom requirement.

9. Input and Output Capacitors

Capacitors are used to improve regulator stability and transient response, and to reduce unwanted voltage variations.

Capacitor Purpose
Input capacitor Reduces input supply disturbances and helps maintain a low-impedance input.
Output capacitor Improves transient response and reduces output disturbances.
Additional bypass capacitor Helps suppress high-frequency noise when correctly selected and placed.

Capacitor values depend on the regulator manufacturer, circuit layout and source impedance. Follow the specific datasheet rather than assuming one value is suitable for every 78XX or 79XX device.

Both outputs use a common 0 V reference when the input supply and grounding arrangement are designed for a dual-rail supply.

Use the correct negative regulator part number: the standard −12 V device is 7912, not 79012.

11. Power Dissipation and Heatsink

Since 78XX and 79XX ICs are linear regulators, the voltage difference between input and output is dissipated as heat.

PD ≈ (Vin − Vout) × Iout

Numerical Example

A 7805 regulator has:

  • Input voltage = 12 V
  • Output voltage = 5 V
  • Load current = 300 mA
PD = (12 − 5) × 0.3

= 7 × 0.3

= 2.1 W

The regulator dissipates approximately 2.1 W as heat. A suitable heatsink or a reduction in load current may be necessary.

Actual thermal design must consider junction temperature, ambient temperature, package thermal resistance, heatsink thermal resistance and mounting conditions.

12. Protection Features

Many standard 78XX and 79XX regulators include internal protection features. Exact implementation depends on the manufacturer and device version.

  • Current limiting: Limits excessive output current.
  • Thermal shutdown: Reduces or stops operation when internal temperature becomes excessive.
  • Safe operating area protection: Helps protect the pass element under certain overload conditions.
Internal protection does not make the IC indestructible. Reverse voltage, incorrect wiring, excessive input voltage and prolonged overheating can still damage the regulator.

13. Advantages

  • Simple three-terminal connection.
  • Fixed and predictable nominal output voltage.
  • Low external component count.
  • Internal protection in many standard versions.
  • Widely used and easy to integrate into power supplies.
  • Available in positive and negative voltage versions.

14. Limitations

  • Lower efficiency than many switching regulators.
  • Input voltage must be sufficiently higher in magnitude than the output voltage.
  • Power dissipation increases with load current and voltage difference.
  • Heatsinking may be required.
  • Output voltage is fixed in standard 78XX and 79XX devices.
  • Not suitable when the available input voltage is below the required output headroom.

15. Applications

  • 5 V microcontroller power supplies.
  • 12 V and 15 V analog circuits.
  • Operational amplifier dual-rail supplies.
  • Embedded system development boards.
  • Communication equipment.
  • Industrial control circuits.
  • Low-noise analog power supply sections.

18. Lesson Summary

  • 78XX regulators provide fixed positive output voltages.
  • 79XX regulators provide fixed negative output voltages.
  • The XX digits indicate the nominal output voltage magnitude.
  • These ICs use internal reference, error amplifier and pass transistor circuits.
  • Input and output capacitors should follow datasheet recommendations.
  • Power dissipation and thermal limits must be checked during design.
  • Positive and negative regulators can be combined for dual-rail supplies.
