2. What Is Astable Mode?

An astable multivibrator has no stable output state. It continuously switches between HIGH and LOW without requiring an external trigger for every transition.

In the standard 555 astable circuit, an external resistor-capacitor network controls the charging and discharging time of the capacitor.

Output HIGH

The internal discharge transistor is OFF. The capacitor charges through RA and RB.

Output LOW

The discharge transistor is ON. The capacitor discharges through RB.

This repeated process generates a continuous rectangular output waveform at pin 3.

3. Standard 555 Astable Circuit

The standard circuit uses two resistors and one timing capacitor.

The resistor path is VCC → RA → pin 7 junction → RB → capacitor node (pins 2 and 6). The capacitor connects from the timing node to ground.

4. Working Principle

Stage 1: Capacitor charging

When the output is HIGH, the internal discharge transistor at pin 7 is OFF. The capacitor charges through both RA and RB.

Its voltage increases from approximately 1/3 VCC towards 2/3 VCC.

Stage 2: Upper threshold reached

When the capacitor voltage reaches approximately 2/3 VCC, the threshold comparator resets the internal latch.

The output becomes LOW and the discharge transistor turns ON.

Stage 3: Capacitor discharging

The capacitor discharges through RB and the internal discharge transistor at pin 7. RA is not part of the ideal discharge path.

Stage 4: Lower threshold reached

When capacitor voltage falls to approximately 1/3 VCC, the trigger comparator sets the latch.

The output becomes HIGH, the discharge transistor turns OFF, and the next charging cycle begins.

Repeated operation: The capacitor continuously moves between approximately 1/3 VCC and 2/3 VCC, causing the output to switch repeatedly.

5. Charging and Discharging Equations

Capacitor charging

The capacitor charges through RA + RB. Its voltage rises from 1/3 VCC to 2/3 VCC.

tH = 0.693 (RA + RB) C

Capacitor discharging

The capacitor discharges through RB. Its voltage falls from 2/3 VCC to 1/3 VCC.

tL = 0.693 RB C

Here, tH is the output HIGH time and tL is the output LOW time.

6. Time Period and Frequency Derivation

The total time period is the sum of the HIGH and LOW intervals:

T = tH + tL

Substituting the two timing equations:

T = 0.693(RA + RB)C + 0.693RBC

T = 0.693(RA + 2RB)C

Frequency is the reciprocal of time period:

f = 1/T = 1.443 / [(RA + 2RB)C]

The commonly used rounded equation is:

f ≈ 1.44 / [(RA + 2RB)C]

7. Duty Cycle

Duty cycle is the percentage of one complete period for which the output remains HIGH.

D = (tH/T) × 100%

Substituting the timing equations:

D = [(RA + RB)/(RA + 2RB)] × 100%

For positive RA and RB, this conventional arrangement has a duty cycle greater than 50%.

Important interview point: The standard two-resistor 555 astable circuit does not produce an exact 50% duty cycle with ordinary positive resistor values. A steering diode can provide separate charging and discharging paths and allow a duty cycle closer to or below 50%, depending on the design.

10. Worked Numerical Problems

Example 1: Calculate frequency and duty cycle

Given:

  • RA = 10 kΩ
  • RB = 47 kΩ
  • C = 10 µF

HIGH time:

tH = 0.693 × (10,000 + 47,000) × 10 × 10−6

tH ≈ 0.395 s

LOW time:

tL = 0.693 × 47,000 × 10 × 10−6

tL ≈ 0.326 s

Time period:

T ≈ 0.721 s

Frequency:

f = 1/0.721 ≈ 1.39 Hz

Duty cycle:

D = (0.395/0.721) × 100 ≈ 54.8%

Example 2: Calculate timing capacitor

Required frequency = 1 kHz. RA = 1 kΩ and RB = 6.8 kΩ.

C = 1.44/[f(RA + 2RB)]

C = 1.44/[1000 × (1000 + 13,600)]

C ≈ 99 µF? No — C ≈ 99 nF

A practical starting value is 100 nF, followed by measurement and adjustment if necessary.

Example 3: Calculate RB

Required frequency = 500 Hz, RA = 2.2 kΩ and C = 100 nF.

RB = [1.44/(fC) − RA]/2

RB = [1.44/(500 × 100 × 10−9) − 2200]/2

RB ≈ 13.3 kΩ

Choose a nearby standard resistor value and verify the resulting frequency.

11. Achieving a Duty Cycle Near 50%

The conventional circuit has separate HIGH and LOW timing paths only in the sense that the capacitor charges through RA + RB and discharges through RB. Consequently, the HIGH time is longer than the LOW time.

A common modification uses a diode in parallel with RB, oriented to steer charging current around RB. This makes the capacitor charge mainly through RA and discharge through RB.

tH ≈ 0.693 RAC
tL ≈ 0.693 RBC

In this modified arrangement, choosing similar effective charging and discharging resistances can produce a duty cycle near 50%.

The diode's forward voltage, orientation, discharge current path and actual IC topology must be checked. Do not apply the standard astable equations unchanged to a diode-modified circuit.

13. Practical Lab Experiment

Objective: Construct a 555 astable multivibrator and measure its frequency and duty cycle.

Components

  • NE555 timer IC or compatible variant
  • Regulated DC supply within the IC datasheet limits
  • RA = 10 kΩ and RB = 47 kΩ
  • C = 10 µF
  • 0.1 µF supply decoupling capacitor
  • Optional 10 nF capacitor from pin 5 to ground
  • Oscilloscope or logic analyser
  • Breadboard and connecting wires

Procedure

  1. Connect pin 1 to ground and pin 8 to the regulated supply.
  2. Connect pin 4 to VCC.
  3. Connect pins 2 and 6 together and connect them to the positive timing-capacitor node.
  4. Connect the capacitor from the pins 2/6 timing node to ground.
  5. Connect RB between the pins 2/6 timing node and pin 7.
  6. Connect RA between VCC and pin 7.
  7. Connect the oscilloscope to pin 3 and observe the output waveform.
  8. Measure HIGH time, LOW time and frequency.
  9. Compare measured values with theoretical calculations.

15. Important Design Considerations

  • Resistor selection: Avoid extreme resistor values that cause excessive leakage sensitivity or excessive discharge current. Follow the selected IC datasheet's recommendations.
  • Capacitor selection: Electrolytic capacitors have wider tolerance and leakage than many film or ceramic types.
  • Supply decoupling: Place a suitable bypass capacitor close to the IC supply pins.
  • Output loading: Use a transistor or MOSFET driver when the load exceeds the timer's output capability.
  • Frequency accuracy: Actual frequency depends on component tolerance, temperature, supply conditions and IC characteristics.
  • High-frequency operation: Parasitic capacitance, propagation delays and discharge-transistor characteristics become increasingly important.
  • CMOS timer variants: Their current consumption, output behavior and recommended operating conditions may differ from bipolar NE555 devices.

16. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

1. What is an astable multivibrator?

A circuit with no stable output state that continuously switches between HIGH and LOW.

2. Why is a 555 timer called astable in this configuration?

Because its internal latch repeatedly sets and resets as the capacitor voltage crosses the two comparator thresholds.

3. What is the frequency equation?

f ≈ 1.44/[(RA + 2RB)C].

4. What is the time-period equation?

T ≈ 0.693(RA + 2RB)C.

5. What is the HIGH-time equation?

tH ≈ 0.693(RA + RB)C.

6. What is the LOW-time equation?

tL ≈ 0.693RBC.

7. Why does the capacitor charge through two resistors?

During charging, the discharge transistor is OFF, so the charging path includes RA and RB.

8. Why does the capacitor discharge through RB?

When the discharge transistor turns ON, pin 7 provides a path to ground through RB.

9. What are the two capacitor threshold levels?

Approximately 1/3 VCC and 2/3 VCC.

10. What is duty cycle?

The percentage of one complete time period for which the output is HIGH.

11. Why is the conventional duty cycle greater than 50%?

Because tH includes RA + RB, while tL depends only on RB.

12. How can a duty cycle near 50% be obtained?

A diode can be added to create separate charging and discharging paths, allowing their effective resistances to be selected independently.

13. What happens if C is doubled?

Both HIGH and LOW times double, so the frequency approximately halves.

14. What happens if both timing resistors are doubled?

The time period approximately doubles and frequency approximately halves, assuming C is unchanged.

15. What is the function of pin 7?

It provides the discharge path for the external timing capacitor through an internal transistor.

16. Can the astable output drive a motor directly?

Generally, a separate power driver is required. The output must remain within the timer's specified voltage and current limits.

17. What is the difference between monostable and astable modes?

Monostable mode produces one timed pulse after a trigger; astable mode produces continuous oscillations.

18. What practical factors affect frequency accuracy?

Resistor and capacitor tolerances, capacitor leakage, temperature, supply conditions and timer characteristics.

17. Numerical Practice

  2. Calculate HIGH and LOW times for RA = 22 kΩ, RB = 33 kΩ and C = 1 µF.
  3. Find C for a 2 kHz astable circuit using RA = 1 kΩ and RB = 4.7 kΩ.
  4. Find RB for a 1 kHz circuit using RA = 2.2 kΩ and C = 100 nF.
  5. Explain what happens to frequency and duty cycle when RA is increased while RB and C remain constant.
Show answers
  1. T = 0.693 × (4.7k + 2 × 10k) × 0.1 µF ≈ 1.71 ms. f ≈ 584 Hz; D ≈ 59.5%.
  2. tH ≈ 38.1 ms; tL ≈ 22.9 ms.
  3. C = 1.44/[2000 × (1000 + 9400)] ≈ 69.2 nF.
  4. RB = [1.44/(1000 × 100 nF) − 2.2k]/2 ≈ 6.1 kΩ.
  5. The HIGH time increases, LOW time remains approximately unchanged, total period increases, frequency decreases and duty cycle increases.
