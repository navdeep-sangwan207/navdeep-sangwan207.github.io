2. What is the Barkhausen Criterion?

The Barkhausen criterion gives the ideal conditions for sustained sinusoidal oscillations in a linear feedback system.

It states that, at the oscillation frequency, the total loop gain must have a magnitude of unity and a phase shift equal to an integer multiple of 360 degrees.

In simple terms, the signal returned through the feedback network must have the correct amplitude and phase to reinforce the signal already present in the circuit.

Placement point: The Barkhausen criterion is associated with sustained oscillations in feedback oscillators. It is not, by itself, a complete guarantee that a practical circuit will start and maintain stable oscillations.

4. Two Conditions of Barkhausen Criterion

Condition 1: Magnitude Condition

The magnitude of the loop gain must be equal to unity for ideal sustained oscillations.

|Aβ| = 1

If the amplifier gain is positive in magnitude:

Aβ = 1

This means that the signal fed back to the input has the same magnitude as the signal required to sustain the oscillation.

Condition 2: Phase Condition

The total phase shift around the feedback loop must be zero degrees or an integer multiple of 360 degrees.

∠Aβ = 0° or 360° × n

Where n is an integer.

The feedback signal must return in phase with the original signal so that it reinforces rather than cancels it.

5. Understanding the Magnitude Condition

Consider an amplifier with a voltage gain of 50 and a feedback network with a feedback factor of 0.02.

Given:
A = 50
β = 0.02

Loop Gain = Aβ
= 50 × 0.02
= 1

Result: Magnitude condition is satisfied.

If the loop gain magnitude is less than unity, an oscillation generally decays. If it is greater than unity, the amplitude initially grows in a small-signal model.

In a practical oscillator, nonlinear effects or an amplitude control circuit reduce the effective loop gain toward unity.

6. Understanding the Phase Condition

Suppose the amplifier introduces a phase shift of 180 degrees. The feedback network must introduce another 180 degrees so that the total phase shift is 360 degrees.

Total Phase Shift
= 180° + 180°
= 360°

360° is equivalent to 0°.

Therefore, the feedback signal returns in phase with the original input signal.

A phase shift of 360 degrees does not mean the signal is delayed by a full cycle in every physical circuit. It describes the net phase relationship at the oscillation frequency.

7. Practical Oscillation Conditions

Loop Gain Magnitude Typical behavior
|Aβ| < 1 Oscillations decay.
|Aβ| = 1 Ideal sustained oscillations.
|Aβ| > 1 Oscillation amplitude initially grows.

A practical oscillator is often designed with sufficient small-signal loop gain to start oscillations. As the output amplitude increases, circuit nonlinearities or amplitude stabilization reduce the effective loop gain.

8. Numerical Example 1: Finding Feedback Factor

Question: An amplifier has a voltage gain of 100. Calculate the feedback factor required to satisfy the magnitude condition of the Barkhausen criterion.

Given:

  • A = 100
  • |Aβ| = 1
|Aβ| = 1

100 × β = 1

β = 1/100

Answer: β = 0.01

9. Numerical Example 2: Checking Loop Gain

Question: An oscillator has an amplifier gain of 40 and a feedback factor of 0.025. Check whether the magnitude condition is satisfied.

A = 40
β = 0.025

Aβ = 40 × 0.025

Aβ = 1

Answer: The magnitude condition is satisfied.

The phase condition must also be satisfied for ideal sustained oscillations.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Barkhausen criterion describes ideal conditions for sustained oscillations.
  • Loop gain is the product of amplifier gain and feedback factor.
  • The magnitude condition is |Aβ| = 1.
  • The phase condition is 0 degrees or an integer multiple of 360 degrees.
  • Practical oscillators need startup margin and amplitude stabilization.
