2. What is a Colpitts Oscillator?

A Colpitts oscillator is an LC sinusoidal oscillator that uses two capacitors and one inductor to form its frequency-determining tank circuit.

It uses a capacitive voltage divider to provide feedback to the amplifier and sustain oscillations.

Important: The main identifying feature of a Colpitts oscillator is its capacitive divider formed by capacitors C1 and C2.

3. Circuit Components

A typical Colpitts oscillator contains:

  • Inductor (L): Stores energy in its magnetic field.
  • Capacitor C1: Forms one part of the capacitive divider.
  • Capacitor C2: Forms the other part of the capacitive divider.
  • Amplifier: Compensates for energy losses in the tank circuit.
  • DC supply: Provides energy to sustain oscillations.

4. Capacitive Voltage Divider

The two capacitors C1 and C2 are connected in series. Their junction provides a feedback voltage to the amplifier.

The equivalent capacitance of two series capacitors is:

1 / Ceq = 1 / C1 + 1 / C2

Ceq = (C1 × C2) / (C1 + C2)

This equivalent capacitance combines with the inductor to determine the resonant frequency.

5. Working Principle

  1. When the DC supply is switched ON, a small initial signal is generated due to circuit noise and transients.
  2. The amplifier increases the amplitude of the signal.
  3. The LC tank circuit selects its resonant frequency.
  4. Capacitors C1 and C2 divide the voltage and provide feedback to the amplifier.
  5. The feedback has the correct phase to reinforce the input signal at the oscillation frequency.
  6. Oscillations build up and reach a stable amplitude through practical circuit nonlinearities or an amplitude-control mechanism.

6. Frequency of Oscillation

The frequency of a Colpitts oscillator is determined by the inductance L and the equivalent capacitance Ceq.

Equivalent Capacitance:

Ceq = (C1 × C2) / (C1 + C2)
Frequency Formula:

f = 1 / (2π√(LCeq))

Therefore:

f = 1 / [2π√(L × C1 × C2 / (C1 + C2))]
This is the ideal resonant-frequency formula for the conventional Colpitts tank circuit. Practical frequency can differ because of transistor capacitances, stray capacitance, loading and component tolerances.

7. Numerical Example 1: Equivalent Capacitance

Question: Two capacitors of 100 pF and 200 pF are connected in series. Calculate their equivalent capacitance.

Given:

  • C1 = 100 pF
  • C2 = 200 pF
Ceq = (C1 × C2) / (C1 + C2)

Ceq = (100 × 200) / (100 + 200)

Ceq = 20000 / 300

Ceq = 66.67 pF

8. Numerical Example 2: Frequency Calculation

Question: A Colpitts oscillator has L = 100 µH, C1 = 100 pF and C2 = 200 pF. Calculate its ideal resonant frequency.

Step 1: Calculate equivalent capacitance.

Ceq = (100 × 200) / (100 + 200)

Ceq = 66.67 pF

Step 2: Apply the frequency formula.

f = 1 / (2π√(LCeq))

L = 100 × 10-6 H
Ceq = 66.67 × 10-12 F

f = 1 / (2π√(100 × 10-6 × 66.67 × 10-12))

f ≈ 1.95 MHz

9. Advantages

  • Suitable for high-frequency signal generation.
  • Uses a simple LC resonant circuit.
  • Provides capacitive feedback.
  • Frequency can be adjusted using L, C1 or C2.
  • Can provide good frequency stability in suitable circuit designs.

10. Disadvantages

  • Frequency can be affected by stray capacitance.
  • Inductor losses reduce circuit efficiency.
  • Component tolerances affect the oscillation frequency.
  • Very high frequency stability generally requires a crystal reference.
  • Practical design requires correct feedback polarity and sufficient loop gain.

11. Applications

  • RF signal generators.
  • Radio-frequency transmitters.
  • Communication equipment.
  • Local oscillator circuits.
  • High-frequency electronic circuits.
  • Laboratory signal-generation systems.

12. Hartley vs. Colpitts Oscillator

Parameter Hartley Oscillator Colpitts Oscillator
Feedback divider Inductive Capacitive
Inductors Two sections or tapped inductor Usually one main inductor
Capacitors One main tank capacitor Two capacitors
Frequency-determining network LC tank LC tank
Feedback mechanism Inductive voltage division Capacitive voltage division
Applications RF oscillators RF oscillators

15. Lesson Summary

  • Colpitts oscillator is an LC sinusoidal oscillator.
  • It uses two capacitors and one inductor.
  • C1 and C2 form a capacitive voltage divider.
  • The equivalent capacitance is C1C2/(C1 + C2).
  • The resonant frequency depends on L and Ceq.
  • It is commonly used in RF signal-generation circuits.
  • Its main difference from Hartley is the capacitive feedback divider.
