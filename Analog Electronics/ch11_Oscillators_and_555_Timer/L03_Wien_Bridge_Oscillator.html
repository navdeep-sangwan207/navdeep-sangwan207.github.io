2. Introduction

A Wien bridge oscillator is a sinusoidal oscillator that uses a lead-lag RC network and a non-inverting amplifier to generate a stable sinusoidal output.

It is widely used in audio-frequency signal generators and laboratory instruments because it can produce low-distortion sine waves when properly designed.

Frequency Network

A lead-lag RC bridge selects the oscillation frequency.

Amplifier

A non-inverting op-amp provides the required gain.

Amplitude Control

A lamp, diode network or AGC circuit stabilizes output amplitude.

3. Basic Wien Bridge Network

The frequency-selective network consists of a series RC arm and a parallel RC arm. The network is connected between the amplifier output and its non-inverting input.

5. Frequency of Oscillation

For the standard Wien bridge network with equal resistor values and equal capacitor values:

R1 = R2 = R
C1 = C2 = C

The frequency at which the phase shift is zero is:

f0 = 1 / (2πRC)

At this frequency, the lead-lag network has zero phase shift and an attenuation factor of 1/3.

Important observations

  • Increasing R decreases frequency.
  • Increasing C decreases frequency.
  • Decreasing R increases frequency.
  • Decreasing C increases frequency.

6. Feedback Factor and Amplifier Gain

At the oscillation frequency, the Wien network has a feedback factor of:

β = 1/3

For the Barkhausen magnitude condition:

|Aβ| = 1

Therefore:

A × 1/3 = 1
A = 3

The non-inverting op-amp gain is:

Av = 1 + (Rf/Rg)

For a gain of 3:

3 = 1 + (Rf/Rg)
Rf = 2Rg

Example: If Rg = 10 kΩ, choose Rf = 20 kΩ for an ideal closed-loop gain of 3.

In practical designs, the initial gain must be slightly above 3 for start-up. A stabilization circuit then reduces the effective gain toward 3 as the output amplitude grows.

7. Barkhausen Criterion in a Wien Bridge Oscillator

Magnitude condition

Aβ = 3 × 1/3 = 1

Phase condition

φ = 0°

The Wien bridge network contributes zero phase shift at its selected frequency. The non-inverting amplifier does not invert the signal, so the total loop phase is zero degrees.

8. Amplitude Stabilization

Without amplitude control, an oscillator designed with gain above 3 can produce a growing output that eventually clips at the amplifier's output limits.

Amplitude stabilization is used to control the output level and reduce distortion.

Incandescent Lamp

A small lamp is used as a temperature-dependent resistor. As output amplitude increases, lamp heating changes its resistance and reduces amplifier gain.

Diode Stabilization

Diodes can be arranged to alter the feedback resistance as signal amplitude increases. This controls gain, but must be designed to avoid excessive waveform distortion.

AGC Circuit

Automatic gain control adjusts the amplifier gain based on output amplitude. It can provide controlled, low-distortion operation.

The classic Wien bridge oscillator is often demonstrated using an incandescent lamp in the negative-feedback path. Its gradual thermal response can provide relatively smooth amplitude control.

13. Worked Numerical Problems

Example 1: Calculate Oscillation Frequency

Given R=10 kΩ and C=10 nF.

f0 = 1 / (2π × 10000 × 10 × 10⁻⁹)
f0 ≈ 1591.55 Hz = 1.592 kHz

Example 2: Calculate Resistance

Required frequency = 1 kHz, C=10 nF.

R = 1 / (2πf0C)
R = 1 / (2π × 1000 × 10⁻⁸)
R ≈ 15.92 kΩ

Example 3: Calculate Amplifier Gain Resistors

Choose Rg=10 kΩ and calculate Rf for gain 3.

Rf = (Av − 1) × Rg
Rf = (3 − 1) × 10 kΩ = 20 kΩ

Example 4: Feedback Factor

At the oscillation frequency, the feedback factor is 1/3.

β = 0.3333

With amplifier gain 3, the loop-gain magnitude is approximately unity.

14. Practical Design Considerations

  • Use closely matched, stable resistors and capacitors in the Wien network.
  • Choose an op-amp with adequate gain-bandwidth product and output swing.
  • Check the op-amp common-mode input range.
  • Design gain slightly above 3 for reliable start-up.
  • Include a suitable amplitude-stabilization mechanism.
  • Use a stable power supply and local bypass capacitors.
  • Keep feedback connections short and avoid unwanted capacitive coupling.
  • Buffer the output if the load would disturb the oscillator.
  • Check waveform distortion and frequency drift.

18. Placement-Oriented Interview Questions

1. What is a Wien bridge oscillator?

A sinusoidal oscillator using a lead-lag RC network and a non-inverting amplifier.

2. What is the frequency formula?

For equal R and C values, f0=1/(2πRC).

3. What is the feedback factor at resonance?

One-third for the standard equal-component Wien network.

4. What amplifier gain is required?

An ideal gain of 3.

5. Why is the amplifier non-inverting?

The Wien bridge network has zero phase shift at its selected frequency, so the total loop phase remains zero.

6. Why is the gain initially designed above 3?

To provide start-up margin.

7. What is the purpose of the lamp?

Its temperature-dependent resistance helps stabilize oscillator amplitude.

8. How can diodes stabilize amplitude?

They can change the effective feedback resistance as signal amplitude increases.

9. What happens if gain remains too high?

Output amplitude grows and eventually clips, increasing distortion.

10. What happens if gain is below 3?

Oscillation cannot be sustained in the ideal standard circuit.

11. What happens if R is doubled?

Frequency becomes half if C remains unchanged.

12. What happens if C is doubled?

Frequency becomes half if R remains unchanged.

13. Why are matched RC components important?

They help achieve the intended bridge balance and oscillation frequency.

14. Why is an output buffer sometimes needed?

To isolate the oscillator from external load variations.

15. What is a major advantage of this oscillator?

It can generate low-distortion sinusoidal signals over a useful low-frequency range.

20. Lecture Summary

  • The Wien bridge oscillator uses a lead-lag RC network.
  • At the selected frequency, the network phase shift is zero.
  • For equal R and C, f0=1/(2πRC).
  • The feedback factor is 1/3.
  • The ideal amplifier gain is 3.
  • The non-inverting op-amp gain is 1+Rf/Rg.
  • Amplitude stabilization helps reduce distortion and maintain a stable output.
