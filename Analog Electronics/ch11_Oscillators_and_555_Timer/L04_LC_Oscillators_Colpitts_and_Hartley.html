2. What is a Wien Bridge Oscillator?

A Wien bridge oscillator is a sinusoidal oscillator that uses a lead-lag RC feedback network and a non-inverting amplifier to generate a low-distortion sine wave.

It is commonly used in audio-frequency signal generators, laboratory instruments and electronic testing equipment.

Important: At the oscillation frequency, the Wien bridge feedback network produces zero phase shift and has a feedback factor of 1/3 when the two resistor values and the two capacitor values are equal.

3. Circuit Configuration

A typical Wien bridge oscillator consists of:

  • An operational amplifier configured as a non-inverting amplifier.
  • A series RC network in the positive-feedback path.
  • A parallel RC network in the positive-feedback path.
  • A resistive negative-feedback network to set amplifier gain.
  • An amplitude-control circuit to maintain a stable output.

4. Working Principle

  1. When the power supply is switched ON, a small initial signal is produced by circuit noise and transients.
  2. The non-inverting amplifier amplifies the signal without introducing a 180-degree phase inversion.
  3. The lead-lag RC network selects the desired oscillation frequency.
  4. At this frequency, the feedback network provides zero net phase shift and returns a fraction of the output signal.
  5. The amplifier gain compensates for the attenuation of the feedback network.
  6. An amplitude-control mechanism stabilizes the output waveform and reduces distortion.

5. Lead-Lag RC Feedback Network

The Wien bridge network contains two RC sections:

Series RC Section

A resistor and capacitor are connected in series. This section provides a frequency-dependent phase response.

Parallel RC Section

A resistor and capacitor are connected in parallel. This section provides the complementary frequency response.

The combined network is called a lead-lag network because its phase response changes with frequency.

At one particular frequency, the phase shifts cancel each other and the net phase shift becomes zero degrees.

6. Frequency of Oscillation

For the standard Wien bridge network, when the series and parallel sections use equal resistors and equal capacitors:

Frequency Formula:

f = 1 / (2πRC)

Where:
f = Frequency of oscillation in Hz
R = Resistance in ohms
C = Capacitance in farads

At this frequency, the network provides zero phase shift and its feedback factor is 1/3.

7. Gain Condition

The Wien bridge feedback network attenuates the output signal to one-third of its value at the oscillation frequency. Therefore, the amplifier must provide a gain of 3 for ideal sustained oscillations.

Feedback factor:
β = 1/3

Barkhausen magnitude condition:
|Aβ| = 1

A × 1/3 = 1

A = 3

For a non-inverting op-amp amplifier:

A = 1 + (Rf/R1)

For A = 3:
3 = 1 + (Rf/R1)
Rf/R1 = 2

Rf = 2R1
In practice, the circuit needs startup gain slightly above 3. An amplitude-control mechanism then reduces the effective gain toward 3 to maintain a stable output.

8. Amplitude Stabilization

If amplifier gain is too high, the output amplitude increases and may become distorted due to clipping. If the gain is too low, oscillations decay.

Common amplitude-control techniques include:

  • Incandescent lamp: Its resistance changes with temperature, helping regulate amplifier gain.
  • Diodes: Back-to-back diodes can provide nonlinear gain control.
  • JFET-based control: A JFET can act as a voltage-controlled resistance in an automatic gain-control circuit.

A properly designed amplitude-control circuit helps produce a stable, low-distortion sine wave.

9. Numerical Example

Question: Calculate the frequency of a Wien bridge oscillator if R = 10 kΩ and C = 0.01 µF.

Given:

  • R = 10,000 Ω
  • C = 0.01 × 10-6 F
f = 1 / (2πRC)

f = 1 / (2π × 10,000 × 0.01 × 10-6)

f ≈ 1591.55 Hz

Answer: Approximately 1.59 kHz

10. Advantages

  • Produces a low-distortion sine wave.
  • Simple RC frequency-selective network.
  • Does not require inductors.
  • Frequency can be adjusted by changing R or C.
  • Suitable for audio-frequency applications.

11. Disadvantages

  • Requires an amplitude-stabilization mechanism for good performance.
  • Frequency stability depends on resistor and capacitor tolerances.
  • Not generally suitable for very high-frequency operation.
  • Op-amp bandwidth and slew rate limit the practical frequency range.

12. Applications

  • Audio-frequency signal generators.
  • Function generators.
  • Electronic testing instruments.
  • Laboratory measurement equipment.
  • Low-distortion sine-wave generation.
  • Frequency-response testing of amplifiers.

16. Lesson Summary

  • Wien bridge oscillator generates a sinusoidal output.
  • It uses a lead-lag RC feedback network.
  • At the oscillation frequency, phase shift is zero degrees.
