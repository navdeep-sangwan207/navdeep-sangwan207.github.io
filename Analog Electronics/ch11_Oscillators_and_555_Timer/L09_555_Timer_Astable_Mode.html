2. What is Astable Mode?

In astable mode, the 555 timer continuously switches its output between HIGH and LOW states without requiring an external trigger for every cycle.

It generates a continuous train of rectangular or square-like output pulses.

Important: Astable means the circuit has no stable output state. It continuously oscillates between two temporary states.

3. Circuit Components

A standard 555 timer astable circuit uses:

  • 555 timer IC.
  • Resistor R1 between VCC and discharge pin 7.
  • Resistor R2 between discharge pin 7 and the timing capacitor node.
  • Capacitor C between the timing node and ground.
  • Pin 2 (TRIGGER) and pin 6 (THRESHOLD) connected together.
  • Pin 4 (RESET) connected to VCC.
  • Pin 5 (CONTROL) usually bypassed to ground with a small capacitor.

4. Working Principle

The astable circuit operates through repeated charging and discharging of capacitor C.

Step 1: Capacitor Charging

  • Initially, the output becomes HIGH.
  • The discharge transistor connected to pin 7 turns OFF.
  • Capacitor C charges through R1 and R2.
  • The capacitor voltage rises toward VCC.

Step 2: Threshold Reached

  • When the capacitor voltage reaches approximately 2/3 VCC, the threshold comparator resets the latch.
  • The output becomes LOW.
  • The discharge transistor turns ON.

Step 3: Capacitor Discharging

  • The capacitor discharges through R2 and pin 7.
  • The capacitor voltage decreases.
  • When it falls below approximately 1/3 VCC, the trigger comparator sets the latch.
  • The output becomes HIGH again.

Step 4: Continuous Operation

The charging and discharging process repeats continuously, generating a periodic output waveform.

Charging and discharging repeat continuously.

The capacitor voltage moves between 1/3 VCC and 2/3 VCC. Its waveform is an exponential charging and discharging curve.

The output at pin 3 is a rectangular waveform, while the timing capacitor voltage is approximately a repeating exponential waveform.

6. HIGH Time (TON)

During the HIGH output state, the capacitor charges through both R1 and R2.

TON = 0.693 (R1 + R2) C

Where:

  • R1 and R2 are timing resistors in ohms.
  • C is the timing capacitor in farads.
  • TON is measured in seconds.

7. LOW Time (TOFF)

During the LOW output state, capacitor C discharges through R2 and the internal discharge transistor.

TOFF = 0.693 R2 C

R1 is not part of the normal discharge path in the standard astable configuration.

8. Total Time Period

The total time period is the sum of HIGH and LOW times.

T = TON + TOFF

T = 0.693(R1 + R2)C + 0.693R2C

T = 0.693(R1 + 2R2)C

9. Frequency of Oscillation

Frequency is the number of output cycles generated per second.

f = 1 / T

f = 1.44 / [(R1 + 2R2)C]

Where:

  • f = Frequency in hertz (Hz).
  • R1 and R2 = Resistance in ohms (Ω).
  • C = Capacitance in farads (F).

10. Duty Cycle

Duty cycle is the percentage of one complete cycle during which the output remains HIGH.

Duty Cycle = (TON / T) × 100%

Duty Cycle = [(R1 + R2) / (R1 + 2R2)] × 100%
In the standard astable configuration with positive R1 and R2, the output duty cycle is greater than 50%. A modified circuit can be used to obtain a duty cycle below 50%.

Output waveform (Pin 3):

Timing capacitor voltage:

The output is HIGH while the capacitor charges from 1/3 VCC to 2/3 VCC. It is LOW while the capacitor discharges back to 1/3 VCC.

12. Numerical Example 1: HIGH and LOW Time

Question: A 555 timer astable circuit has R1 = 10 kΩ, R2 = 20 kΩ and C = 10 µF. Calculate HIGH time and LOW time.

Given:

  • R1 = 10,000 Ω
  • R2 = 20,000 Ω
  • C = 10 × 10-6 F
TON = 0.693(R1 + R2)C

= 0.693(10,000 + 20,000)(10 × 10-6)

TON = 0.2079 s

TOFF = 0.693R2C

= 0.693(20,000)(10 × 10-6)

TOFF = 0.1386 s

13. Numerical Example 2: Frequency

Using the same values:

  • R1 = 10 kΩ
  • R2 = 20 kΩ
  • C = 10 µF
T = 0.693(R1 + 2R2)C

T = 0.693(10,000 + 40,000)(10 × 10-6)

T = 0.3465 s

f = 1 / T

f ≈ 2.89 Hz

14. Numerical Example 3: Duty Cycle

Using R1 = 10 kΩ and R2 = 20 kΩ:

Duty Cycle = [(R1 + R2) / (R1 + 2R2)] × 100%

= [(10 + 20) / (10 + 40)] × 100%

= (30 / 50) × 100%

Duty Cycle = 60%

15. Applications

  • LED flashing circuits.
  • Clock pulse generation.
  • Square-wave generation.
  • Pulse generation for digital circuits.
  • Frequency generation.
  • Audio tone generation.
  • Timing and control circuits.
  • Simple PWM and switching experiments.

16. Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • Simple circuit design.
  • Low component count.
  • Adjustable frequency and timing.
  • Useful for continuous pulse generation.
  • Low-cost implementation.

Limitations

  • Frequency accuracy depends on R and C tolerances.
  • Standard astable duty cycle is greater than 50%.
  • Output timing can vary with supply and temperature.
  • Not ideal for very high-precision clock generation.

  • Astable mode produces continuous output pulses.

  • The capacitor discharges through R2 and pin 7.
  • HIGH time = 0.693(R1 + R2)C.
  • LOW time = 0.693R2C.
  • Time period = 0.693(R1 + 2R2)C.
