2. What is a Crystal Oscillator?

A crystal oscillator is an electronic oscillator that uses the mechanical resonance of a piezoelectric crystal, commonly quartz, to generate a highly stable periodic signal.

The crystal acts as a high-Q resonant element and determines the operating frequency of the oscillator circuit.

Important: Crystal oscillators are widely used where accurate and stable clock signals are required, such as microcontrollers, computers and communication systems.

3. Piezoelectric Effect

The piezoelectric effect is the property of certain materials to produce an electrical voltage when mechanical stress is applied.

The reverse piezoelectric effect occurs when an applied electrical voltage causes mechanical deformation or vibration in the material.

  • Mechanical pressure can produce electrical charge.
  • An applied electric field can produce mechanical vibration.
  • Quartz exhibits piezoelectric properties.
  • At resonance, the crystal vibrates strongly at a particular frequency.

4. Construction of a Quartz Crystal

A typical quartz crystal unit consists of:

  • A thin quartz crystal plate cut in a particular orientation.
  • Metal electrodes deposited on opposite surfaces.
  • A protective enclosure to protect the crystal from contamination and damage.
  • Electrical terminals for connection to an oscillator circuit.

The dimensions and cut of the crystal influence its resonant frequency and temperature characteristics.

5. Equivalent Electrical Circuit

A quartz crystal can be represented by an equivalent electrical circuit consisting of:

  • R: Represents mechanical and electrical losses.
  • L: Represents the vibrating mass of the crystal.
  • C: Represents the mechanical elasticity.
  • C0: Represents the capacitance between the electrodes.

6. Series Resonance

At series resonance, the inductive reactance and capacitive reactance of the motional branch become equal.

XL = XC

2πfsL = 1 / (2πfsC)

fs = 1 / (2π√(LC))

At this frequency, the motional branch has minimum impedance, approximately equal to its resistance R.

  • Impedance is minimum in the ideal series-resonance model.
  • Current through the motional branch is maximum for a given voltage.
  • The crystal can provide a highly selective resonant response.

7. Parallel Resonance

Parallel resonance occurs at a frequency slightly higher than the series-resonant frequency in the usual crystal equivalent circuit.

The motional branch interacts with the shunt capacitance C0, producing a high-impedance resonance.

Approximate parallel-resonant frequency:

fp ≈ fs √(1 + C/C0)

Where:
fs = Series-resonant frequency
C = Motional capacitance
C0 = Shunt capacitance
This is an approximation for a high-Q crystal with small motional capacitance relative to C0. Actual behavior also depends on crystal losses and the oscillator's load capacitance.

8. Series vs. Parallel Resonance

Parameter Series Resonance Parallel Resonance
Frequency fs fp, usually slightly higher
Impedance Minimum Maximum
Resonant behavior Motional branch resonates Motional branch interacts with C0

9. Why is a Crystal Oscillator Highly Stable?

A quartz crystal has a high quality factor (Q-factor). This means it has a narrow resonant bandwidth and relatively low energy loss per cycle.

Q = Resonant Frequency / Bandwidth

A high Q-factor indicates a narrow resonance.

Frequency stability is influenced by:

  • Crystal material and cut.
  • Temperature variations.
  • Mechanical stress and vibration.
  • Load capacitance and circuit design.
  • Ageing of the crystal.

10. Numerical Example: Series Resonant Frequency

Question: A crystal has motional inductance L = 10 mH and motional capacitance C = 0.01 pF. Calculate its series-resonant frequency.

Given:

  • L = 10 × 10-3 H
  • C = 0.01 × 10-12 F
fs = 1 / (2π√(LC))

fs = 1 / (2π√(10 × 10-3 × 0.01 × 10-12))

fs ≈ 503.3 kHz

11. Advantages

  • High frequency stability.
  • High Q-factor.
  • Excellent frequency selectivity.
  • Small size and low power consumption in many designs.
  • Reliable clock generation.

12. Disadvantages

  • Frequency adjustment range is limited.
  • Crystal units can be sensitive to mechanical shock.
  • Temperature can affect resonant frequency.
  • Each crystal is designed for a particular frequency range.
  • High-frequency operation may require specialized crystal designs.

13. Applications

  • Microcontroller clock circuits.
  • Computer and digital system clocks.
  • Communication equipment.
  • Frequency references in RF systems.
  • Real-time clock circuits.
  • Digital instruments and measurement equipment.

14. Crystal Oscillator vs. LC Oscillator
