2. What Is Monostable Operation?

A monostable multivibrator has one stable state and one temporary state. A trigger pulse causes the circuit to leave its stable state for a predetermined time. It then automatically returns to the stable state.

Stable state

Output LOW. The internal discharge transistor is ON and the timing capacitor is held near ground.

Temporary state

Output HIGH. The discharge transistor is OFF and the timing capacitor charges through the external resistor.

It is called a one-shot circuit because a valid trigger produces one output pulse. The pulse duration is primarily determined by the external resistor and capacitor.

T = 1.1 × R × C

Here, T is in seconds, R is in ohms and C is in farads.

4. Working Principle – Step by Step

Step 1: Stable state

Initially, the output is LOW. The internal discharge transistor connected to pin 7 is ON. The timing capacitor is discharged, so its voltage is approximately zero.

Step 2: Trigger applied

A negative-going trigger pulse brings pin 2 below approximately one-third of VCC. The lower comparator sets the internal latch.

The output becomes HIGH and the discharge transistor turns OFF.

Step 3: Capacitor charging

The timing capacitor charges through the external resistor towards VCC. Its voltage rises exponentially.

Step 4: Threshold reached

When the capacitor voltage reaches approximately two-thirds of VCC, the upper comparator resets the latch.

The output becomes LOW and the discharge transistor turns ON.

Step 5: Return to stable state

The timing capacitor discharges through the internal discharge transistor. The circuit is ready for the next valid trigger.

Remember: Trigger below 1/3 VCC starts the pulse. Capacitor voltage reaching 2/3 VCC ends the pulse.

6. Derivation of Pulse Width

During the HIGH output state, the capacitor charges through R from approximately zero towards VCC.

The capacitor charging equation is:

VC(t) = VCC (1 − e−t/RC)

At the end of the timing interval:

VC(T) = 2VCC/3

Substituting:

2VCC/3 = VCC (1 − e−T/RC)

2/3 = 1 − e−T/RC

e−T/RC = 1/3

T = RC ln(3)

T ≈ 1.0986 RC ≈ 1.1 RC

This is the standard approximate pulse-width equation for a 555 monostable circuit.

Example 2: Calculate timing capacitor

A monostable circuit requires a 0.5 second pulse. R = 47 kΩ. Find C.

C = T/(1.1R)

C = 0.5/(1.1 × 47,000)

11. Practical Lab Experiment

Objective: Construct a 555 monostable circuit and measure its output pulse width.

Components

  • NE555 timer IC or compatible variant
  • Regulated DC supply within the selected IC's specified operating range
  • 100 kΩ timing resistor
  • 10 µF timing capacitor
  • Push-button trigger circuit with suitable pull-up resistor
  • 0.1 µF supply decoupling capacitor
  • Optional 10 nF capacitor from pin 5 to ground
  • Oscilloscope or logic analyser; LED with suitable current-limiting resistor for visual indication

Procedure

  1. Connect pin 1 to ground and pin 8 to the regulated supply.
  2. Connect pin 4 to VCC so the timer is not held in reset.
  3. Connect pins 6 and 7 to the timing network as shown in a verified standard monostable schematic: pin 6 to the capacitor node, pin 7 to the resistor-capacitor junction.
  4. Connect the timing resistor between VCC and the pin 7/capacitor junction; connect the timing capacitor from pin 6 to ground.
  5. Connect pin 2 to a normally HIGH trigger input. Use a push button to momentarily pull pin 2 LOW, ensuring the trigger pulse is below 1/3 VCC.
  6. Connect pin 3 to the oscilloscope or a suitable indicator load.
  7. Apply power and trigger the circuit.
  8. Measure the output HIGH duration and compare it with the calculated value.
Safety: Check the selected IC datasheet and supply rating. Do not connect an LED directly without a current-limiting resistor. Never connect the timer output directly to a relay coil or other inductive load without an appropriate driver and protection.

13. Design Considerations and Limitations

  • Component tolerances: The actual pulse width depends on resistor and capacitor tolerances.
  • Capacitor leakage: Leakage becomes important with large timing resistors and long timing intervals.
  • Trigger pulse: The trigger must fall below approximately 1/3 VCC. Avoid holding it LOW unintentionally.
  • Supply decoupling: Place a bypass capacitor close to the IC supply pins.
  • Output loading: Check the datasheet's output current and voltage specifications before connecting a load.
  • Maximum timing: Very long delays can become inaccurate because of leakage, tolerance and noise.
  • IC variants: Bipolar NE555 and CMOS-compatible timer variants have different electrical characteristics.
The 1.1RC equation is a nominal design approximation. It does not guarantee exact timing under all operating conditions. For precision timing, consider component tolerances, leakage, temperature and the timer's datasheet specifications.

14. Placement Interview Questions and Answers

1. What is a monostable multivibrator?

A circuit with one stable state and one temporary state. A trigger initiates a pulse of predetermined duration.

2. Why is it called a one-shot multivibrator?

One valid trigger event initiates one output pulse, after which the circuit returns to its stable state.

3. What is the pulse-width equation of a 555 monostable?

T = 1.1RC.

4. What voltage triggers the 555 timer?

The trigger input must fall below approximately 1/3 of VCC.

5. What ends the timing interval?

The threshold voltage at pin 6 reaches approximately 2/3 of VCC, resetting the internal latch.

6. What happens to pin 7 during the output HIGH interval?

The internal discharge transistor is OFF, allowing the timing capacitor to charge through the resistor.

7. What happens when the output becomes LOW?

The discharge transistor turns ON and discharges the timing capacitor.

8. Why is pin 4 normally connected to VCC?

RESET is active LOW. Connecting it HIGH prevents unintended resetting.

9. Why is the capacitor voltage exponential?

Because the capacitor charges through a resistor, and the charging current decreases as the capacitor voltage approaches the supply voltage.

10. Can a 555 monostable be retriggered?

A standard 555 monostable can be retriggered under suitable trigger timing conditions. A new trigger during an active interval can extend the output pulse. Exact behavior should be checked against the selected IC datasheet and trigger waveform.

11. What determines the pulse width?

The external timing resistor and capacitor, primarily.

12. Why might the actual pulse differ from 1.1RC?

Component tolerance, capacitor leakage, IC characteristics, temperature and supply conditions contribute to timing error.

13. What is the stable output state in the standard monostable circuit?

LOW.

14. What is the role of pin 5?

It provides access to the internal control-voltage node and can be bypassed to reduce noise.

15. How can a monostable circuit control a relay?

Use pin 3 to control a suitable transistor or MOSFET driver, with appropriate inductive-load protection.

15. Numerical Practice

  1. Calculate the pulse width for R = 220 kΩ and C = 4.7 µF.
  2. Find the capacitor required for a 1-second pulse using R = 91 kΩ.
  3. Find the resistor required for a 250 ms pulse using C = 10 µF.
  4. A 555 monostable uses R = 470 kΩ and C = 22 µF. Calculate its nominal pulse width.
  5. If the timing resistor increases by 20% while C remains constant, what happens to the pulse width?
Show answers
  1. T = 1.1 × 220,000 × 4.7 × 10−6 ≈ 1.137 s.
  2. C = 1/(1.1 × 91,000) ≈ 9.99 µF.
  3. R = 0.25/(1.1 × 10 × 10−6) ≈ 22.73 kΩ.
  4. T = 1.1 × 470,000 × 22 × 10−6 ≈ 11.37 s.
  5. Pulse width increases by approximately 20%, assuming all other conditions remain unchanged.

17. Lecture Summary

  • A 555 monostable has one stable state and one temporary state.
  • A negative trigger below approximately 1/3 VCC sets the internal latch.
  • The output becomes HIGH and the timing capacitor charges through R.
  • At approximately 2/3 VCC, the threshold comparator resets the latch.
  • The output becomes LOW and the discharge transistor discharges the capacitor.
  • The nominal output pulse width is T = 1.1RC.
