2. What Is TTL?

TTL stands for Transistor-Transistor Logic. It is a digital logic family that uses bipolar junction transistors (BJTs) to perform logic operations.

In TTL circuits, transistors are used for both the logic function and signal amplification or switching.

TTL is a bipolar logic family commonly associated with the 74-series digital integrated circuits.

Important Features

  • Uses bipolar junction transistors.
  • Classic standard TTL normally operates from a 5 V supply.
  • Provides defined logic HIGH and LOW voltage levels.
  • Has relatively higher input current than typical CMOS inputs.
  • Provides useful output current drive capability.
  • Available in different speed and power subfamilies.

3. Basic TTL NAND Gate

The NAND gate is a fundamental TTL logic circuit. A classic TTL NAND gate uses a multi-emitter input transistor and additional transistors in its internal circuit.

A simplified explanation of its operation is given below.

4. Working Principle of TTL NAND Gate

Case 1: At Least One Input Is LOW

If any input is LOW, the input transistor stage conducts through the corresponding input path. This keeps the output stage in its HIGH-output condition.

Therefore, the output is:

Y = 1

Case 2: Both Inputs Are HIGH

When all inputs are HIGH, the input transistor stage allows the following stage to drive the output circuit into its LOW-output condition.

Therefore, the output is:

Y = 0
A NAND gate produces LOW only when all its inputs are HIGH. For every other input combination, its output is HIGH.

5. TTL Logic Voltage Levels

Standard 5 V TTL logic uses specified voltage limits to identify logic HIGH and logic LOW.

Typical Standard TTL Specifications

Parameter Meaning Typical Standard TTL Limit
VIL Maximum LOW input voltage 0.8 V
VIH Minimum HIGH input voltage 2.0 V
VOL Maximum LOW output voltage 0.4 V
VOH Minimum HIGH output voltage 2.4 V

These are commonly quoted guaranteed limits for standard 5 V TTL devices under specified test conditions.

Actual output voltage and current specifications depend on the particular TTL subfamily and device. Always consult the manufacturer's datasheet.

6. TTL Supply Voltage

Classic standard 74-series TTL devices are designed for a nominal 5 V supply.

VCC = 5 V (nominal)

The permitted operating range is specified in the individual IC datasheet. Do not assume every device marked with a 74-series prefix has identical supply requirements.

7. TTL Subfamilies

TTL devices are available in different subfamilies to provide different combinations of propagation delay and power consumption.

TTL Subfamily Description
74 Standard TTL
74L Low-power TTL
74H High-speed TTL
74S Schottky TTL
74LS Low-power Schottky TTL
74AS Advanced Schottky TTL
74ALS Advanced low-power Schottky TTL

Why Schottky TTL?

Schottky TTL uses Schottky transistor structures to reduce transistor saturation and storage delay. This helps improve switching speed.

Low-Power Schottky TTL

The 74LS family combines relatively low power consumption with improved switching speed compared with standard TTL.

Many of these functions are also available in CMOS-compatible families. The exact suffix and manufacturer datasheet determine the device's electrical ratings.

9. TTL Output Configurations

TTL ICs may use different output configurations depending on their intended application.

1. Totem-Pole Output

A totem-pole output uses an active transistor arrangement to drive the output HIGH and LOW. It provides active drive in both directions.

2. Open-Collector Output

An open-collector output uses an output transistor whose collector is available externally. A suitable pull-up resistor is generally required to obtain a HIGH output.

3. Tri-State Output

A tri-state output can produce logic HIGH, logic LOW or a high-impedance state.

These output arrangements are covered in greater detail in Lesson 7: Tri-State Logic and Open-Collector Outputs.

10. Advantages of TTL

  • Well-established and widely documented technology.
  • Good output drive capability in many standard devices.
  • Available in several speed and power subfamilies.
  • Simple to use in many 5 V digital logic circuits.
  • Large range of traditional logic IC functions.

11. Limitations of TTL

  • Generally consumes more static power than comparable CMOS logic.
  • Input current is higher than that of typical CMOS inputs.
  • Classic standard TTL is mainly associated with 5 V operation.
  • Direct interfacing with other logic families requires voltage and current compatibility checks.
  • Modern high-density digital systems generally use CMOS-based technologies.

12. Practical TTL Interfacing Example

Suppose a standard TTL output drives a compatible standard TTL input.

The designer must verify:

  1. The output HIGH voltage is sufficient for the receiving input.
  2. The output LOW voltage is low enough for the receiving input.
  3. The output source and sink currents meet the load requirements.
  4. The total fan-out does not exceed the output drive capability.
  5. The devices share a suitable common reference ground.
Never connect IC outputs together unless their output structures are specifically designed for that arrangement. Connecting ordinary totem-pole outputs together can cause excessive current and damage.

13. Applications of TTL

  • Digital logic gate circuits.
  • Binary counters and frequency dividers.
  • Flip-flop and register circuits.
  • Digital control circuits.
  • Educational digital electronics laboratories.
  • Legacy industrial and computer hardware.
  • Small-scale digital interfacing circuits.
