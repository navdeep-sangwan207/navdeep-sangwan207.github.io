Lesson 4: TTL and CMOS Comparison

Chapter 9 – Digital Logic Families and Interfacing

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand TTL and CMOS logic technologies.
  • Compare their internal construction.
  • Differentiate their voltage and current characteristics.
  • Compare power consumption and switching speed.
  • Understand input impedance and noise immunity.
  • Learn practical TTL-CMOS interfacing considerations.
  • Identify suitable logic families for embedded systems.

2. Introduction to TTL and CMOS

TTL – Transistor-Transistor Logic

TTL is a bipolar digital logic family that uses Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJTs) to implement logic operations.

Classic TTL devices commonly operate from a nominal 5 V supply. Examples include the 7400 NAND gate and 7404 inverter.

CMOS – Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor

CMOS uses complementary NMOS and PMOS MOSFETs to implement digital logic circuits.

CMOS devices are available in several families and supply-voltage ranges. Examples include CD4011, 74HC00 and 74HCT00.

TTL and CMOS describe different logic technologies. Their electrical characteristics depend on the specific device family, supply voltage and operating conditions.

3. Internal Construction

TTL Construction

  • Uses bipolar junction transistors.
  • Uses resistors and transistor stages.
  • Classic TTL NAND gates use multi-emitter input transistors.
  • Output stages may use totem-pole or open-collector configurations.

CMOS Construction

  • Uses NMOS and PMOS transistors.
  • Uses complementary pull-up and pull-down networks.
  • Logic functions are implemented using MOSFET switching arrangements.
  • High integration density is possible.

4. Supply Voltage Comparison

Classic standard TTL devices commonly use a nominal 5 V supply.

CMOS devices are available in different voltage families. For example, some CD4000-series devices support a wider supply range than classic TTL devices.

Logic Family Supply Voltage Information
Standard TTL 74 Nominal 5 V supply.
74LS TTL Nominal 5 V supply.
74HC CMOS family with device-specific supply range.
74HCT CMOS family with TTL-compatible input thresholds.
CD4000 Many devices support a comparatively wide supply range.
Never assume that every device in a logic family supports the same supply voltage. Check the recommended operating conditions in the manufacturer's datasheet.

5. Logic Voltage Level Comparison

Digital circuits recognize input signals as either LOW or HIGH using specified voltage thresholds.

Standard 5 V TTL Logic Levels

Parameter Standard TTL Value
VIL (maximum) 0.8 V
VIH (minimum) 2.0 V
VOL (maximum) 0.4 V
VOH (minimum) 2.4 V

CMOS Logic Levels

CMOS voltage thresholds depend on the device family and supply voltage. Some 74HC devices use input thresholds related to fractions of VDD, while 74HCT devices use TTL-compatible input thresholds.

A 74HC input should not automatically be treated as TTL-compatible. A 74HCT device is often a better choice when accepting standard TTL-level signals, provided its supply and other specifications match the application.

6. Power Consumption Comparison

TTL Power Consumption

TTL circuits generally draw appreciable supply current even when their logic state remains unchanged.

Their power consumption depends on the TTL subfamily, supply voltage, switching activity and output loading.

CMOS Power Consumption

CMOS circuits generally have very low static power consumption in stable logic states. Their dynamic power increases when internal and load capacitances are switched.

Pdynamic ≈ α C VDD² f

Here α is the switching activity factor, C is effective switched capacitance, VDD is the supply voltage and f is the switching frequency.

CMOS is often preferred for battery-powered and low-power systems. However, actual power consumption must be compared using the datasheets of the particular devices.

7. Speed and Propagation Delay

Propagation delay is the time between an input transition and the corresponding output transition.

tPD = Time delay between input and output transitions

TTL Speed

  • Classic TTL families were designed for relatively fast switching.
  • Schottky TTL families reduce transistor saturation and storage delay.
  • 74S and 74AS are examples of Schottky-based TTL families.

CMOS Speed

  • Speed varies significantly between CMOS families.
  • 74HC and 74AC families support high-speed operation.
  • Load capacitance and supply voltage affect propagation delay.
It is incorrect to say that TTL is always faster than CMOS or that CMOS is always faster than TTL. Compare the actual propagation-delay specifications under similar operating conditions.

8. Input Impedance and Input Current

TTL

TTL inputs are transistor-based and draw input current. The required current depends on whether the input is LOW or HIGH and on the particular TTL family.

CMOS

MOSFET gates are insulated, resulting in very high DC input impedance and very low steady-state input current.

Feature TTL CMOS
Input impedance Lower than typical CMOS inputs Very high
DC input current Generally higher Generally very low
Input capacitance Device-dependent Important consideration

9. Noise Immunity

Noise immunity describes the ability of a digital circuit to tolerate unwanted electrical disturbances without changing its interpreted logic state.

CMOS families often provide good noise margins, especially when operated at suitable supply voltages. TTL noise margins are determined by its specified input and output voltage limits.

Noise Margin HIGH = VOH(min) - VIH(min)
Noise Margin LOW = VIL(max) - VOL(max)

These equations calculate the guaranteed HIGH and LOW noise margins for compatible output and input specifications.

10. Fan-Out Comparison

Fan-out is the number of standard logic inputs that one output can drive while maintaining valid logic levels and acceptable timing.

TTL Fan-Out

TTL fan-out is limited by the output current capability and the input currents of the connected gates.

CMOS Fan-Out

CMOS inputs draw very little DC current, so DC fan-out can be high. However, every additional input adds capacitance, which can slow switching and increase dynamic power.

Do not calculate CMOS fan-out using DC input current alone. Consider input capacitance, output drive strength, switching frequency and timing requirements.

12. TTL-CMOS Interfacing

Interfacing means connecting the output of one logic family to the input of another logic family.

TTL Output to CMOS Input

A standard TTL output may not provide a sufficiently high guaranteed HIGH voltage for every CMOS input family.

A 74HCT input is often suitable for standard TTL-level signals because it uses TTL-compatible input thresholds. Check the actual output and input specifications before connecting.

CMOS Output to TTL Input

A CMOS output may provide adequate voltage levels for TTL inputs, but its ability to source and sink the required current must also be checked.

Important Interfacing Checks

  • Check the HIGH and LOW voltage compatibility.
  • Check output source and sink current.
  • Verify the supply-voltage range of each IC.
  • Connect the circuit grounds together when required.
  • Consider output loading and propagation delay.
  • Use a suitable level shifter when direct connection is not safe.
A 3.3 V microcontroller output is not automatically guaranteed to drive every 5 V CMOS input. For example, a 5 V 74HC input may require a HIGH voltage greater than 3.3 V. Check the specified VIH. A suitable HCT device or level translator may be needed, depending on the direction and voltage levels.

13. Applications of TTL and CMOS

TTL Applications

  • Legacy digital logic systems.
  • Educational digital electronics circuits.
  • Industrial control equipment using classic logic ICs.
  • Existing 5 V logic boards.

CMOS Applications

  • Microcontrollers and microprocessors.
  • Memory devices.
  • Battery-powered electronic systems.
  • Embedded systems.
  • Digital communication devices.
  • Modern integrated circuits.

14. Which Logic Family Should You Choose?

Application Requirement Possible Choice
Existing classic 5 V TTL circuit Compatible TTL family
Low static power Suitable CMOS family
TTL-compatible input thresholds 74HCT or 74ACT, where appropriate
High-speed logic Suitable 74HC, 74AC or other specified family
Wide supply-voltage operation Suitable CD4000-series device
The correct selection depends on voltage compatibility, speed, power, output drive, temperature range and component availability. Always compare the exact datasheets.

17. Lesson Summary

  • TTL uses bipolar junction transistors.
  • CMOS uses complementary NMOS and PMOS transistors.
  • Classic TTL devices commonly operate from a nominal 5 V supply.
  • CMOS families support different supply voltages and logic thresholds.
  • CMOS generally has lower static power consumption.
  • Switching speed depends on the exact logic family and operating conditions.
  • Noise margin and fan-out are important design parameters.
  • 74HCT devices provide TTL-compatible input thresholds.
  • Always verify voltage and current compatibility before interfacing logic families.
