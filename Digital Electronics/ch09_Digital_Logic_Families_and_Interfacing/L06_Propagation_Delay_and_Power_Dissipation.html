2. What Is Propagation Delay?

Propagation delay is the time required for a change at the input of a digital logic circuit to produce the corresponding change at its output.

A logic gate does not respond instantaneously to an input transition. Internal transistor switching and circuit capacitance introduce a small time delay.

Propagation Delay = Output Transition Time - Input Transition Time
Propagation delay is generally measured in nanoseconds (ns) for digital logic ICs.

3. Types of Propagation Delay

1. tPLH – LOW-to-HIGH Propagation Delay

tPLH is the time between the input transition and the corresponding output transition from LOW to HIGH.

2. tPHL – HIGH-to-LOW Propagation Delay

tPHL is the time between the input transition and the corresponding output transition from HIGH to LOW.

For an inverting gate such as a NOT gate, the input and output transitions occur in opposite directions.

Input Transition Output Transition Delay
LOW to HIGH HIGH to LOW tPHL
HIGH to LOW LOW to HIGH tPLH

For an inverting gate, the input LOW-to-HIGH transition causes the output HIGH-to-LOW transition. For a non-inverting gate, the output transition direction is the same as the input transition.

4. Average Propagation Delay

The average propagation delay is commonly calculated using the two propagation-delay values.

tPD = (tPLH + tPHL) / 2

Numerical Example

Consider a logic gate with:

  • tPLH = 12 ns
  • tPHL = 8 ns
tPD = (12 + 8) / 2
tPD = 10 ns

Therefore, the average propagation delay is 10 nanoseconds.

This average is a useful comparison measure. Timing analysis must still consider the individual maximum tPLH and tPHL values specified by the datasheet.

5. Rise Time and Fall Time

Rise Time (tr)

Rise time is the time required for an output signal to rise from a specified LOW-level percentage to a specified HIGH-level percentage.

It is commonly measured between 10% and 90% of the signal amplitude.

Fall Time (tf)

Fall time is the time required for an output signal to fall from a specified HIGH-level percentage to a specified LOW-level percentage.

It is commonly measured between 90% and 10%.

Rise time and fall time describe output transition duration. Propagation delay describes the time shift between input and output transitions. They are different parameters.

6. Factors Affecting Propagation Delay

  • Logic family and transistor technology.
  • Supply voltage.
  • Output load capacitance.
  • Output load resistance.
  • Operating temperature.
  • Input transition time.
  • Internal circuit architecture.

A larger capacitive load can increase the time required for an output to change its voltage.

Propagation-delay values are valid only under specified test conditions. Compare datasheets using similar supply voltage, load and temperature conditions.

7. What Is Power Dissipation?

Power dissipation is the electrical power consumed by a digital IC and converted mainly into heat and other internal energy losses.

P = V × I

Where:

  • P = Power in watts.
  • V = Voltage in volts.
  • I = Current in amperes.

For a digital IC supplied from VCC, average supply power can be estimated as:

Pavg = VCC × Iavg

8. Static Power Dissipation

Static power is the power consumed when a digital circuit remains in a stable logic state without switching.

TTL Static Power

TTL circuits generally draw supply current even when their logic state remains unchanged.

CMOS Static Power

CMOS circuits ideally have very low static power consumption because one transistor network is OFF in each stable logic state.

Actual CMOS devices consume some static power due to leakage currents and other internal circuit paths.

Pstatic = VDD × Istatic

9. Dynamic Power Dissipation

Dynamic power is consumed when a digital circuit switches between logic states.

During switching, internal and external capacitances are charged and discharged.

Pdynamic ≈ α C VDD² f

Where:

  • α = Switching activity factor.
  • C = Effective switched capacitance.
  • VDD = Supply voltage.
  • f = Clock or switching frequency.
Dynamic power increases with supply voltage squared. Reducing the supply voltage can therefore significantly reduce dynamic power, provided the circuit remains within its specified operating range.

10. Short-Circuit Power

During an input transition in a CMOS gate, both the PMOS and NMOS transistors may conduct briefly at the same time.

This creates a temporary current path from VDD to ground.

The associated power is known as short-circuit power.

  • Depends on input transition time.
  • Depends on transistor characteristics.
  • Depends on supply voltage and switching activity.
  • Can increase when input transitions are slow.

11. Total Power Dissipation

The total power consumed by a digital circuit can be considered as the combination of several components.

Ptotal = Pstatic + Pdynamic + Pshort-circuit

The exact power model depends on the technology and the operating conditions.

This is a useful conceptual decomposition. Some datasheets report total supply power directly, so do not add separately estimated terms to a measured total without checking for double counting.

12. Numerical Example: Power Calculation

Consider a digital IC operating with the following conditions:

  • Supply voltage = 5 V.
  • Average supply current = 20 mA.
Step 1: Write the power equation
P = V × I
Step 2: Substitute values
P = 5 × 0.020
Step 3: Calculate power
P = 0.1 W = 100 mW

Therefore, the average power consumption is 100 milliwatts.

13. Comparison of TTL and CMOS Power

Parameter TTL CMOS
Static power Generally higher Generally very low
Dynamic power Depends on switching and load Depends strongly on capacitance, voltage and frequency
Leakage current Device-dependent Device-dependent
Low-power applications Less common for new designs Widely used
Power optimization Family and circuit dependent Voltage and switching optimization are important

14. Methods to Reduce Power Consumption

  • Use a suitable lower supply voltage within device specifications.
  • Reduce unnecessary switching activity.
  • Use clock gating where appropriate.
  • Reduce unnecessary capacitive loading.
  • Use low-power CMOS devices.
  • Disable unused peripherals and logic blocks where supported.
  • Use suitable operating frequencies.
  • Choose components with appropriate output drive strength.

15. Practical Embedded Systems Applications

Microcontroller Timing

Propagation delay affects the timing of signals passing through external logic circuits connected to a microcontroller.

Digital Communication

Propagation delay and rise and fall times affect signal integrity and timing margins in communication interfaces.

Battery-Powered Devices

Static and dynamic power consumption affect battery life in embedded products.

Digital Circuit Design

Gate delays accumulate along combinational logic paths. Designers must ensure that the circuit meets its timing requirements.

18. Lesson Summary

  • Propagation delay is the time between an input transition and its corresponding output transition.
  • tPLH represents output LOW-to-HIGH delay.
  • tPHL represents output HIGH-to-LOW delay.
  • Rise time and fall time describe output transition duration.
  • Power dissipation is electrical power consumed by a circuit.
  • Static power is consumed in stable logic states.
  • Dynamic power is associated with switching activity.
  • Short-circuit power can occur during CMOS transitions.
  • Reducing voltage, frequency and capacitance can reduce dynamic power.
  • Timing and power specifications must be checked in IC datasheets.
