Chapter 9: Digital Logic Families and Interfacing

Digital Electronics – NS Learning Hub

Chapter Overview

Digital logic families define the electrical characteristics and operating behaviour of digital integrated circuits. Understanding TTL and CMOS technologies is important for selecting ICs and connecting different digital devices reliably.

This chapter covers logic voltage levels, fan-in, fan-out, noise margin, propagation delay, power dissipation, tri-state logic and open-collector outputs. These concepts are useful in electronics hardware design, embedded systems and technical interviews.

Learning Outcomes

After completing this chapter, you will be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of digital logic families.
  • Differentiate between TTL and CMOS technologies.
  • Understand digital IC input and output voltage levels.
  • Calculate fan-out and noise margins from datasheet specifications.
  • Interpret propagation delay and switching characteristics.
  • Estimate static and dynamic power dissipation.
  • Understand tri-state and open-collector interfacing.
  • Use datasheet parameters when selecting digital ICs.
