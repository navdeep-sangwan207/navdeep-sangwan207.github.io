2. What Is a Digital Logic Family?

A digital logic family is a group of digital integrated circuits (ICs) that use similar electronic circuit technology and have compatible electrical characteristics.

Logic families define how digital circuits represent binary 0 and binary 1, how much current their inputs require, how much current their outputs can supply or sink, and how quickly they can switch.

Examples of commonly studied digital logic families include TTL and CMOS. They are widely used in digital systems, microcontrollers, computers and electronic control circuits.

Examples of Digital ICs

  • 7400 – Quad 2-input NAND gates.
  • 7404 – Hex inverter.
  • 7408 – Quad 2-input AND gates.
  • 7432 – Quad 2-input OR gates.
  • 74HC00 – CMOS quad 2-input NAND gates.
  • CD4011 – CMOS quad 2-input NAND gates.

3. Why Are Logic Families Required?

Digital electronic systems contain many logic gates and integrated circuits. A standard set of electrical characteristics helps designers connect these devices reliably.

Logic families help determine:

  • Operating voltage requirements.
  • Logic HIGH and LOW voltage ranges.
  • Input and output current capabilities.
  • Switching speed.
  • Power consumption.
  • Noise immunity.
  • Compatibility between digital ICs.

Practical Example

Suppose one digital IC produces an output signal that must drive the input of another IC.

The output voltage must satisfy the receiving IC's input voltage requirements. The output current must also be sufficient to drive the load.

If these conditions are not met, the receiving IC may interpret the signal incorrectly or operate unreliably.

4. Classification of Digital Logic Families

Digital logic families are broadly classified according to the electronic devices used in their internal circuits.

1. Bipolar Logic Families

Bipolar logic families use bipolar junction transistors (BJTs) as their main active devices.

Examples include:

  • RTL – Resistor-Transistor Logic.
  • DTL – Diode-Transistor Logic.
  • TTL – Transistor-Transistor Logic.
  • ECL – Emitter-Coupled Logic.

2. MOS Logic Families

MOS logic families use metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs).

Examples include:

  • PMOS – P-channel MOS logic.
  • NMOS – N-channel MOS logic.
  • CMOS – Complementary MOS logic.
TTL and CMOS are especially important for understanding digital IC interfacing and practical electronics.

5. Bipolar Technology

Bipolar technology uses bipolar junction transistors, which involve both electrons and holes as charge carriers.

Important Characteristics

  • Uses NPN and PNP transistors.
  • Can provide relatively high output drive capability.
  • Requires base current to control transistor operation.
  • Power consumption depends on the circuit design and operating state.
  • TTL is a common bipolar logic family.

Example: TTL IC

The 7400 IC contains four independent two-input NAND gates and is a classic example of a standard TTL device.

6. MOS Technology

MOS technology uses field-effect transistors controlled by an electric field applied to an insulated gate.

Important Characteristics

  • Very high input impedance.
  • Low steady-state input current under normal conditions.
  • Suitable for high-density integrated circuits.
  • CMOS can provide low static power consumption.
  • Switching power increases with operating frequency and load capacitance.

Example: CMOS IC

The CD4011 contains four independent two-input NAND gates implemented using CMOS technology.

7. Understanding Digital Logic Levels

Digital circuits use two logical states:

  • Logic 0: LOW state.
  • Logic 1: HIGH state.

These logical states are represented by voltage ranges rather than one exact voltage value.

Important Voltage Parameters

Parameter Meaning
VIL Maximum input voltage guaranteed to be recognized as LOW.
VIH Minimum input voltage guaranteed to be recognized as HIGH.
VOL Maximum output voltage when the output is LOW.
VOH Minimum output voltage when the output is HIGH.

Typical Standard 5 V TTL Input Specifications

Logic Level Voltage Range
LOW input 0 V to 0.8 V
HIGH input 2.0 V to 5 V
Undefined region Above 0.8 V and below 2.0 V
These are common standard TTL input limits, not universal values for every logic family. Always check the exact IC datasheet and supply voltage.

8. Logic Voltage Level Diagram

A voltage in the undefined region is not guaranteed to be interpreted as either logic 0 or logic 1. Reliable digital designs keep input voltages within specified valid logic ranges.

9. Important Electrical Parameters of Digital ICs

1. Supply Voltage (VCC or VDD)

The supply voltage required for the proper operation of an IC.

2. Input Current (II)

The current entering or leaving an input terminal, depending on the IC's electrical convention and input state.

3. Output Current (IO)

The current an output pin can source or sink while maintaining its specified logic voltage.

4. Propagation Delay (tPD)

The time between a change at an input and the corresponding change at the output.

5. Power Dissipation (PD)

The electrical power consumed by the IC during operation.

6. Noise Margin

The maximum noise voltage that can be tolerated without causing an incorrect logic interpretation, under the specified conditions.

7. Fan-Out

The maximum number of compatible standard logic inputs that one output can reliably drive.

10. TTL and CMOS: Basic Overview

Feature TTL CMOS
Technology BJT MOSFET
Input impedance Generally lower Generally very high
Static power Generally higher Generally very low
Input current Usually higher Usually very low
Supply voltage Classic 74 series commonly 5 V Depends on CMOS series
Examples 7400, 7404 74HC00, CD4011

These are broad comparisons. Actual performance depends on the particular logic family, IC variant, supply voltage, temperature and load.

11. Practical IC Datasheet Reading

Before connecting digital ICs, engineers should check their datasheets.

Important specifications include:

  1. Recommended operating supply voltage.
  2. Absolute maximum ratings.
  3. Input HIGH and LOW voltage limits.
  4. Output HIGH and LOW voltage limits.
  5. Input and output current specifications.
  6. Propagation delay.
  7. Operating temperature range.
  8. Package type and pin configuration.
Absolute maximum ratings are stress limits, not recommended operating conditions. Operating a device beyond its recommended conditions can cause malfunction or permanent damage.

15. Lesson Summary

  • A digital logic family defines the electrical characteristics of digital ICs.
  • Logic families are broadly classified into bipolar and MOS technologies.
  • TTL uses bipolar junction transistors.
  • CMOS uses complementary MOSFETs.
  • Digital circuits represent information using logic 0 and logic 1.
  • VIL, VIH, VOL and VOH define important logic voltage limits.
  • Propagation delay, fan-out, power dissipation and noise margin are important IC parameters.
  • Datasheets must be checked before connecting digital ICs.
