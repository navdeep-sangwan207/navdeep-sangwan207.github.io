2. Introduction to Race Around Condition

Race around condition is an unwanted behaviour that can occur in a level-triggered JK flip-flop when both inputs J and K are HIGH.

Under this condition, the flip-flop operates in toggle mode. If the active clock pulse remains HIGH long enough for the output to change and feed back into the internal logic, the output may toggle repeatedly during the same clock pulse.

Race around condition is associated with level-triggered JK flip-flops in toggle mode when the active clock pulse is sufficiently wide relative to the feedback and propagation delays.

3. Review of JK Flip-Flop

A JK flip-flop is a sequential logic circuit with two data inputs: J and K.

Its operation depends on the combination of these inputs and the active clock event.

J K Operation
0 0 Hold
0 1 Reset
1 0 Set
1 1 Toggle

The characteristic equation of a JK flip-flop is:

Q(next) = JQ̅ + K̅Q

When J = K = 1:

Q(next) = Q̅

Therefore, the output changes to its opposite state at each effective toggle event.

4. How Race Around Condition Occurs

Consider a level-triggered JK flip-flop with:

  • J = 1
  • K = 1
  • Clock = HIGH

The flip-flop enters toggle mode. Suppose the initial output is Q = 0.

  1. The clock becomes HIGH.
  2. The output changes from 0 to 1.
  3. The changed output is fed back into the internal logic.
  4. While the clock remains active, the circuit may respond again.
  5. The output can toggle from 1 to 0.
  6. Further toggles may occur while the clock pulse remains active.
The repeated toggling is caused by feedback during the active clock level. The final output may be unpredictable when the clock pulse ends.

6. Why Does Race Around Condition Occur?

The main reason is the feedback mechanism in a level-triggered JK flip-flop.

When J = K = 1, the output is required to toggle. If the clock remains at its active level, the output feedback can cause further state changes.

Race around is possible when the active clock pulse width is greater than the effective time needed for repeated internal response.

Tpulse > trequired for feedback response

A simplified textbook condition is often expressed as:

Tpulse > tpd

Here, tpd represents an effective propagation delay in the simplified model. The exact condition depends on the flip-flop architecture and feedback path.

7. Effects of Race Around Condition

  • Output toggles multiple times during one clock pulse.
  • Final output state may be unpredictable.
  • Incorrect data may be stored.
  • Counter operation may become unreliable.
  • Sequential circuit behaviour may become unstable.
Race around condition makes it difficult to predict the final state of a level-triggered JK flip-flop when it is operating in toggle mode with a sufficiently wide active clock pulse.

8. Methods to Eliminate Race Around Condition

1. Master-Slave JK Flip-Flop

A master-slave JK flip-flop uses two internal stages:

  • Master stage.
  • Slave stage.

The master and slave operate during opposite clock phases. This prevents repeated output feedback toggling during the same active clock level.

This is a conceptual block diagram, not a complete internal circuit.

2. Edge-Triggered JK Flip-Flop

An edge-triggered JK flip-flop responds only at a specified clock transition rather than remaining responsive throughout an active clock level.

This prevents the repeated level-sensitive toggling that causes the classic race around condition.

3. Narrow Clock Pulse

In traditional level-triggered designs, reducing the active clock pulse width below the required feedback response time can prevent repeated toggling.

Narrow-pulse clocking is not the preferred general-purpose solution. It requires careful timing design and adequate margins. Master-slave or edge-triggered flip-flops are more robust approaches.

9. Comparison of Flip-Flop Types

Type Operation Race Around
Level-Triggered JK Responds while clock is at active level Possible in toggle mode
Master-Slave JK Uses two stages operating on opposite phases Prevented in normal operation
Edge-Triggered JK Responds at a specified clock edge Classic race around is avoided

10. Practical Applications and Importance

Understanding race around condition is important when designing and analysing:

  • JK flip-flop circuits.
  • Digital counters.
  • Frequency divider circuits.
  • Sequential logic circuits.
  • Clock-controlled digital systems.
  • Flip-flop-based memory and control circuits.

In practical digital design, edge-triggered flip-flops are commonly used in synchronous registers and counters.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Race around condition is a problem associated with level-triggered JK flip-flops.
  • It occurs when J = K = 1 and the active clock pulse allows repeated feedback toggling.
  • The output may toggle multiple times during one clock pulse.
  • The final output state may be unpredictable.
  • Master-slave JK flip-flops prevent the classic race around behaviour.
  • Edge-triggered flip-flops also avoid this level-sensitive feedback problem.
  • Correct clocking is important for reliable sequential circuit operation.
