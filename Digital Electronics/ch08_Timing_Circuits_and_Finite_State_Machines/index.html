Chapter Overview

This chapter explains timing concepts used in synchronous digital systems. It covers clock signals, clock frequency, timing diagrams, propagation delay, setup time and hold time.

It also introduces the race-around condition in JK flip-flops and finite state machines (FSMs), including Moore and Mealy models. These concepts are essential for digital circuit design, microcontrollers, FPGA development and embedded controllers.

Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the characteristics of digital clock signals.
  • Calculate clock period, frequency and duty cycle.
  • Interpret timing diagrams of sequential circuits.
  • Calculate propagation delay and understand its effect on circuit speed.
  • Explain setup time and hold time requirements.
  • Understand metastability and timing violations.
  • Explain the race-around condition in JK flip-flops.
  • Differentiate Moore and Mealy finite state machines.
  • Construct basic state diagrams and state tables.
  • Understand FSM applications in embedded systems.

Placement and Interview Relevance

Timing analysis and finite state machines are important topics in digital electronics, embedded systems, FPGA design and hardware verification interviews.

Focus on clock frequency calculations, setup and hold time, propagation delay, race-around prevention, Moore and Mealy machine differences, and state diagram construction.
