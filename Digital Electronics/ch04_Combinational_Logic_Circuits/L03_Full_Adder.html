2. What Is a Full Adder?

A full adder is a combinational logic circuit that adds three single-bit binary inputs and produces two outputs: Sum and Carry.

The three inputs are:

  • A: First binary input.
  • B: Second binary input.
  • Cin: Carry input from the previous stage.

The two outputs are:

  • Sum (S): Least significant bit of the result.
  • Carry-out (Cout): Carry generated for the next stage.
The main difference between a half adder and a full adder is that a full adder accepts a third input called carry-in (Cin).
Inputs: A, B, Cin
Outputs: Sum, Cout

The Sum output is 1 when an odd number of the three inputs are 1. The Carry output is 1 when at least two of the three inputs are 1.

5. Boolean Expression for Sum

From the truth table, Sum is HIGH when an odd number of input bits are HIGH.

S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin

Expanded sum-of-products expression:

S = A̅B̅Cin + A̅BCin̅ + AB̅Cin̅ + ABCin
The XOR operation is useful for generating the Sum because XOR produces 1 when its inputs differ. Cascading two XOR gates gives the full adder Sum.

6. Boolean Expression for Carry-out

Carry-out becomes HIGH whenever at least two of the three input bits are HIGH.

Cout = AB + ACin + BCin

This expression is also called the majority function because it produces 1 when the majority of inputs are 1.

Alternative Carry Expression

Cout = AB + (A ⊕ B)Cin

This form is convenient when implementing a full adder using two half adders.

7. Full Adder Block Diagram

The full adder processes A, B and Cin and produces the Sum and Carry-out outputs.

8. Full Adder Using Two Half Adders

A full adder can be constructed using two half adders and one OR gate.

First Half Adder

Inputs: A and B

S1 = A ⊕ B
C1 = A · B

Second Half Adder

Inputs: S1 and Cin

S = S1 ⊕ Cin
C2 = S1 · Cin

Final Carry-out

The two carry outputs are combined using an OR gate.

Cout = C1 + C2

Complete Expressions

S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin
Cout = AB + (A ⊕ B)Cin
The first half adder generates an intermediate sum and carry. The second half adder adds the intermediate sum to Cin. An OR gate combines the two carry outputs.

9. Full Adder Using Logic Gates

The full adder can be implemented using:

  • Two XOR gates.
  • Two AND gates.
  • One OR gate.
Gate Quantity Purpose
XOR2Generate Sum
AND2Generate carry terms
OR1Combine carry terms

Common Digital ICs

  • 7486: XOR gates.
  • 7408: AND gates.
  • 7432: OR gates.

10. Worked Example

Consider the following inputs:

A = 1, B = 1, Cin = 1

Step 1: Calculate Sum

S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin
S = 1 ⊕ 1 ⊕ 1 = 1

Step 2: Calculate Carry-out

Cout = AB + ACin + BCin
Cout = 1 + 1 + 1 = 1

Final Result

Cout = 1, Sum = 1

The binary result of 1 + 1 + 1 is 11.

11. Multi-Bit Binary Addition

Full adders can be connected in series to add multi-bit binary numbers.

The carry output of one stage becomes the carry input of the next higher-order stage.

Example: Adding Two 4-Bit Numbers

       0101
     + 0011
     -------
       1000

In a four-bit ripple-carry adder, the carry generated by each lower-order stage is passed to the next stage.

The propagation of carry through successive full adders is the basis of a ripple-carry adder. It is covered in the arithmetic circuits chapter.

13. Practical Experiment – Full Adder Using ICs

Components Required

  • 7486 XOR gate IC.
  • 7408 AND gate IC.
  • 7432 OR gate IC.
  • Regulated 5 V supply compatible with the IC family.
  • Three input switches.
  • Two LEDs with current-limiting resistors.
  • Breadboard and connecting wires.

Basic IC Pin Connections

7486 XOR IC:

  • Pin 14 – VCC.
  • Pin 7 – GND.
  • Pin 1 and Pin 2 – First XOR inputs.
  • Pin 3 – First XOR output.
  • Pin 4 and Pin 5 – Second XOR inputs.
  • Pin 6 – Second XOR output (Sum).

7408 AND IC:

  • Pin 14 – VCC.
  • Pin 7 – GND.
  • Pin 1 and Pin 2 – First AND inputs.
  • Pin 3 – First AND output (C1).
  • Pin 4 and Pin 5 – Second AND inputs.
  • Pin 6 – Second AND output (C2).

7432 OR IC:

  • Pin 14 – VCC.
  • Pin 7 – GND.
  • Pin 1 and Pin 2 – OR inputs.
  • Pin 3 – OR output (Cout).

Connections

  1. Connect A and B to the first XOR gate.
  2. Connect the first XOR output to one input of the second XOR gate.
  3. Connect Cin to the other input of the second XOR gate.
  4. The second XOR output is Sum.
  5. Connect A and B to the first AND gate.
  6. Connect the first XOR output and Cin to the second AND gate.
  7. Connect both AND outputs to the OR gate.
  8. The OR gate output is Cout.
  9. Apply all eight input combinations and verify the truth table.
Use defined logic levels on all inputs. Check the manufacturer's datasheets for supply voltage, pinout and output-current limits. Use appropriate LED resistors and do not connect LEDs in a way that overloads the IC outputs.

16. Lesson Summary

  • A full adder adds three single-bit binary inputs.
  • Inputs are A, B and Cin.
  • Outputs are Sum and Cout.
  • Sum = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin.
  • Cout = AB + ACin + BCin.
  • It can be constructed using two half adders and one OR gate.
  • It can also be implemented using two XOR, two AND and one OR gate.
  • Multiple full adders can be connected to form multi-bit adders.
