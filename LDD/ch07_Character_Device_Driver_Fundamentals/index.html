Chapter Overview

Character device drivers are one of the fundamental types of Linux device drivers. They provide access to devices that commonly transfer data as a stream of bytes. Examples include serial ports, terminal devices and many embedded peripheral interfaces.

This chapter introduces character device architecture, device-number management and the relationship between major and minor numbers. It also explains the kernel APIs used to allocate and register device numbers before implementing a character driver.

Important Character Device Concepts

  • Character Device: A device accessed through a byte-oriented interface.
  • Major Number: Identifies the driver associated with a device number.
  • Minor Number: Identifies a particular device or instance handled by a driver.
  • dev_t: Kernel data type representing a device number.
  • MKDEV(): Creates a device number from major and minor values.
  • MAJOR(): Extracts the major number.
  • MINOR(): Extracts the minor number.
  • alloc_chrdev_region(): Dynamically allocates a range of device numbers.
  • register_chrdev_region(): Registers a specified range of device numbers.
  • unregister_chrdev_region(): Releases a registered device-number range.

Useful Linux Commands

  • ls -l /dev – Display device files and their major/minor numbers.
  • ls -l /dev/tty – Inspect a terminal device node.
  • cat /proc/devices – Display registered character and block device major numbers.
  • ls -l /dev/null – Inspect the null character device.
  • stat /dev/null – Display device-file metadata.
  • lsmod – Inspect loaded kernel modules.
  • dmesg – Inspect kernel messages from driver registration.
