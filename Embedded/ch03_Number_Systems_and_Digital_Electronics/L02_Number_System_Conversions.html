Lesson 02: Number System Conversions

Learn how to convert numbers between binary, octal, decimal and hexadecimal systems using positional expansion, repeated division, repeated multiplication and bit grouping.

1. Introduction

Number system conversion is the process of representing the same numerical value in a different number system.

In digital electronics and embedded systems, engineers frequently convert values between binary, decimal, octal and hexadecimal representations.

For example, the decimal value 45 can be represented as:

Number SystemRepresentation
Decimal(45)10
Binary(101101)2
Octal(55)8
Hexadecimal(2D)16

The numerical value remains unchanged. Only the representation changes.

3. Conversion from Any Base to Decimal

To convert a number from another base into decimal, multiply each digit by its positional weight and add the results.

General formula:

N = dnBn + ... + d1B1 + d0B0

Where B is the original number system's base.

3.1 Binary to Decimal

Example: Convert (1101)2 into decimal.

Binary digit1101
Position3210
Weight8421
Product8401

Decimal value:

= (1 × 8) + (1 × 4) + (0 × 2) + (1 × 1)

= 8 + 4 + 0 + 1

= (13)10

3.2 Octal to Decimal

Example: Convert (347)8 into decimal.

= (3 × 82) + (4 × 81) + (7 × 80)

= (3 × 64) + (4 × 8) + (7 × 1)

= 192 + 32 + 7

= (231)10

3.3 Hexadecimal to Decimal

Example: Convert (2AF)16 into decimal.

A = 10 and F = 15.

= (2 × 162) + (10 × 161) + (15 × 160)

= 512 + 160 + 15

= (687)10

Always start positional expansion from the rightmost integer digit with power zero. Move left by increasing the power by one.

4. Decimal to Binary Conversion

To convert a positive decimal integer into binary, repeatedly divide the number by 2 and record the remainders.

Method: Repeated Division by 2

  1. Divide the decimal number by 2.
  2. Record the remainder, which is either 0 or 1.
  3. Divide the quotient by 2 again.
  4. Continue until the quotient becomes zero.
  5. Read the remainders from bottom to top.

Example: Convert (25)10 into binary.

DivisionQuotientRemainder
25 ÷ 2121
12 ÷ 260
6 ÷ 230
3 ÷ 211
1 ÷ 201

Read the remainders from bottom to top:

(25)10 = (11001)2

Verification

(11001)2

= 16 + 8 + 0 + 0 + 1

= 25

Another Example: Convert (42)10 into binary.

DivisionQuotientRemainder

21 ÷ 2101
10 ÷ 250
5 ÷ 221
2 ÷ 210
1 ÷ 201

(42)10 = (101010)2

5. Decimal to Octal Conversion

To convert a decimal integer into octal, repeatedly divide it by 8 and record the remainders.

Example: Convert (156)10 into octal.

DivisionQuotientRemainder
156 ÷ 8194
19 ÷ 823
2 ÷ 802

Read the remainders from bottom to top:

(156)10 = (234)8

Verification

(234)8 = (2 × 64) + (3 × 8) + (4 × 1)

= 128 + 24 + 4

= 156

6. Decimal to Hexadecimal Conversion

To convert a decimal integer into hexadecimal, repeatedly divide it by 16.

Remainders from 10 to 15 are represented by A to F.

Example: Convert (687)10 into hexadecimal.

DivisionQuotientRemainderHex Digit
687 ÷ 164215F
42 ÷ 16210A
2 ÷ 16022

Read the hexadecimal remainders from bottom to top:

(687)10 = (2AF)16

Verification

(2AF)16 = (2 × 256) + (10 × 16) + 15

= 512 + 160 + 15

= 687

7. Binary to Octal Conversion

One octal digit represents exactly three binary bits.

Therefore, binary digits are grouped into sets of three, starting from the right for integer values.

Example: Convert (1101011)2 into octal.

Step 1: Group the binary digits from right to left.

Binary:  1 101 011

Groups: 001 101 011

Step 2: Convert each group into an octal digit.

Binary groupOctal digit
0011
1015
0113

(1101011)2 = (153)8

If the leftmost group has fewer than three bits, add leading zeros to complete the group. Leading zeros do not change the numerical value.

8. Octal to Binary Conversion

Replace every octal digit with its corresponding three-bit binary representation.

Example: Convert (725)8 into binary.

Combine the groups:

(725)8 = (111010101)2

9. Binary to Hexadecimal Conversion

One hexadecimal digit represents four binary bits.

Group the binary digits into sets of four, starting from the right for integer values.

Example: Convert (10111100)2 into hexadecimal.

Step 1: Group the bits.

1011 1100

Step 2: Convert each group.

Binary groupHexadecimal digit
1011B
1100C

(10111100)2 = (BC)16

Another Example

Convert (110101)2 into hexadecimal.

0011 0101

0011 = 3
0101 = 5

(110101)2 = (35)16

10. Hexadecimal to Binary Conversion

Replace each hexadecimal digit with its four-bit binary equivalent.

Example: Convert (2AF)16 into binary.

Hex digitFour-bit binary
20010
A1010
F1111

Combine the groups:

(2AF)16 = (001010101111)2

The leading zeros may be removed when writing the same unsigned numerical value without a fixed-width requirement:

(1010101111)2

When representing a fixed-width register or data type, retain all required leading zeros. For example, a 12-bit representation must contain exactly 12 bits.

11. Octal to Hexadecimal Conversion

There is no direct three-bit or four-bit grouping relationship between octal and hexadecimal. A convenient method is to convert through binary.

Example: Convert (725)8 into hexadecimal.

Step 1: Convert octal to binary.

7 = 111
2 = 010
5 = 101

Binary = 111010101

Step 2: Group binary digits into sets of four from the right.

0001 1101 0101

Step 3: Convert each group into hexadecimal.

Binary groupHex digit
00011
1101D
01015

(725)8 = (1D5)16

Convert hexadecimal to binary first, then group the binary digits into sets of three.

Example: Convert (2AF)16 into octal.

Step 1: Convert hexadecimal to binary.

2 = 0010
A = 1010
F = 1111

Binary = 001010101111

Step 2: Group into sets of three from the right.

Step 3: Convert each group into octal.

13. Conversion of Fractional Numbers

Number systems can also represent fractional values. Digits to the right of the radix point have negative positional powers.

13.1 Binary Fraction to Decimal

Example: Convert (101.101)2 into decimal.

Integer part:

(101)2 = 4 + 1 = 5

Fractional part:

(0.101)2 = (1 × 2-1) + (0 × 2-2) + (1 × 2-3)

= 0.5 + 0 + 0.125

= 0.625

Total:

(101.101)2 = (5.625)10

13.2 Decimal Fraction to Binary

To convert a positive decimal fraction less than 1 into binary, repeatedly multiply the fraction by 2.

Record the integer part of each product in order. Continue with the remaining fractional part.

Example: Convert (0.625)10 into binary.

MultiplicationProductInteger bitRemaining fraction
0.625 × 21.25010.250
0.250 × 20.50000.500
0.500 × 21.00010.000

Read the integer bits from top to bottom:

(0.625)10 = (0.101)2

13.3 Decimal Fraction to Binary with a Non-Terminating Result

Some decimal fractions cannot be represented exactly using a finite number of binary fractional bits.

For example, converting 0.1 in decimal to binary produces a repeating binary fraction:

(0.1)10 ≈ (0.0001100110011...)2

In practical digital systems, such values are represented using a finite number of bits, which introduces a rounding or representation error.

13.4 Fractional Octal and Hexadecimal Conversions

The positional-weight method also applies to octal and hexadecimal fractional numbers.

Example:

(12.4)8 = (1 × 81) + (2 × 80) + (4 × 8-1)

= 8 + 2 + 0.5

= (10.5)10

Hexadecimal example:

(A.8)16 = (10 × 160) + (8 × 16-1)

= 10 + 0.5

= (10.5)10

15. Common Mistakes

  • Reading repeated-division remainders from top to bottom instead of bottom to top.
  • Grouping binary digits from the wrong side for integer conversion.
  • Forgetting to add leading zeros to complete binary groups.
  • Using an invalid digit for a particular base.
  • Forgetting that A through F represent values 10 through 15.
  • Using powers of the wrong base during positional expansion.
  • Reading fractional binary multiplication bits in reverse order.
  • Removing leading zeros when a fixed-width representation is required.

16. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What are the common methods for converting a number into decimal?

Use positional expansion, multiplying each digit by the corresponding power of the original base.

Q2. How is decimal converted into binary?

Repeatedly divide the integer by 2 and read the remainders from bottom to top.

Q3. How many binary bits correspond to one octal digit?

Three bits.

Q4. How many binary bits correspond to one hexadecimal digit?

Four bits.

Q5. How do you convert binary into octal?

Group binary digits into sets of three from the right and convert each group into an octal digit.

Q6. How do you convert binary into hexadecimal?

Group binary digits into sets of four from the right and convert each group into a hexadecimal digit.

Q7. How do you convert octal into hexadecimal?

Convert octal into binary and then group the binary digits into sets of four.

Q8. How is a decimal fraction converted into binary?

Repeatedly multiply the fractional part by 2 and record each product's integer part in order.

Q9. Why can some decimal fractions not be represented exactly in binary?

Some decimal fractions have repeating binary expansions, so a finite number of bits can only approximate their values.

Q10. Why are number-system conversions important in embedded systems?

They help engineers interpret binary data, hexadecimal register values, memory addresses and digital signals.

19. Lesson Summary

  • Number system conversion changes the representation, not the numerical value.
  • Positional expansion converts a number from any base into decimal.
  • Repeated division converts positive decimal integers into another base.
  • Repeated multiplication converts decimal fractions into another base.
  • Binary and octal conversion uses groups of three bits.
  • Binary and hexadecimal conversion uses groups of four bits.
  • Octal and hexadecimal can be converted through binary.
  • Fractional values use negative powers of the base.
  • Some decimal fractions have repeating binary representations.
  • Number-system conversion is essential for reading and configuring digital hardware.
