Chapter 01 – Introduction to Embedded Systems

2. Introduction

Embedded systems are used in almost every modern industry. They are integrated into electronic products, machines, vehicles, communication equipment and medical devices.

These systems perform dedicated functions such as measurement, monitoring, communication, automation and control.

A typical embedded application combines a processor, memory, input/output interfaces, firmware and application-specific electronic circuits.

Example: A washing machine contains an embedded controller that reads user commands and sensor signals, manages washing cycles and controls the motor and water valves.

3. Automotive Applications

Modern automobiles contain multiple embedded systems responsible for engine control, braking, safety, navigation, comfort and communication.

3.1 Engine Control Unit (ECU)

An Engine Control Unit monitors engine operating conditions and controls functions such as fuel injection and ignition timing.

Inputs may include:

  • Engine temperature sensor.
  • Crankshaft position sensor.
  • Throttle position sensor.
  • Airflow or manifold pressure sensor.
  • Oxygen sensor.

Outputs may include:

  • Fuel injector control signals.
  • Ignition control signals.
  • Electronic throttle control signals.
  • Cooling fan control.

3.2 Anti-lock Braking System (ABS)

ABS uses wheel-speed sensors and an electronic controller to help prevent wheel lock during braking.

The controller processes wheel-speed information and controls hydraulic modulator valves according to the braking algorithm.

3.3 Airbag Control System

An airbag control unit processes information from crash sensors and other safety-related inputs. It determines whether deployment conditions have been met.

3.4 Other Automotive Applications

  • Electronic Power Steering (EPS).
  • Electronic Stability Control (ESC).
  • Automatic climate control.
  • Parking assistance systems.
  • Automotive infotainment systems.
  • Battery Management Systems (BMS) in electric vehicles.
  • Vehicle networking using CAN and automotive Ethernet.
Automotive embedded systems often require reliable operation, environmental protection, diagnostics and well-defined timing behavior. Safety-critical functions require appropriate engineering and validation.

4. Industrial Automation Applications

Embedded systems are widely used in industrial plants to monitor equipment, control machines and automate manufacturing processes.

4.1 Programmable Logic Controller (PLC)

A PLC is an industrial controller designed to monitor input signals and control industrial processes.

Typical applications:

  • Conveyor belt control.
  • Motor starting and stopping.
  • Packaging machines.
  • Assembly-line automation.
  • Industrial process monitoring.

4.2 Industrial Motor Control

Embedded controllers are used to control motor speed, direction, acceleration and protection.

Common technologies include:

  • Pulse Width Modulation (PWM).
  • Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs).
  • Current and voltage sensing.
  • Overcurrent and overvoltage protection.
  • Temperature monitoring.

4.3 Robotics

Industrial robots use embedded controllers to coordinate motors, sensors, actuators and movement.

4.4 Process Control

Embedded controllers monitor industrial variables such as temperature, pressure, flow and liquid level.

Applications include:

  • Boiler temperature control.
  • Water treatment plants.
  • Chemical process monitoring.
  • Industrial furnace control.
  • Pressure and flow regulation.

5. Medical and Healthcare Applications

Embedded systems are used in medical equipment for patient monitoring, measurement, diagnosis and treatment-related control.

5.1 Patient Monitoring Systems

Patient monitors acquire physiological measurements and display them for healthcare professionals.

Common measurements include:

  • Heart rate.
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG).
  • Blood oxygen saturation (SpO2).
  • Blood pressure.
  • Body temperature.

5.2 Infusion Pumps

Infusion pumps use embedded controllers to manage the delivery of fluids or medication according to configured parameters.

5.3 Ventilators

Ventilators use electronic control systems to manage airflow, pressure, timing and monitoring functions.

5.4 Other Medical Applications

  • Blood glucose meters.
  • Digital thermometers.
  • Ultrasound equipment.
  • Medical imaging equipment.
  • Portable diagnostic devices.
  • Wearable health monitoring devices.
Medical embedded systems require careful design, verification, risk management and compliance with applicable medical-device requirements.

7. Telecommunications and Networking

Communication equipment uses embedded systems to process data, manage communication protocols and control network hardware.

7.1 Network Routers

Routers use embedded processors and software to forward network packets between different networks.

7.2 Network Switches

Network switches connect devices within a local network and forward Ethernet frames.

7.3 Wireless Communication Equipment

12. Energy and Power Electronics Applications

Embedded controllers play an important role in electrical power conversion, energy monitoring and power-system control.

Applications

  • Solar inverter control.
  • Battery Management Systems.
  • Electric vehicle charging stations.
  • Digital energy meters.
  • Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS).
  • Power factor correction systems.
  • Motor drives and industrial converters.

These applications may involve ADC measurement, PWM generation, voltage and current sensing, communication and protection functions.

Embedded systems connect electronics and firmware: a power converter may use sensors and ADC channels to measure electrical quantities while a controller generates PWM signals for switching devices.

14. Practical Example: Automatic Temperature-Controlled Fan

A temperature-controlled fan is a simple example of an embedded system used in consumer electronics and industrial applications.

Required Components

  • Microcontroller.
  • Temperature sensor.
  • Fan or DC motor.
  • Motor driver circuit.
  • Power supply.

Working Principle

  1. The temperature sensor measures the surrounding temperature.
  2. The microcontroller reads the sensor output using an ADC or digital interface.
  3. The firmware compares the temperature with configured thresholds.
  4. The controller generates a control signal.
  5. The motor driver adjusts the fan operation.

Example Control Logic

if (temperature < 25) { fan_speed = 0; } else if (temperature < 30) { fan_speed = 30; } else if (temperature < 35) { fan_speed = 60; } else { fan_speed = 100; }

The values represent illustrative fan-speed percentages. Actual speed control requires a suitable PWM output and motor driver.

15. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is an ECU?

An Electronic Control Unit is an embedded controller used to manage a specific function or subsystem in a vehicle.

Q2. What is ABS?

Anti-lock Braking System is an automotive safety system that helps prevent wheel lock during braking.

Q3. What is a PLC?

A Programmable Logic Controller is an industrial controller used to monitor inputs and control industrial processes.

Q4. How are embedded systems used in medical equipment?

They are used for measurement, patient monitoring, signal processing, diagnostics and equipment control.

Q5. What is an IoT embedded device?

It is an embedded device that uses communication capabilities to exchange data with other devices or network services.

Q6. How are embedded systems used in power electronics?

They perform measurement, PWM generation, switching control, protection and communication functions.

Q7. What is a BMS?

A Battery Management System monitors and manages battery parameters such as voltage, current, temperature and state of charge.

Q8. What is the role of embedded systems in robotics?

Embedded controllers process sensor data and coordinate actuators and motors to achieve the required robotic movement.

Q9. Why are embedded systems important in automotive applications?

They provide electronic control, monitoring, safety-related functions, communication and automation in modern vehicles.

Q10. Give examples of embedded systems in smart agriculture.

Soil moisture sensors, automatic irrigation controllers, greenhouse monitoring systems and weather stations.

18. Lesson Summary

Embedded systems are used across automotive, industrial, medical, consumer electronics, telecommunications, aerospace, defence, banking, energy and IoT applications.

Their primary functions include monitoring, measurement, signal processing, communication, automation and control.

Microcontrollers, processors, sensors, actuators, communication interfaces and firmware work together to provide application-specific functionality.
