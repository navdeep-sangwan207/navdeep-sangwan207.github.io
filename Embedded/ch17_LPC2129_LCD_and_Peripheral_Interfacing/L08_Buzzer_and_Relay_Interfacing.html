2. Introduction to Buzzers and Relays

A buzzer is an output device that produces an audible signal. It is commonly used to provide alarms, warnings and user feedback.

A relay is an electrically controlled switch. It allows a low-voltage control circuit to operate another electrical circuit through isolated switching contacts.

DeviceMain FunctionTypical Application
BuzzerProduces soundAlarm and warning indication
RelaySwitches an electrical circuitLoad control and automation
The LPC2129 GPIO provides control signals. External driver circuitry is normally required to operate devices that draw more current or use voltages beyond the GPIO electrical limits.

3. Buzzer Architecture

A buzzer converts electrical energy into sound. Depending on its construction, it may use an electromagnetic mechanism or a piezoelectric element.

Common Buzzer Types

3.1 Active Buzzer

An active buzzer contains an internal oscillator or drive circuit. Applying its specified DC supply causes it to produce a tone.

  • Requires a suitable DC supply.
  • Internal oscillator generates the sound frequency.
  • Simple ON/OFF control is possible.
  • Sound frequency is generally fixed or internally determined.

3.2 Passive Buzzer

A passive buzzer requires an external alternating or pulsed signal to generate sound. The frequency of the applied signal determines the tone.

  • Requires an external drive waveform.
  • Can produce different tones by changing frequency.
  • Can be controlled using timer-generated signals or PWM.
  • Its voltage and frequency limits depend on the device datasheet.
FeatureActive BuzzerPassive Buzzer
Internal oscillatorUsually presentUsually absent
ControlDC ON/OFFExternal waveform
Tone frequencyGenerally fixedControlled by drive frequency
Typical useSimple alarmsVariable tones and melodies

4. Active Buzzer Interfacing

An active buzzer can be controlled by switching its supply through a transistor or a suitable driver module.

LPC2129 GPIO
↓
Transistor Driver
↓
Active Buzzer
↓
Audible Output

Operating Principle

  1. The LPC2129 sets its GPIO control output HIGH.
  2. The transistor driver turns ON.
  3. The buzzer receives its rated supply current.
  4. The buzzer produces sound.
  5. The GPIO is set LOW to turn the driver OFF.
Some buzzer modules include their own transistor driver. Check the module schematic and input specifications before connecting it directly to a GPIO output.

5. Passive Buzzer and Frequency Control

A passive buzzer requires a changing electrical signal. A square wave can be used to drive a suitable passive buzzer.

Important Circuit Components

  • NPN transistor or suitable logic-level MOSFET.
  • Base resistor for a BJT, or appropriate gate drive for a MOSFET.
  • Rated buzzer supply.
  • Common ground between control and driver circuits when using a non-isolated low-side driver.

If the buzzer is inductive, such as an electromagnetic buzzer, a suitable flyback suppression component may be needed. A piezoelectric element is primarily capacitive and does not require a coil flyback diode in the same way.

7. LPC2129 Active Buzzer Control Program

The following program demonstrates ON/OFF control of an active buzzer driver using P0.5. The GPIO is connected to the driver input, not directly to an unspecified buzzer load. 

#include <LPC21xx.h>

#define BUZZER_PIN (1U << 5)

void Delay(volatile unsigned int count)
{
    while (count--)
    {
        /* Demonstration delay */
    }
}

void Buzzer_Init(void)
{
    /* Configure P0.5 as GPIO */
    PINSEL0 &= ~(3U << 10);

    /* Configure P0.5 as output */
    IODIR0 |= BUZZER_PIN;

    /* Driver initially OFF */
    IOCLR0 = BUZZER_PIN;
}

void Buzzer_ON(void)
{
    IOSET0 = BUZZER_PIN;
}

void Buzzer_OFF(void)
{
    IOCLR0 = BUZZER_PIN;
}

int main(void)
{
    Buzzer_Init();

    while (1)
    {
        Buzzer_ON();
        Delay(500000U);

        Buzzer_OFF();
        Delay(500000U);
    }
}

Program Explanation

  • BUZZER_PIN identifies P0.5.
  • Buzzer_Init() selects GPIO and configures output direction.
  • Buzzer_ON() sets the driver-control output HIGH.
  • Buzzer_OFF() clears the driver-control output LOW.
  • The main loop repeatedly switches the buzzer driver ON and OFF.
This example assumes an active-HIGH transistor-driver input. Some modules are active-LOW; reverse the control logic if required by the module design. The software delay is not calibrated to a specific audible ON/OFF duration.

8. Introduction to Relays

A relay is an electrically operated switch. A typical electromechanical relay uses an electromagnetic coil to move a mechanical armature between contacts.

Main Relay Components

  • Coil: produces a magnetic field when energized.
  • Armature: moves in response to the magnetic field.
  • Common contact (COM): movable electrical contact.
  • Normally Open (NO): open when the coil is de-energized.
  • Normally Closed (NC): closed when the coil is de-energized.
  • Spring: returns the armature when the coil is de-energized.

9. Relay Contact Types

Normally Open (NO)

The NO contact is open when the relay coil is not energized. It closes when the relay is energized.

Normally Closed (NC)

The NC contact is closed when the relay coil is not energized. It opens when the relay is energized.

Changeover Relay

A single-pole double-throw relay commonly has COM, NO and NC contacts. The common contact switches between the two fixed contacts.

Relay StateCOM-NOCOM-NC
Coil OFFOpenClosed
Coil ONClosedOpen

10. Why a Relay Cannot Normally Be Driven Directly by GPIO

A relay coil requires a specific operating voltage and current. A microcontroller GPIO is designed for logic-level signaling and has limited source and sink current capability.

Direct coil connection can cause:

  • Excessive GPIO current.
  • Unreliable relay operation.
  • Voltage disturbances on the supply.
  • Damage to the microcontroller output stage.
  • Inductive voltage transients when the coil is switched OFF.
Use an external transistor, MOSFET or dedicated relay-driver IC to switch the relay coil. Select the driver based on the coil voltage, coil current and switching requirements.

Operating Sequence

  1. GPIO output is LOW: transistor is OFF.
  2. Relay coil is de-energized.
  3. GPIO output becomes HIGH: base current flows through the resistor.
  4. Transistor turns ON and allows coil current to flow.
  5. Relay energizes and changes contact state.
  6. GPIO becomes LOW: transistor turns OFF and the relay releases.
The diagram is conceptual. Choose the transistor, base resistor, coil supply and diode from the actual relay and driver datasheets. The transistor must tolerate the coil current and its switching dissipation.

12. Flyback Diode Protection

A relay coil is an inductive load. When the transistor turns OFF, the collapsing magnetic field produces a voltage that opposes the change in current. This can generate a high voltage transient.

A flyback diode connected across a DC relay coil provides a path for the coil current after the driver switches OFF. This limits the voltage transient across the switching transistor. 

       +VRELAY
          |
       RELAY COIL
          |
          +--------- Collector of transistor
          |
          |
      DIODE
  Cathode | Anode
    to +V | to coil low side
          |
         GND through driver

Diode Orientation

  • Cathode connects to the positive coil supply.
  • Anode connects to the transistor-side coil terminal.
  • During normal energization, the diode is reverse-biased.
  • When the coil is switched OFF, the diode conducts the decaying coil current.
A simple flyback diode reduces the voltage transient but can increase relay release time. If fast release is required, a suitable TVS, Zener or dedicated clamp network may be selected according to the driver and relay specifications.

13. LPC2129 GPIO Configuration for Relay Control

The example uses P0.10 as a relay-driver control output. This pin must be checked against the rest of the project because it may already be assigned to another peripheral. 

#include <LPC21xx.h>

#define RELAY_PIN (1U << 10)

void Relay_Init(void)
{
    /* Select GPIO function for P0.10 */
    PINSEL0 &= ~(3U << 20);

    /* Configure output direction */
    IODIR0 |= RELAY_PIN;

    /* Relay driver initially OFF */
    IOCLR0 = RELAY_PIN;
}

void Relay_ON(void)
{
    IOSET0 = RELAY_PIN;
}

void Relay_OFF(void)
{
    IOCLR0 = RELAY_PIN;
}

The GPIO signal controls the transistor driver. The relay coil is powered from its own correctly rated supply.

15. Buzzer and Relay Combined Application

A buzzer and relay can be combined in an alarm or control system. For example, an over-temperature condition can activate a buzzer and switch a cooling device through a relay.

if (temperature >= TEMP_LIMIT)
{
    Buzzer_ON();
    Relay_ON();

else
{

This is an application-level logic example. A real system also needs sensor validation, hysteresis, fault handling, safe startup states and correctly rated driver circuits.

16. Electrical and Safety Considerations

  • Check the LPC2129 GPIO voltage and current limits.
  • Do not connect a relay coil directly to a GPIO pin.
  • Use a driver transistor or suitable relay-driver IC.
  • Use a flyback diode or an appropriate coil suppression network for DC relays.
  • Use a separate regulated coil supply when required.
  • Connect grounds correctly in a non-isolated driver circuit.
  • Choose contact ratings for the voltage, current and load type.
  • Account for inrush current and inductive load switching.
  • Use proper fusing, enclosure, creepage and clearance for hazardous voltages.
  • Never handle exposed mains wiring while powered.
Relay contact isolation does not make an unsafe circuit automatically safe. Mains-voltage work requires correctly rated components, insulation, fusing, enclosure design and qualified electrical practice. Keep mains circuits separate from the low-voltage microcontroller development setup.

19. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is a buzzer?

A buzzer is an audible output device that converts electrical energy into sound.

Q2. What is the difference between an active and passive buzzer?

An active buzzer generally contains an internal oscillator and can sound from a suitable DC supply. A passive buzzer needs an external drive waveform.

Q3. What is a relay?

A relay is an electrically controlled switch, commonly operated by an electromagnetic coil.

Q4. What are NO and NC contacts?

NO is open when the coil is OFF; NC is closed when the coil is OFF.

Q5. Why is a transistor used with a relay?

It switches the relay coil current without requiring the microcontroller GPIO to supply that current.

Q6. What is a flyback diode?

It provides a current path for an inductive coil when its driver is switched OFF, limiting the voltage transient.

Q7. What happens if a flyback diode is connected in the wrong direction?

It may conduct during normal coil energization and cause excessive current or circuit damage.

Q8. Can a GPIO pin drive a relay coil directly?

Normally no. A properly rated external driver is required.

Q9. What is a relay driver?

A transistor, MOSFET or driver IC that switches the relay coil using a low-power control signal.

Q10. Why can a relay cause microcontroller resets?

Coil switching and contact switching can create supply disturbances and electromagnetic interference.

23. Lesson Summary

  • Buzzers provide audible feedback and alarms.
  • Active buzzers generally contain an internal oscillator.
  • Passive buzzers require an external drive waveform.
  • Relays operate as electrically controlled switches.
  • NO and NC contacts have different de-energized states.
  • A transistor or suitable driver IC switches relay coil current.
  • A DC relay coil needs appropriate transient suppression.
  • A flyback diode protects the driver from inductive voltage transients.
  • LPC2129 GPIO provides the control signal, not the relay coil power.
  • Electrical load ratings and isolation requirements must be observed.
