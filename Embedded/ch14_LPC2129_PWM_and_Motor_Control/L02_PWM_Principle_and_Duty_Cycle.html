Lesson 02: PWM Principle and Duty Cycle

Study the operation of PWM signals, pulse width, duty cycle, frequency, time period, average voltage, and waveform calculations used in embedded systems.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain the operating principle of PWM.
  • Identify the HIGH and LOW portions of a PWM waveform.
  • Calculate ON-time, OFF-time, and total period.
  • Calculate PWM frequency and duty cycle.
  • Understand the effect of duty cycle on average voltage.
  • Compare PWM waveforms at different duty cycles.
  • Perform PWM timing calculations for embedded applications.

2. PWM Operating Principle

PWM controls the average power delivered to a load by switching a digital output between HIGH and LOW states. The switching waveform repeats at a defined frequency.

The output remains HIGH for a selected duration and LOW for the rest of the period. The HIGH duration is called the pulse width or ON-time.

Operating principle: A PWM controller varies the pulse width within a repeating time period to control the ratio of ON-time to total period.

One Complete PWM Cycle

Voltage
  HIGH     ┌──────────┐
           │          │
  LOW  ────┘          └────────────────
           <--- ON ---><---- OFF ---->

           |<------ Period T -------->|

A PWM waveform is periodic. Once one period finishes, the next period begins and the same timing pattern repeats unless the controller changes its configuration.

3. PWM Timing Parameters

3.1 ON-Time (TON)

ON-time is the interval during which the PWM output remains in its active HIGH state.

3.2 OFF-Time (TOFF)

OFF-time is the interval during which the PWM output remains in its LOW state.

3.3 Total Period (T)

The period is the time taken to complete one entire PWM cycle.

T = TON + TOFF

3.4 Frequency (f)

Frequency is the number of complete PWM cycles generated in one second.

f = 1 / T

If the period is measured in seconds, the frequency is obtained in Hertz.

4. Understanding Duty Cycle

Duty cycle indicates how much of one PWM period is spent in the HIGH state. It is expressed as a percentage.

Duty Cycle (%) = (TON / T) × 100

Where:

  • TON = HIGH-state duration.
  • T = total PWM period.

OFF-Time Calculation

TOFF = T - TON

Duty Cycle as a Decimal

D = TON / T

For example, a 40% duty cycle is represented as 0.40 in decimal form.

Convention: This lesson defines duty cycle using the HIGH portion of the PWM waveform. Some active-LOW applications define the useful load activation interval differently, so always identify the signal polarity.

5. PWM Waveforms at Different Duty Cycles

5.1 25% Duty Cycle

HIGH  ┌────┐
      │    │
LOW ──┘    └────────────────────────
      ON = 25% of period
      OFF = 75% of period

5.2 50% Duty Cycle

HIGH  ┌────────┐
      │        │
LOW ──┘        └────────────────────
      ON = 50% of period
      OFF = 50% of period

5.3 75% Duty Cycle

HIGH  ┌────────────────┐
      │                │
LOW ──┘                └────────────
      ON = 75% of period
      OFF = 25% of period

5.4 100% Duty Cycle

HIGH  ──────────────────────────────
      Output remains HIGH

5.5 0% Duty Cycle

LOW   ──────────────────────────────
      Output remains LOW

At intermediate duty cycles, the output repeatedly switches between HIGH and LOW. At the ideal 0% and 100% endpoints, the output is continuously LOW or HIGH respectively.

6. Relationship Between Frequency and Duty Cycle

Frequency and duty cycle describe different properties of a PWM waveform.

  • Frequency determines how quickly the waveform repeats.
  • Duty cycle determines the fraction of each period spent HIGH.
  • Changing duty cycle does not necessarily change frequency.
  • Changing frequency does not necessarily change duty cycle.

Same Frequency, Different Duty Cycles

      │  │      │  │      │  │
──────┘  └──────┘  └──────┘  └────

50%:  ┌────┐    ┌────┐    ┌────┐
      │    │    │    │    │    │

75%:  ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐  ┌──────┐
      │      │  │      │  │      │
──────┘      └──┘      └──┘      └

The period remains the same in these examples. Only the pulse width changes.

7. Average Voltage Calculation

For an ideal PWM signal switching between 0 V and VHIGH, the average voltage over one complete period is:

VAVG = VHIGH × D

Here, D is the duty cycle expressed as a decimal between 0 and 1.

Example

A 3.3 V PWM output has a duty cycle of 40%.

VAVG = 3.3 × 0.40 = 1.32 V
Result: The ideal average voltage is 1.32 V.

The instantaneous output is still switching between 0 V and 3.3 V. A low-pass filter or the load's electrical and mechanical response may smooth the effect of this switching.

For a waveform with a nonzero LOW voltage, use:
VAVG = D × VHIGH + (1-D) × VLOW.

8. Solved Numerical Problems

Example 1: Duty Cycle

A PWM signal has ON-time of 2 ms and OFF-time of 6 ms. Calculate its duty cycle.

Step 1: Calculate total period.

T = 2 + 6 = 8 ms

Step 2: Calculate duty cycle.

D = (2 / 8) × 100 = 25%
Answer: Duty cycle = 25%.

Example 2: Frequency

A PWM signal has a period of 250 microseconds. Calculate its frequency.

f = 1 / (250 × 10-6) = 4000 Hz = 4 kHz
Answer: Frequency = 4 kHz.

Example 3: ON-Time

A PWM signal operates at 2 kHz with a duty cycle of 30%. Calculate ON-time.

Step 1: Calculate period.

T = 1 / 2000 = 0.5 ms

Step 2: Calculate ON-time.

TON = 0.5 × 0.30 = 0.15 ms
Answer: ON-time = 150 µs.

Example 4: OFF-Time

A PWM signal has a period of 4 ms and a duty cycle of 65%. Calculate OFF-time.

Step 1: Calculate ON-time.

TON = 4 × 0.65 = 2.6 ms

Step 2: Calculate OFF-time.

TOFF = 4 - 2.6 = 1.4 ms
Answer: OFF-time = 1.4 ms.

Example 5: Average Voltage

A 5 V PWM waveform has a duty cycle of 75%. Calculate its ideal average voltage.

VAVG = 5 × 0.75 = 3.75 V
Answer: Average voltage = 3.75 V.

9. Practical PWM Applications

9.1 LED Brightness Control

Increasing the duty cycle increases the average electrical power delivered to a compatible LED circuit. The perceived brightness generally increases as well.

9.2 DC Motor Speed Control

A PWM signal controls a motor driver. The driver switches motor power, and changing duty cycle changes the average voltage applied to the motor.

9.3 Switching Power Supply

A switching controller adjusts its PWM duty cycle to regulate energy transfer and maintain the desired output.

9.4 Fan Speed Control

PWM can control compatible DC fans through their specified power or control interface.

10. PWM in LPC2129

LPC2129 generates PWM using its dedicated hardware peripheral. The peripheral uses a timer counter and match registers to control the PWM period and channel pulse widths.

  • MR0 is used for the PWM period.
  • MR1 to MR6 are associated with PWM channel match values.
  • PWM control registers select enabled PWM channels and modes.
  • Latch Enable Register transfers updated match values at the appropriate PWM boundary.
  • PINSEL configuration connects selected pins to PWM functions.
The LPC2129 PWM peripheral has six output channels. The actual output pin selection and available single-edge or double-edge operation depend on the device pin multiplexing and PWM channel configuration. These details are covered in the next lessons.

11. Common Mistakes

  • Using ON-time instead of total period in the duty-cycle formula.
  • Confusing frequency with duty cycle.
  • Forgetting to convert milliseconds or microseconds into seconds when calculating frequency in Hz.
  • Using a percentage directly where a decimal is required.
  • Assuming the PWM pin produces a steady analog voltage.
  • Assuming a motor can be connected directly to a microcontroller pin.
  • Changing the period without recalculating the required pulse width.

12. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is the duty-cycle formula?

Duty cycle = (ON-time / total period) × 100.

Q2. What is the relationship between period and frequency?

Frequency is the reciprocal of the period: f = 1/T.

Q3. What happens when duty cycle increases?

The HIGH portion of each period increases. For an ideal fixed-voltage PWM waveform, its average voltage increases.

Q4. Can frequency change while duty cycle remains constant?

Yes. The ON-time and OFF-time can both be scaled while maintaining the same ratio.

Q5. Can duty cycle change while frequency remains constant?

Yes. The ON-time can change while the total period remains fixed.

Q6. What is a 50% duty cycle?

The signal remains HIGH for half of the total period and LOW for the other half.

Q7. What is the average voltage of a 3.3 V PWM signal at 25% duty?

3.3 × 0.25 = 0.825 V, under ideal 0 V to 3.3 V switching.

Q8. Why is PWM useful in motor control?

It allows the average electrical power delivered through a motor driver to be controlled efficiently.

15. Lesson Summary

  • PWM controls average power using a digital switching waveform.
  • ON-time is the duration of the HIGH state.
  • OFF-time is the duration of the LOW state.
  • Total period equals ON-time plus OFF-time.
  • Frequency equals the reciprocal of the period.
  • Duty cycle is the percentage of the period spent HIGH.
  • Frequency and duty cycle are independent waveform parameters.
  • Average voltage depends on duty cycle and switching levels.
  • LPC2129 uses a dedicated hardware PWM peripheral.
