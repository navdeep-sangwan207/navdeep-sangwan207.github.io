Lesson 01: Introduction to PWM

Understand Pulse Width Modulation, its operating principle, waveform characteristics, frequency, duty cycle, applications, and use in LPC2129-based embedded systems.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Define Pulse Width Modulation (PWM).
  • Explain how PWM controls average power.
  • Understand HIGH and LOW periods of a PWM signal.
  • Differentiate ON-time, OFF-time, and total period.
  • Understand PWM frequency and duty cycle.
  • Identify applications of PWM in embedded systems.
  • Describe the basic role of the LPC2129 PWM peripheral.

2. What is PWM?

PWM stands for Pulse Width Modulation. It is a technique used in electronic and embedded systems to control the average power delivered to a load by changing the width of digital pulses.

Definition: Pulse Width Modulation is a method of controlling the average power delivered to a load by varying the ON-time of a periodic digital signal while maintaining its switching levels.

A PWM signal switches between two logic levels:

  • HIGH state: The output is at its active voltage level.
  • LOW state: The output is at its inactive voltage level.

Instead of continuously changing the output voltage, PWM rapidly switches the output ON and OFF. The ratio of ON-time to the total signal period determines the duty cycle.

3. Basic Working Principle of PWM

Consider a digital output connected to a suitable load. The output is switched HIGH for a certain duration and then switched LOW for the remaining duration of each cycle.

By changing the HIGH duration while controlling the total period, the average voltage and power delivered to the load can be controlled.

Basic PWM Signal

HIGH  ┌────────┐          ┌────────┐
      │        │          │        │
LOW ──┘        └──────────┘        └────
      <------ One period ------>

      ON-time  = HIGH duration
      OFF-time = LOW duration
      Period   = ON-time + OFF-time

The output voltage does not need to remain at an intermediate level. It switches between HIGH and LOW, but the average voltage depends on the relative duration of each state.

Important: PWM does not normally generate a continuously varying analog voltage directly. It generates a digital switching waveform. The load or an appropriate low-pass filter responds to its average value.

4. Important PWM Parameters

4.1 ON-Time (TON)

ON-time is the duration for which the PWM output remains HIGH during one complete cycle.

4.2 OFF-Time (TOFF)

OFF-time is the duration for which the PWM output remains LOW during one complete cycle.

4.3 Time Period (T)

The time period is the total time required to complete one PWM cycle.

T = TON + TOFF

4.4 Frequency (f)

Frequency represents the number of complete PWM cycles generated per second. Its unit is Hertz (Hz).

f = 1 / T

If the period is 1 millisecond:

f = 1 / 0.001 = 1000 Hz = 1 kHz

4.5 Duty Cycle

Duty cycle is the percentage of the total PWM period during which the signal remains HIGH.

Duty Cycle (%) = (TON / T) × 100

Common duty-cycle values are:

Duty Cycle ON-Time OFF-Time Meaning
0% 0% 100% Always LOW
25% 25% 75% Short HIGH pulse
50% 50% 50% Equal HIGH and LOW time
75% 75% 25% Long HIGH pulse
100% 100% 0% Always HIGH

As the duty cycle increases, the HIGH duration becomes longer. If the supply voltage and switching frequency remain constant, the average voltage across an appropriate load increases.

6. Average Output Voltage in PWM

For an ideal PWM waveform switching between 0 V and a supply voltage VHIGH, the average voltage over one period is:

VAVG = VHIGH × (Duty Cycle / 100)

This relationship assumes an ideal waveform, a LOW level of 0 V, and a duty cycle defined by the HIGH portion of the period.

Example

Assume the following values:

  • HIGH voltage = 3.3 V
  • Duty cycle = 50%
VAVG = 3.3 × 0.50 = 1.65 V

Therefore, the ideal average voltage is 1.65 V.

Another Example: 75% Duty Cycle

For a 3.3 V PWM signal with a 75% duty cycle:

VAVG = 3.3 × 0.75 = 2.475 V
Practical distinction: The instantaneous output still switches between 0 V and 3.3 V. The calculated average is not necessarily the voltage measured directly at the PWM pin by every instrument. A low-pass filter can produce a smoother analog-like voltage.

7. PWM Signal vs Analog Signal

Parameter PWM Signal Analog Signal
Signal type Digital switching waveform Continuously varying signal
Voltage levels Typically two switching levels Can vary across a voltage range
Control method Changes pulse width Changes signal amplitude
Common applications Motor control, LED dimming, power conversion Audio, sensor signals, analog control
Conversion Can be filtered to obtain an analog-like voltage Already represents an analog quantity

PWM is useful because many digital controllers can generate precise switching signals without requiring a dedicated analog output for every control task.

8. Applications of PWM in Embedded Systems

8.1 LED Brightness Control

PWM controls the average power delivered to an LED. Changing the duty cycle changes its perceived brightness.

8.2 DC Motor Speed Control

PWM is commonly used with a motor driver to control the average voltage applied to a DC motor. Changing the duty cycle changes the motor's operating point and can regulate its speed.

8.3 Fan Speed Control

PWM can regulate the power supplied to compatible DC fans through a suitable driver or control interface.

8.4 Switching Power Supplies

PWM is used in switching regulators and converters to control energy transfer and regulate output voltage or current.

8.5 Servo Control

Some servo systems use pulse-width-coded control signals. The required pulse repetition rate and pulse widths depend on the servo specification.

9. Introduction to LPC2129 PWM Peripheral

The LPC2129 microcontroller from NXP provides a hardware PWM peripheral. It can generate multiple PWM output signals without requiring the CPU to manually toggle a GPIO pin for every HIGH and LOW transition.

The LPC2129 PWM peripheral is based on timer and match-register operation. It uses a period match and channel match values to determine when PWM outputs change state.

Important LPC2129 PWM Features

  • One PWM peripheral with six PWM output channels.
  • Seven match registers: MR0 to MR6.
  • MR0 is commonly used to define the PWM period.
  • MR1 to MR6 provide match values for PWM channels.
  • Supports single-edge and double-edge PWM operation.
  • Provides PWM control and latch-enable mechanisms.
  • Output pins require appropriate PINSEL configuration.

Conceptual LPC2129 PWM Operation

             Peripheral Clock (PCLK)

The PWM peripheral uses hardware timing and match events to generate the output waveform. This reduces the need for software-controlled pin toggling and allows the CPU to perform other tasks.

Note: Detailed LPC2129 PWM registers, pin configuration, match registers, period setup, and programming will be covered in the following lessons.

10. Hardware PWM vs Software PWM

Feature Hardware PWM Software PWM
Generation Dedicated peripheral CPU instructions and GPIO control
CPU usage Low after configuration Higher during waveform generation
Timing stability Generally more consistent Can be affected by interrupts and execution time
CPU availability Available for other operations May need frequent timing service
Typical use Motor control and power control Simple applications or devices without PWM hardware

Hardware PWM is generally preferred when stable timing, higher switching frequency, or multiple PWM channels are required.

11. Solved Numerical Examples

Example 1: Calculate Duty Cycle

A PWM signal has an ON-time of 2 ms and an OFF-time of 6 ms. Calculate its duty cycle.

Step 1: Calculate the period.

T = 2 + 6 = 8 ms

Step 2: Calculate the duty cycle.

Duty Cycle = (2 / 8) × 100 = 25%
Answer: The duty cycle is 25%.

Example 2: Calculate PWM Frequency

A PWM signal has a period of 500 microseconds. Calculate its frequency.

f = 1 / (500 × 10-6)
f = 2000 Hz = 2 kHz
Answer: The PWM frequency is 2 kHz.

Example 3: Calculate ON-Time

A PWM signal has a frequency of 1 kHz and a duty cycle of 60%. Calculate its ON-time.

Step 1: Calculate the period.

T = 1 / 1000 = 1 ms

Step 2: Calculate ON-time.

TON = 1 ms × 0.60 = 0.6 ms
Answer: ON-time = 0.6 ms.

12. Advantages of PWM

  • Efficient power control through switching operation.
  • Precise control of duty cycle.
  • Suitable for motor and LED control.
  • Can be generated using hardware peripherals.
  • Reduces the need for continuous analog output control.
  • Supports multiple output channels on suitable controllers.

13. Limitations and Design Considerations

  • Switching frequency must be suitable for the connected load.
  • High switching frequency can increase switching losses and electromagnetic interference.
  • Low switching frequency may cause visible flicker in LEDs or audible noise in some motors.
  • A motor or other high-current load requires a suitable driver stage.
  • PWM output voltage levels must be compatible with the receiving circuit.
  • Hardware dead-time and protection requirements must be considered in power-electronics applications.
Motor safety: Never connect a DC motor directly to an LPC2129 GPIO or PWM pin. Use a properly rated transistor, MOSFET, or H-bridge motor driver and an appropriate external power supply.

14. Interview Questions and Answers

Q1. What is PWM?

PWM is a technique used to control average power by varying the ON-time of a periodic digital signal.

Q2. What is duty cycle?

Duty cycle is the percentage of one complete PWM period during which the signal remains HIGH.

Q3. What is the formula for PWM frequency?

Frequency is the reciprocal of the time period: f = 1/T.

Q4. What happens when duty cycle increases?

The HIGH duration increases. For a fixed supply voltage and ideal PWM waveform, the average output voltage also increases.

Q5. Can PWM generate an analog voltage?

PWM itself produces a digital switching waveform. A suitable low-pass filter can convert it into a smoother analog-like voltage.

Q6. Why is PWM used for DC motor speed control?

PWM controls the average voltage and power delivered through a motor driver, allowing the motor operating speed to be regulated.

Q7. What is the difference between frequency and duty cycle?

Frequency indicates how many cycles occur per second. Duty cycle indicates the percentage of each cycle spent in the HIGH state.

Q8. How many PWM output channels are available in LPC2129?

The LPC2129 provides six PWM output channels.

Q9. What is the role of MR0 in LPC2129 PWM?

MR0 is commonly used to define the PWM period through the match event that controls the PWM cycle.

Q10. Why is hardware PWM preferred over software PWM?

Hardware PWM provides more consistent timing with lower CPU involvement after configuration.
